# Moses: Freedom From Slavery

A fugitive shepherd encounters a miraculous burning bush in the wilderness. He is commanded to return to the kingdom he fled to confront the most powerful ruler on earth. This humble man's trust will lead to the liberation of an entire nation from centuries of slavery.

## The Oppression of Israel

The descendants of Jacob were fruitful and multiplied greatly in Egypt (Exodus 1:7). A new king arose over Egypt who did not know Joseph and feared the growing number of Israelites (Exodus 1:8-9). He set taskmasters over them to afflict them with heavy burdens (Exodus 1:11). The Egyptians made the lives of the people bitter with hard service in mortar and brick (Exodus 1:14). Pharaoh commanded that every Hebrew son born be cast into the Nile (Exodus 1:22). The people groaned because of their slavery and cried out for help (Exodus 2:23). Their cry rose up to God (Exodus 2:23). Moses had fled to Midian after killing an Egyptian and lived as a stranger in a foreign land (Exodus 2:11-15,22). The problem was that the people of Israel were trapped in a genocidal system of bondage with no human hope for escape (Exodus 1:22, 2:23).

## Moses' Humble Condition

Moses was keeping the flock of his father-in-law Jethro in the far side of the desert (Exodus 3:1). He had been a shepherd in Midian for forty years (Acts 7:30). He did not seek God's attention through fasting or special prayers before this encounter (Exodus 3:1-2). He was simply performing his daily duties when he came to Horeb, the mountain of God (Exodus 3:1). He was a man who had settled into a quiet life far from the corridors of power (Exodus 2:21).

## The Divine Calling

The LORD appeared to Moses in a flame of fire out of the midst of a bush.

> And the angel of the LORD appeared to him in a flame of fire out of the midst of a bush. He looked, and behold, the bush was burning, yet it was not consumed. And Moses said, “I will turn aside to see this great sight, why the bush is not burned.” When the LORD saw that he turned aside to see, God called to him out of the bush, “Moses, Moses!” And he said, “Here I am.”
>
> Then **He** said, “Do not come near; take your sandals off your feet, for the place on which you are standing is holy ground.” And **He** said, “I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob.” And Moses hid his face, for he was afraid to look at God.
>
> Then the LORD said, “I have surely seen the affliction of **My** people who are in Egypt and have heard their cry because of their taskmasters. I know their sufferings, and **I have come down to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians and to bring them up out of that land to a good and broad land, a land flowing with milk and honey, to the place of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites.** And now, behold, the cry of the people of Israel has come to **Me**, and I have also seen the oppression with which the Egyptians oppress them. Come, **I will send you to Pharaoh that you may bring My people, the children of Israel, out of Egypt.**”
>
> — Exodus 3:2-10 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice

Moses had to sacrifice his peaceful life and safety in Midian (Exodus 4:18-19). He returned to the land where he was a wanted man for murder (Exodus 2:15, 4:19). He risked his life by standing before Pharaoh, who had the power to execute him (Exodus 5:1-2). He also sacrificed his privacy and his comfort to lead a stubborn and complaining people through the wilderness (Exodus 16:2, 17:3).

## The Initial Response

Moses responded with deep insecurity and multiple excuses (Exodus 3:11, 4:1). He questioned his own identity and worthiness for the task (Exodus 3:11). He worried that the people would not believe him (Exodus 4:1). He complained that he was not eloquent and was slow of speech and tongue (Exodus 4:10). Finally, he pleaded with the LORD to send someone else instead (Exodus 4:13). His initial reaction was one of reluctance and fear (Exodus 4:13-14).

## Moses' Eventual Reaction

Despite his initial resistance, Moses eventually obeyed the LORD'S command (Exodus 4:18). He took his wife and sons and started back to the land of Egypt (Exodus 4:20). He met with the elders of Israel and performed the signs God had given him (Exodus 4:29-30). He went before Pharaoh and demanded that he let the people go (Exodus 5:1). When Pharaoh increased the people's burdens, Moses returned to the LORD in prayer (Exodus 5:22). He continued to appear before Pharaoh despite repeated rejections and threats (Exodus 7:10, 8:1, 10:28). He faithfully announced each plague that the LORD brought upon the land (Exodus 7:17, 8:2, 9:1, 10:1). He instructed the people on how to observe the first Passover to protect their firstborn (Exodus 12:21-23). Moses led the massive host of Israel out of Egypt when Pharaoh finally relented (Exodus 12:37). When trapped between the Red Sea and the Egyptian army, he told the people to stand firm and see the salvation of the LORD (Exodus 14:13). He stretched out his hand over the sea as commanded, trusting God to make a way (Exodus 14:21). He spent the rest of his life shepherding the nation through the desert (Exodus 15:22). He interceded for the people when they sinned and sought God's presence for them (Exodus 32:11, 33:12-13). Moses became a humble leader who spoke with God face to face (Exodus 33:11).

## The Divine Miracle

God demonstrated His power over Egypt and nature to fulfill His promise of deliverance.

> Then Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and **the LORD drove the sea back by a strong east wind all night and made the sea dry land, and the waters were divided**. And **the people of Israel went into the midst of the sea on dry ground**, the waters being a wall to them on their right hand and on their left. . . Thus **the LORD saved Israel that day from the hand of the Egyptians**, and Israel saw the Egyptians dead on the seashore. — Exodus 14:21-22,30 (ESV)

Through these wonders, the LORD fulfilled **His** promise to **deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians** and to **bring My people . . . out of Egypt**.

## The Lasting Result

The miracle resulted in the total liberation of the nation of Israel from four hundred years of slavery (Exodus 12:41). The Egyptian army was destroyed, ending their pursuit and oppression (Exodus 14:28). The surrounding nations heard of God's mighty acts and were filled with dread (Exodus 15:14-16). Israel entered into a covenant with the LORD at Mount Sinai and received **His** laws (Exodus 19:5-6, 20:1). Moses' family and his entire nation were given a new identity as a kingdom of priests and a holy nation (Exodus 19:6). The victory provided the foundation for the journey to the Promised Land (Exodus 13:21).

## Conclusion

Moses faced the problem of a genocidal empire and his own past as a fugitive. He was limited by his lack of eloquence and his fear of Pharaoh. Despite these failures, the LORD promised to be with him and to deliver Israel. Moses sacrificed his safety in Midian and his quiet life as a shepherd to answer the call. God's intervention through the plagues and the parting of the Red Sea proved **His** absolute faithfulness. Moses sacrificed his comfort, but he gained the privilege of leading a nation to freedom and seeing the glory of God. This story shows that the LORD uses the weak to shame the strong. We can trust **Him** to fulfill **His** word regardless of our personal limitations. **He** is the LORD Who delivers **His** people.
