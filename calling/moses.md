# Moses: Deliver My People

A Hebrew prince turned shepherd must return to the empire that once wanted him dead. He must confront the most powerful man on earth to free his people. His only weapon is a staff and a promise from a voice in a burning bush.

## The Problem of Bondage
The people of Israel were in slavery in Egypt (Exodus 1:11). They groaned under their heavy burdens (Exodus 2:23). Pharaoh had commanded that every Hebrew son be cast into the Nile (Exodus 1:22). Moses had fled to Midian after killing an Egyptian (Exodus 2:12,15). He lived as a stranger in a foreign land (Exodus 2:22). The Egyptians made the lives of the Israelites bitter with hard service (Exodus 1:14). The king of Egypt died, but the people continued to cry out because of their slavery (Exodus 2:23). Their cry for rescue came up to God (Exodus 2:23). The problem was that the chosen people were trapped in a cruel bondage they could not escape by their own strength (Exodus 3:7).

## Moses' Quiet Condition
Moses was keeping the flock of his father-in-law Jethro in the wilderness (Exodus 3:1). He was leading the flock to the west side of the wilderness (Exodus 3:1). He came to Horeb, the mountain of God (Exodus 3:1). Moses did nothing special to get God's attention (Exodus 3:1-2). He was simply performing his daily duties as a shepherd when the LORD appeared (Exodus 3:1).

## The Divine Calling
The angel of the LORD appeared to him in a flame of fire out of the midst of a bush (Exodus 3:2).

> Then the LORD said, “I have surely seen the affliction of My people who are in Egypt and have heard their cry because of their taskmasters. I know their sufferings, and **I have come down to deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians and to bring them up out of that land to a good and broad land**, a land flowing with milk and honey, to the place of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites. And now, behold, the cry of the people of Israel has come to Me, and I have also seen the oppression with which the Egyptians oppress them. Come, I will send you to Pharaoh that **you may bring My people, the children of Israel, out of Egypt**.”
>
> — Exodus 3:7-10 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Moses had to sacrifice his quiet life in Midian (Exodus 3:1). He left his father-in-law and the security of his family (Exodus 4:18). He risked his life by returning to Egypt where he was a wanted man for murder (Exodus 2:15). He sacrificed his anonymity to become a public leader (Exodus 3:11).

## The Initial Response
Moses responded with multiple excuses and doubt (Exodus 3:11). He asked who he was to go to Pharaoh (Exodus 3:11). He worried the people would not believe him (Exodus 4:1). He complained that he was slow of speech (Exodus 4:10). Finally, he pleaded with the LORD to send someone else (Exodus 4:13).

## The Eventual Reaction
Moses eventually obeyed the LORD (Exodus 4:18). He took his wife and sons and returned to the land of Egypt (Exodus 4:20). He took the staff of God in his hand (Exodus 4:20). Moses and Aaron gathered all the elders of the people of Israel (Exodus 4:29). He performed the signs God had given him (Exodus 4:30). He stood before Pharaoh multiple times to demand the release of his people (Exodus 5:1). He persevered through ten plagues (Exodus 7:14-12:30). He led the people out of Egypt by the way of the wilderness (Exodus 13:18). He told the people to stand firm and see the salvation of the LORD (Exodus 14:13).

## The Divine Miracle
God performed a mighty wonder to deliver Israel and destroy their enemies.

> Then Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and the LORD drove the sea back by a strong east wind all night and made the sea dry land, and the waters were divided. And **the people of Israel went into the midst of the sea on dry ground**, the waters being a wall to them on their right hand and on their left. . . And **the LORD overthrew the Egyptians in the midst of the sea**. The waters returned and covered the chariots and the horsemen; of all the host of Pharaoh that had followed them into the sea, not one of them remained.
>
> — Exodus 14:21-22,27-28 (ESV)

God fulfilled **His** promise to **deliver them out of the hand of the Egyptians** and to **bring My people... out of Egypt** (Exodus 3:8,10).

## The Lasting Result
The people of Israel were liberated from four hundred years of slavery (Exodus 12:40). They saw the great power that the LORD used against the Egyptians and feared **Him** (Exodus 14:31). They believed in the LORD and in His servant Moses (Exodus 14:31). Neighboring nations heard of the miracle and were afraid (Exodus 15:14). Moses became the leader of a new nation and received the Law on Mount Sinai (Exodus 19:1, 20:1). The miracle established Israel as God's chosen possession (Exodus 19:5).

## Conclusion
Moses faced the problem of a nation in bondage and his own feelings of inadequacy. He was a fugitive shepherd with no political power. However, the LORD promised to be with him and to deliver His people. Moses sacrificed his safety in Midian to face the wrath of Pharaoh. God's intervention through the parting of the Red Sea proved His absolute faithfulness. Moses sacrificed his comfortable life, but he gained the freedom of his nation and a unique relationship with God. This story demonstrates that we can trust the LORD even when we feel unqualified. He is the LORD Who delivers His people.
