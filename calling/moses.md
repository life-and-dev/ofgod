# Moses: Deliverance From Egypt

A fugitive shepherd encounters a miraculous burning bush in the wilderness and receives a command that will change the course of history. Moses must choose between his quiet life and the daunting task of confronting the most powerful empire on earth to liberate an enslaved people.

## The Bondage of Israel
The descendants of Israel had become numerous in the land of Egypt and a new king arose who did not know Joseph (Exodus 1:7-8). This king feared the Israelites and set taskmasters over them to afflict them with heavy burdens (Exodus 1:9-11). The Egyptians made their lives bitter with hard service in mortar and brick and in all kinds of work in the field (Exodus 1:14). When the people continued to multiply, the Pharaoh commanded that every son born to the Hebrews be cast into the Nile (Exodus 1:22). Moses was born during this time and was eventually raised in Pharaoh's house (Exodus 2:1-10). After killing an Egyptian who was beating a Hebrew, Moses fled to Midian to escape Pharaoh's death sentence (Exodus 2:11-15). He lived as a stranger in a foreign land for forty years while his people groaned under their slavery (Exodus 2:22-23). The core problem was that Israel was trapped in a state of brutal bondage and genocide under a tyrant who would not let them go (Exodus 2:23-25, 3:7).

## Moses' Humble Condition
Moses was keeping the flock of his father-in-law Jethro at Horeb, the mountain of God (Exodus 3:1). He was a humble shepherd, far from the Egyptian court he once knew (Exodus 3:1). Moses did not perform any special religious acts like fasting or long prayers BEFORE God called to him from the bush (Exodus 3:2-4). He was simply going about his daily work when the LORD got his attention through the miracle of the fire (Exodus 3:2).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD appeared to Moses in a flame of fire out of the midst of a bush. He called Moses by name and revealed His plan to deliver Israel.

> Then the LORD said, “I have surely seen the affliction of My people who are in Egypt and have heard their cry because of their taskmasters. I know their sufferings, and **I have come down to deliver them** out of the hand of the Egyptians and to bring them up out of that land to a good and broad land, a land flowing with milk and honey, to the place of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites. And now, behold, the cry of the people of Israel has come to Me, and I have also seen the oppression with which the Egyptians oppress them. Come, I will send you to Pharaoh that **you may bring My people, the children of Israel, out of Egypt**.”
>
> — Exodus 3:7-10 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Moses had to sacrifice the safety and anonymity he had found in Midian (Exodus 3:1). By returning to Egypt, he risked being arrested and executed for the murder he had committed forty years earlier (Exodus 2:15, 4:19). He also sacrificed his comfort and his role as a shepherd to become a leader of a rebellious nation (Exodus 3:10-11). He risked the lives of his wife and sons by taking them back into a conflict zone (Exodus 4:20).

## The Initial Response
Moses responded with a series of excuses and expressions of inadequacy (Exodus 3:11). He asked, "Who am I that I should go to Pharaoh?" (Exodus 3:11). He doubted that the people would believe him and worried about his lack of eloquence (Exodus 4:1,10). He even pleaded with the LORD to send someone else instead (Exodus 4:13). His response was characterized by fear and a lack of self-confidence in his own abilities (Exodus 4:10-13).

## Moses' Eventual Reaction
Despite his initial resistance, Moses eventually obeyed the LORD'S command (Exodus 4:18-20). He took his staff and returned to Egypt with his family (Exodus 4:20). He met with his brother Aaron and together they gathered the elders of Israel to announce God's promise (Exodus 4:27-31). Moses appeared before Pharaoh and boldly demanded the release of the people (Exodus 5:1). Even when Pharaoh increased the workload of the Israelites and the people turned against him, Moses continued to seek the LORD (Exodus 5:22-23, 6:1). He persevered through ten devastating plagues that struck the land of Egypt (Exodus 7-12). Moses showed growing trust as he stood before the Red Sea with an army behind him and the water in front of him (Exodus 14:10-14). He told the people to fear not and stand firm to see the salvation of the LORD (Exodus 14:13). He raised his staff over the sea as commanded, trusting that God would make a way where there was no way (Exodus 14:15-21). Moses also led the people through the wilderness, trusting the LORD for food and water in a barren land (Exodus 15-17). He became the mediator between God and Israel, receiving the Law on Mount Sinai (Exodus 19-20). Throughout the journey, he remained faithful to the calling despite the constant complaining and rebellion of the people he was leading (Exodus 32:11-14).

## The Divine Miracle
God performed many signs, but the parting of the Red Sea was the definitive act of deliverance.

> Then Moses stretched out his hand over the sea, and the **LORD drove the sea back** by a strong east wind all night and **made the sea dry land**, and the **waters were divided**. And the **people of Israel went into the midst of the sea on dry ground**, the waters being a wall to them on their right hand and on their left. . . Thus **the LORD saved Israel that day** from the hand of the Egyptians, and Israel saw the Egyptians dead on the seashore.
>
> — Exodus 14:21-22,30 (ESV)

## The Lasting Result
The Israelites were permanently freed from Egyptian bondage and the power of Pharaoh was broken (Exodus 14:30-31). The people saw the great power of the LORD and believed in Him and in His servant Moses (Exodus 14:31). This event established Israel as a nation and set them on the path to the Promised Land (Exodus 15:13-17). The news of the miracle spread to other nations, striking them with fear and revealing the supremacy of the God of Israel (Exodus 15:14-16). Moses' family was reunited and his legacy as the great lawgiver was established (Exodus 18:1-7). Most importantly, the deliverance from Egypt became the central theme of Israel's identity and a foreshadowing of the greater salvation to come through the Messiah.

## Conclusion
Moses faced the monumental problem of Egyptian slavery and his own past failures as a fugitive. He was a man of slow speech and heavy heart who initially resisted God's call with multiple excuses. However, the LORD promised to be with him and to personally deliver His people. Moses responded by sacrificing his peaceful life in Midian and risking his safety to confront Pharaoh. God's intervention through the plagues and the miracle of the Red Sea demonstrated His absolute power and faithfulness. Moses sacrificed his comfort, but he gained the privilege of seeing God face to face and leading a nation to freedom. This story proves that God does not forsake His people or His servants, regardless of their limitations. We can trust the LORD to fulfill His word and provide the strength we lack.
