# Jeremiah: The Weeping Prophet

A young man is told he was chosen before he was even born. He is commanded to speak words of fire to a nation that will not listen. Discover how Jeremiah trusted God while everything he knew crumbled around him.

## The Problem of a Rebellious Nation
Jeremiah lived in the kingdom of Judah during its final decades before the Babylonian exile (Jeremiah 1:1-3). The political environment was unstable as Judah was caught between the rivaling powers of Egypt and Babylon (Jeremiah 2:18,36). Spiritually, the people had forsaken the LORD to worship idols and had filled Jerusalem with the blood of the innocent (Jeremiah 2:13, 19:4). The kings, priests, and even the prophets were corrupt and led the people astray (Jeremiah 2:8, 5:30-31). The nation had broken the covenant made at Sinai (Jeremiah 11:1-10). God's patience was reaching its limit, and judgment was imminent (Jeremiah 1:14-16). The problem was that the people were stubborn in their rebellion and faced certain destruction from the north if they did not repent (Jeremiah 4:6, 6:1).

## Jeremiah's Humble Condition
Jeremiah was a young man from a priestly family in Anathoth (Jeremiah 1:1,6). He did nothing special to get God's attention like fasting or performing sacrifices before the LORD spoke to him (Jeremiah 1:4-5). He was chosen and consecrated by God before he was even formed in his mother's womb (Jeremiah 1:5). He was in a state of youth and felt inadequate for the task set before him (Jeremiah 1:6).

## The Divine Calling
God spoke directly to Jeremiah to reveal His sovereign choice and his mission.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations.**" Then I said, "Ah, LORD God! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you you shall go, and whatever I command you you shall speak. **Do not be afraid of them, for I am with you to deliver you, declares the LORD.**" Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth. See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant.**" — Jeremiah 1:4-10 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice his desire for a normal life and family, as the LORD commanded him not to marry or have children (Jeremiah 16:1-2). He sacrificed his reputation and social standing, becoming a laughingstock and an object of mockery (Jeremiah 20:7-8). He risked his life repeatedly by speaking against the king and the religious establishment (Jeremiah 26:8, 38:4-6). He gave up the comfort of his own people to be a "fortified city" against them (Jeremiah 1:18).

## Jeremiah's Initial Response
Jeremiah initially tried to refuse the calling by pleading his youth and lack of speaking ability (Jeremiah 1:6). He expressed fear and hesitation at the prospect of being a prophet (Jeremiah 1:6,8). Later, in his distress, he even cursed the day of his birth and complained that the LORD had deceived him (Jeremiah 20:7,14-18). He struggled with the weight of the message he was forced to carry (Jeremiah 20:9).

## Jeremiah's Faithful Reaction
Despite his initial fears and ongoing suffering, Jeremiah eventually submitted to the LORD'S will (Jeremiah 1:7). He spoke every word that the LORD commanded him, even when it meant predicting the fall of Jerusalem and the destruction of the temple (Jeremiah 7:1-15, 26:12-15). He stood alone against the kings of Judah, warning them to submit to Babylon to save their lives (Jeremiah 27:12, 38:17-18). When he tried to remain silent, the word of God became like a burning fire shut up in his bones that he could not contain (Jeremiah 20:9). He remained in Jerusalem during the terrible siege, enduring hunger and imprisonment (Jeremiah 37:15-16, 38:6). He continued to pray for his people even when they treated him as an enemy (Jeremiah 18:20). He demonstrated his trust in God's future promise by buying a field in Anathoth while the Babylonian army was at the gates (Jeremiah 32:6-15). This act showed his belief that houses and fields would once again be bought in the land (Jeremiah 32:15). He remained faithful to his mission for over forty years, seeing the fulfillment of the very judgments he had predicted (Jeremiah 39:1-8). Even when he was forced to go to Egypt against his will, he continued to speak the word of the LORD to the survivors (Jeremiah 43:8-13). His life was a testament to enduring faithfulness in the face of total rejection (Jeremiah 1:18-19).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD performed the miracle of preserving Jeremiah's life in the midst of a falling nation and fulfilled the words he spoke.

> "But I will deliver you on that day, declares the LORD, and you shall not be given into the hand of the men of whom you are afraid. For **I will surely save you, and you shall not fall by the sword, but you shall have your life as a prize of war, because you have put your trust in Me, declares the LORD.**" — Jeremiah 39:17-18 (ESV)

The LORD also fulfilled **His** promise to **pluck up and to break down** when Jerusalem was destroyed.

> In the ninth year of Zedekiah king of Judah, in the tenth month, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon and all his army came against Jerusalem and besieged it. . . **The Chaldeans burned the king's house and the house of the people, and broke down the walls of Jerusalem.** — Jeremiah 39:1,8 (ESV)

Despite the destruction, the LORD'S promise to **deliver** Jeremiah was kept as the Babylonians released him and gave him provisions (Jeremiah 40:1-5).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the preservation of a faithful witness during Judah's darkest hour (Jeremiah 40:1-6). Jeremiah's prophecies provided the theological framework for the exiles to understand their situation as divine discipline rather than abandonment (Jeremiah 29:1-14). His message of a New Covenant offered hope for a future restoration of the heart (Jeremiah 31:31-34). The survivors in Judah and the exiles in Babylon were forced to recognize that a true prophet had been among them (Ezekiel 33:33). Jeremiah's life became a pattern for future servants of God who would face opposition for speaking the truth (Matthew 16:14). His faithfulness ensured that the word of the LORD was preserved for future generations (Jeremiah 36:27-32).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced the problem of a rebellious nation and the weight of a message of judgment. He was limited by his youth and sensitive nature, often wishing he had never been born. However, the LORD promised to be with him and to put **His** words in his mouth. Jeremiah sacrificed his personal happiness and safety to remain faithful to his calling. God intervened by protecting him through sieges and prisons, fulfilling every word of judgment and hope. The result was a lasting legacy of hope and a New Covenant that would change the world. Jeremiah sacrificed a temporary home and family but gained an eternal place in God's plan. Jeremiah's story proves that even when we feel weak and alone, we can trust in the LORD'S strength and faithfulness. **He** is the God **Who** never forsakes **His** servants.
