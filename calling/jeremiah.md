# Jeremiah: Prophet to the Nations

A young man from a priestly family receives a message that will make him the most hated person in his country. He must choose between his own safety and the word of a God **Who** refuses to be silent. Discover how his trust survived the ruins of a nation.

## The Problem of National Unfaithfulness
The kingdom of Judah had fallen into a cycle of shallow reforms and deep-seated idolatry (Jeremiah 2:11,13, 3:10). Following the reign of Manasseh, who filled Jerusalem with innocent blood, the people's hearts remained far from the LORD (2 Kings 21:16; Jeremiah 3:10). The political landscape was a volatile tug-of-war between the fading power of Assyria and the rising empires of Egypt and Babylon (Jeremiah 2:36). Judah's leaders foolishly trusted in shifting alliances rather than in their God (Jeremiah 2:18,37). False prophets promised peace while the nation's social and spiritual foundations crumbled (Jeremiah 6:14, 8:11). Injustice was rampant as the rich oppressed the poor and the fatherless (Jeremiah 5:27-28). The spiritual condition had reached a point where the people no longer knew how to blush at their abominations (Jeremiah 6:15). The clear problem was that Judah had forsaken the fountain of living waters and was facing imminent divine judgment through a Babylonian invasion (Jeremiah 1:14-15, 2:13).

## Jeremiah's Humble Origins
Jeremiah was a young man from the priests who were in Anathoth in the land of Benjamin (Jeremiah 1:1). He was the son of Hilkiah (Jeremiah 1:1). There is no record that he performed any special fast, prayer, or ritual to attract God's attention (Jeremiah 1:4-5). Instead, the LORD revealed that **He** had chosen and consecrated Jeremiah before he was even formed in his mother's womb (Jeremiah 1:5). Jeremiah was simply living as a youth in his father's house when the word of the LORD first came to him (Jeremiah 1:2,6).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came to Jeremiah to appoint him as a prophet during the thirteenth year of the reign of Josiah.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations.**" — Jeremiah 1:4-5 (ESV)

> Then I said, "Ah, LORD God! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." — Jeremiah 1:6 (ESV)

> But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you you shall go, and whatever I command you you shall speak. **Do not be afraid of them, for I am with you to deliver you**, declares the LORD." — Jeremiah 1:7-8 (ESV)

> Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth. See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant.**" — Jeremiah 1:9-10 (ESV)

> "But you, dress yourself for work; arise, and say to them everything that I command you. Do not be dismayed by them, lest I dismay you before them. For behold, **I make you this day a fortified city, an iron pillar, and bronze walls**, against the whole land, against the kings of Judah, its officials, its priests, and the people of the land. **They will fight against you, but they shall not prevail against you, for I am with you, declares the LORD, to deliver you.**" — Jeremiah 1:17-19 (ESV)

## The Weight of Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice the possibility of a normal family life, as the LORD forbade him from marrying or having children (Jeremiah 16:2). He sacrificed his reputation and was mocked daily by his own people (Jeremiah 20:7-8). He risked his life repeatedly by delivering unpopular messages of surrender to the king and the princes (Jeremiah 26:11, 38:4). He lost his freedom when he was beaten and imprisoned in a foul dungeon and later thrown into a muddy cistern to die (Jeremiah 37:15; 38:6).

## Jeremiah's Initial Hesitation
Jeremiah initially responded with deep insecurity and hesitation (Jeremiah 1:6). He tried to excuse himself by claiming that he was too young and lacked the ability to speak effectively (Jeremiah 1:6). He was overwhelmed by the magnitude of the task and the authority of the One calling him (Jeremiah 1:6). Later, under the weight of persecution, he even cursed the day he was born and accused the LORD of deceiving him (Jeremiah 20:7,14).

## A Lifetime of Faithful Witness
Despite his fears and the intense opposition, Jeremiah eventually surrendered to the divine fire burning in his bones (Jeremiah 20:9). He stood in the court of the LORD'S house and at the gates of the city to proclaim God's warnings to all who entered (Jeremiah 7:1-2, 17:19-20). He did not hold back a single word, even when the priests and prophets demanded his death (Jeremiah 26:12-15). When King Jehoiakim burned the scroll of his prophecies, Jeremiah simply dictated them again to his scribe Baruch, adding even more words (Jeremiah 36:23,27-32). He remained in Jerusalem during the terrifying siege by the Babylonians, continuing to urge the people to repent and the king to surrender (Jeremiah 21:8-10, 38:17-18). He showed his trust in God's future promise by buying a field in Anathoth while the Babylonian army was already occupying the land (Jeremiah 32:6-15). This act demonstrated his belief that houses and fields would again be bought in the land after the exile (Jeremiah 32:15,44). Even when he was sinking in the mire of a dark cistern, he did not renounce his calling (Jeremiah 38:6,12-13). He chose to stay with the poor remnant in the land rather than seeking a comfortable life in Babylon when given the choice by the enemy commander (Jeremiah 40:1-6). His entire life became a living sermon of obedience in the face of apparent failure (Jeremiah 1:17-19). He continued to speak the truth even when the remaining leaders forced him to go to Egypt against the LORD'S command (Jeremiah 43:1-7). Throughout forty years of ministry, he never saw the national repentance he desired, yet he never stopped being the LORD'S mouthpiece (Jeremiah 25:3).

## The Miracle of Divine Preservation
God miraculously preserved Jeremiah's life through multiple assassination plots and the violent fall of Jerusalem. **He** moved the hearts of foreigners and even a court eunuch to protect **His** prophet.

> "Then Ebed-melech the Ethiopian said to Jeremiah, 'Put the rags and clothes between your armpits and the ropes.' Jeremiah did so. **So they drew up Jeremiah with ropes and lifted him out of the cistern.** And Jeremiah remained in the court of the guard." — Jeremiah 38:12-13 (ESV)

> "See, **I release you today from the chains on your hands.** If it seems good to you to come with me to Babylon, come, and I will look after you well. But if it seems wrong to you to come with me to Babylon, do not come. Behold, the whole land is before you; go wherever you think it is good and right to go." — Jeremiah 40:4 (ESV)

## The Result of Prophetic Obedience
The miracle of Jeremiah's survival ensured that the word of the LORD was preserved for the exiles in Babylon and for all future generations (Jeremiah 30:2, 36:32). His prophecies provided hope by predicting a return from exile after seventy years (Jeremiah 25:11, 29:10). Most significantly, he announced the New Covenant that would be written on the hearts of God's people (Jeremiah 31:31-34). While his own generation largely rejected him, the fulfillment of his words proved to the world that the LORD is sovereign over the rise and fall of nations (Jeremiah 33:14-16, 44:28-29). His neighbors in Anathoth and the leaders in Jerusalem suffered the consequences of ignoring his warnings, but the message of God's faithfulness outlived the kingdom of Judah (Jeremiah 11:21-23, 52:12-15).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah felt the crushing weight of a nation's rebellion and the loneliness of a forbidden life. He was limited by his youth and sensitive spirit, often weeping over the destruction he announced. Despite his complaints, he could not resist the LORD'S command. God promised to make him a fortified city and to deliver him. Jeremiah responded with a lifetime of unpopular but faithful service. God intervened by protecting him from death and giving him favor with his captors. He sacrificed marriage and the respect of his peers, but gained the honor of announcing the New Covenant. His story proves we can trust the LORD to sustain us even when our work seems to bear no fruit. **He** is the God **Who** stays with **His** prophets in the fire and the mire.
