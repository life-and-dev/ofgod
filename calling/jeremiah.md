# Jeremiah: The Fortified Prophet

A young man is chosen by God before his birth to be a prophet. He must deliver a message of destruction to a rebellious people. Discover how his trust in God's protection was tested during the final days of Jerusalem.

## The Problem of National Rebellion
The kingdom of Judah had forsaken the LORD and turned to the worship of idols (Jeremiah 1:16). The people had made offerings to other gods and worshiped the works of their own hands (Jeremiah 1:16). King Josiah was reigning, but the spiritual decay of the nation was deep and systemic (Jeremiah 1:2). Disaster was looming from the north as the LORD was calling all the tribes of the kingdoms of the north to execute judgment (Jeremiah 1:14-15). The political environment was one of impending Babylonian invasion and internal religious corruption (Jeremiah 1:14-16). The problem was that the nation had broken its covenant with God and faced imminent destruction from a powerful enemy (Jeremiah 1:15-16).

## Jeremiah's Chosen Condition
Jeremiah was the son of Hilkiah, one of the priests who were in Anathoth in the land of Benjamin (Jeremiah 1:1). He was only a youth when the word of the LORD first came to him (Jeremiah 1:6). He had done nothing special to seek God's attention or to receive a prophetic calling (Jeremiah 1:5). In fact, the LORD had consecrated and appointed him before he was even formed in the womb (Jeremiah 1:5). He was simply living as a member of a priestly family before his divine appointment (Jeremiah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying,

> "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; I appointed you a prophet to the nations." Then I said, "Ah, Lord GOD! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you, you shall go, and whatever I command you, you shall speak. Do not be afraid of them, **for I am with you to deliver you,** declares the LORD." Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth. See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant.**" And the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Jeremiah, what do you see?" And I said, "I see an almond branch." Then the LORD said to me, "You have seen well, for I am watching over My word to perform it." The word of the LORD came to me a second time, saying, "What do you see?" And I said, "I see a boiling pot, facing away from the north." Then the LORD said to me, "Out of the north disaster shall be let loose upon all the inhabitants of the land. For behold, I am calling all the tribes of the kingdoms of the north, declares the LORD, and they shall come, and every one shall set his throne at the entrance of the gates of Jerusalem, against all its walls all around and against all the cities of Judah. And I will declare My judgments against them, for all their evil in forsaking Me. They have made offerings to other gods and worshiped the works of their own hands. But you, dress yourself for work; arise, and say to them everything that I command you. Do not be dismayed by them, lest I dismay you before them. And I, behold, **I make you this day a fortified city, an iron pillar, and bronze walls, against the whole land, against the kings of Judah, its officials, its priests, and the people of the land. They will fight against you, but they shall not prevail against you, for I am with you, declares the LORD, to deliver you.**" — Jeremiah 1:4-19 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice the possibility of a normal family life (Jeremiah 16:2). The LORD commanded him not to take a wife or have sons and daughters in that place (Jeremiah 16:2). He sacrificed his reputation and social standing among his people (Jeremiah 15:10). He was treated as a man of strife and contention to the whole land (Jeremiah 15:10). He also risked his life and physical safety by speaking against the kings and priests (Jeremiah 20:1-2). He gave up his peace and became a social outcast (Jeremiah 15:17).

## Jeremiah's Initial Response
Jeremiah initially protested his calling by claiming he was too young and lacked the ability to speak (Jeremiah 1:6). He cried out to the LORD in hesitation (Jeremiah 1:6). Later in his ministry, he expressed deep sorrow and even cursed the day of his birth (Jeremiah 20:14). He felt that the word of the LORD had become a reproach and a derision to him all day long (Jeremiah 20:8). He even thought about not mentioning the LORD or speaking anymore in His name (Jeremiah 20:9).

## Jeremiah's Faithful Reaction
Jeremiah eventually responded with persistent and faithful obedience despite the extreme opposition he faced (Jeremiah 20:9). He found that the word of the LORD was in his heart like a burning fire shut up in his bones (Jeremiah 20:9). He was weary with holding it in and he could not (Jeremiah 20:9). He spoke everything that the LORD commanded him to the kings, officials, and people of Judah (Jeremiah 26:12-15). He endured being beaten and put in the stocks by Pashhur the priest (Jeremiah 20:2). He was imprisoned in the house of Jonathan the secretary (Jeremiah 37:15). He was even cast into a muddy cistern to die (Jeremiah 38:6). Through all these trials, he did not change his message or stop trusting in the LORD'S protection (Jeremiah 1:19). He continued to call the nation to repentance even when it seemed hopeless (Jeremiah 25:3-7). He faithfully recorded the words of the LORD in a scroll, which was later burned by King Jehoiakim (Jeremiah 36:23). Jeremiah simply dictated the words again to his scribe Baruch (Jeremiah 36:32). He remained in Jerusalem during the final siege, witnessing the destruction he had long prophesied (Jeremiah 39:1-2). His trust was not in his own strength but in the promise that the LORD was with him (Jeremiah 20:11).

## The Divine Miracle
God performed a miracle by protecting Jeremiah during the violent fall of Jerusalem to the Babylonians. While the city was destroyed and the people were exiled, Jeremiah was sovereignly spared and treated with honor by the enemy king.

> Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon gave command concerning Jeremiah through Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, saying, "**Take him, look after him well, and do him no harm, but deal with him as he tells you.**" — Jeremiah 39:11-12 (ESV)

The LORD also fulfilled His promise to set Jeremiah free for his good.

> The LORD said, "Have I not **set you free for their good**? Have I not **pleaded for you before the enemy in the time of trouble** and in the time of distress?" — Jeremiah 15:11 (ESV)

In these events, God fulfilled His promise that the enemy would **not prevail against** him and that He would **deliver** him (Jeremiah 1:19).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in Jeremiah's physical survival and his freedom to choose his own path (Jeremiah 40:4). The captain of the guard released his chains and gave him an allowance of food and a present (Jeremiah 40:4-5). Jeremiah was able to remain among the people who were left in the land of Judah (Jeremiah 40:6). His ministry and the words he spoke were vindicated before the entire nation (Jeremiah 40:2-3). The Babylonians themselves recognized that the disaster had come because the people had sinned against the LORD (Jeremiah 40:2-3). Jeremiah's faithfulness ensured that the word of the LORD was preserved for future generations (Jeremiah 36:32). His prophecies provided hope for the eventual return of the exiles (Jeremiah 29:10-14). The result was a lasting legacy of a prophet who stood firm when everything else crumbled (Jeremiah 1:18).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced the problem of a rebellious nation and the certainty of divine judgment. He was limited by his youth and his own feelings of inadequacy. However, the LORD promised to make him a fortified city and to deliver him from his enemies. Jeremiah responded with lifelong faithfulness despite being beaten, imprisoned, and ridiculed. God intervened by moving the heart of the Babylonian king to show him favor during the destruction of Jerusalem. The result was his survival and the preservation of his message for all time. Jeremiah sacrificed the joys of marriage and the respect of his peers. In return, he gained the honor of being a faithful witness and the personal protection of the Almighty. His story proves that we can trust the LORD'S presence even in the midst of national collapse. He never forsakes those who stand for His truth.
