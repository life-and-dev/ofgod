# Jeremiah: The Prophet to the Nations

A young man is chosen to be a prophet to the nations during the final days of a crumbling kingdom. He must deliver a message of doom to a people who refuse to listen. Discover how Jeremiah trusted the LORD'S protection.

## The Problem of National Decay
The kingdom of Judah was in a state of severe spiritual and moral decay (Jeremiah 1:16). The people had forsaken the LORD and burned incense to other gods (Jeremiah 1:16). Politically, the nation was caught between the competing powers of Egypt and the rising Babylonian Empire (Jeremiah 2:18). King Josiah had attempted reforms, but the hearts of the people remained far from God (Jeremiah 3:10). Injustice, greed, and idolatry were rampant throughout the land (Jeremiah 5:26-28). The religious leaders, including priests and prophets, were corrupt and led the people astray (Jeremiah 2:8). The problem was that Judah had broken the covenant with the LORD and faced imminent judgment and destruction from the north (Jeremiah 1:14-15).

## Jeremiah's Chosen Condition
Jeremiah was a young man from a priestly family in the land of Benjamin (Jeremiah 1:1). He did nothing special to get God's attention or to receive a calling (Jeremiah 1:4-5). The LORD had consecrated him and appointed him as a prophet before he was even formed in the womb (Jeremiah 1:5). He was likely in his youth when the word of the LORD first came to him (Jeremiah 1:6).

## The Sovereign Calling
God spoke directly to Jeremiah, declaring **His** sovereign choice and the mission **He** had for him.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb **I knew you**, and before you were born **I consecrated you**; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations**." . . . But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you you shall go, and whatever I command you you shall speak. **Do not be afraid of them, for I am with you to deliver you**, declares the LORD." Then the LORD put out **His** hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth**. See, **I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant**." . . . "And I, behold, **I make you this day a fortified city, an iron pillar, and bronze walls**, against the whole land, against the kings of Judah, its officials, its priests, and the people of the land. They will fight against you, but **they shall not prevail against you, for I am with you, declares the LORD, to deliver you**." — Jeremiah 1:4-10,18-19 (ESV)

## The Weight of Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice his reputation and his standing in society (Jeremiah 15:10). He was commanded by the LORD not to take a wife or have children in that place because of the coming judgment (Jeremiah 16:1-2). He lived a life of isolation and was often mocked and ridiculed by his own people (Jeremiah 20:7-8). He risked his life repeatedly by speaking the truth to powerful kings and officials who wanted him dead (Jeremiah 26:8-11).

## Jeremiah's Initial Fear
Jeremiah initially felt inadequate and afraid because of his youth and his perceived inability to speak (Jeremiah 1:6). He expressed his concern to the LORD, saying that he did not know how to speak (Jeremiah 1:6). He later struggled with the weight of his calling and even cursed the day he was born (Jeremiah 20:14-18). He often felt overwhelmed by the hostility of those around him (Jeremiah 15:15).

## A Life of Steadfast Reaction
Despite his initial fears and ongoing struggles, Jeremiah surrendered to the will of the LORD (Jeremiah 1:7). He faithfully delivered the messages of judgment and repentance to the people of Judah for over forty years (Jeremiah 25:3). When the religious leaders and the people conspired to kill him for his prophecies, he remained steadfast, declaring that the LORD had truly sent him (Jeremiah 26:12-15). He endured being beaten and put in the stocks by Pashhur the priest (Jeremiah 20:1-2). He was imprisoned in a muddy cistern where he would have died if not for the intervention of Ebed-melech (Jeremiah 38:6-13). Jeremiah even wrote down the words of the LORD in a scroll, which was then burned by King Jehoiakim, yet he dictated them again to Baruch (Jeremiah 36:23,32). He continued to advise the kings of Judah, even when they refused to listen to his counsel (Jeremiah 37:17). He stayed with the remnant of the people after the fall of Jerusalem, continuing to serve as the LORD'S witness (Jeremiah 40:6). His reaction was one of persistent obedience in the face of absolute rejection and suffering (Jeremiah 1:17-19). Jeremiah's life was a testament to his trust in the LORD'S presence, even when he felt like a "man of strife and contention to the whole land" (Jeremiah 15:10). He found his strength in the word of the LORD, which became to him "a joy and the delight of his heart" (Jeremiah 15:16).

## The Miracle of Preservation
The LORD performed a miracle by preserving Jeremiah's life through the violent destruction of Jerusalem and the Babylonian siege (Jeremiah 39:11). **He** fulfilled **His** promise that they would not prevail against him (Jeremiah 1:19).

> "Now Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon gave command concerning Jeremiah through Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, saying, 'Take him, **look after him well and do him no harm**, but deal with him as he tells you.' . . . The captain of the guard took Jeremiah and said to him, . . . 'Behold, **I release you today from the chains on your hands**. If it seems good to you to come with me to Babylon, come, and **I will look after you well**.'" — Jeremiah 39:11-12; 40:2,4 (ESV)

In this miracle, the LORD fulfilled the promise **to deliver you** and demonstrated that Jeremiah was indeed like **a fortified city** (Jeremiah 1:18-19).

## The Enduring Result
The miracle of Jeremiah's preservation served as a powerful witness to the truth of his prophecies (Jeremiah 40:2-3). The Babylonians themselves recognized that the LORD had brought the destruction as Jeremiah had spoken (Jeremiah 40:2-3). While the nation of Judah was taken into exile, Jeremiah's words provided the foundation for their hope of restoration (Jeremiah 29:10-14). His message of the New Covenant pointed forward to a time when the law would be written on the hearts of the people (Jeremiah 31:31-34). His faithfulness encouraged a small remnant to remain in the land, although they eventually disobeyed and fled to Egypt (Jeremiah 42:1-2; 43:1-7). The result of Jeremiah's trust was the preservation of the LORD'S word and the promise of a future for the people of God (Jeremiah 32:37-41).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced the problem of a rebellious nation and the impending Babylonian conquest. He was limited by his youth and his sensitivity to the suffering of his people. However, the LORD promised to be with him and to make him a fortified city. Jeremiah responded with decades of faithful preaching despite intense persecution and personal loss. The LORD intervened by protecting him during the fall of Jerusalem and granting him favor with his enemies. The result was the survival of the prophetic word and the hope of a New Covenant. Jeremiah sacrificed his desire for a normal family and a peaceful life, but he gained the honor of being the LORD'S mouthpiece to the nations. His story proves that we can trust the LORD to sustain us when the world rejects us. **He** is faithful to deliver those who speak **His** truth.
