# Jeremiah: The Fortified City

A young man from a priestly family is told by the Almighty that he was chosen before he was even born. He is given a message of impending doom for his nation, a task that will make him the most hated man in Jerusalem.

## The Problem of Spiritual and Political Decay
The kingdom of Judah had fallen into deep spiritual and moral decay after the reign of King Hezekiah (2 Kings 21:1-2). Under King Manasseh, the people practiced gross idolatry, even sacrificing their children to false gods (2 Kings 21:6). Although King Josiah later attempted reforms, the hearts of the people remained far from the LORD (2 Kings 23:25-26; Jeremiah 3:10). The political landscape was precarious, as the rising power of Babylon threatened the sovereignty of Judah from the north (Jeremiah 1:14-15). The religious leaders and priests were corrupt, misleading the people with false promises of peace (Jeremiah 6:13-14). False prophets encouraged rebellion against the divine warnings, creating an environment of widespread deception (Jeremiah 5:30-31). The people had forsaken the fountain of living waters to hew out broken cisterns for themselves (Jeremiah 2:13). The problem was that Judah had completely abandoned her covenant with God and faced inevitable judgment from an unstoppable northern enemy (Jeremiah 1:16).

## Jeremiah's Humble Condition
Jeremiah was a young man from the town of Anathoth, the son of Hilkiah, one of the priests (Jeremiah 1:1). He was likely in his late teens or early twenties when the word of the LORD first came to him (Jeremiah 1:2,6). There is no record that he did anything special to get God's attention, such as fasting or praying for a mission, before the LORD spoke to him (Jeremiah 1:4-5). Instead, the LORD declared that **He** had already chosen and consecrated Jeremiah before his conception (Jeremiah 1:5).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke directly to Jeremiah, initiating a dialogue that would define his entire life (Jeremiah 1:4).

> "Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying,
>
> 'Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations.**'
>
> Then I said, 'Ah, Lord GOD! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth.'
>
> But the LORD said to me, 'Do not say, I am only a youth; for to all to whom I send you you shall go, and whatever I command you you shall speak. Do not be afraid of them, for **I am with you to deliver you**, declares the LORD.'
>
> Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, 'Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth.** See, **I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant.**'
>
> And the word of the LORD came to me, saying, 'Jeremiah, what do you see?' And I said, 'I see an almond branch.' Then the LORD said to me, 'You have seen well, for **I am watching over My word to perform it.**'
>
> The word of the LORD came to me a second time, saying, 'What do you see?' And I said, 'I see a boiling pot, facing away from the north.' Then the LORD said to me, 'Out of the north disaster shall be let loose upon all the inhabitants of the land. For behold, **I am calling all the tribes of the kingdoms of the north**, declares the LORD, and they shall come and every one shall set his throne at the entrance of the gates of Jerusalem, against all its walls all around and against all the cities of Judah. They will fight against you, but they shall not prevail against you, for **I am with you**, declares the LORD, **to deliver you.**' " — Jeremiah 1:4-19 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice his personal safety, his reputation, and the possibility of a normal family life (Jeremiah 16:1-2). He was forbidden by the LORD from marrying or having children as a sign of the coming judgment (Jeremiah 16:2-4). Jeremiah also risked his standing within his own community and family in Anathoth, who plotted to kill him because of his prophecies (Jeremiah 11:19-21). He gave up the comfort of being accepted by his peers to become a man of strife and contention to the whole land (Jeremiah 15:10).

## Jeremiah's Initial Response
When the LORD first called him, Jeremiah's response was one of deep insecurity and hesitation (Jeremiah 1:6). He protested that he did not know how to speak because he was only a youth (Jeremiah 1:6). He felt inadequate for the massive task of being a prophet to the nations (Jeremiah 1:5-6). Despite his initial doubt, he did not flatly refuse the call but expressed his fear of the responsibility (Jeremiah 1:6-8).

## Jeremiah's Faithful Reaction
Jeremiah's reaction was one of enduring obedience through decades of intense suffering and isolation (Jeremiah 20:7-9). He preached the word of the LORD faithfully for over forty years, even when no one listened (Jeremiah 25:3-4). He was beaten, put in stocks, and thrown into a muddy cistern to die (Jeremiah 20:2, 38:6). Despite the physical and emotional toll, Jeremiah could not stop speaking because the word of the LORD was like a burning fire shut up in his bones (Jeremiah 20:9). He consistently urged the people to repent and then to submit to the yoke of Nebuchadrezzar as a form of divine discipline (Jeremiah 27:12). Even when King Jehoiakim burned the scroll of his prophecies, Jeremiah simply dictated them again to his scribe Baruch (Jeremiah 36:23, 27-28, 32). He chose to stay with the poor remnant in the land rather than seeking a comfortable life in Babylon when given the choice by the captain of the guard (Jeremiah 40:4-6). His trust in the LORD was tested when he was accused of being a traitor to his own people (Jeremiah 37:13-15). Yet, he remained steadfast, continuing to deliver the message **He** had given him, even when it meant certain imprisonment (Jeremiah 38:1-4). Jeremiah also demonstrated his faith in the future restoration of Israel by buying a field in Anathoth while the city was under siege (Jeremiah 32:6-9). This act showed his absolute conviction that houses and fields would once again be bought in the land, according to the promise of the LORD (Jeremiah 32:15, 44). His life was a testament to the fact that one can be a "fortified city" through the strength of the LORD alone (Jeremiah 1:18).

## The Divine Miracle
The miracle in Jeremiah's life was not a sudden removal of his problems, but the supernatural preservation of his life and the precise fulfillment of every word he spoke (Jeremiah 1:12, 18-19, 39:11-14). God protected him from multiple death plots and ensured that he survived the horrific fall of Jerusalem (Jeremiah 11:18-23, 39:11-14).

> "Then the LORD said to me, 'You have seen well, for **I am watching over My word to perform it.**' . . . 'They will fight against you, but **they shall not prevail against you**, for **I am with you**, declares the LORD, **to deliver you.**' " — Jeremiah 1:12,19 (ESV)

> "The captain of the guard took Jeremiah and said to him, 'The LORD your God pronounced this disaster against this place. The LORD has brought it about, and has done as He said. . . . Now, behold, **I release you today from the chains on your hands.**' " — Jeremiah 40:2-4 (ESV)

The LORD fulfilled **His** promise to make Jeremiah a **fortified city** as his enemies could not destroy him (Jeremiah 1:18). **He** also performed **His** word by bringing the northern army to judge Judah exactly as Jeremiah had prophesied (Jeremiah 39:1-2).

## The Lasting Result
The result of God's faithfulness to Jeremiah was the preservation of a remnant and the recording of a message that would sustain the exiles in Babylon (Jeremiah 29:10-14). His letter to the exiles gave them hope, promising that after seventy years, the LORD would bring them back to their land (Jeremiah 29:10). His prophecies provided the theological framework for understanding the exile not as the end of Israel, but as a refining process (Jeremiah 24:5-7). Through Jeremiah, the LORD also promised a New Covenant where the law would be written on the hearts of **His** people (Jeremiah 31:31-34). This promise became the foundation for the New Testament understanding of God's relationship with man (Hebrews 8:8-12). Although his contemporaries rejected him, Jeremiah's words eventually gained the respect they deserved, influencing later prophets like Daniel (Daniel 9:2). The preservation of his life amidst the ruins of Jerusalem served as a sign that the LORD does not forsake **His** faithful servants (Jeremiah 39:11-14).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced the problem of a nation that had abandoned God and was headed for certain destruction. He was limited by his youth and his deep sense of inadequacy, yet the LORD promised to be with him and to make him like a fortified city. Jeremiah responded by sacrificing his personal life and enduring decades of persecution to deliver a message of judgment and hope. God intervened by preserving Jeremiah's life through multiple death plots and by fulfilling every prophecy he uttered. The result was a recorded testimony that guided the Jewish people through their exile and pointed toward the New Covenant. Jeremiah sacrificed his reputation and comfort, but he gained the honor of being the LORD'S chosen vessel and seeing God's word vindicated. His story demonstrates that even when the world rejects us, we can trust in the LORD **Who** is always faithful to **His** word.
