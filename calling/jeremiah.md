# Jeremiah: The Fortified Prophet

A young man is chosen to deliver a message of doom to a nation that refuses to listen. Surrounded by enemies and abandoned by his own people, he must decide if God's word is worth his life. Discover how he found strength in the LORD'S faithfulness.

## The Corruption of Judah
Jeremiah lived in the land of Benjamin during the final decades of the Kingdom of Judah (Jeremiah 1:1). The political environment was volatile as the nation shifted between the influence of Egypt and the rising power of Babylon (2 Kings 23:34, 24:1). The people of Judah had forsaken the LORD and burned offerings to other gods (Jeremiah 1:16). They worshiped the works of their own hands and practiced widespread idolatry (Jeremiah 1:16). The kings, officials, and priests were deeply corrupt and rejected the commands of God (Jeremiah 1:18). Successive kings like Jehoiakim and Zedekiah failed to lead the people in righteousness (Jeremiah 1:3). The nation was under the constant threat of a massive invasion from the north (Jeremiah 1:14-15). False prophets were misleading the people by promising peace when there was no peace (Jeremiah 6:14). The problem was that the people had abandoned their covenant with God and faced inevitable destruction and captivity (Jeremiah 1:3,16).

## A Prophet's Youthful Condition
Jeremiah was the son of Hilkiah, one of the priests in Anathoth (Jeremiah 1:1). He was a young man when the word of the LORD first came to him (Jeremiah 1:6). There is no record that he performed any special ritual, fast, or prayer before God initiated the conversation (Jeremiah 1:4). He was simply going about his life in the land of Benjamin when the LORD chose him (Jeremiah 1:1-2). God had already consecrated and appointed him before he was even formed in the womb (Jeremiah 1:5).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came directly to Jeremiah, revealing his pre-ordained purpose.

> "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations.**" — Jeremiah 1:5 (ESV)

When Jeremiah expressed his fear, the LORD reached out and touched his mouth to empower him.

> Then I said, "Ah, LORD God! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you, you shall go, and whatever I command you, you shall speak. Do not be afraid of them, for **I am with you to deliver you**, declares the LORD." Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth. See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant.**" — Jeremiah 1:6-10 (ESV)

The LORD also promised to make Jeremiah as strong as a fortress against his opponents.

> "And I, behold, **I make you this day a fortified city, an iron pillar, and bronze walls, against the whole land**, against the kings of Judah, its officials, its priests, and the people of the land. They will fight against you, but they shall not prevail against you, for **I am with you**, declares the LORD, **to deliver you**." — Jeremiah 1:18-19 (ESV)

## The Weight of Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice his reputation and personal safety to deliver God's unpopular message (Jeremiah 20:7-8). He was forbidden from marrying or having children to symbolize the coming judgment (Jeremiah 16:1-4). He risked his life repeatedly as he was beaten and put in stocks by temple officials (Jeremiah 20:1-2). He was eventually thrown into a muddy cistern to die (Jeremiah 38:6). He gave up a life of priestly comfort to endure decades of ridicule and imprisonment (Jeremiah 37:15-16).

## A Hesitant Initial Response
Jeremiah's first response was one of deep insecurity and hesitation (Jeremiah 1:6). He protested to the LORD God that he did not know how to speak because he was only a youth (Jeremiah 1:6). He felt inadequate for the monumental task of addressing nations and kingdoms (Jeremiah 1:6,10). He feared the reaction of the people to whom he was being sent (Jeremiah 1:8). His initial reaction was to focus on his own limitations rather than on the power of the One **Who** called him (Jeremiah 1:6-7).

## A Life of Persistent Reaction
Jeremiah eventually submitted to his calling and spoke everything the LORD commanded him (Jeremiah 1:17). He remained faithful for over forty years despite receiving almost no positive response from his audience (Jeremiah 1:2-3). He stood before kings and officials to declare the coming Babylonian captivity (Jeremiah 21:1-10). Even when he tried to remain silent, the word of God became like a burning fire shut up in his bones (Jeremiah 20:9). He endured the destruction of Jerusalem and the burning of the temple (Jeremiah 39:8). When he was accused of being a traitor for advising surrender to Babylon, he did not recant his message (Jeremiah 37:13-15). He continued to trust God's word even when he was sinking in the mud of a cistern (Jeremiah 38:6). He dictated his prophecies to Baruch the scribe even when the king burned the original scrolls (Jeremiah 36:27-32). He chose to stay with the poor remnant in the land of Judah rather than seeking a comfortable life in Babylon (Jeremiah 40:6). Throughout his ministry, he exemplified total reliance on the LORD'S promise to deliver him (Jeremiah 1:19). His reaction was one of persistent obedience in the face of universal opposition (Jeremiah 1:18).

## The Miraculous Preservation
God fulfilled **His** promise to deliver Jeremiah by protecting him during the violent fall of Jerusalem. While the kings and nobles were slaughtered or blinded, Jeremiah was treated with kindness by the invading army.

> Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon gave command concerning Jeremiah through Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, saying, "Take him, **look after him well, and do him no harm, but deal with him as he tells you.**" So Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard . . . and all the chief officers of the king of Babylon sent and **took Jeremiah from the court of the guard. They entrusted him to Gedaliah . . . that he should take him home. So he lived among the people.** — Jeremiah 39:11-14 (ESV)

The LORD also performed a miracle by using an Ethiopian eunuch named Ebed-melech to rescue Jeremiah from a certain death in the cistern (Jeremiah 38:7-13).

> "So Ebed-melech took the men with him . . . and **took from there old rags and worn-out clothes, which he let down to Jeremiah in the cistern by ropes.** . . . Then **they drew Jeremiah up with ropes and lifted him out of the cistern.**" — Jeremiah 38:11,13 (ESV)

## The Result of Faithfulness
The miracle of Jeremiah's preservation resulted in the survival of a faithful witness to God's word (Jeremiah 40:1-6). His prophecies were eventually compiled and served as a guide for the exiles in Babylon (Jeremiah 29:1-10). The result was that the people knew that a true prophet had been among them when every word he spoke came to pass (Jeremiah 28:9). His life also brought salvation and a promise of protection to Ebed-melech because he had put his trust in the LORD (Jeremiah 39:16-18). The nation of Judah was devastated, but the remnant was given hope for a future restoration (Jeremiah 30:1-3).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced a nation in rebellion and the threat of total destruction. He was limited by his youth and his deep sense of inadequacy. Despite his fears, the LORD promised to be his deliverer and to make him as strong as bronze walls. Jeremiah responded with lifelong obedience even when he was beaten, imprisoned, and abandoned. God intervened by sovereignly protecting him during the Babylonian invasion and rescuing him from a deadly cistern. The result was a lasting testimony to God's justice and **His** mercy toward a remnant. Jeremiah sacrificed his social standing, his freedom, and his desire for a normal family life. In the end, he gained the honor of being God's mouth and the assurance that the LORD'S word never fails. His story proves that we can trust the LORD to sustain us through any storm.
