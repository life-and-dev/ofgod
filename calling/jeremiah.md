# Jeremiah: The Prophet to the Nations

A young man is told that before he was even formed in the womb, he was appointed as a prophet to the nations. His mission involves tearing down and rebuilding kingdoms during a time of national collapse. Discover how Jeremiah survived the destruction of Jerusalem through God's faithfulness.

## The Problem of a National Collapse
Jeremiah lived during the final decades of the kingdom of Judah (Jeremiah 1:1-3). The nation was deeply entrenched in idolatry and social injustice (Jeremiah 2:5,13, 7:5-7). The political environment was unstable as the rising power of Babylon threatened to crush Jerusalem (Jeremiah 1:14-15). False prophets and corrupt leaders promised peace while disaster was imminent (Jeremiah 6:13-14). The people had forsaken the LORD, the fountain of living waters, and carved out broken cisterns for themselves (Jeremiah 2:13). The problem was that the people of Judah had broken their covenant with God and were facing inevitable judgment and exile (Jeremiah 11:10-11).

## Jeremiah's Priestly Condition
Jeremiah was the son of Hilkiah, one of the priests in Anathoth (Jeremiah 1:1). He was likely a young man, possibly in his late teens or early twenties, when the word of the LORD first came to him (Jeremiah 1:6). He did not perform any special sacrifice or fast to initiate this encounter (Jeremiah 1:4). He was simply living his life in the territory of Benjamin when **He** **Who** knew him from the womb decided it was time to speak (Jeremiah 1:5).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came to Jeremiah directly, revealing his predestined purpose and the nature of his difficult mission.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations**." — Jeremiah 1:4-5 (ESV)

> But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you you shall go, and whatever I command you you shall speak. **Do not be afraid of them, for I am with you to deliver you, declares the LORD**." — Jeremiah 1:7-8 (ESV)

> Then the LORD put out **His** hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth**. See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, **to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant**." — Jeremiah 1:9-10 (ESV)

> And the word of the LORD came to me a second time, saying, "What do you see?" And I said, "I see a boiling pot, facing away from the north." Then the LORD said to me, "Out of the north disaster shall be let loose upon all the inhabitants of the land. For behold, **I am calling all the tribes of the kingdoms of the north, declares the LORD, and they shall come and every one shall set his throne at the entrance of the gates of Jerusalem**, against all its walls around and against all the cities of Judah. And **I will declare My judgments against them, for all their evil in forsaking Me**. They have made offerings to other gods and worshiped the works of their own hands. But you, dress yourself for work; arise, and say to them everything that I command you. Do not be dismayed by them, lest I dismay you before them. For behold, **I make you this day a fortified city, an iron pillar, and bronze walls, against the whole land, against the kings of Judah, its officials, its priests, and the people of the land. They will fight against you, but they shall not prevail against you, for I am with you, declares the LORD, to deliver you**." — Jeremiah 1:13-19 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice his desire for a normal family life, as the LORD commanded him not to take a wife or have children in that place (Jeremiah 16:1-2). He risked his reputation and safety by delivering unpopular messages of judgment (Jeremiah 20:7-8). He was rejected by his own family and the people of his hometown (Jeremiah 11:21, 12:6). He gave up the comfort of being accepted by his peers to become a man of strife and contention (Jeremiah 15:10).

## Jeremiah's Initial Response
Jeremiah initially tried to excuse himself from the calling due to his perceived inadequacy. He was afraid of the responsibility and the opposition he would face (Jeremiah 1:6,8).

> Then I said, "Ah, LORD God! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." — Jeremiah 1:6 (ESV)

## Jeremiah's Faithful Reaction
Despite his initial fear, Jeremiah eventually surrendered to the command of the LORD (Jeremiah 1:17). He stood boldly before the people, the priests, and the kings of Judah to deliver the messages God gave him (Jeremiah 1:18). He endured public mockery, beatings, and imprisonment in a muddy cistern (Jeremiah 20:2, 38:6). There were times when the burden was so heavy that he cursed the day of his birth (Jeremiah 20:14-18). He even felt that the LORD had deceived him (Jeremiah 20:7). However, whenever he tried to remain silent, the word of the LORD became like a burning fire shut up in his bones (Jeremiah 20:9). He could not hold it in (Jeremiah 20:9). He continued to warn the people of the coming Babylonian invasion, even when they accused him of treason (Jeremiah 37:13-15). He trusted the LORD to be with him as a dread warrior to overcome his persecutors (Jeremiah 20:11). He remained in Jerusalem throughout the siege, choosing to suffer with his people rather than seeking safety (Jeremiah 38:17-28).

## The Divine Miracle
God fulfilled **His** promise to deliver Jeremiah and put **His** words in his mouth. Even when Jerusalem fell to the Babylonians, Jeremiah was supernaturally protected and given favor by the enemy king.

> Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon gave command concerning Jeremiah through Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, saying, "**Take him, look after him well, and do him no harm, but deal with him as he tells you.**" — Jeremiah 39:11-12 (ESV)

> The captain of the guard took Jeremiah and said to him . . . "Behold, I release you today from the chains on your hands. If it seems good to you to come with me to Babylon, come, and I will look after you well . . . **Behold, the whole land is before you; go wherever you think it good and right to go.**" — Jeremiah 40:2,4 (ESV)

The miracle fulfilled [God's promise to deliver him from his enemies](https://prophecies.ofgod.info/political/jeremiah-1-19) and to be with him to save him (Jeremiah 1:19).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in Jeremiah being granted his freedom while the rest of the leadership was taken into exile or executed (Jeremiah 40:1,4). He chose to stay with the remnant of the people in the land of Judah (Jeremiah 40:6). His survival was a living testimony to the truth of the words the LORD had put in his mouth (Jeremiah 40:2-3). Even the Babylonian captain acknowledged that the LORD had brought the disaster just as **He** said (Jeremiah 40:2). Jeremiah's ministry continued among the remnant, providing them with guidance and hope during a time of total devastation (Jeremiah 42:1-7). His written prophecies were preserved to encourage the exiles in Babylon and to promise a future restoration (Jeremiah 29:10-14).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced a nation that had abandoned the LORD and was on the brink of destruction. He was limited by his youth and his deep fear of conflict. However, God promised to be with him and to put **His** own words in his mouth to judge and rebuild. Jeremiah responded with decades of faithful, albeit painful, service despite constant persecution. God intervened by moving the heart of a pagan king to protect His prophet during the fall of Jerusalem. The result was the preservation of Jeremiah's life and the continuation of his message for future generations. Jeremiah sacrificed his personal comfort, family, and reputation. In return, he gained the honor of being the LORD'S mouthpiece and witnessed the faithfulness of the God **Who** delivers **His** servants even in the midst of national judgment.
