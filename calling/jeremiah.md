# Jeremiah: The Prophet of the New Covenant

A young man is called to deliver a message of doom to a nation that hates him. He faces imprisonment, death threats, and isolation while his beloved city crumbles. This is the story of how Jeremiah trusted God with his life and his sanity amidst total national collapse.

## The Problem of National Rebellion and Imminent Judgment
Jeremiah lived in the kingdom of Judah during its final decades before the Babylonian exile (Jeremiah 1:1-3). The political environment was volatile, with Judah caught between the superpowers of Egypt and Babylon (Jeremiah 2:18,36). Spiritually, the nation had abandoned the LORD, practicing idolatry and social injustice (Jeremiah 2:5,13, 7:5-6). The kings and priests were corrupt, leading the people away from God's laws (Jeremiah 2:8, 5:30-31). God warned that because of their persistent rebellion, Jerusalem would be destroyed and the people taken captive (Jeremiah 1:14-16, 25:8-11). Jeremiah was commanded to speak these unpopular truths to a people who refused to listen (Jeremiah 1:17, 7:27). He faced intense opposition from his own family, the religious elite, and the royal court (Jeremiah 11:18-21, 20:1-2, 26:8-11). The problem was the imminent divine judgment on a stubborn nation and the mortal danger Jeremiah faced for delivering God's warning (Jeremiah 1:18-19, 11:19).

## Jeremiah's Humble and Chosen Condition
Jeremiah was a young man from a priestly family in Anathoth when the word of the LORD first came to him (Jeremiah 1:1,6). He did not do anything special to get God's attention, such as fasting or praying for a calling (Jeremiah 1:4-5). Instead, God informed him that he had been chosen and consecrated before he was even formed in his mother's womb (Jeremiah 1:5). He was in a state of humble insecurity when he was called (Jeremiah 1:6).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke directly to Jeremiah, commissioning him as a prophet to the nations and promising **His** protection.

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice his desire for a normal life, including the right to marry and have children (Jeremiah 16:1-2). He gave up his reputation and became a laughingstock and an object of mockery (Jeremiah 20:7-8). He risked his life repeatedly, facing the threat of execution for treason (Jeremiah 26:11). He also sacrificed the comfort of his hometown, as the men of Anathoth sought to kill him (Jeremiah 11:21). He lived in constant social isolation (Jeremiah 15:17).

## Initial Response
Jeremiah initially responded with doubt and a sense of inadequacy due to his age (Jeremiah 1:6). He cried out that he did not know how to speak (Jeremiah 1:6). Later, under the weight of persecution, he even accused God of deceiving him and wished he had never been born (Jeremiah 20:7,14-18). He tried to stop speaking God's word, but it became like a burning fire shut up in his bones (Jeremiah 20:9).

## Jeremiah's Reaction
Despite his inner turmoil and the external threats, Jeremiah eventually responded with unwavering obedience to God's difficult calling (Jeremiah 1:17, 26:12-15). He stood before kings and false prophets, refusing to soften the message of judgment (Jeremiah 27:12, 28:15-16). When his scrolls were burned by King Jehoiakim, he simply dictated them again, adding even more words of warning (Jeremiah 36:27-32). He remained in Jerusalem during the horrific Babylonian siege, enduring starvation and the sight of his city's ruin (Jeremiah 37:21, 39:1-8). Even when he was thrown into a miry cistern to die, he did not recant his prophecies (Jeremiah 38:6,14-18). He chose to stay with the remnant of his people in the land rather than seeking a comfortable life in Babylon when offered the choice (Jeremiah 40:1-6). His reaction was characterized by a painful but persistent trust in the LORD's presence, even when he felt abandoned (Jeremiah 15:15-21, 20:11-13). He continued to pray for the people who hated him, showing a heart that mirrored God's own compassion (Jeremiah 14:7-9,19-22). Ultimately, Jeremiah surrendered his entire life to being the mouth-piece of the LORD, trusting that God's word would stand even if he perished (Jeremiah 1:17-19).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD performed the miracle of preserving Jeremiah's life through multiple assassination plots and the total destruction of Jerusalem. **He** also fulfilled the promise that **His** word would prevail.

> "Then the officials said to the king, 'Let this man be put to death . . . ' So they took Jeremiah and cast him into the cistern . . . And Jeremiah sank in the mire . . . Ebed-melech . . . took the men with him . . . and **they drew Jeremiah up with ropes and lifted him out of the cistern.**" — Jeremiah 38:4,6,11,13 (ESV)
>
> "The captain of the guard took Jeremiah and said to him . . . 'Behold, I release you today from the chains on your hands. If it seems good to you to come with me to Babylon, come, and I will look after you . . . ' **So the captain of the guard gave him an allowance of food and a present, and let him go.**" — Jeremiah 40:2,4-5 (ESV)

God fulfilled **His** promise to be with Jeremiah **to deliver you** and to make him a **fortified city, an iron pillar, and bronze walls** against the whole land (Jeremiah 1:18-19).

## The Result
The miracle of Jeremiah's preservation resulted in the survival of God's word during the exile. His prophecies provided the hope and direction the exiles needed, including the promise of a seventy-year limit on the captivity (Jeremiah 29:10). His life served as a living testimony to the faithfulness of the LORD amidst judgment (Jeremiah 39:15-18). The neighbors and nations witnessed that the words of the "weeping prophet" were indeed from God when Jerusalem fell exactly as he predicted (Jeremiah 40:2-3). His writings, including the Book of Lamentations, helped the nation process their grief and find a path back to God (Lamentations 3:21-25). Ultimately, his ministry pointed toward the New Covenant, where God's law would be written on the hearts of **His** people (Jeremiah 31:31-34).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced the problem of an imminent national catastrophe and his own paralyzing fear. He was a young priest with no political power and a message that everyone hated. God promised to be with him and to put **His** words in his mouth. Jeremiah responded with a lifetime of faithful, albeit painful, obedience. God intervened by miraculously protecting him from his enemies and the Babylonian sword. The result was the preservation of a prophetic witness that guided Israel through its darkest hour and pointed toward an eternal hope. Jeremiah sacrificed a life of comfort, family, and reputation, but he gained the honor of being God's faithful messenger and the assurance that the LORD is a dread warrior **Who** stands with **His** own. His life proves that we can trust God even when everything around us is falling apart.
