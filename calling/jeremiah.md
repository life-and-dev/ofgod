# Jeremiah: The Prophet to the Nations

A young man from a priestly family is chosen before birth for a task he feels unqualified to perform. He must deliver a message of judgment to a nation that will hate him for it. Discover how Jeremiah's trust in God sustained him through a lifetime of rejection.

## The Problem of National Apostasy
The people of Judah had forsaken the LORD and turned to the worship of idols (Jeremiah 1:16). They made offerings to other gods and worshiped the works of their own hands (Jeremiah 1:16). The political environment was unstable as the Babylonian Empire, led by Nebuchadrezzar, began to threaten the region (Jeremiah 21:2, 25:9). The kings, officials, priests, and people of the land were in active rebellion against God's commands (Jeremiah 1:18). They had broken the covenant and lived in systemic wickedness (Jeremiah 11:10). The problem was that Judah's persistent sin had reached a point where divine judgment was inevitable, and they needed a voice to call them to repentance (Jeremiah 1:10,14-16).

## Jeremiah's Humble Condition
Jeremiah was the son of Hilkiah, one of the priests who were in Anathoth in the land of Benjamin (Jeremiah 1:1). He was a young man when the word of the LORD first came to him (Jeremiah 1:6). Jeremiah did nothing special to get God's attention before **He** spoke (Jeremiah 1:4-5). He was simply a member of a priestly family living in his appointed land (Jeremiah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke directly to Jeremiah to appoint him as a prophet and to give him his life's mission.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born **I consecrated you; I appointed you a prophet to the nations.**" Then I said, "Ah, Lord GOD! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you you shall go, and whatever I command you you shall speak. Do not be afraid of them, for **I am with you to deliver you, declares the LORD.**" Then the LORD put out **His** hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth. See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant.**" . . . And the word of the LORD came to me a second time, saying, "What do you see?" And I said, "I see a boiling pot, facing away from the north." Then the LORD said to me, "Out of the north disaster shall be let loose upon all the inhabitants of the land. For behold, I am calling all the tribes of the kingdoms of the north, declares the LORD, and they shall come, and every one shall set his throne at the entrance of the gates of Jerusalem, against all its walls all around and against all the cities of Judah. And I will declare My judgments against them, for all their evil in forsaking Me. They have made offerings to other gods and worshiped the works of their own hands. But you, dress yourself for work; arise, and say to them everything that I command you. Do not be dismayed by them, lest I dismay you before them. And I, behold, **I make you this day a fortified city, an iron pillar, and bronze walls, against the whole land**, against the kings of Judah, its officials, its priests, and the people of the land. They will fight against you, but **they shall not prevail against you, for I am with you, declares the LORD, to deliver you.**" — Jeremiah 1:4-10,13-19 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice his social standing and the possibility of a normal, peaceful life (Jeremiah 16:2). The LORD commanded him not to take a wife or have sons and daughters in that place (Jeremiah 16:2). He sacrificed his reputation as he became known as a bringer of bad news (Jeremiah 20:8). He risked his life by speaking against the temple and the king (Jeremiah 26:8). He gave up the comfort of his home and was eventually taken to Egypt against his will (Jeremiah 43:6-7).

## Jeremiah's Initial Response
Jeremiah responded with immediate doubt and a sense of inadequacy (Jeremiah 1:6). He cried out to the Lord GOD that he did not know how to speak (Jeremiah 1:6). He used his youth as an excuse to avoid the calling (Jeremiah 1:6). He was afraid of the people to whom he was being sent (Jeremiah 1:8). Later, he even complained that the LORD had deceived him because of the suffering he endured (Jeremiah 20:7).

## Jeremiah's Faithful Reaction
Despite his fears, Jeremiah eventually surrendered to the word of the LORD (Jeremiah 1:17). He dressed himself for work and spoke everything that **He** commanded him (Jeremiah 1:17). He stood in the court of the LORD'S house and spoke to all the people of Judah (Jeremiah 26:2). He did not hold back a word (Jeremiah 26:2). Jeremiah remained in Jerusalem during the Babylonian sieges, continuing to deliver God's message even when he was imprisoned (Jeremiah 37:15, 38:6). He wrote the words of the LORD on a scroll to be read to the people (Jeremiah 36:2). When the king burned the scroll, Jeremiah dictated it again with even more words added (Jeremiah 36:27-32). He chose to stay with the remnant of the people rather than taking a position of comfort in Babylon (Jeremiah 40:6). He continued to pray for the people and seek the LORD'S guidance even when they refused to listen (Jeremiah 42:1-4). His reaction was one of persistent, agonizing obedience in the face of total national collapse (Jeremiah 9:1). He proved his trust by continuing to buy land in Anathoth as a sign of future hope while the city was being destroyed (Jeremiah 32:6-15).

## The Divine Miracle
God performed a miracle by preserving Jeremiah's life through decades of extreme hostility and multiple assassination attempts. He fulfilled **His** promise that the people would not prevail against him.

> "Then the officials said to the king, 'Let this man be put to death, for he is weakening the hands of the soldiers who are left in this city . . . ' So they took Jeremiah and cast him into the cistern of Malchiah . . . and there was no water in the cistern, but only mud, and Jeremiah sank in the mud. . . Ebed-melech . . . said to the king . . . 'these men have done evil in all that they did to Jeremiah the prophet by casting him into the cistern, for he will die there of hunger.' . . . So **Ebed-melech took the men with him and . . . drew Jeremiah up with ropes and lifted him out of the cistern.** . . And the captain of the guard took Jeremiah and said to him . . . 'Behold, **I release you today from the chains on your hands.** If it seems good to you to come with me to Babylon, come, and I will look after you.' " — Jeremiah 38:4-6,9-13, 40:2-4 (ESV)

In these events, the LORD fulfilled **His** promise to **deliver you** and that **they shall not prevail against you** (Jeremiah 1:8,19). He also made Jeremiah a **fortified city** and an **iron pillar** that remained standing while the rest of Judah fell (Jeremiah 1:18).

## The Lasting Result
The preservation of Jeremiah allowed the word of the LORD to be recorded for all future generations (Jeremiah 36:32). His prophecies provided the theological explanation for the exile, helping the survivors understand that God was still in control (Jeremiah 25:8-11). He gave the people the promise of a New Covenant, where the law would be written on their hearts (Jeremiah 31:31-34). His faithfulness served as a witness to the kings and officials of Judah and even to the Babylonians (Jeremiah 39:11-14). The result of his ministry was a preserved remnant and a roadmap for restoration after seventy years (Jeremiah 29:10). His life remains a testament to the fact that success in God's eyes is found in obedience rather than in popular approval (Jeremiah 1:19).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced the problem of a nation in rebellion and the personal limitation of his own fear and youth. He was a reluctant prophet who was rejected by his family, his neighbors, and his king. However, the LORD promised to be with him and to make him a fortified wall against his enemies. Jeremiah sacrificed his desire for a normal life and endured prison and mockery to remain faithful. God's intervention through various people and even enemy commanders proved His faithfulness to protect His servant. Jeremiah sacrificed his earthly peace and reputation, but he gained the honor of being God's mouth and the author of hope for an exiled nation. His story shows that we can trust the LORD to sustain us through the longest and darkest trials. He is the God Who remembers **His** servants and fulfills **His** word.
