# Jeremiah: A Prophet to the Nations

A young man is chosen to be a voice of warning to a nation on the brink of collapse. He must speak hard truths to kings and priests who want him dead. Discover how Jeremiah trusted God through dungeons, pits, and the destruction of his beloved city.

## The Problem of a Rebellious Nation

The people of Judah had forsaken the LORD and turned to idols (Jeremiah 1:16). They burned incense to other gods and worshiped the works of their own hands (Jeremiah 1:16). The kings of Judah, its officials, its priests, and the people of the land were in rebellion against God's law (Jeremiah 1:18). False prophets were speaking lies, promising peace when there was no peace (Jeremiah 6:14, 14:14). The political environment was volatile as the Babylonian Empire under Nebuchadnezzar began to threaten the sovereignty of Judah (Jeremiah 25:1,9). The nation was filled with injustice, oppression of the sojourner, the fatherless, and the widow (Jeremiah 7:6). Spiritual adultery was rampant throughout the land (Jeremiah 3:8-9). The people had stiffened their necks and refused to listen to the warnings of the former prophets (Jeremiah 7:25-26). The problem was that Judah's persistent sin had reached a point of no return, and divine judgment in the form of exile was imminent (Jeremiah 1:14-15, 7:15).

## Jeremiah's Humble Condition

Jeremiah was a young man from a priestly family in Anathoth when the word of the LORD first came to him (Jeremiah 1:1). He was likely in his late teens or early twenties (Jeremiah 1:6). He did nothing special like fasting or making a sacrifice to get God's attention (Jeremiah 1:4-5). The LORD had consecrated him and appointed him as a prophet to the nations even before he was formed in his mother's womb (Jeremiah 1:5). He was simply living his life when the divine call interrupted his youth (Jeremiah 1:4).

## The Divine Calling

The word of the LORD came to Jeremiah directly, establishing his identity and mission before his birth and promising him protection for the difficult task ahead.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; I appointed you a prophet to the nations."
>
> Then I said, "Ah, LORD God! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth."
>
> But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you you shall go, and whatever I command you you shall speak. Do not be afraid of them, for **I am with you to deliver you**, declares the LORD."
>
> Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth. See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant.**"
>
> — Jeremiah 1:4-10 (ESV)

Later, the LORD added:

> "They will fight against you, but **they shall not prevail against you, for I am with you, declares the LORD, to deliver you.**"
>
> — Jeremiah 1:19 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice

Jeremiah had to [sacrifice](https://word.ofgod.info/terms/sacrifice) his desire for a normal life, including marriage and children, as the LORD commanded him not to take a wife (Jeremiah 16:1-2). He sacrificed his reputation and social standing, becoming a laughingstock and a mock to everyone (Jeremiah 20:7-8). He risked his life repeatedly as he was beaten, put in stocks, and thrown into a muddy cistern to die (Jeremiah 20:2, 38:6). He gave up the comfort of his own people to remain faithful to a message of judgment (Jeremiah 15:10, 17).

## Jeremiah's Initial Response

Jeremiah initially tried to excuse himself by claiming he was too young and lacked the ability to speak (Jeremiah 1:6). He expressed fear and hesitation at the magnitude of the calling (Jeremiah 1:6). Later in his ministry, he struggled with deep despair and even cursed the day he was born (Jeremiah 20:14-18). He complained that the LORD had deceived him and that [the word of the LORD](https://word.ofgod.info) had become a reproach to him (Jeremiah 20:7-8). He even thought about not mentioning Him or speaking anymore in His name (Jeremiah 20:9).

## Jeremiah's Faithful Reaction

Despite his inner turmoil and outward persecution, Jeremiah eventually surrendered to the "burning fire" in his bones that compelled him to speak (Jeremiah 20:9). 

He faithfully delivered every message the LORD gave him, even when it led to his imprisonment (Jeremiah 32:2, 37:15). He wrote the words of the LORD on a scroll through his scribe Baruch, and when King Jehoiakim burned it, Jeremiah dictated it all again with many similar words added (Jeremiah 36:27-32). He remained in Jerusalem during the terrifying siege by the Babylonians, urging the king and the people to surrender to save their lives (Jeremiah 21:8-10, 38:17). He showed his trust in the future restoration of Israel by buying a field in Anathoth while the land was being conquered (Jeremiah 32:6-15). He continued to pray for the people even when the LORD told him to stop (Jeremiah 7:16, 14:11, 15:1). 

Jeremiah stayed with the remnant of the people after the fall of Jerusalem, refusing the offer of comfort in Babylon (Jeremiah 40:1-6). When the remnant insisted on fleeing to Egypt against God's command, Jeremiah went with them to continue being God's voice among them (Jeremiah 43:1-7). 

Throughout forty years of ministry, he never saw the national repentance he longed for, yet he never stopped speaking the truth (Jeremiah 25:3). He chose to suffer with his people rather than seek his own safety (Jeremiah 40:6). His life became a living parable of the messages he preached (Jeremiah 13:1-11, 18:1-6, 19:1-13). He proved that his trust was not in the outcomes but in the character of the One Who called him (Jeremiah 17:7-8). He demonstrated that even in the midst of total national collapse, a person can find hope in the steadfast love of the LORD (Lamentations 3:21-24). His faithfulness during the darkest time in Judah's history provided a bridge to the hope of a New Covenant (Jeremiah 31:31-34). He remained a "fortified city" and an "iron pillar" just as the LORD had promised (Jeremiah 1:18).

## The Divine Miracle

The LORD performed the miracle of preserving Jeremiah's life through multiple assassination attempts and the total destruction of Jerusalem. He ensured that the word spoken through Jeremiah was perfectly fulfilled.

> "And the LORD said to me, 'You have seen well, for **I am watching over My word to perform it.**'"
>
> — Jeremiah 1:12 (ESV)

When the city fell, the Babylonian commander gave specific orders to protect Jeremiah.

> "Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon gave command concerning Jeremiah through Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, saying, 'Take him, look after him well, and **do him no harm, but deal with him as he tells you.**'"
>
> — Jeremiah 39:11-12 (ESV)

This fulfilled the promise that **they shall not prevail against you** and **I am with you to deliver you** (Jeremiah 1:8,19). God also performed the miracle of giving Jeremiah the strength to endure forty years of rejection and sorrow without losing his faith (Jeremiah 15:19-21, 20:11).

## The Lasting Result

The miracle of Jeremiah's preservation resulted in the survival of a faithful witness during the exile (Jeremiah 40:6). His prophecies provided the framework for understanding the judgment as a temporary discipline rather than a final end (Jeremiah 29:10-11). 

Daniel, while in Babylon, studied the writings of Jeremiah and understood that the desolation of Jerusalem would last seventy years, which led him to pray for his people's return (Daniel 9:2). [Jeremiah's message of the New Covenant pointed forward to the ultimate restoration through the Christ](https://prophecies.ofgod.info/messianic/jeremiah-31-31) (Jeremiah 31:31-34; Hebrews 8:8-12). His faithfulness encouraged the faithful remnant to maintain their hope in the LORD even in a foreign land (Jeremiah 29:1-7). The fulfillment of his hard words established the certainty of God's promises for future blessing (Jeremiah 32:42-44). His life of suffering prefigured the ministry of the "Man of Sorrows" who would also be rejected by his own people (Isaiah 53:3; Matthew 16:14).

## Conclusion

Jeremiah faced the problem of a rebellious nation and the impending doom of his city. He was a young man with a sensitive heart who felt unqualified for the heavy task of a prophet. 

However, the LORD promised to be with him and to put His words in his mouth. Jeremiah sacrificed his personal happiness, safety, and reputation to remain obedient. God's promise to deliver him was miraculously fulfilled when the Babylonians spared him amidst the slaughter of Jerusalem. 

Jeremiah sacrificed a life of comfort, but he gained the honor of being God's faithful messenger and the herald of the New Covenant. His story proves that we can trust the LORD even when our world is falling apart and everyone is against us. He is faithful to protect His servants and to watch over His word until it is accomplished.
