# Jeremiah: The Prophet of Hope

A young man is chosen before birth to confront a rebellious nation. He must endure imprisonment and the destruction of his home while proclaiming a message no one wants to hear. Discover how his trust in God's presence transformed a message of judgment into a legacy of hope.

## Spiritual Rebellion
Judah had forsaken the LORD and turned to idols (Jeremiah 1:16). The people made offerings to other gods and worshiped the works of their own hands (Jeremiah 1:16). King Josiah had initiated reforms, but the hearts of the people remained far from God (2 Kings 23:25-26). The political environment was unstable as the Babylonian empire grew in power (Jeremiah 1:14-15). False prophets were telling the people that there would be peace, even though judgment was coming (Jeremiah 6:14). The leaders and priests were corrupt and did not seek the LORD (Jeremiah 2:8). Jerusalem was threatened by an invasion from the north that would bring disaster upon all the inhabitants of the land (Jeremiah 1:14). The people refused to repent despite many warnings (Jeremiah 7:25-26). The problem was that the nation's spiritual rebellion had reached a point where divine judgment was inevitable and the city of Jerusalem was marked for destruction (Jeremiah 1:15-16).

## Jeremiah's Humble Condition
Jeremiah was a young man from a priestly family in Anathoth (Jeremiah 1:1). He was only a youth when the word of the LORD first came to him (Jeremiah 1:6). There is no record that he did anything special like fasting or making a great sacrifice to get God's attention before the LORD revealed **His** word to him (Jeremiah 1:4-5). The LORD chose and consecrated him before he was even formed in the womb (Jeremiah 1:5).

## God's Word
The LORD spoke directly to Jeremiah to appoint him as a prophet to the nations (Jeremiah 1:4-5).

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations.**" Then I said, "Ah, LORD God! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you, you shall go, and whatever I command you, you shall speak. **Do not be afraid of them, for I am with you to deliver you**, declares the LORD." Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth. See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant.**" And the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Jeremiah, what do you see?" And I said, "I see an almond branch." Then the LORD said to me, "You have seen well, for I am watching over My word to perform it." The word of the LORD came to me a second time, saying, "What do you see?" And I said, "I see a boiling pot, facing away from the north." Then the LORD said to me, "Out of the north disaster shall be let loose upon all the inhabitants of the land. For behold, I am calling all the tribes of the kingdoms of the north, declares the LORD, and they shall come, and every one shall set his throne at the entrance of the gates of Jerusalem, against all its walls all around and against all the cities of Judah. And I will declare My judgments against them, for all their evil in forsaking Me. They have made offerings to other gods and worshiped the works of their own hands. But you, dress yourself for work; arise, and say to them everything that I command you. **Do not be dismayed by them, lest I dismay you before them. And I, behold, I make you this day a fortified city, an iron pillar, and bronze walls, against the whole land**, against the kings of Judah, its officials, its priests, and the people of the land. They will fight against you, but they shall not prevail against you, for **I am with you**, declares the LORD, **to deliver you**." — Jeremiah 1:4-19 (ESV)

## Personal Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice his desire for a normal family life (Jeremiah 16:2). The LORD commanded him not to take a wife or have children in that place because of the coming judgment (Jeremiah 16:2-4). He also had to sacrifice his reputation and the friendship of his peers (Jeremiah 20:10). He became a laughingstock and was mocked by everyone all day long (Jeremiah 20:7). He risked his life by speaking the truth to powerful kings and officials (Jeremiah 26:11).

## Initial Response
Jeremiah initially tried to refuse the calling by claiming that he was inadequate (Jeremiah 1:6). He told the LORD that he did not know how to speak because he was only a youth (Jeremiah 1:6). Later in his ministry, he even accused the LORD of deceiving him because the burden of the message was so heavy (Jeremiah 20:7). He felt that the word of the LORD had become a reproach and derision for him (Jeremiah 20:8).

## Jeremiah's Faithful Reaction
Despite his fears and the constant opposition, Jeremiah eventually obeyed every command from the LORD (Jeremiah 1:17). He stood before kings and priests to deliver messages of judgment and calls to repentance (Jeremiah 26:2). He was beaten and put in stocks by Pashhur the priest, yet he continued to speak (Jeremiah 20:1-3). He was thrown into a muddy cistern to die because he told the people to surrender to the Babylonians (Jeremiah 38:6). Even in the cistern, he did not change his message to please men (Jeremiah 38:14-18). Jeremiah struggled with his calling and even cursed the day he was born (Jeremiah 20:14). However, when he tried to remain silent, he found that God's word was like a burning fire shut up in his bones (Jeremiah 20:9). He became weary with holding it in and could not stay quiet (Jeremiah 20:9). He recognized that the LORD was with him as a dread warrior, so his persecutors would not prevail (Jeremiah 20:11). He remained in Jerusalem during the long and terrible siege by Nebuchadrezzar (Jeremiah 38:28). He continued to pray for the people even when they plotted against his life (Jeremiah 18:20). He bought a field in Anathoth as a sign of hope that the people would one day return to the land (Jeremiah 32:6-15). He dictated the words of the LORD to his scribe Baruch so that they would be preserved (Jeremiah 36:4). When the first scroll was burned by King Jehoiakim, he dictated it all again with additional words (Jeremiah 36:27-32). Jeremiah proved that his trust was in the LORD rather than in the approval of his nation (Jeremiah 17:7). He demonstrated that a faithful servant must speak God's truth even when it leads to suffering (Jeremiah 1:19).

## Divine Miracle
The LORD fulfilled **His** promise to deliver Jeremiah and to bring the judgment **He** had announced (Jeremiah 39:11-12). When Jerusalem finally fell to the Babylonians, the enemy king gave specific orders to protect the prophet (Jeremiah 39:11-12).

> "Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon gave command concerning Jeremiah through Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, saying, '**Take him, look after him well, and do him no harm, but deal with him as he tells you.**'" — Jeremiah 39:11-12 (ESV)

> "The captain of the guard took Jeremiah and said to him, 'The LORD your God pronounced this disaster against this place. . . . Now, behold, **I release you today from the chains on your hands. . . . See, the whole land is before you; go wherever you think it good and right to go.**'" — Jeremiah 40:2,4 (ESV)

The miracle fulfilled God's promise that Jeremiah would be a **fortified city** and that his enemies would **not prevail against him** (Jeremiah 1:18-19). Even the pagan king of Babylon was moved to **deliver him** and treat him with honor while the city around him was destroyed (Jeremiah 39:12).

## Final Result
The result of God's miracle was the survival of Jeremiah and the preservation of **His** word through the prophet (Jeremiah 40:6). Jeremiah was allowed to stay in the land with the remnant of the people who were not exiled to Babylon (Jeremiah 40:6). The fulfillment of his prophecies proved that he was indeed a true prophet of the LORD (Jeremiah 28:9). His writings provided comfort and direction for the exiles in Babylon, telling them that they would return after seventy years (Jeremiah 29:10-14). The preservation of Jeremiah showed the surrounding nations that the LORD protects those who are faithful to **Him** (Jeremiah 39:17-18). His life served as a witness to the Babylonian officials that the God of Israel is the true judge of nations (Jeremiah 40:2-3). The remnant of Judah was given a leader in Gedaliah, and Jeremiah lived among them for a time (Jeremiah 40:5-6).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced a rebellious nation and the destruction of Jerusalem. Though he felt inadequate and suffered intense persecution, he remained faithful to the LORD. God promised to deliver him, a promise fulfilled when the Babylonian army treated him with kindness while others were exiled. Jeremiah sacrificed his comfort, family life, and reputation, but he gained the honor of being a vessel for God's eternal word. His story proves that the LORD is faithful to **His** servants even in national disaster. We can trust that God will sustain us and fulfill **His** promises despite our limitations or the opposition we face.
