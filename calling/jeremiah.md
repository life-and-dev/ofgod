# Jeremiah: The Prophet to the Nations

A young man is called to deliver a message of judgment to a rebellious nation. Despite fear and overwhelming opposition, he remains faithful to the word of the LORD. His story is a testament to God's sustaining grace in the midst of national collapse.

## The Problem of Rebellion and Impending Judgment
Jeremiah lived during the final decades of the kingdom of Judah (Jeremiah 1:1-3). The people had abandoned the LORD to worship idols and had filled the land with injustice (Jeremiah 2:11-13; 7:5-7). King Josiah had attempted reforms, but the hearts of the people remained far from God (Jeremiah 3:10; 2 Kings 23:25-26). The political environment was volatile as the Babylonian Empire rose to power, threatening to destroy Jerusalem (Jeremiah 1:13-15). False prophets were everywhere, promising peace when there was no peace (Jeremiah 6:14; 8:11). The problem was that Judah's persistent rebellion had reached a point where divine judgment was inevitable, and they needed a voice to call them to repentance or prepare them for the coming destruction (Jeremiah 1:16; 4:1-4).

## Jeremiah's Humble Condition
Jeremiah was a young man from a priestly family in Anathoth when the word of the LORD came to him (Jeremiah 1:1). He had done nothing special to seek God's attention before **He** called him (Jeremiah 1:4-5). God had already chosen and consecrated him before he was even formed in his mother's womb (Jeremiah 1:5). He was in a state of youthful inadequacy and felt he lacked the ability to speak for the Almighty (Jeremiah 1:6).

## The Divine Calling
God communicated directly with Jeremiah through a verbal dialogue and a physical touch.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations**." — Jeremiah 1:4-5 (ESV)

> But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you you shall go, and **whatever I command you you shall speak**. Do not be afraid of them, for **I am with you to deliver you**, declares the LORD." — Jeremiah 1:7-8 (ESV)

> Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth**. See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, **to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant**." — Jeremiah 1:9-10 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice his personal happiness and social standing (Jeremiah 15:17). The LORD commanded him not to marry or have children as a sign of the coming judgment (Jeremiah 16:1-4). He lost the friendship of his neighbors and even his own family, who plotted against him (Jeremiah 11:19-21; 12:6). He was often mocked, beaten, and imprisoned for his message (Jeremiah 20:2,7-8; 37:15).

## The Initial Response
Jeremiah initially resisted the calling by claiming he was too young and unskilled in speech (Jeremiah 1:6). He expressed doubt in his ability to fulfill such a massive responsibility (Jeremiah 1:6). Later, under the weight of persecution, he even cursed the day of his birth and accused the LORD of deceiving him (Jeremiah 20:7,14-18).

## Jeremiah's Faithful Reaction
Jeremiah eventually submitted to the will of the LORD and spoke **His** words faithfully for over forty years (Jeremiah 1:3; 25:3). Even when his heart was broken for **His** people, he did not hold back the message of judgment (Jeremiah 4:19; 9:1). He confronted kings, priests, and false prophets with the truth (Jeremiah 21:11-12; 26:10-11; 28:15-17). He stayed in Jerusalem even as it was besieged, continuing to urge the people to surrender to the Babylonians to save their lives (Jeremiah 21:8-10; 38:17-18). When the king burned his scroll, Jeremiah simply dictated it again with even more words (Jeremiah 36:27-32). He remained in the land with the remnant after the city fell, still serving as God's mouthpiece (Jeremiah 40:1-6). His life was a constant act of trust in the LORD **Who** had promised to be with him (Jeremiah 1:19). He chose to suffer with the people of God rather than seek comfort elsewhere (Jeremiah 40:4-6).

## The Miracle of Divine Protection
The miracle was not a sudden rescue from all trouble, but God's supernatural protection and the fulfillment of **His** word exactly as **He** promised.

> "They will fight against you, but **they shall not prevail against you, for I am with you, declares the LORD, to deliver you**." — Jeremiah 1:19 (ESV)

> "Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon gave command concerning Jeremiah . . . '**Take him, look after him well, and do him no harm, but deal with him as he tells you**.'" — Jeremiah 39:11-12 (ESV)

God fulfilled this when the Babylonian commander, Nebuzaradan, released Jeremiah and gave him the choice to go anywhere he wanted, providing him with food and a gift (Jeremiah 40:4-5). While the city was **destroyed and overthrown**, Jeremiah was **delivered** just as the LORD had said (Jeremiah 39:11-14).

## The Result of a Vindicated Word
The result of Jeremiah's faithfulness was that God's word was vindicated before the entire nation (Jeremiah 39:1-10). Although the people suffered the consequences of their sin, Jeremiah's prophecies provided hope for a future restoration and a new covenant (Jeremiah 31:31-34). His witness ensured that the survivors in exile knew that their God was still in control and would bring them back (Jeremiah 29:10-14). Nations around Judah saw the power of the LORD's word fulfilled (Jeremiah 1:10; 40:2-3). Jeremiah himself survived the catastrophe and continued to guide the remnant (Jeremiah 42:1-6).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced a nation in rebellion and a heart of personal inadequacy. He had no power to change the people's hearts or stop the Babylonian army. God promised to make him a fortified city against the whole land. Jeremiah sacrificed his family life and social acceptance to remain a faithful witness. God intervened by protecting his life through multiple assassination attempts and providing for him even when Jerusalem fell. The result was a preserved word of God that still speaks to us today about the new covenant. Jeremiah sacrificed his earthly comfort but gained an eternal legacy as a prophet of the Most High. His life proves that even when everything else falls apart, the word of the LORD stands firm, and **He** is faithful to those who trust **Him**.
