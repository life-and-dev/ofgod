# Jeremiah: Fortified City

A young man from a priestly family is chosen before birth to confront kings and nations with a message of impending doom. He must endure decades of rejection and physical danger, only to witness the very destruction he spent his life trying to prevent.

## The Decay of Judah

The kingdom of Judah had fallen into deep spiritual decay and idol worship during the reign of Josiah and his successors (Jeremiah 1:1-3, 2:11-13). The people had forsaken the LORD, the fountain of living waters, and hewed out broken cisterns for themselves (Jeremiah 2:13). Politically, Judah was caught between the competing powers of Egypt and the rising Babylonian empire (Jeremiah 2:18,36). The religious leaders and prophets were leading the people astray by speaking lies and promising peace when there was no peace (Jeremiah 6:13-14). Judgment was coming from the north as the Babylonians prepared to besiege Jerusalem (Jeremiah 1:14-15, 39:1). The people refused to repent and instead trusted in their temple and their alliances (Jeremiah 7:4; 37:5-7). The core problem was a nation that had utterly abandoned its covenant with God and faced certain national annihilation (Jeremiah 1:16, 11:10).

## A Divine Appointment

Jeremiah was a young man from the priestly family in Anathoth when the LORD first spoke to him (Jeremiah 1:1,6). He did not seek a prophetic office through fasting, specific prayers, or any other ritual (Jeremiah 1:4-5). God chose and consecrated him before he was even formed in his mother's womb (Jeremiah 1:5). He was simply living his life as a youth when the word of the LORD came to him with a divine appointment (Jeremiah 1:4).

## The Prophetic Calling

God spoke directly to Jeremiah, declaring His sovereign plan for his life from before birth.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations.**" — Jeremiah 1:4-5 (ESV)

> Then I said, "Ah, Lord GOD! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you, you shall go, and **whatever I command you, you shall speak.** Do not be afraid of them, for **I am with you to deliver you**, declares the LORD." — Jeremiah 1:6-8 (ESV)

> Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth.** See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant." — Jeremiah 1:9-10 (ESV)

> "But you, dress yourself for work; arise, and say to them everything that I command you. Do not be dismayed by them, lest I dismay you before them. And I, behold, I make you this day **a fortified city, an iron pillar, and bronze walls**, against the whole land, against the kings of Judah, its officials, its priests, and the people of the land. They will fight against you, but they shall not prevail against you, for **I am with you**, declares the LORD, **to deliver you.**" — Jeremiah 1:17-19 (ESV)

## The Cost of Truth

Jeremiah had to sacrifice a normal life, including the comfort of marriage and family, as a sign of the coming judgment (Jeremiah 16:1-4). He lost his reputation and became a laughingstock, enduring constant mockery and derision from his peers (Jeremiah 20:7-8). He was rejected by his own family and the people of his hometown (Jeremiah 11:21, 12:6). He chose a life of isolation and sorrow over the social acceptance of his community (Jeremiah 15:17).

## Hesitation and Doubt

Jeremiah initially responded with doubt and hesitation, citing his lack of speaking ability and his young age (Jeremiah 1:6). He expressed fear and dismay at the overwhelming nature of the task (Jeremiah 1:6,8). Later, he even accused God of deceiving him because of the intense suffering his calling produced (Jeremiah 20:7). He struggled with the burden of the word of the LORD, which felt like a burning fire in his bones (Jeremiah 20:9).

## A Life of Submission

Despite his initial fears and ongoing suffering, Jeremiah eventually submitted to the LORD'S will and faithfully proclaimed every word He commanded (Jeremiah 1:17, 26:2). He stood alone against the kings, priests, and false prophets of Judah (Jeremiah 1:18, 28:1-17). He endured being put in stocks, beaten, and thrown into a muddy cistern where he nearly died of hunger (Jeremiah 20:2, 37:15, 38:6,9). Even when he tried to remain silent, he found that he could not contain the message God had placed within him (Jeremiah 20:9). He remained in Jerusalem during the horrific siege by the Babylonians, continuing to speak the truth even when it was viewed as treason (Jeremiah 21:1-10, 38:1-4). He dictated his prophecies to Baruch the scribe so they could be read to the people, and when the king burned the scroll, he recorded them again with even more words (Jeremiah 36:4,23,32). He lived out the reality of being a fortified city and an iron pillar, refusing to compromise the truth despite the extreme pressure to do so (Jeremiah 1:18, 15:20). He chose to stay with the remnant in Judah after the fall of Jerusalem rather than seeking comfort in Babylon (Jeremiah 40:1-6). His life became a living testimony of endurance and trust in the face of total national collapse (Jeremiah 39:11-12, 42:1-6).

## The Divine Protection

The miracle was God's supernatural protection and the precise fulfillment of His word. Despite the violent opposition of kings and the final destruction of the city, Jeremiah was preserved exactly as promised.

> Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon gave command concerning Jeremiah through Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, saying, "**Take him, look after him well, and do him no harm, but deal with him as he tells you.**" — Jeremiah 39:11-12 (ESV)

Through the favor of a pagan king, the LORD fulfilled His promise to **be with you to deliver you** (Jeremiah 1:8,19). God made him **a fortified city** that the whole land could not prevail against (Jeremiah 1:18-19). While Jerusalem fell and its leaders were captured or killed, Jeremiah was set free and given provision (Jeremiah 39:1-10, 40:4-5).

## The Invincible Witness

The result of God's intervention was the preservation of a faithful witness during Judah's darkest hour (Jeremiah 40:6). Jeremiah's prophecies provided the theological framework for the exiles to understand their situation not as God's defeat, but as His righteous judgment (Jeremiah 25:8-11, 29:10-14). His letter to the exiles in Babylon encouraged them to seek the welfare of the city and promised a future and a hope after seventy years (Jeremiah 29:7,11). His life proved that God's word is invincible even when the messenger is weak and despised (Jeremiah 1:12, 20:11). The neighboring nations witnessed the literal fulfillment of his words, vindicating the LORD'S sovereignty over all kingdoms (Jeremiah 1:10, 40:2-3). Ultimately, his record of God's faithfulness laid the groundwork for the eventual return of the remnant to the land (Jeremiah 30:1-3).

## Conclusion

Jeremiah faced the impossible problem of a rebellious nation destined for judgment and his own limitations as a fearful youth. He was beaten, imprisoned, and mocked, yet he remained faithful to the LORD'S command to speak the truth. God promised to deliver him and make him an iron pillar, a promise fulfilled when even the Babylonian conquerors were moved to protect him. Jeremiah sacrificed his social standing, his family life, and his personal safety. In return, he gained the honor of being God's mouth and the assurance that the LORD was with him as a dread warrior. His story demonstrates that we can trust God to sustain us through any trial, for His purposes will stand even when everything else crumbles. He never forsakes those He has appointed.
