# Jeremiah: The Reluctant Prophet

A young man from a small village receives a message that will set him against an entire nation. He is called to speak words of doom to kings and priests while facing betrayal. His survival depends entirely on the promise of a God he feels too young to serve.

## The Problem of a Falling Nation
The people of Judah had forsaken the LORD and turned to the worship of false gods (Jeremiah 1:16). They built high places to burn their sons in the fire as offerings to Baal (Jeremiah 19:5). The political environment was unstable as the Babylonian Empire emerged as a dominant threat to Jerusalem (Jeremiah 1:14-15). The leaders and priests were corrupt and led the people into deeper rebellion (Jeremiah 2:8). There was a systemic failure of justice and mercy toward the poor and the fatherless (Jeremiah 5:28). The nation was spiritually blind and refused to listen to the warnings of the few remaining faithful (Jeremiah 6:10). The problem was that Judah had broken its covenant with the LORD and faced imminent destruction from the north (Jeremiah 1:14).

## Jeremiah's Youthful Condition
Jeremiah was the son of Hilkiah, one of the priests who were in Anathoth (Jeremiah 1:1). He was a young man without experience in public speaking or leadership (Jeremiah 1:6). He did nothing special to get God's attention like fasting or long prayers (Jeremiah 1:4). He was simply a member of a priestly family living in his hometown (Jeremiah 1:1). The word of the LORD came to him during the thirteenth year of the reign of Josiah (Jeremiah 1:2).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke directly to Jeremiah to appoint him as a prophet to the nations.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations**."
>
> Then I said, "Ah, LORD God! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you you shall go, and whatever I command you you shall speak. **Do not be afraid of them, for I am with you to deliver you, declares the LORD.**"
>
> Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth**. See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant."
>
> — Jeremiah 1:4-10 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice the normal joys of life, including marriage and children (Jeremiah 16:2). He was forbidden from entering houses of mourning or feasting (Jeremiah 16:5,8). He sacrificed his reputation and became a laughingstock to his people (Jeremiah 20:7). He risked his life multiple times by speaking against the king and the temple (Jeremiah 26:8). He gave up the support of his own family and the people of his hometown who plotted to kill him (Jeremiah 11:21).

## Jeremiah's Initial Response
Jeremiah responded with a sense of inadequacy and fear (Jeremiah 1:6). He protested that he was only a youth and did not know how to speak (Jeremiah 1:6). Later, he expressed deep anguish and even cursed the day he was born (Jeremiah 20:14). He felt deceived by the LORD because of the intense suffering his calling brought (Jeremiah 20:7). He even attempted to stop speaking in the name of the LORD but found the word was like a fire shut up in his bones (Jeremiah 20:9).

## Jeremiah's Eventual Reaction
Despite his inner turmoil, Jeremiah remained faithful for over forty years (Jeremiah 1:2-3). He spoke every word the LORD commanded him, even when it led to his arrest (Jeremiah 37:15). He wore a wooden yoke around his neck to symbolize the coming Babylonian captivity (Jeremiah 27:2). He continued to prophesy even while confined in the court of the guard (Jeremiah 32:2). Jeremiah showed his trust by buying a field in Anathoth during the siege, believing God's promise of future restoration (Jeremiah 32:9-15). He refused to change his message to please the kings Zedekiah or Jehoiakim (Jeremiah 36:27-32, 38:14-18). He endured being cast into a cistern where he sank into the mire (Jeremiah 38:6). Through every trial, he looked to the LORD as a dread warrior who was with him (Jeremiah 20:11). He faithfully recorded the prophecies through his scribe Baruch (Jeremiah 36:4). His reaction was one of persistent, suffering obedience in the face of universal rejection (Jeremiah 1:18-19).

## The Divine Miracle
God miraculously protected Jeremiah and fulfilled every word spoken through him. When Jerusalem finally fell, the LORD ensured Jeremiah's safety through the hand of his enemies.

> Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon gave charge concerning Jeremiah through Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, saying, "**Take him, look after him well, and do him no harm, but deal with him as he tells you.**" . . . The captain of the guard took Jeremiah and said to him, . . . "Behold, **I release you today from the chains on your hands.** If it seems good to you to come with me to Babylon, come, and I will look after you well."
>
> — Jeremiah 39:11-12, 40:2,4 (ESV)

God fulfilled **His** promise that **I am with you to deliver you** and that **I have put My words in your mouth** (Jeremiah 1:8-9). The word of the LORD proved true as Jerusalem was destroyed exactly as prophesied (Jeremiah 39:1-8).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the preservation of a faithful remnant who would eventually return from exile (Jeremiah 29:10-14). Jeremiah's prophecies provided hope and direction for the exiles in Babylon (Jeremiah 29:1). He delivered the promise of a new covenant where the Law would be written on the hearts of the people (Jeremiah 31:31-34). His faithfulness served as a witness to the nations that the God of Israel is sovereign over history (Jeremiah 1:10). The neighboring nations saw the fulfillment of his words and feared the power of the LORD (Jeremiah 40:2-3). Jeremiah's life became a standard for prophetic endurance for future generations (Matthew 16:14).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced the problem of a rebellious nation destined for judgment and his own limitations as a young, fearful man. He was a social outcast who suffered deeply for his message. However, the LORD promised to make him a fortified city and to deliver him from his enemies. Jeremiah sacrificed his comfort, his family life, and his reputation to remain a faithful witness. God's intervention through the protection of the Babylonian officials proved **His** absolute faithfulness. Jeremiah sacrificed his earthly peace, but he gained an eternal legacy as the prophet who spoke of the new covenant. His story proves that we can trust the LORD even in the midst of national disaster and personal suffering. **He** is the LORD **Who** watches over **His** word to perform it.
