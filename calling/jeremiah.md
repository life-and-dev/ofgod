# Jeremiah: The Reluctant Prophet

A young priest is called to deliver a message of judgment to a rebellious nation. Despite intense opposition, he must stand alone against kings and priests. Discover how God's presence sustained Jeremiah through the fall of a kingdom and a terrifying muddy death.

## The Problem of National Rebellion
The kingdom of Judah was in a state of spiritual and moral decay (Jeremiah 2:13). The people had forsaken the LORD, the fountain of living waters, and hewed out cisterns for themselves, broken cisterns that could hold no water (Jeremiah 2:13). They worshipped idols and practiced injustice (Jeremiah 7:5-6, 9:2-3). The political environment was volatile, with the threat of the Babylonian Empire looming on the horizon (Jeremiah 1:14-15). The kings and priests of Judah were leading the people astray, ignoring the warnings of the prophets (Jeremiah 5:30-31, 23:1-2). Jeremiah was the son of Hilkiah, one of the priests in Anathoth (Jeremiah 1:1). He lived during the reigns of several kings, including Josiah, Jehoiakim, and Zedekiah (Jeremiah 1:2-3). The problem was that the nation was on the brink of total destruction and exile because of its persistent rebellion against the LORD (Jeremiah 25:8-11).

## Jeremiah's Youthful Condition
Jeremiah was a young man when the word of the LORD first came to him (Jeremiah 1:6). He was from a priestly family in the land of Benjamin (Jeremiah 1:1). There is no record that Jeremiah did anything special, like fasting or praying, to seek God's attention before his calling (Jeremiah 1:4-5). God chose him and consecrated him before he was even formed in his mother's womb (Jeremiah 1:5). He was simply a youth in a priestly household when the LORD spoke (Jeremiah 1:6).

## The Divine Calling
God spoke directly to Jeremiah to appoint him as a prophet to the nations.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations**." Then I said, "Ah, LORD God! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you, you shall go, and **whatever I command you, you shall speak**. Do not be afraid of them, for **I am with you to deliver you**, declares the LORD." Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth**. See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant." — Jeremiah 1:4-10 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice his personal comfort and his reputation among his peers (Jeremiah 15:17). He was forbidden by the LORD from marrying or having children as a sign of the coming judgment (Jeremiah 16:1-4). He faced constant ridicule, beatings, and imprisonment (Jeremiah 20:2, 37:15). He risked his life by delivering messages that were seen as treasonous by the kings of Judah (Jeremiah 26:8-11, 38:4). He was an outcast in his own community (Jeremiah 15:10).

## The Initial Response
Jeremiah initially tried to refuse God's calling because of his perceived limitations (Jeremiah 1:6). He argued that he did not know how to speak and that he was only a youth (Jeremiah 1:6). He expressed fear of the people to whom he was being sent (Jeremiah 1:8). Later, in his moments of deep despair, he even cursed the day he was born (Jeremiah 20:14-18). He accused the LORD of deceiving him when the message brought him nothing but reproach (Jeremiah 20:7-8).

## Jeremiah's Eventual Reaction
Despite his initial hesitations and ongoing struggles, Jeremiah eventually obeyed the LORD's command (Jeremiah 1:17). He stood boldly in the court of the LORD's house and in the gates of the city to proclaim the words he was given (Jeremiah 7:1-2, 17:19-20). He dictated the words of the LORD to his scribe Baruch, who read them to the people and the officials (Jeremiah 36:1-4,10). When King Jehoiakim burned the scroll, Jeremiah dictated it again, adding even more words (Jeremiah 36:27-32). He wore a wooden yoke around his neck to symbolize the coming subjection to Babylon (Jeremiah 27:2). When the false prophet Hananiah broke the wooden yoke, Jeremiah returned with a message that the LORD would replace it with a yoke of iron (Jeremiah 28:10-14). He continued to preach submission to Babylon as the only way to survive, even when he was accused of deserting to the enemy (Jeremiah 37:13-14, 38:17-18). He was cast into a muddy cistern to die, but he did not recant his message (Jeremiah 38:6). He showed his trust in God's future promise by purchasing a field in Anathoth while the city was under siege (Jeremiah 32:6-15). Jeremiah's reaction was one of faithful, though often painful, endurance in the face of universal opposition (Jeremiah 15:19-21).

## The Divine Miracle
God miraculously preserved Jeremiah's life through multiple assassination plots and the final destruction of Jerusalem. **He** moved the hearts of unexpected people to protect **His** prophet.

> So they took Jeremiah and cast him into the cistern of Malchiah, the king's son, which was in the court of the guard, letting Jeremiah down by ropes. And there was no water in the cistern, but only mud, and Jeremiah sank in the mud. . . Then the king commanded Ebed-melech the Ethiopian, "Take thirty men with you from here, and **lift Jeremiah the prophet out of the cistern before he dies**." . . . Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon gave command concerning Jeremiah through Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, saying, "Take him, look after him well, and do him no harm, but **deal with him as he tells you**." — Jeremiah 38:6,10, 39:11-12 (ESV)

In these events, the LORD fulfilled **His** promise that **"I am with you to deliver you"** and that **"I have put My words in your mouth"** to ensure his survival (Jeremiah 1:8-9).

## The Lasting Result
The result of **His** intervention was that Jeremiah survived the fall of Jerusalem while the kings and officials who opposed **Him** were judged (Jeremiah 39:4-7, 52:10-11). He was given the choice to go to Babylon or stay in the land, and he chose to remain with the remnant of the people (Jeremiah 40:1-6). His prophecies were vindicated when the city was burned and the people were taken into exile (Jeremiah 39:8-9, 52:12-15). His purchase of the field became a sign of hope that houses and fields would again be bought in the land (Jeremiah 32:15,44). His words continued to provide comfort and guidance to the exiles in Babylon, pointing towards a future New Covenant (Jeremiah 31:31-34). The nations around Judah saw the fulfillment of his words and recognized the power of the LORD (Jeremiah 40:2-3).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced a nation in rebellion and personal struggles with fear. He was a "weeping prophet" who felt the weight of his message and his people's pain. Despite his desire to quit, the LORD promised to be with him and put **His** words in his mouth. Jeremiah sacrificed his family, reputation, and freedom to remain faithful. God intervened by rescuing him from a muddy death and granting him favor with enemies. The result was a legacy of hope and a witness to God's sovereignty. Jeremiah sacrificed a normal life but gained the honor of being God's mouthpiece. His story teaches us to trust the LORD's presence in our darkest moments. **He** is the LORD **Who** never forsakes **His** own.
