# Jeremiah: Hesitant Prophet

A young man from a priestly family is chosen for a mission he feels unqualified to perform. His words will determine the fate of a nation, but his message brings him nothing but hatred and imprisonment. Witness how God sustains His prophet in the face of total rejection.

## The Problem of a Rebellious Nation
Jeremiah lived in the land of Benjamin during the final years of the Kingdom of Judah (Jeremiah 1:1-3). The political environment was one of extreme instability as the Babylonian Empire rose to power (Jeremiah 25:1,9). The kings of Judah had forsaken the LORD and turned to the worship of idols and the works of their own hands (Jeremiah 1:16). The priests and the people were corrupt and refused to listen to God's warnings (Jeremiah 1:18-19). They sought security in political alliances with Egypt rather than trusting in the LORD (Jeremiah 2:18,36). The nation faced imminent destruction and exile at the hands of the Chaldeans because of their persistent rebellion (Jeremiah 21:4-10). Jeremiah was born into a society that was spiritually bankrupt and under the shadow of divine judgment (Jeremiah 1:14-16). The problem was that the people of Judah had abandoned their covenant with God and faced certain national collapse unless they repented (Jeremiah 2:13,19).

## Jeremiah's Humble Condition
Jeremiah was a young man when the LORD first spoke to him (Jeremiah 1:6). He was the son of Hilkiah, one of the priests who lived in Anathoth (Jeremiah 1:1). There is no record that Jeremiah prayed, fasted, or performed any special religious ritual to get God's attention before the LORD spoke to him (Jeremiah 1:4-5). God had already consecrated and appointed him before he was even formed in the womb (Jeremiah 1:5). He was simply a youth in a priestly household when the word of the LORD came to him (Jeremiah 1:2,6).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came directly to Jeremiah, declaring His sovereignty and giving him a mandate to speak to the nations.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born **I consecrated you; I appointed you a prophet to the nations**."
>
> Then I said, "Ah, Lord GOD! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom **I send you, you shall go, and whatever I command you, you shall speak**. Do not be afraid of them, for **I am with you to deliver you**," declares the LORD.
>
> Then the LORD **put out His hand and touched my mouth**. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth**. See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant."
>
> And the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Jeremiah, what do you see?" And I said, "I see an almond branch." Then the LORD said to me, "You have seen well, for **I am watching over My word to perform it**."
>
> The word of the LORD came to me a second time, saying, "What do you see?" And I said, "I see a boiling pot, facing away from the north." Then the LORD said to me, "Out of the north disaster shall be let loose upon all the inhabitants of the land. For behold, I am calling all the tribes of the kingdoms of the north," declares the LORD, "and they shall come, and every one shall set his throne at the entrance of the gates of Jerusalem, against all its walls all around and against all the cities of Judah. And I will declare **My** judgments against them, for all their evil in forsaking **Me**. They have made offerings to other gods and worshiped the works of their own hands.
>
> But you, dress yourself for work; arise, and say to them everything that I command you. Do not be dismayed by them, lest I dismay you before them. And **I, behold, I make you this day a fortified city, an iron pillar, and bronze walls**, against the whole land, against the kings of Judah, its officials, its priests, and the people of the land. They will fight against you, but they shall not prevail against you, for **I am with you**, declares the LORD, **to deliver you**." — Jeremiah 1:4-19 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice his personal safety and social standing (Jeremiah 20:1-2, 26:8). He was forbidden from marrying or having children as a sign of the coming judgment (Jeremiah 16:1-4). He lost the support of his own family and neighbors in Anathoth who conspired to kill him (Jeremiah 11:18-21). He risked his life by delivering a message of surrender to the Babylonian king which was seen as treason (Jeremiah 38:1-4). He gave up a life of priestly comfort for one of constant rejection and suffering (Jeremiah 15:10).

## Jeremiah's Initial Response
Jeremiah initially refused the calling by claiming that he was too young and unqualified (Jeremiah 1:6). He expressed his fear and inadequacy by stating that he did not know how to speak (Jeremiah 1:6). He struggled with the weight of the message and even cursed the day of his birth in his despair (Jeremiah 20:14-18). He complained to God that he had become a laughingstock and was mocked all day long (Jeremiah 20:7-8). He even tried to stop speaking in the name of the LORD but found that the word was like a fire shut up in his bones (Jeremiah 20:9).

## Jeremiah's Faithful Reaction
Jeremiah eventually obeyed God's command and faithfully delivered the words of the LORD for over forty years (Jeremiah 25:3). He stood before kings and officials to declare their sins and the coming judgment (Jeremiah 22:1-5, 26:12-15). He suffered being beaten and put in stocks by the priest Pashhur (Jeremiah 20:1-2). He was nearly put to death by the priests and prophets for prophesying against the temple (Jeremiah 26:8-11). He remained in Jerusalem while it was under siege and continued to preach surrender to Babylon as the only way to live (Jeremiah 21:8-10, 38:17-18). He was cast into a muddy cistern by officials who wanted him dead but was rescued by Ebed-melech (Jeremiah 38:6-13). He refused to flee to Egypt despite the threats of the remaining leaders after the fall of Jerusalem (Jeremiah 42:1-22). His reaction was characterized by a persistence that overcame his own fear and the hostility of the entire nation (Jeremiah 1:17-19). He trusted the LORD even when he felt alone and abandoned by everyone (Jeremiah 15:15-21).

## The Divine Miracle
God miraculously preserved Jeremiah's life throughout the siege and the fall of Jerusalem as He had promised. He raised up unlikely allies to protect His prophet from those who sought to kill him.

> Then the king commanded Ebed-melech the Ethiopian, "Take thirty men with you from here, and **lift Jeremiah the prophet out of the cistern before he dies**." . . So Ebed-melech took the men with him . . . and **they drew Jeremiah up with ropes and lifted him out of the cistern**. . . Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon gave command concerning Jeremiah . . . "Take him, look after him well, and do him no harm, but **deal with him as he tells you**." So . . . they sent and took Jeremiah from the court of the guard. . . **He dwelt among the people.** — Jeremiah 38:10-13, 39:11-14 (ESV)

In this miracle, God fulfilled His promise to be with Jeremiah to deliver him from his enemies (Jeremiah 1:8,19). He also made Jeremiah a fortified city and an iron pillar that the whole land could not prevail against (Jeremiah 1:18-19).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the survival of Jeremiah and a small remnant of the people (Jeremiah 39:11-14, 40:1-6). The Babylonian king showed Jeremiah great favor and allowed him to choose where he would live (Jeremiah 40:4). Jeremiah's prophecies were vindicated by the total destruction of Jerusalem and the exile of the people as he had foretold (Jeremiah 39:1-8). His writings provided hope for the future restoration of Israel and the promise of a new covenant (Jeremiah 31:31-34). The faithful Ethiopian Ebed-melech was also saved from death because he trusted in the LORD (Jeremiah 39:15-18). Jeremiah's life served as a witness to God's judgment and His mercy to those who obey Him (Jeremiah 40:5-6).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah was a young and hesitant man who faced the problem of a rebellious nation destined for destruction. He was limited by his youth and his fear of the people who hated his message. Despite his personal struggles and the physical suffering he endured, he remained faithful to the LORD'S calling. God fulfilled His promise to deliver Jeremiah and provided him with protection even in the midst of a national catastrophe. Jeremiah sacrificed his reputation, his family life, and his comfort to serve as God's prophet. In return, he gained the protection of the LORD and the honor of being the messenger of the new covenant. His story proves that even when we feel unqualified and alone, we can trust in the LORD'S presence and power. He is the God Who watches over His word to perform it.
