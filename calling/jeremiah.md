# Jeremiah: The Weeping Prophet

A young man is told that before he was born, God had already chosen him for a dangerous mission. He must stand alone against kings, priests, and an entire nation to deliver a message of inevitable destruction. His only hope is a promise of protection.

## The Problem of National Rebellion
Jeremiah was the son of Hilkiah, one of the priests in Anathoth (Jeremiah 1:1). He began his ministry in the thirteenth year of King Josiah (Jeremiah 1:2). The political and spiritual environment in Judah was one of deep corruption and idolatry (Jeremiah 1:16). The people had forsaken the LORD and made offerings to other gods (Jeremiah 1:16). They worshiped the works of their own hands (Jeremiah 1:16). Their fathers had also forsaken the LORD and gone after other gods (Jeremiah 16:11). The people had done worse than their fathers, following their own stubborn, evil will (Jeremiah 16:12). They refused to listen to the voice of the LORD (Jeremiah 16:12). The spiritual leadership was failing as the priests and prophets did not know the LORD (Jeremiah 2:8). The problem was that Judah's systemic rebellion and idolatry had made a severe national judgment from the north unavoidable (Jeremiah 1:14-15).

## Jeremiah's Humble Condition
Jeremiah was only a youth when the word of the LORD first came to him (Jeremiah 1:6). He did not do anything special like fasting or visiting the temple to get God's attention (Jeremiah 1:4-5). The LORD chose him and consecrated him before he was even formed in the womb (Jeremiah 1:5). He felt completely inadequate and lacked the confidence to speak (Jeremiah 1:6). He was in a state of humble dependence on his priestly heritage before **He** intervened (Jeremiah 1:1,6).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke directly to Jeremiah and even touched his mouth to confirm the message he was to deliver to the nations.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations**."
>
> Then I said, "Ah, LORD God! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you, you shall go, and whatever I command you, you shall speak. Do not be afraid of them, for **I am with you to deliver you**, declares the LORD."
>
> Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth. See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant.**"
>
> — Jeremiah 1:4-10 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice the normal joys of life, including marriage and children, to serve as a living sign of coming judgment (Jeremiah 16:2). He had to sacrifice his reputation and became a laughingstock and a target of mockery (Jeremiah 20:7-8). He risked his life multiple times and was even put in the stocks (Jeremiah 20:2). He gave up the approval of his own family and friends who plotted against him (Jeremiah 11:19-21; 20:10).

## Jeremiah's Initial Response
Jeremiah initially tried to refuse the calling by claiming he was too young and did not know how to speak (Jeremiah 1:6). He struggled with the weight of the message and even cursed the day of his birth (Jeremiah 20:14). He complained that the LORD had deceived him because of the intense persecution he faced (Jeremiah 20:7). At one point, he decided not to mention the LORD or speak anymore in **His** name (Jeremiah 20:9).

## Jeremiah's Faithful Reaction
Despite his inner turmoil, Jeremiah could not stop speaking because the word of the LORD was like a burning fire shut up in his bones (Jeremiah 20:9). He eventually submitted to the LORD'S command and spoke everything he was commanded (Jeremiah 1:17). He stood as a fortified city and an iron pillar against the whole land (Jeremiah 1:18). He continued to warn the kings of Judah even when he was imprisoned in the court of the guard (Jeremiah 37:15-17; 38:13-14). He dictated the words of the LORD to Baruch the scribe so they could be read to the people (Jeremiah 36:4). When the first scroll was burned by King Jehoiakim, he faithfully wrote another one with even more words (Jeremiah 36:27-32). He chose to stay with the poor remnant in Judah rather than taking a comfortable position in Babylon (Jeremiah 40:4-6). He consistently placed his trust in the protection of the LORD despite constant threats (Jeremiah 20:11).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD miraculously protected Jeremiah's life during the siege and fall of Jerusalem, just as **He** had promised in his calling.

> "Take him, look after him well, and **do him no harm, but deal with him as he tells you.**" . . . They entrusted him to Gedaliah . . . so he lived among the people. . . . "For I will surely save you, and you shall not fall by the sword, but you shall have your **life as a prize of war, because you have put your trust in Me**, declares the LORD." — Jeremiah 39:12,14,18 (ESV)

In these events, the LORD fulfilled **His** promise that **I am with you to deliver you** (Jeremiah 1:8,19). **He** turned the heart of the pagan king Nebuchadnezzar to favor **His** prophet (Jeremiah 39:11).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the preservation of the word of the LORD through the Babylonian exile (Jeremiah 40:1). Jeremiah's prophecies provided hope for the future restoration of Israel and the promise of a New Covenant (Jeremiah 31:31-34). His faithfulness served as a witness to the nations that God's word is true and will be performed (Jeremiah 1:12). The people who remained in the land were given a chance to live under a governor and gather a great abundance of fruits (Jeremiah 40:12). His writings became a foundational part of the scriptures, guiding the exiles back to the LORD (Daniel 9:2). The effect on his neighbors was a demonstration that one man standing with God is stronger than a nation in rebellion (Jeremiah 1:18-19).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced a rebellious nation with a message of judgment that brought him only pain and isolation. He was limited by his youth and his sensitive heart, often weeping for the people who hated him. However, the LORD promised to be with him and to put **His** words in his mouth. Jeremiah's initial attempts to quit were overcome by the fire of God's word within him. God intervened by protecting him from his enemies and the destruction of the city. Jeremiah sacrificed his social status, family life, and physical comfort to honor his calling. In return, he gained an eternal legacy as a faithful prophet and the joy of seeing God's word vindicated. His life proves that the LORD is faithful to deliver those who trust in **Him** despite their own weakness. We can always trust that **His** purpose will stand.
