# Jeremiah: The Reluctant Prophet

A young man is appointed to be a prophet to the nations before he is even born. He must deliver a message of certain doom to a people who will despise him. Discover how God protected **His** messenger amidst total national collapse.

## The Corruption of Judah
The people of Judah had forsaken the LORD and turned to the worship of idols (Jeremiah 1:16). They burned offerings to other gods and worshiped the works of their own hands (Jeremiah 1:16). The kings and the people were consistently rebellious and refused to listen to the warnings of the prophets (Jeremiah 1:18). Jerusalem was threatened by kingdoms from the north which God would call to execute judgment (Jeremiah 1:14-15). The political environment was unstable as different kings rose and fell while Babylon's power grew (Jeremiah 1:1-3). Wickedness was prevalent from the least to the greatest among the people (Jeremiah 5:1). The religious leaders and priests were also corrupt and led the people astray (Jeremiah 2:8). The central problem was that the nation had broken its covenant with God and faced imminent destruction from Nebuchadrezzar (Jeremiah 21:2,7).

## Jeremiah's Humble Beginnings
Jeremiah was the son of Hilkiah who was one of the priests in Anathoth (Jeremiah 1:1). He was a young man when the word of the LORD first came to him (Jeremiah 1:6). He did not perform any special fast or ritual to seek a calling from God (Jeremiah 1:4-5). He did not visit the temple to ask for a vision (Jeremiah 1:4). Instead, the LORD chose him and consecrated him before he was even formed in his mother's womb (Jeremiah 1:5). He was simply living his life in the land of Benjamin when God intervened (Jeremiah 1:1).

## The Prophetic Calling
The LORD spoke directly to Jeremiah to appoint him as a prophet and to prepare him for the intense opposition he would face.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; I appointed you a prophet to the nations." Then I said, "Ah, LORD God! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you you shall go, and whatever I command you you shall speak. **Do not be afraid of them, for I am with you to deliver you, declares the LORD.**" Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, I have put My words in your mouth. See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, **to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant.**" And the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Jeremiah, what do you see?" And I said, "I see an almond branch." Then the LORD said to me, "You have seen well, for I am watching over My word to perform it." The word of the LORD came to me a second time, saying, "What do you see?" And I said, "I see a boiling pot, facing away from the north." Then the LORD said to me, "Out of the north disaster shall be let loose upon all the inhabitants of the land. For behold, I am calling all the tribes of the kingdoms of the north, declares the LORD, and they shall come, and every one shall set his throne at the entrance of the gates of Jerusalem, against all its walls all around and against all the cities of Judah. And I will declare My judgments against them, for all their evil in forsaking Me. They have made offerings to other gods and worshiped the works of their own hands. But you, dress yourself for work; arise, and say to them everything that I command you. Do not be dismayed by them, lest I dismay you before them. And I, behold, **I make you this day a fortified city, an iron pillar, and bronze walls, against the whole land**, against the kings of Judah, its officials, its priests, and the people of the land. They will fight against you, but they shall not prevail against you, for **I am with you, declares the LORD, to deliver you.**" — Jeremiah 1:4-19 (ESV)

## The Personal Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice the possibility of having a normal family life (Jeremiah 16:2). The LORD commanded him not to take a wife or have sons and daughters in that place because of the coming judgment (Jeremiah 16:3-4). He also had to sacrifice his social standing and reputation (Jeremiah 15:10). He became a man of strife and contention to the whole land (Jeremiah 15:10). He risked his life every time he spoke the unpopular message of surrender to Babylon (Jeremiah 26:8). He faced constant loneliness and was forbidden from joining in social feasts or mourning (Jeremiah 16:5,8).

## Initial Fear and Doubt
Jeremiah initially tried to refuse the calling because he felt unqualified (Jeremiah 1:6). He argued that he did not know how to speak because he was only a youth (Jeremiah 1:6). He expressed great distress over the burden of his message (Jeremiah 4:19). At times, he even cursed the day of his birth because of the sorrow he endured (Jeremiah 20:14). He complained to God that he had become a laughingstock and that everyone mocked him (Jeremiah 20:7). He once decided not to speak in the name of the LORD anymore because of the reproach (Jeremiah 20:9).

## A Life of Faithful Reaction
Despite his fears and the intense persecution, Jeremiah chose to obey the LORD (Jeremiah 1:17). He spoke every word that God commanded him even when it led to his arrest (Jeremiah 26:12-15). He endured being beaten and put in stocks by Pashhur the priest (Jeremiah 20:1-2). He remained faithful even when he was thrown into a cistern to die of hunger (Jeremiah 38:6). When he tried to remain silent, he found that God's word was like a burning fire shut up in his bones (Jeremiah 20:9). He continued to warn the kings of Judah even as the Babylonian army surrounded the city (Jeremiah 34:1-7). He purchased a field in Anathoth as a sign of trust that God would one day restore the people to the land (Jeremiah 32:6-15). He stayed in Jerusalem during the siege and shared in the suffering of his people (Jeremiah 39:14). He never stopped interceding for the nation even when they plotted against his life (Jeremiah 18:20). He dictated all the words of the LORD to Baruch the scribe to ensure they were preserved (Jeremiah 36:4).

## The Divine Miracle
God fulfilled **His** promise to deliver Jeremiah and make him like a fortified city (Jeremiah 1:18-19). Even when Jerusalem fell to the Babylonians, the LORD sovereignly moved the heart of the pagan king to show mercy to **His** prophet (Jeremiah 39:11-12).

> Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon gave command concerning Jeremiah through Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, saying, "**Take him, look after him well, and do him no harm, but deal with him as he tells you.**" . . . So they sent and **took Jeremiah from the court of the guard. They entrusted him to Gedaliah the son of Ahikam, son of Shaphan, that he should take him home. So he lived among the people.** . . . "And now, behold, **I release you today from the chains on your hands.** If it seems good to you to come with me to Babylon, come, and **I will look after you well** . . . **go wherever you think it is good and right to go.**" — Jeremiah 39:11-12,14; 40:4 (ESV)

## The Lasting Result
The miracle of Jeremiah's survival proved that the word of the LORD is true and that **He** protects those who trust **Him** (Jeremiah 39:18). Jeremiah was given the freedom to choose where he wanted to live by the Babylonians (Jeremiah 40:4-5). He chose to stay with the remnant of the people in the land to continue his ministry (Jeremiah 40:6). His prophecies about the seventy years of exile provided a timeline for the eventual return of the Jews (Jeremiah 25:11). His words later encouraged Daniel to pray for the restoration of Jerusalem (Daniel 9:2). The preservation of his writings ensured that future generations would understand that God's judgment is balanced with the promise of a New Covenant (Jeremiah 31:31-34).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah was a man who felt completely inadequate for the task God gave him. He faced a lifetime of rejection, physical abuse, and the trauma of seeing his nation destroyed. Despite his personal failures and his desire to give up, he remained faithful because God's word was in him. The LORD was faithful to **His** promise and protected Jeremiah from his enemies and the sword of Babylon. Jeremiah sacrificed his family, his comfort, and his reputation to obey a difficult calling. In exchange, he gained the honor of being God's voice to the nations and was supernaturally preserved while those who rejected the word perished. His life demonstrates that we can trust the LORD to be our strength and our deliverer even when the world around us is falling apart.
