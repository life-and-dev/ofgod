# Jeremiah: The Reluctant Prophet

A young man is told he was chosen for a global mission before he was even born. He must stand alone against kings, priests, and an entire nation while a massive empire threatens to consume his homeland. Will his message of doom be his own undoing?

## The Crumbling Kingdom
Jeremiah lived in the land of Benjamin during a time of extreme political and spiritual turmoil (Jeremiah 1:1). The nation of Judah had forsaken the LORD to worship idols and the works of their own hands (Jeremiah 1:16). King Josiah had initiated reforms, but the people's hearts remained distant from God (Jeremiah 3:10). Following Josiah's death, the subsequent kings led the nation deeper into rebellion and alliance with foreign powers (Jeremiah 22:10-13). To the north, the Babylonian empire under Nebuchadrezzar was rising as a formidable threat to Judah's existence (Jeremiah 25:9). The people faced imminent disaster and judgment for their persistent unfaithfulness (Jeremiah 1:14). Jeremiah was born into a priestly family, yet he was called to deliver a message that would pit him against his own people (Jeremiah 1:1,18). The core problem was the spiritual adultery of Israel and the approaching Babylonian judgment (Jeremiah 3:6-10).

## A Divine Appointment
Jeremiah was a young man when the word of the LORD first came to him (Jeremiah 1:6). He was the son of Hilkiah, one of the priests in Anathoth (Jeremiah 1:1). There is no record that he performed any special fast, prayer, or sacrifice to seek God's attention (Jeremiah 1:4-5). God responded to Jeremiah's existence based on a divine appointment made before his birth (Jeremiah 1:5). He was simply living his life as a member of a priestly family when his calling was revealed (Jeremiah 1:1-2).

## The Words of the LORD
The word of the LORD came directly to Jeremiah.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations.**" Then I said, "Ah, LORD God! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you you shall go, and whatever I command you you shall speak. Do not be afraid of them, for **I am with you to deliver you**, declares the LORD." Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth. See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant.**" And the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Jeremiah, what do you see?" And I said, "I see an almond branch." Then the LORD said to me, "You have seen well, for **I am watching over My word to perform it.**" The word of the LORD came to me a second time, saying, "What do you see?" And I said, "I see a boiling pot, facing away from the north." Then the LORD said to me, "Out of the north disaster shall be let loose upon all the inhabitants of the land. For behold, **I am calling all the tribes of the kingdoms of the north, declares the LORD, and they shall come, and every one shall set his throne at the entrance of the gates of Jerusalem**, against all its walls all around and against all the cities of Judah. And **I will declare My judgments against them**, for all their evil in forsaking Me. They have made offerings to other gods and worshiped the works of their own hands. But you, dress yourself for work; arise, and say to them everything that I command you. Do not be dismayed by them, lest I dismay you before them. And I, behold, **I make you this day a fortified city, an iron pillar, and bronze walls, against the whole land, against the kings of Judah, its officials, its priests, and the people of the land. They will fight against you, but they shall not prevail against you, for I am with you, declares the LORD, to deliver you.**" — Jeremiah 1:4-19 (ESV)

## Solitary Sacrifice
Jeremiah was commanded not to marry or have children due to the coming judgment (Jeremiah 16:1-4). He sacrificed the comfort of a normal life and the respect of his community (Jeremiah 15:10, 20:7). He risked his life repeatedly by speaking against the kings and priests of Judah (Jeremiah 26:8-11). He lost his freedom when he was beaten and put in the stocks or imprisoned in a cistern (Jeremiah 20:2, 38:6). He endured total isolation and the hatred of his own countrymen (Jeremiah 15:17).

## Resistance and Fear
Jeremiah initially tried to refuse God's calling because of his perceived inadequacy (Jeremiah 1:6). He argued that he was too young and lacked the ability to speak effectively (Jeremiah 1:6). He was terrified of the people and the message he was tasked to deliver (Jeremiah 1:8). Even after he began his ministry, he often lamented his calling and wished he had never been born (Jeremiah 20:14-18). He accused God of deceiving him (Jeremiah 20:7).

## Fire in the Bones
Despite his fears and inner turmoil, Jeremiah ultimately surrendered to the word of the LORD (Jeremiah 20:9). He found that he could not keep God's message inside, as it became like a burning fire shut up in his bones (Jeremiah 20:9). He faithfully proclaimed the coming Babylonian invasion and the necessity of surrender to Nebuchadrezzar (Jeremiah 21:8-10). He confronted false prophets who promised peace when there was no peace (Jeremiah 28:15-17). Jeremiah remained in Jerusalem throughout the siege, enduring starvation and constant threats to his life (Jeremiah 37:21). He continued to write God's words even after King Jehoiakim burned his first scroll (Jeremiah 36:27-32). He stayed true to his mission for over forty years, from the reign of Josiah until the fall of Jerusalem (Jeremiah 1:2-3). Even when offered comfort in Babylon, he chose to stay with the poor remnant in the ruined land of Judah (Jeremiah 40:1-6). His life became a living testimony of the message he preached (Jeremiah 13:1-11). He trusted that God would fulfill **His** word even when everything seemed lost (Jeremiah 32:6-15,25).

## Preservation Amidst Destruction
God miraculously preserved Jeremiah through the horrific fall of Jerusalem. While the kings and nobles of Judah were captured or killed, Jeremiah was granted favor by the very enemies he had prophesied about. Nebuchadrezzar gave specific orders for his safety.

> Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon gave command concerning Jeremiah through Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, saying, "**Take him, look after him well, and do him no harm, but deal with him as he tells you.**" . . . The captain of the guard took Jeremiah and said to him . . . "**Behold, I release you today from the chains on your hands. If it seems good to you to come with me to Babylon, come, and I will look after you well . . . See, the whole land is before you; go wherever it seems good and right to you to go.**" — Jeremiah 39:11-12, 40:2,4 (ESV)

## Bridge to Future Hope
The miracle of Jeremiah's survival proved that God is faithful to protect **His** servants even in the midst of national judgment (Jeremiah 39:17-18). Jeremiah was able to provide guidance and hope to the remnant left in the land (Jeremiah 42:7-12). His written prophecies were preserved to provide comfort and instruction to the exiles in Babylon, specifically Daniel (Daniel 9:2). The fulfillment of his words regarding the seventy-year exile ensured that the people would look for God's restoration (Jeremiah 29:10-14). His story demonstrated that God's word is sovereign over nations and that **He** keeps **His** promises to individuals who trust **Him** despite their personal weaknesses. Jeremiah's life became a bridge between the old kingdom and the future hope of a new covenant (Jeremiah 31:31-34).

## Faithfulness in Ruin
Jeremiah faced a crumbling nation and the terrifying rise of the Babylonian empire. He was a young man who felt incapable and afraid, often wishing he could escape his heavy burden. Despite his personal failures and moments of deep despair, he chose to trust God's promise that **He** would be with him to deliver him. God was faithful to **His** word, making Jeremiah like a fortified city that the kings and priests could not prevail against. While the proud leaders of Judah lost their kingdom and lives, Jeremiah was spared by the hand of the LORD. He sacrificed the joys of family and social acceptance for a life of isolation and suffering. In the end, he gained the honor of being God's true voice and the assurance that the LORD'S purposes would prevail through the darkness of exile.
