# Jeremiah: The Weeping Prophet

A young man from a priestly family is called to deliver a message of judgment to a rebellious nation. He faces imprisonment, ridicule, and the threat of death. Yet he remains faithful to the God **Who** promised to deliver him.

## The Rebellious Nation
The people of Judah had forsaken the LORD and turned to the worship of idols (Jeremiah 1:16). They had built altars to Baal and offered their children as sacrifices (Jeremiah 19:5). The political environment was unstable as the rising power of Babylon threatened the sovereignty of Judah (Jeremiah 1:14-15). False prophets were deceiving the people by promising peace when there was no peace (Jeremiah 6:14). The kings of Judah were leading the nation into alliances with Egypt instead of trusting in the LORD (Jeremiah 2:36). The spiritual and moral decay had reached a point where judgment was inevitable (Jeremiah 5:29-31). The clear problem was that the nation had broken its covenant with God and was facing total destruction and exile (Jeremiah 11:10-11).

## A Priestly Background
Jeremiah was the son of Hilkiah, one of the priests who were in Anathoth in the land of Benjamin (Jeremiah 1:1). He was a young man when the word of the LORD first came to him (Jeremiah 1:6). The Bible does not record Jeremiah performing any special fast, prayer, or sacrifice to get God's attention (Jeremiah 1:4-5). God chose and consecrated him before he was even formed in his mother's womb (Jeremiah 1:5). He was simply a member of a priestly family living in his appointed territory (Jeremiah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
God spoke directly to Jeremiah, declaring **His** sovereign choice and giving him a daunting commission.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations.**" Then I said, "Ah, LORD God! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you you shall go, and whatever I command you you shall speak. **Do not be afraid of them, for I am with you to deliver you**, declares the LORD." Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth.** See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant." — Jeremiah 1:4-10 (ESV)

## The Personal Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice his desire for a normal life and family, as the LORD commanded him not to take a wife or have children (Jeremiah 16:2). He sacrificed his reputation and social standing, becoming a laughingstock and a target of mockery (Jeremiah 20:7-8). He risked his life by delivering unpopular messages of surrender to the king and officials (Jeremiah 38:4). He gave up his peace and comfort to become a man of strife to the whole land (Jeremiah 15:10).

## A Hesitant Response
Jeremiah's initial response was one of hesitation and self-doubt (Jeremiah 1:6). He protested that he did not know how to speak because he was only a youth (Jeremiah 1:6). He felt inadequate for the task of speaking to nations and kingdoms (Jeremiah 1:10). His focus was on his own youth and limitations rather than on the power of the One **Who** called him (Jeremiah 1:6).

## Faithful Under Fire
Despite his fears, Jeremiah eventually submitted to the LORD'S calling and began to proclaim the messages given to him (Jeremiah 2:1). He stood in the court of the LORD'S house and spoke to all the people of Judah, holding nothing back (Jeremiah 26:2). He endured being beaten and put in the stocks by Pashhur the priest (Jeremiah 20:2). He was cast into a muddy cistern to die by officials who hated his message (Jeremiah 38:6). Even when he felt like giving up, he found that God's word was like a burning fire shut up in his bones (Jeremiah 20:9). He continued to warn the people and the kings for over forty years (Jeremiah 1:2-3). He remained in Jerusalem even as the city was besieged, trusting that God would preserve him (Jeremiah 39:11-14). He even bought a field in Anathoth as a sign of trust that God would one day restore the people (Jeremiah 32:6-15).

## The Promise Fulfilled
God fulfilled **His** promise to deliver Jeremiah and put **His** words in his mouth (Jeremiah 1:9,19). Every prophecy against Jerusalem came to pass exactly as spoken (Jeremiah 39:1-2). Even when the city fell, the LORD sovereignly protected **His** servant through the hands of the conquerors (Jeremiah 39:11-14).

> The word that came to Jeremiah from the LORD after Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard had let him go from Ramah... The captain of the guard took Jeremiah and said to him, "The LORD your God pronounced this disaster against this place. **The LORD has brought it about, and has done as He said.** . . Behold, I release you today from the chains on your hands. If it seems good to you to come with me to Babylon, come, and **I will look after you**. . . **Wherever it seems good and right for you to go, go there.**" — Jeremiah 40:1-4 (ESV)

## A Witness to Nations
The result of God's miracle was the physical preservation of Jeremiah amidst the total destruction of Jerusalem (Jeremiah 39:11-14). While the kings and officials were killed or taken into exile, Jeremiah was given freedom and provision (Jeremiah 40:4-5). His message stood as a witness to the nations of God's justice and faithfulness (Jeremiah 40:2-3). The remnant that remained in the land had a prophet of God to guide them (Jeremiah 42:1-6). Ultimately, Jeremiah's words of hope concerning the New Covenant and the return from exile provided comfort for future generations (Jeremiah 31:31-34).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced a nation that had utterly rejected the LORD for idols and violence. He was a young man with no natural oratorical skills who faced intense persecution and near-death experiences. Despite his initial doubts and the heavy burden of his message, he chose to trust the God **Who** had consecrated him before birth. God was faithful to **His** promise to deliver Jeremiah, even using the Babylonian army to ensure his safety. Jeremiah sacrificed his family, his reputation, and his personal safety. In return, he gained the honor of being God's mouthpiece and was one of the few to survive the fall of his nation. His life demonstrates that when the LORD calls us, **He** also empowers and protects us regardless of our limitations.
