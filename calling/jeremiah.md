# Jeremiah: The Reluctant Prophet

A young priest is chosen for a mission that would make him the most hated man in Jerusalem. He is tasked with delivering a message of certain doom to a rebellious nation. His journey reveals how God sustains those who carry **His** word through the darkest of times.

## The Problem of a Rebellious Nation
Jeremiah lived during the final decades of the kingdom of Judah (Jeremiah 1:1-3). The people of Judah had forsaken the LORD and burned incense to other gods (Jeremiah 1:16). They worshiped the works of their own hands (Jeremiah 1:16). King Josiah had attempted reforms but the hearts of the people remained far from God (2 Kings 23:25-27). Political tension was high as the Babylonian Empire rose to power and threatened to destroy Jerusalem (Jeremiah 1:14-15). The religious leaders and prophets were corrupt and spoke lies to the people (Jeremiah 5:30-31). They promised peace when there was no peace (Jeremiah 6:14). The people were unrepentant and refused to listen to the warnings of the LORD (Jeremiah 7:23-26). The problem was that Judah's persistent idolatry and rebellion had made divine judgment and national destruction inevitable (Jeremiah 1:14-16).

## Jeremiah's Humble Condition
Jeremiah was the son of Hilkiah, one of the priests in Anathoth (Jeremiah 1:1). He was a very young man when the LORD first spoke to him (Jeremiah 1:6). Jeremiah did nothing special to seek God's attention or receive a vision (Jeremiah 1:4-5). He did not fast, pray, or offer a sacrifice before the divine word came (Jeremiah 1:4-5). God had already chosen and consecrated him before his birth (Jeremiah 1:5).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke directly to Jeremiah to reveal his destiny as a prophet to the nations.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations.**" Then I said, "Ah, LORD God! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you you shall go, and whatever I command you you shall speak. Do not be afraid of them, for **I am with you to deliver you, declares the LORD.**" Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth.** See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, **to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant.**" — Jeremiah 1:4-10 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice his social standing and personal comfort (Jeremiah 15:17). The LORD commanded him not to take a wife or have children in that place (Jeremiah 16:1-2). He was forbidden from joining in social gatherings or mourning feasts (Jeremiah 16:5,8). He risked his life every time he spoke the truth to the kings and priests (Jeremiah 26:8). He was viewed as a traitor and faced constant loneliness and persecution (Jeremiah 20:7-8).

## Jeremiah's Initial Response
Jeremiah initially responded with fear and a sense of inadequacy (Jeremiah 1:6). He protested that he did not know how to speak because he was only a youth (Jeremiah 1:6). He was overwhelmed by the weight of the calling (Jeremiah 1:6). Later, when the persecution became intense, he even cursed the day he was born (Jeremiah 20:14). He tried to stop speaking in the name of the LORD but found that the word was like a fire in his bones (Jeremiah 20:9).

## Jeremiah's Faithful Reaction
Despite his fears and the intense opposition, Jeremiah remained faithful to the LORD'S command for over forty years (Jeremiah 1:2-3). He stood in the court of the LORD'S house and warned all the people of Judah (Jeremiah 26:2). He confronted King Jehoiakim and King Zedekiah with the truth of the coming Babylonian conquest (Jeremiah 27:12, 36:1-4). When the officials beat him and put him in the stocks, he continued to prophesy judgment (Jeremiah 20:1-6). He was cast into a muddy cistern to die but did not retract his message (Jeremiah 38:6). He dictated the words of the LORD to his scribe Baruch when he was banned from the temple (Jeremiah 36:4-5). Jeremiah trusted that the LORD would sustain him even when his own people and family turned against him (Jeremiah 12:6, 15:20-21). He lamented the destruction of Jerusalem but still pointed the people toward a future hope (Lamentations 3:22-24). He purchased a field in Anathoth as a sign of trust that God would eventually restore the people to their land (Jeremiah 32:6-15). Jeremiah showed that true trust is not the absence of doubt or pain but the choice to obey despite them (Jeremiah 20:9, 38:17-18). He consistently urged the people to surrender to the Babylonians to save their lives, prioritizing God's word over national pride (Jeremiah 21:8-10). He wept for his people and pleaded for their repentance even as they mocked him (Jeremiah 9:1, 13:17). His life was a constant struggle between his human emotions and his divine duty (Jeremiah 15:15-18). Yet, he never permanently abandoned his post (Jeremiah 1:17-19). He proved that the power to endure comes from the LORD rather than from human strength (Jeremiah 20:11).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD performed the miracle of preserving Jeremiah's life through multiple assassination attempts and the fall of Jerusalem. **He** fulfilled **His** promise to be with him and to deliver him.

> They will fight against you, but **they shall not prevail against you, for I am with you, declares the LORD, to deliver you.** — Jeremiah 1:19 (ESV)

When Jerusalem finally fell to the Babylonians, God moved the heart of the enemy commander to show him favor.

> Behold, I release you today from the chains on your hands. If it seems good to you to come with me to Babylon, come, and I will look after you well. — Jeremiah 40:4 (ESV)

> **The captain of the guard gave him an allowance of food and a present, and let him go.** — Jeremiah 40:5 (ESV)

In these events, God fulfilled the promise to deliver him and to be with him to protect him from his enemies (Jeremiah 1:8,19).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the survival of Jeremiah and a faithful remnant (Jeremiah 40:6). His prophecies were precisely fulfilled when the Babylonians destroyed the temple and carried the people into exile (Jeremiah 39:1-8). This served as a powerful witness to the truth of the word of the LORD (Jeremiah 44:28-29). Jeremiah's message of a New Covenant provided hope for future generations (Jeremiah 31:31-34). His faithfulness ensured that the history and spiritual identity of Israel would be preserved even in captivity (Jeremiah 52:31-34). The nations around Judah saw that the LORD indeed does what **He** says (Jeremiah 40:2-3). Jeremiah's life became a template for the suffering servant and a precursor to the ministry of Jesus.

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced the problem of a nation's terminal rebellion and the threat of total destruction. He was limited by his youth and his sensitive heart. However, the LORD promised to put **His** words in his mouth and to deliver him from all his enemies. Jeremiah responded by sacrificing his personal happiness and enduring decades of rejection. God intervened by protecting him from death and giving him favor even in the hands of the enemy. The result was the preservation of a hope that would eventually lead to the New Covenant. Jeremiah sacrificed a normal life and the approval of his peers, but he gained the eternal honor of being the LORD'S mouthpiece during Israel's darkest hour. His story proves that we can trust the LORD to sustain us through any trial when we are faithful to **His** word.
