# Jeremiah: The Prophet to the Nations

A young man is told that before he was even formed in the womb, God had a specific purpose for him. He is sent to a nation that will not listen and to a city destined for destruction. Jeremiah must stand firm through the collapse of his world.

## The Problem of National Rebellion
The kingdom of Judah had forsaken the LORD and turned to the worship of false gods (Jeremiah 1:16). They made offerings to other gods and worshiped the works of their own hands (Jeremiah 1:16). The kings, officials, and priests were all in rebellion against the divine commandments (Jeremiah 1:18). This persistent idolatry and wickedness had provoked the LORD to declare judgment against the whole land (Jeremiah 1:16). A great disaster from the north was being prepared to be let loose upon all the inhabitants of the land (Jeremiah 1:14). The people were stubborn and refused to repent of their evil ways (Jeremiah 5:23). The political environment was one of spiritual decay and impending military conquest by the Babylonians (Jeremiah 1:15). The problem was that the chosen nation had broken its covenant with the LORD and was facing imminent destruction that they could not avoid by their own alliances or power (Jeremiah 1:16,19).

## Jeremiah's Humble Condition
Jeremiah was the son of Hilkiah, one of the priests who were in Anathoth (Jeremiah 1:1). He was only a youth when the word of the LORD first came to him (Jeremiah 1:6). Jeremiah did not seek out a prophetic ministry or perform any special act to gain God's attention (Jeremiah 1:4-5). He was simply a young member of a priestly family when the LORD intervened in his life (Jeremiah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke directly to Jeremiah, revealing **His** sovereign plan and appointing him to a difficult mission.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations.**" Then I said, "Ah, Lord GOD! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you, you shall go, and **whatever I command you, you shall speak**. Do not be afraid of them, for **I am with you to deliver you**, declares the LORD." Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, I have put My words in your mouth. See, **I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms**, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant."
>
> — Jeremiah 1:4-10 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice his reputation and his personal comfort (Jeremiah 20:7-8). He was commanded not to marry or have children in that place (Jeremiah 16:2). He sacrificed the approval of his family and his nation, becoming a man of strife and contention (Jeremiah 15:10). He risked his life repeatedly by delivering messages of judgment to powerful kings and officials (Jeremiah 26:11, 38:4).

## The Initial Response
Jeremiah's first response was one of inadequacy and fear (Jeremiah 1:6). He protested that he did not know how to speak because he was only a youth (Jeremiah 1:6). He was later overwhelmed by the burden of his message and even cursed the day of his birth (Jeremiah 20:14). He felt deceived by the LORD because of the derision he faced (Jeremiah 20:7).

## Jeremiah's Faithful Reaction
Despite his inner turmoil, Jeremiah eventually surrendered to the LORD'S will (Jeremiah 20:9). He found that the word of the LORD was like a fire shut up in his bones that he could not contain (Jeremiah 20:9). He stood as a fortified city, an iron pillar, and bronze walls against the whole land (Jeremiah 1:18). He spent forty years warning Judah of the coming judgment, even when he was beaten and put in the stocks (Jeremiah 20:2). He remained in Jerusalem during the terrible Babylonian siege, continuing to speak the truth even from the court of the guard (Jeremiah 37:21). When he was cast into a miry cistern to die, he did not recant his message (Jeremiah 38:6). He chose to suffer with his people rather than seek a life of ease elsewhere (Jeremiah 40:6). He demonstrated that his trust was not in his own strength but in the presence of the One **Who** promised to deliver him (Jeremiah 1:19). He faithfully recorded the words of the LORD even when the king burned the scrolls (Jeremiah 36:23,32). His life was a testament to the power of God's word to sustain a man through total isolation and national catastrophe (Jeremiah 15:16).

## The Divine Miracle
God preserved Jeremiah's life during the violent fall of Jerusalem and gave him favor with his enemies.

> Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon gave command concerning Jeremiah through Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, saying, "**Take him, look after him well, and do him no harm, but deal with him as he tells you.**" . . . The captain of the guard took Jeremiah and said to him, "The LORD your God pronounced this disaster against this place. . . . Behold, I release you today from the chains on your hands. If it seems good to you to come with me to Babylon, come, and I will look after you well. . . . **Go wherever you think it right to go.**"
>
> — Jeremiah 39:11-12, 40:2,4 (ESV)

In this miracle, the LORD fulfilled **His** promise that **I am with you to deliver you** and that they would not prevail against him (Jeremiah 1:19).

## The Lasting Result
Jeremiah was one of the few who survived the destruction of Jerusalem with his life as a prize of war (Jeremiah 39:18). He was allowed to stay in the land of Judah among the remnant of the people (Jeremiah 40:6). His prophecies were precisely fulfilled, proving to all that he was a true prophet of the LORD (Jeremiah 44:28). His words provided hope and direction for the exiles in Babylon, as he had also promised their eventual return after seventy years (Jeremiah 29:10). Neighboring nations and future generations saw that the LORD indeed performs **His** word (Jeremiah 1:12). The miracle of his survival served as a sign of God's faithfulness to those who trust **Him** even in the darkest times (Jeremiah 39:18).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced a nation that had rejected its Creator and a city on the brink of ruin. He was limited by his youth, his sensitive spirit, and the overwhelming hostility of his peers. However, the LORD promised to make him a fortified city and to deliver him from his enemies. Jeremiah sacrificed his social standing, his family life, and his safety to be the mouth of God. God's intervention through the protection of the Babylonian king proved **His** absolute faithfulness to **His** servant. Jeremiah sacrificed a life of peace and acceptance, but he gained an eternal legacy and the honor of being the LORD'S witness during Israel's most tragic hour. His story demonstrates that we can trust the LORD to sustain us when everything around us falls apart. **He** is the LORD **Who** knows us from the womb and never forsakes **His** own.
