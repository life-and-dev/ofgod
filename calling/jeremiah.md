# Jeremiah: The Protected Prophet

A young man is appointed as a prophet before he is even born. He must deliver a message of certain destruction to his own people and their kings. Despite intense persecution and the fall of his city, he finds that God is a fortified wall around him.

## The Problem of a Rebellious Nation
The people of Judah had forsaken the LORD to worship other gods (Jeremiah 1:16). They burned incense to foreign deities and worshiped the works of their own hands (Jeremiah 1:16). The kings, officials, and priests were all in rebellion against God's commands (Jeremiah 1:18). The political environment was unstable as the Babylonian Empire emerged as a global threat (Jeremiah 1:14). **He** declared that disaster would be poured out from the north upon all the inhabitants of the land (Jeremiah 1:14). Jeremiah was tasked with speaking all that **He** commanded him to a hostile audience (Jeremiah 1:17). He faced a nation that would fight against him at every turn (Jeremiah 1:19). The problem was that the people's persistent idolatry had made divine judgment inevitable and his own life was in constant danger from his countrymen (Jeremiah 1:16-19).

## Jeremiah's Youthful Condition
Jeremiah was the son of Hilkiah, one of the priests in Anathoth (Jeremiah 1:1). The word of the LORD came to him during the reign of King Josiah (Jeremiah 1:2). Jeremiah did nothing special to seek God's attention or a prophetic office (Jeremiah 1:4-5). He was a young man who felt completely unqualified for the task (Jeremiah 1:6). He acknowledged his inability to speak effectively because of his youth (Jeremiah 1:6).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke directly to Jeremiah, declaring **His** sovereign plan and promising **His** constant presence.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, “Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations**.” Then I said, “Ah, LORD God! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth.” But the LORD said to me, “Do not say, ‘I am only a youth’; for to all to whom I send you, you shall go, and whatever I command you, you shall speak. Do not be afraid of them, for **I am with you to deliver you**, declares the LORD.” Then the LORD put out **His** hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, “Behold, I have put My words in your mouth. See, **I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms**, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant.” . . . “But you, dress yourself for work; arise, and say to them everything that I command you. Do not be dismayed by them, lest I dismay you before them. And I, behold, **I make you this day a fortified city, an iron pillar, and bronze walls**, against the whole land, against the kings of Judah, its officials, its priests, and the people of the land. They will fight against you, but they shall not prevail against you, for **I am with you, declares the LORD, to deliver you.**”
>
> — Jeremiah 1:4-10,17-19 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice his personal comfort and social standing (Jeremiah 15:10,17). He was forbidden from marrying or having children as a sign of the coming judgment (Jeremiah 16:1-4). He sacrificed his reputation and was seen as a traitor for advising surrender to Babylon (Jeremiah 38:2-4). He risked his life repeatedly as he was beaten, imprisoned, and thrown into a muddy cistern (Jeremiah 20:2, 37:15, 38:6).

## Jeremiah's Initial Response
Jeremiah initially resisted the call by pleading his lack of skill and maturity (Jeremiah 1:6). He expressed fear and dismay at the thought of facing the leaders of Judah (Jeremiah 1:8,17). Later in his ministry, he even accused God of deceiving him because of the intense suffering he endured (Jeremiah 20:7). He tried to stop speaking in the name of the LORD, but found that the word was like a fire shut up in his bones (Jeremiah 20:9).

## Jeremiah's Faithful Reaction
Despite his inner turmoil and the outward opposition, Jeremiah remained faithful for over forty years (Jeremiah 1:2-3). He spoke every word that the LORD commanded him, regardless of the consequences (Jeremiah 26:12-15). He wore a wooden yoke to symbolize the coming bondage of the nations (Jeremiah 27:2). When the scroll of his prophecies was burned by the king, he dictated it all again with additional words (Jeremiah 36:27-32). He stayed in Jerusalem during the terrifying siege by the Babylonian army (Jeremiah 38:28). He continued to trust God's promise even when he was shut up in the court of the guard (Jeremiah 32:1-2). He even purchased a field in Anathoth as a sign of faith that God would one day restore the people to the land (Jeremiah 32:6-15). Jeremiah never wavered in his commitment to the truth, even when he was the only one speaking it (Jeremiah 1:19). He proved that his trust was not in his own strength but in the One **Who** had made him a fortified city (Jeremiah 1:18).

## The Divine Miracle
God performed a miracle of preservation for Jeremiah when Jerusalem finally fell to the Babylonians. While the kings and officials were captured or killed, Jeremiah was granted favor by the pagan conquerors.

> Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon gave command concerning Jeremiah through Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, saying, “Take him, **look after him well, and do him no harm, but deal with him as he tells you.**” . . . The captain of the guard took Jeremiah and said to him . . . “Now, behold, **I release you today from the chains on your hands.** If it seems good to you to come with me to Babylon, come, and I will look after you well . . . See, the whole land is before you; **go wherever you think it good and right to go.**”
>
> — Jeremiah 39:11-12, 40:2-4 (ESV)

The miracle fulfilled **His** promise that his enemies would not prevail against him and that **He** would **deliver him** (Jeremiah 1:19). He was set free while his persecutors were taken into exile.

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in Jeremiah's physical survival and his freedom to remain in the land with the remnant of Judah (Jeremiah 40:6). He lived among the people under the protection of Gedaliah the governor (Jeremiah 40:6). His prophecies were vindicated as every word regarding the destruction of the city came to pass (Jeremiah 39:1-8). The neighbors and survivors saw that the LORD indeed protects **His** servants even in the midst of national catastrophe (Jeremiah 40:2-3). Jeremiah's writings provided comfort and hope for the exiles in Babylon, promising a future restoration after seventy years (Jeremiah 29:10-14). His witness ensured that the knowledge of the true God was preserved during the darkest hour of Israel's history (Jeremiah 31:31-34).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced the problem of a rebellious nation and the impending wrath of God. He was limited by his youth and his own deep-seated fears. However, the LORD promised to make him a fortified city and to deliver him from all who fought against him. Jeremiah sacrificed his family life, his reputation, and his personal safety to remain faithful to his calling. God's intervention through the favor of the Babylonian king proved that **He** is faithful to protect **His** own. Jeremiah sacrificed a comfortable life among his people, but he gained the assurance of God's presence and the honor of being a prophet to the nations. His story proves that we can trust the LORD to sustain us when we are surrounded by hostility and chaos. **He** is the LORD **Who** delivers the faithful.
