# Jeremiah: The Fortified City

A young man is told he was appointed as a prophet before he was born. He must deliver a message of doom to a nation that hates him. Will his God protect him when he is thrown into a muddy cistern to die?

## The Problem of a Rebellious Nation
Jeremiah lived during the final years of the Kingdom of Judah (Jeremiah 1:1-3). The people had forsaken the LORD and turned to idols (Jeremiah 1:16). King Josiah had attempted reforms but the heart of the nation remained rebellious (2 Kings 23:25-27). After Josiah died, his successors led the people back into wickedness (2 Kings 23:32,37). The political environment was tense as the Babylonian Empire under Nebuchadrezzar grew in power (Jeremiah 25:1). Judah was caught between the competing interests of Egypt and Babylon (Jeremiah 2:36). The spiritual leaders were corrupt and the prophets spoke lies to please the people (Jeremiah 5:30-31). The nation was on the brink of total destruction because of their persistent sin (Jeremiah 6:1). The problem was that Judah had broken their covenant with the LORD and faced imminent judgment and exile (Jeremiah 11:10-11).

## Jeremiah's Humble Condition
Jeremiah was the son of Hilkiah, one of the priests who were in Anathoth (Jeremiah 1:1). He was a very young man when the word of the LORD first came to him (Jeremiah 1:6). There is no record that he did anything special like fasting or praying to get God's attention (Jeremiah 1:4-5). The LORD chose him and consecrated him before he was even born (Jeremiah 1:5). Jeremiah was simply living his life in his hometown when God intervened (Jeremiah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke directly to Jeremiah.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations.**" Then I said, "Ah, Lord GOD! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you you shall go, and whatever I command you you shall speak. Do not be afraid of them, for **I am with you to deliver you**, declares the LORD." Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth**. See, **I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant**." And the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Jeremiah, what do you see?" And I said, "I see an almond branch." Then the LORD said to me, "You have seen well, for **I am watching over My word to perform it**." The word of the LORD came to me a second time, saying, "What do you see?" And I said, "I see a boiling pot, facing away from the north." Then the LORD said to me, "Out of the north disaster shall be let loose upon all the inhabitants of the land. For behold, I am calling all the tribes of the kingdoms of the north, declares the LORD, and they shall come and every one shall set his throne at the entrance of the gates of Jerusalem, against all its walls around and against all the cities of Judah. And I will declare My judgments against them, for all their evil in forsaking Me. They have made offerings to other gods and worshiped the works of their own hands. But you, dress yourself for work; arise, and say to them everything that I command you. Do not be dismayed by them, lest I dismay you before them. And I, behold, **I make you this day a fortified city, an iron pillar, and bronze walls, against the whole land, against the kings of Judah, its officials, its priests, and the people of the land. They will fight against you, but they shall not prevail against you, for I am with you, declares the LORD, to deliver you.**" — Jeremiah 1:4-19 (ESV)

## The Weight of the Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice his reputation and his personal comfort (Jeremiah 20:7-8). He was forbidden by the LORD from marrying or having children because of the coming judgment (Jeremiah 16:1-4). He lost the support of his family and his community in Anathoth (Jeremiah 11:21). He risked his life every time he spoke the unpopular message of surrender to Babylon (Jeremiah 38:4). He sacrificed his peace of mind as he wept for the destruction of his people (Jeremiah 9:1).

## Jeremiah's Initial Response
Jeremiah initially tried to excuse himself by claiming he was too young and lacked the ability to speak (Jeremiah 1:6). He expressed great hesitation and fear about the task ahead (Jeremiah 1:6). He did not immediately embrace the calling but rather pointed out his own limitations (Jeremiah 1:6). His response was one of humble inadequacy rather than direct rebellion (Jeremiah 1:6). He felt overwhelmed by the weight of the office he was being called to fill (Jeremiah 1:6).

## Jeremiah's Faithful Reaction
Despite his fears, Jeremiah eventually obeyed the LORD and spoke every word commanded of him (Jeremiah 26:12-15). He faced constant persecution, including being beaten and put in stocks (Jeremiah 20:1-2). He struggled deeply with the burden of his message and at times cursed the day of his birth (Jeremiah 20:14-18). He even accused the LORD of deceiving him because the persecution was so intense (Jeremiah 20:7). However, whenever he tried to remain silent, the word of the LORD became like a fire shut up in his bones (Jeremiah 20:9). He could not hold it in (Jeremiah 20:9). During the final siege of Jerusalem, he was accused of desertion and thrown into a muddy cistern to die (Jeremiah 38:4-6). Yet, he never changed his message to please the king or the people (Jeremiah 38:17-18). He continued to urge the people to trust the word of the LORD even when it seemed like treason (Jeremiah 38:2). He remained in Jerusalem during its fall, suffering alongside his people (Jeremiah 39:14). His loyalty to Him was tested through decades of rejection (Jeremiah 25:3). He chose to trust His promise of protection even when he was surrounded by enemies (Jeremiah 39:11-12). Jeremiah's life was a testament to enduring faithfulness in the face of absolute opposition (Jeremiah 1:19).

## The Miracle of Preservation
God miraculously preserved Jeremiah's life through the very enemies he prophesied against (Jeremiah 39:11). The LORD used Ebed-melech to rescue Jeremiah from the cistern (Jeremiah 38:7-13).

> Nebuchadrezzar king of Babylon gave command concerning Jeremiah through Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, saying, "**Take him, look after him well, and do him no harm, but deal with him as he tells you.**" — Jeremiah 39:11-12 (ESV)

In these actions, He fulfilled His promise that they will fight against you, but they shall not prevail against you and that I am with you to deliver you (Jeremiah 1:19). Jeremiah was set free while the kings and officials who fought him were captured or killed (Jeremiah 39:4-7). God's word was performed exactly as He said it would be (Jeremiah 39:16).

## The Result for the Remnant
The result of God's miracle was that Jeremiah survived the horrific fall of Jerusalem (Jeremiah 39:14). He was given the choice to go to Babylon or stay in the land, and he chose to stay with the remnant (Jeremiah 40:4-6). His prophecies were vindicated before the entire nation as they saw the city burned and the people exiled (Jeremiah 39:8-9). Though the nation suffered, a small remnant was preserved through whom God would later fulfill His promise of a new covenant (Jeremiah 31:31-34). Jeremiah's words continued to provide hope and direction for the exiles in Babylon (Jeremiah 29:10-14). His faithfulness ensured that the true word of the LORD was preserved for future generations (Jeremiah 36:32).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced a nation in spiritual decay and the threat of an unstoppable empire. He was a young man who felt incapable of speaking. Yet, the LORD promised to be with him and make him a fortified city. Jeremiah sacrificed his family, his reputation, and his safety to speak the truth. Despite his internal struggles and the external persecution that led him to a muddy pit, he remained faithful. God's intervention through a foreign king proved that He is sovereign over all nations. Jeremiah lost his earthly comfort but gained the honor of being the prophet who announced the new covenant. His life demonstrates that we can trust Him to protect us even when the whole world is against us. His faithfulness is our only true security.
