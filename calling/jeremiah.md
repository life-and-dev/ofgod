# Jeremiah: A Prophet to the Nations

A young man from a priestly family is told by God that he was appointed as a prophet before he was even born. He must deliver a message of doom to a rebellious nation, facing intense persecution and the destruction of his home.

## The Problem of Rebellion
The people of Judah had forsaken the LORD and turned to idols (Jeremiah 1:16, 2:13). They were stubborn and refused to repent despite many warnings (Jeremiah 3:10, 5:3). The Babylonian empire was rising as a terrifying threat from the north (Jeremiah 1:14-15). The political environment was unstable with kings seeking alliances with Egypt instead of trusting in the LORD (Jeremiah 2:36). False prophets were speaking lies and promising peace when there was no peace (Jeremiah 6:14). The social fabric was tearing apart as people oppressed one another (Jeremiah 7:5-6). The priesthood and the prophets were corrupt (Jeremiah 5:31). The problem was that the nation was on the brink of divine judgment and needed a faithful voice to call them back to the LORD before it was too late (Jeremiah 1:10).

## Jeremiah's Youthful Condition
Jeremiah was a young man from the priests who were in Anathoth in the land of Benjamin (Jeremiah 1:1). He was likely in his late teens or early twenties (Jeremiah 1:6). He did nothing special to get God's attention; he was not seeking to be a prophet through prayer or fasting (Jeremiah 1:5). The word of the LORD simply came to him during the thirteenth year of the reign of Josiah (Jeremiah 1:2). He was living a quiet life in his hometown before the LORD intervened (Jeremiah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came to Jeremiah directly.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations.**" Then I said, "Ah, Lord GOD! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you, you shall go, and **whatever I command you, you shall speak. Do not be afraid of them, for I am with you to deliver you, declares the LORD.**" Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth. See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant.**" — Jeremiah 1:4-10 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
The LORD commanded Jeremiah not to marry or have children because of the coming judgment on the land (Jeremiah 16:1-4). He sacrificed his reputation and social standing, as he was mocked and called a traitor for advising surrender to Babylon (Jeremiah 20:7-8, 38:4). He risked his life multiple times, facing death at the hands of his own neighbors and the king's officials (Jeremiah 11:18-21, 26:8). He gave up a life of peace for one of constant conflict and sorrow (Jeremiah 15:10).

## Jeremiah's Initial Response
Jeremiah pleaded that he could not speak because he was only a youth and lacked experience (Jeremiah 1:6). He was overwhelmed by the magnitude of the task (Jeremiah 1:6).

## Jeremiah's Eventual Reaction
Despite his initial fear and the "fire shut up in his bones" that made him unable to keep silent, Jeremiah spoke every word the LORD commanded (Jeremiah 20:9). He remained faithful for over forty years, even when no one listened to his warnings (Jeremiah 25:3). He chose to stay in Jerusalem during the terrifying siege, continuing to prophesy even when he was imprisoned in a muddy cistern (Jeremiah 38:6-13). When the city finally fell, he chose to stay with the poor remnant in Judah rather than taking an offer of a comfortable life in Babylon (Jeremiah 40:1-6). He consistently put the LORD'S word above his own safety and desires (Jeremiah 1:17-19). Jeremiah endured beatings, stocks, and threats of death (Jeremiah 20:2, 26:8). He lamented his birth but never abandoned his post (Jeremiah 20:14-18). He was a man of deep sorrow who wept for the sins of his people (Jeremiah 9:1). His life was a living testimony of obedience in the face of absolute rejection (Jeremiah 7:27). He stood as a fortified city against the kings, princes, and priests of Judah just as the LORD had commanded (Jeremiah 1:18).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD fulfilled the words spoken to Jeremiah by bringing the promised judgment and protecting His servant.

> The Chaldeans burned the king's house and the houses of the people, and **broke down the walls of Jerusalem**. — Jeremiah 39:8 (ESV)

The LORD also moved the heart of the Babylonian king to ensure Jeremiah's safety.

> Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon gave command concerning Jeremiah through Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, saying, "**Take him, look after him well, and do him no harm, but deal with him as he tells you.**" — Jeremiah 39:11-12 (ESV)

The destruction of the city fulfilled the promise to **pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow** (Jeremiah 1:10). The command to the captain of the guard fulfilled the promise to **deliver you** and that Jeremiah should not **be afraid of them** (Jeremiah 1:8).

## The Lasting Result
Jerusalem fell and the temple was destroyed as the LORD had warned through Jeremiah (Jeremiah 39:1-10). However, a small remnant was preserved in the land under the care of Gedaliah (Jeremiah 40:7). Jeremiah's faithfulness provided a clear witness that the tragedy was not a failure of God, but a fulfillment of His righteous judgment (Jeremiah 40:2-3). His prophecies also gave the exiles hope for a future return after seventy years (Jeremiah 29:10-14). He spoke of a new covenant where the LORD would write His law on their hearts (Jeremiah 31:31-34). Jeremiah's legacy as a faithful prophet endured, influencing future generations of Israelites to return to the LORD (Ezra 1:1; Daniel 9:2).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced a nation in open rebellion and the looming threat of the Babylonian empire. He was limited by his youth and his own feelings of inadequacy. However, the LORD promised to be with him and to put His words in his mouth. Jeremiah responded with a life of total, though often painful, obedience. God intervened by protecting him amidst the carnage of Jerusalem's fall, while also fulfilling every word of judgment spoken through the prophet. Jeremiah sacrificed marriage, family, and peace, but he gained an eternal legacy as a faithful servant and the honor of being the LORD'S voice during Israel's darkest hour. His story proves that even when we feel weak and rejected, the LORD is faithful to His promises and will never forsake those who trust in Him.
