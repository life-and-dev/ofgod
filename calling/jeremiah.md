# Jeremiah: Prophet to the Nations

A young man from a priestly family receives a message that will set him against kings and nations. He must speak words of judgment while his world crumbles around him. His journey reveals a God **Who** protects **His** servants in the midst of total collapse.

## A Rebellious Nation
Jeremiah lived in the land of Benjamin during the final decades of the Kingdom of Judah (Jeremiah 1:1). The spiritual environment was one of deep rebellion and idolatry among the people (Jeremiah 1:16). King Josiah had attempted reforms, but the nation quickly returned to forsaking the LORD to worship other gods (Jeremiah 1:2,16). Politically, the threat of a massive invasion from the north loomed like a boiling pot (Jeremiah 1:13-14). The LORD promised to call all the tribes of the kingdoms of the north to execute judgment against Jerusalem (Jeremiah 1:15). The people had made offerings to idols and worshiped the works of their own hands (Jeremiah 1:16). The priests and officials were often corrupt and did not seek the LORD (Jeremiah 2:8). The problem was that the nation faced imminent destruction and exile because of their persistent unfaithfulness to the covenant (Jeremiah 1:14-16).

## Priestly Condition
Jeremiah was the son of Hilkiah and belonged to a priestly family in Anathoth (Jeremiah 1:1). He was a young man, likely in his late teens or early twenties, when the word of the LORD first came to him (Jeremiah 1:2,6). He did not perform any special fasts or rituals to gain divine attention (Jeremiah 1:4). He was simply living in his homeland when the LORD initiated the conversation (Jeremiah 1:1,4).

## Divine Calling
The LORD spoke directly to Jeremiah to appoint him as a prophet even before he was born. God communicated the weight of the mission and promised **His** constant protection.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying,
>
> "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations**."
>
> Then I said, "Ah, Lord GOD! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." But the LORD said to me,
>
> "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you, you shall go, and whatever I command you, you shall speak. Do not be afraid of them, for **I am with you to deliver you**, declares the LORD."
>
> Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me,
>
> "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth. See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant**."
>
> — Jeremiah 1:4-10 (ESV)

## Necessary Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice his desire for a normal family life when the LORD commanded him not to take a wife or have children (Jeremiah 16:2). He sacrificed his reputation and social standing by delivering unpopular messages of judgment (Jeremiah 20:7-8). He risked his life repeatedly as he was beaten, put in stocks, and thrown into a muddy cistern to die (Jeremiah 20:2, 38:6). He gave up his peace of mind to carry the burden of the LORD'S word (Jeremiah 23:9).

## Initial Response
Jeremiah initially responded with hesitation and a sense of inadequacy (Jeremiah 1:6). He pleaded that he did not know how to speak because he was only a youth (Jeremiah 1:6). He felt that his age and lack of experience made him an unsuitable candidate for such a monumental task (Jeremiah 1:6). His response was not one of rebellion, but of deep humility and fear of the responsibility (Jeremiah 1:6-8).

## Jeremiah's Reaction
Jeremiah eventually accepted the calling and became one of the most persistent voices in Israel's history (Jeremiah 1:17-19). He spoke the words of the LORD even when it brought him nothing but derision and reproach (Jeremiah 20:8). At times, he felt overwhelmed by the suffering and even cursed the day of his birth (Jeremiah 20:14-18). However, when he tried to remain silent, the word of the LORD became like a burning fire shut up in his bones that he could not contain (Jeremiah 20:9). He continued to warn the kings of Judah, including Jehoiakim and Zedekiah, despite their rejection (Jeremiah 22:1, 38:14). During the final siege of Jerusalem, Jeremiah remained in the court of the guard and continued to deliver messages from the LORD (Jeremiah 38:28). He told King Zedekiah that the only way to survive was to surrender to the Babylonians (Jeremiah 38:17). Even when he was accused of desertion and thrown into a cistern, he did not change his message to please the officials (Jeremiah 37:13-15, 38:6). He remained faithful to the LORD'S command to speak everything he was told, regardless of the personal cost (Jeremiah 1:17). His trust was not in his own strength, but in the promise that the LORD was with him as a dread warrior (Jeremiah 20:11).

## Divine Miracle
God performed the miracle of preserving Jeremiah's life during the violent fall of Jerusalem. While the nobles were slaughtered and the king was blinded, Jeremiah received supernatural favor from the invading king.

> Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon gave command concerning Jeremiah through Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, saying, "**Take him, look after him well, and do him no harm**, but deal with him as he tells you."  — Jeremiah 39:11-12 (ESV)

In this miracle, the LORD fulfilled **His** promise to be with Jeremiah and **to deliver him** from his enemies (Jeremiah 1:8,19). While the city was destroyed, the LORD ensured that **His** servant was protected by the very nation that brought the judgment. God used the captain of the guard to release Jeremiah and offer him total freedom (Jeremiah 40:4).

## Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in Jeremiah's safety and his decision to remain among the remnant of the people in Judah (Jeremiah 40:6). He lived under the protection of Gedaliah, the governor appointed by Babylon (Jeremiah 40:5-6). His survival stood as a powerful testimony that the word of the LORD was true and that **He** protects those who trust **Him**. The scrolls containing Jeremiah's prophecies were preserved, providing a record of God's faithfulness and a promise of future restoration for Israel (Jeremiah 30:1-3, 36:32). His neighbors and the remaining officials saw that the prophet they had persecuted was the one the LORD had indeed vindicated.

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced the problem of a crumbling nation and the daunting task of speaking judgment to a hostile audience. He was a young man with a sense of personal inadequacy and a desire for a normal life. The LORD promised to put **His** words in Jeremiah's mouth and to deliver him from all who fought against him. Jeremiah responded with a lifetime of persistent obedience, sacrificing his reputation, his safety, and his right to a family. God's intervention through the favor of a pagan king proved **His** absolute faithfulness. Jeremiah sacrificed his comfort and social acceptance, but he gained the honor of being a true witness for the LORD and the preservation of his life during a national catastrophe. This story proves that we can trust the LORD even when everything around us falls apart. **He** is faithful to **His** word and never forsakes **His** servants.
