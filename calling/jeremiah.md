# Jeremiah: The Iron Pillar

A young man is called to be a prophet to a nation that will hate him. He must announce the destruction of his own city and temple while facing constant threats to his life. Discover how God protected **His** messenger in the midst of fire and war.

## The Problem of Rebellion
The people of Judah had forsaken the LORD and made offerings to other gods (Jeremiah 1:16). They worshiped the works of their own hands (Jeremiah 1:16). The kings, officials, priests, and the people of the land were in rebellion against God's commands (Jeremiah 1:18). Judah had broken the covenant that God made with their fathers (Jeremiah 11:10). They had turned their backs to **Him** and not their faces (Jeremiah 32:33). The city of Jerusalem was under the threat of a boiling pot facing away from the north, representing the Babylonian empire (Jeremiah 1:13-14). Disaster was about to be let loose upon all the inhabitants of the land (Jeremiah 1:14). The Babylonian army was coming to besiege and destroy the city (Jeremiah 21:10, 39:1). The problem was that Judah's persistent idolatry and refusal to repent had made their destruction inevitable (Jeremiah 25:8-11).

## Jeremiah's Priestly Condition
Jeremiah was the son of Hilkiah, one of the priests who were in Anathoth in the land of Benjamin (Jeremiah 1:1). The word of the LORD came to him during the thirteenth year of the reign of Josiah (Jeremiah 1:2). Jeremiah was a young man, possibly still a teenager, when God first spoke to him (Jeremiah 1:6). He did not perform any special religious act, fast, or sacrifice to get God's attention (Jeremiah 1:4-5). God had already known and consecrated him before he was even formed in the womb (Jeremiah 1:5). He was simply a youth from a priestly family when **He** chose him (Jeremiah 1:1,5-6).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD communicated directly with Jeremiah, revealing **His** sovereign plan for his life from before birth.

> Now the word of the LORD came to me, saying, "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; I appointed you a prophet to the nations." Then I said, "Ah, LORD God! Behold, I do not know how to speak, for I am only a youth." But the LORD said to me, "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for to all to whom I send you, you shall go, and whatever I command you, you shall speak. **Do not be afraid of them, for I am with you to deliver you**, declares the LORD." Then the LORD put out His hand and touched my mouth. And the LORD said to me, "Behold, I have put My words in your mouth. See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant." — Jeremiah 1:4-10 (ESV)

> "But you, dress yourself for work; arise, and say to them everything that I command you. Do not be dismayed by them, lest I dismay you before them. And I, behold, **I make you this day a fortified city, an iron pillar, and bronze walls**, against the whole land, against the kings of Judah, its officials, its priests, and the people of the land. **They will fight against you, but they shall not prevail against you, for I am with you, declares the LORD, to deliver you.**" — Jeremiah 1:17-19 (ESV)

## The Cost of Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice his desire for a normal family life (Jeremiah 16:1-2). The LORD commanded him not to take a wife or have sons and daughters in that place (Jeremiah 16:2). He sacrificed his reputation and social standing among his people (Jeremiah 15:10). He was mocked and derided by his neighbors and even his own family (Jeremiah 11:19, 20:7-8). He risked his life multiple times by delivering unpopular messages of judgment (Jeremiah 26:8, 38:4-6).

## Initial Fear and Doubt
Jeremiah's initial response was one of inadequacy and fear (Jeremiah 1:6). He protested that he did not know how to speak because he was only a youth (Jeremiah 1:6). Later, when the pressure of the calling became intense, he even cursed the day of his birth (Jeremiah 20:14). He felt that the LORD had deceived him and that he had become a laughingstock (Jeremiah 20:7).

## Faithful Reaction and Endurance
Jeremiah eventually submitted to the LORD'S will and spoke **His** words faithfully (Jeremiah 1:17). Even when he tried to remain silent, God's word became like a burning fire shut up in his bones (Jeremiah 20:9). He stood before kings and officials to declare the coming destruction (Jeremiah 21:1-10, 37:16-17). He remained in Jerusalem during the Babylonian siege, enduring imprisonment in a muddy cistern where he almost died (Jeremiah 38:6). He refused to flee or compromise his message even when threatened with death by the priests and prophets (Jeremiah 26:12-15). Jeremiah trusted that the LORD would be with him as a dread warrior (Jeremiah 20:11). He continued to warn King Zedekiah to surrender to the Babylonians to save his life and the city (Jeremiah 38:17-18). His life was a constant testimony of obedience in the face of universal rejection (Jeremiah 1:18-19).

## The Miracle of Protection
God miraculously protected Jeremiah during the violent fall of Jerusalem to the Babylonians. While the kings and officials were captured or killed, Jeremiah was specifically favored by the enemy commander under God's sovereign direction.

> Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon gave command concerning Jeremiah through Nebuzaradan, the captain of the guard, saying, "Take him, look after him well, and **do him no harm, but deal with him as he tells you**." So Nebuzaradan the captain of the guard, Nebushazban the Rab-saris, Nergal-sharezer the Rab-mag, and all the chief officers of the king of Babylon sent and took Jeremiah from the court of the guard. They entrusted him to Gedaliah the son of Ahikam, son of Shaphan, that he should take him home. So **he dwelt among the people**. — Jeremiah 39:11-14 (ESV)

The LORD fulfilled **His** promise that **they shall not prevail against you** and that **I am with you to deliver you** (Jeremiah 1:19). God made Jeremiah like **a fortified city** that the Babylonians were commanded to protect (Jeremiah 1:18).

## The Freedom of the Result
The result of God's miracle was that Jeremiah survived the destruction of Jerusalem while many others perished or were taken into exile (Jeremiah 39:1-10, 40:1). He was given the freedom to choose where he wanted to live (Jeremiah 40:4). The captain of the guard even gave him an allowance of food and a present (Jeremiah 40:5). Jeremiah's survival served as a sign that God's word is true and that **He** preserves **His** faithful servants (Jeremiah 1:12). His writings were preserved for the exiles in Babylon, giving them hope for a future restoration (Jeremiah 29:10-14, 31:31-34).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced the problem of a rebellious nation and the terrifying threat of the Babylonian army. He was a young and hesitant priest with no desire for conflict. However, the LORD promised to make him an iron pillar and to deliver him from his enemies. Jeremiah sacrificed his right to marry and his safety to proclaim God's truth. Despite being cast into a cistern and facing constant mockery, he remained faithful. God's intervention during the fall of Jerusalem proved that **He** is a dread warrior **Who** guards **His** own. Jeremiah sacrificed a comfortable life but gained the honor of being God's voice for generations and the assurance of divine protection. His story shows that even when we are overwhelmed by opposition, we can trust the LORD'S promise to be with us. **He** is the LORD **Who** watches over **His** word to perform it.
