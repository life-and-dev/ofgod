# Jeremiah: Real Estate Investment

A prophet is ordered to purchase a piece of land in a city surrounded by a conquering army. His investment seems like total madness as Jerusalem is on the brink of collapse. Yet, this act of faith carries a promise that defies the current disaster.

## The Siege of Jerusalem
Jerusalem was under a brutal siege by the army of the king of Babylon (Jeremiah 32:2). It was the tenth year of Zedekiah king of Judah (Jeremiah 32:1). Jeremiah the prophet was shut up in the court of the guard in the palace (Jeremiah 32:2). King Zedekiah had imprisoned him because he prophesied the city's fall (Jeremiah 32:3-5). The people of Israel and Judah had done nothing but evil in God's sight (Jeremiah 32:30). They did not obey **His** voice or walk in **His** law (Jeremiah 32:23). They had provoked the LORD to anger by worshiping other gods (Jeremiah 32:29). Because of their persistent rebellion, the LORD made disaster come upon them (Jeremiah 32:23). The city was being given into the hands of the Chaldeans by sword, famine, and pestilence (Jeremiah 32:24,36). The problem was that Jerusalem faced total destruction as a judgment for centuries of unfaithfulness to God (Jeremiah 32:31).

## Jeremiah's Predetermined Condition
Jeremiah did not perform any special religious act or prayer to receive his initial commission from the LORD (Jeremiah 1:4-5). He was simply a young man from a priestly family in Anathoth (Jeremiah 1:1). The LORD chose him before he was even conceived (Jeremiah 1:5). Jeremiah was already a prisoner in the court of the guard when the command to buy the field came to him (Jeremiah 32:2,8). He was in a state of confinement and national crisis when God required this extreme act of trust (Jeremiah 32:2).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came to Jeremiah directly to appoint him as a prophet and later to command him to buy a field (Jeremiah 1:4; 32:1).

> "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; **I appointed you a prophet to the nations.**" — Jeremiah 1:5 (ESV)

> "Do not say, 'I am only a youth'; for **to all to whom I send you, you shall go, and whatever I command you, you shall speak.** Do not be afraid of them, for **I am with you to deliver you**, declares the LORD." — Jeremiah 1:7-8 (ESV)

> "Behold, **I have put My words in your mouth. See, I have set you this day over nations and over kingdoms, to pluck up and to break down, to destroy and to overthrow, to build and to plant.**" — Jeremiah 1:9-10 (ESV)

> "Behold, Hanamel the son of Shallum your uncle will come to you and say, '**Buy my field that is at Anathoth, for the right of redemption by purchase is yours.**'" — Jeremiah 32:7 (ESV)

## The Costly Sacrifice
Jeremiah had to sacrifice his meager financial resources while being a prisoner (Jeremiah 32:2,9). He weighed out seventeen shekels of silver for a field in Anathoth (Jeremiah 32:9). This money was spent on land that was currently occupied by the Babylonian army (Jeremiah 32:2,24). To anyone else, this would have seemed like a total loss of money for a worthless piece of ground in a doomed nation (Jeremiah 32:25). He risked his reputation by making a purchase that appeared nonsensical during a national catastrophe (Jeremiah 32:25).

## Jeremiah's Humble Response
Jeremiah did not initially rebel, but he did protest his inadequacy (Jeremiah 1:6). He told the LORD that he did not know how to speak because he was only a youth (Jeremiah 1:6). His concern was his lack of experience and authority in the face of a daunting task (Jeremiah 1:6). However, he did not refuse the call once the LORD reassured him (Jeremiah 1:7-8).

## Jeremiah's Faithful Reaction
Jeremiah obeyed the LORD's command to buy the field despite being imprisoned (Jeremiah 32:8). When his cousin Hanamel arrived with the offer, Jeremiah recognized it as the word of the LORD (Jeremiah 32:8). He signed the deed and sealed it in the presence of witnesses (Jeremiah 32:10). He weighed out the seventeen shekels of silver on scales (Jeremiah 32:10). Jeremiah then gave the deeds to his assistant Baruch (Jeremiah 32:12). He commanded Baruch to put them in an earthenware vessel so they would last for a long time (Jeremiah 32:14). This was a public demonstration of his trust in God's future restoration (Jeremiah 32:15). After completing the transaction, Jeremiah prayed to the LORD for understanding (Jeremiah 32:16). He acknowledged that nothing is too hard for the LORD (Jeremiah 32:17). Yet, he expressed his confusion that God would command him to buy land when the city was already falling to the Chaldeans (Jeremiah 32:24-25). He laid his doubts before **Him** while still maintaining his obedience (Jeremiah 32:25). His reaction was one of practical compliance followed by a humble seeking of God's wisdom (Jeremiah 32:16-17,25). He did not let his lack of understanding stop him from following the divine instruction (Jeremiah 32:8).

## The Promised Miracle
God responded to Jeremiah's prayer by confirming the coming judgment but promising an even greater miracle of restoration (Jeremiah 32:26-37). **He** would gather **His** people from all nations and bring them back to their land (Jeremiah 32:37).

> "Behold, **I will gather them from all the countries to which I drove them in My anger and My wrath and in great indignation. I will bring them back to this place, and I will make them dwell in safety.**" — Jeremiah 32:37 (ESV)

> "**I will rejoice in doing them good, and I will plant them in this land in faithfulness, with all My heart and all My soul.**" — Jeremiah 32:41 (ESV)

> "**Fields shall be bought in this land** of which you are saying, 'It is a desolation, without man or beast; it is given into the hand of the Chaldeans.' **Fields shall be bought for money, and deeds shall be signed and sealed and witnessed** . . . for **I will restore their fortunes**, declares the LORD." — Jeremiah 32:43-44 (ESV)

## The Restoration Result
The miracle resulted in the eventual return of the Judeans to their homeland after seventy years of exile (Jeremiah 32:37,44). God restored their fortunes just as **He** had promised through Jeremiah (Jeremiah 32:44). This restoration affected not just individuals, but the entire nation, including the hill country, the Shephelah, and the Negeb (Jeremiah 32:44). The purchase of land and the signing of deeds once again became a reality in the places about Jerusalem (Jeremiah 32:44). This fulfillment proved to all neighboring nations that the God of Israel is sovereign over history and faithful to **His** word (Jeremiah 32:20).

## Conclusion
Jeremiah faced the problem of a nation under divine judgment and a city facing imminent destruction. He was limited by his youth and his confinement in prison. However, the LORD promised to restore the people and use Jeremiah's bizarre purchase as a sign of hope. Jeremiah responded with obedience despite his confusion, trusting God with his silver and his life. God intervened by preserving the nation through exile and miraculously bringing them back to their land. The result was a restored community where life and commerce could flourish again. Jeremiah sacrificed his financial security and faced public ridicule, but he gained the honor of being God's faithful witness. His story proves that we can trust the LORD's promises even when the current circumstances seem hopeless. **He** is always faithful to fulfill **His** word.
