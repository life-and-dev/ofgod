# Solomon: the Gift of Wisdom

A young king inherited a vast empire but felt like a child in the face of his massive responsibility. When given a blank check from the Creator, his choice would define his legacy. Discover how a simple request for help transformed a kingdom.

## The Problem of Governing a Multitude
Solomon was the son of David who established himself in his kingdom (2 Chronicles 1:1). He made a marriage alliance with Pharaoh king of Egypt (1 Kings 3:1). He brought Pharaoh's daughter into the city of David until he finished building his own house and the house of the LORD (1 Kings 3:1). The people were still sacrificing at the high places because no house had yet been built for the name of the LORD (1 Kings 3:2). Solomon loved the LORD and walked in the statutes of David his father (1 Kings 3:3). However, he also sacrificed and made offerings at the high places (1 Kings 3:3). He went to Gibeon because it was the great high place (1 Kings 3:4). He was in the midst of a great people who were too many to be numbered or counted for multitude (1 Kings 3:8). The problem was that Solomon felt inadequate and lacked the discernment needed to govern the vast and chosen people of God (1 Kings 3:7-9).

## Solomon's Devout Condition
Solomon went to the high place at Gibeon to sacrifice to the LORD (1 Kings 3:4; 2 Chronicles 1:3). He offered a thousand burnt offerings on the bronze altar that was before the tent of meeting (1 Kings 3:4; 2 Chronicles 1:5-6). He was actively seeking the LORD through worship and sacrifice before God responded (1 Kings 3:4-5). While he was at Gibeon, the LORD appeared to him in a dream by night (1 Kings 3:5; 2 Chronicles 1:7). He was in a state of humble devotion when God chose to speak to him (1 Kings 3:3-5).

## The Dream of Divine Calling
The LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream and invited him to make a request.

> And God said, “Ask what **I shall give you**.” . . . And God said to him, “Because you have asked this, and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies, but have asked for yourself understanding to discern what is right, behold, **I now do according to your word. Behold, I give you a wise and discerning mind, so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you. I give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor, so that no other king shall compare with you, all your days. And if you will walk in My ways, keeping My statutes and My commandments, as your father David walked, then I will lengthen your days.**”
>
>  — 1 Kings 3:5,11-14 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Solomon had to sacrifice his own personal ambitions and desires for the sake of the kingdom (1 Kings 3:11). He could have asked for long life, great wealth, or the destruction of his political enemies (1 Kings 3:11). Instead, he risked being seen as weak or lacking in ambition by prioritizing the needs of his people over his own (1 Kings 3:9). He sacrificed the opportunity to secure his own earthly comfort and power through traditional means (1 Kings 3:11-12).

## Solomon's Initial Response
Solomon responded with deep humility and gratitude for God's steadfast love toward his father David (1 Kings 3:6). He acknowledged that he was but a little child who did not know how to go out or come in (1 Kings 3:7). He did not refuse the opportunity but expressed his total dependence on God (1 Kings 3:7-8). He recognized the greatness of the people he was called to lead (1 Kings 3:8). He did not initially seek greatness for himself but rather the ability to serve (1 Kings 3:9).

## Solomon's Eventual Reaction
Solomon asked the LORD for an understanding mind to govern the people and to discern between good and evil (1 Kings 3:9). It pleased the LORD that Solomon had asked for wisdom rather than selfish gain (1 Kings 3:10). After the dream ended, Solomon returned to Jerusalem and stood before the ark of the covenant of the LORD (1 Kings 3:15). He offered up burnt offerings and peace offerings and made a feast for all his servants (1 Kings 3:15). He immediately put his trust in God's promise of wisdom (1 Kings 3:12). He did not doubt that God would fulfill His word (1 Kings 3:15). He showed his commitment by worshiping the LORD in the city of David (1 Kings 3:15). His reaction was one of joyful celebration and renewed dedication to his royal duties (1 Kings 3:15).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD fulfilled His promise by granting Solomon extraordinary wisdom, which was immediately tested by two prostitutes (1 Kings 3:16). One woman's son had died, and she claimed the living child of the other woman was hers (1 Kings 3:17-22). Solomon proposed to divide the living child with a sword (1 Kings 3:24-25).

> Then the woman whose son was alive said to the king . . . “Oh, my lord, give her the living child, and by no means put him to death.” But the other said, “He shall be neither mine nor yours; divide him.” Then the king answered and said, “Give the living child to the first woman, and by no means put him to death; she is his mother.” And all Israel heard of the judgment that the king had rendered, and they stood in awe of the king, because **they perceived that the wisdom of God was in him to do justice.**
>
>  — 1 Kings 3:26-28 (ESV)

In this miracle, the LORD fulfilled His promise that **I give you a wise and discerning mind** (1 Kings 3:12).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the entire nation of Israel standing in awe of their king (1 Kings 3:28). They recognized that Solomon possessed a divine wisdom for judgment (1 Kings 3:28). This wisdom brought peace and prosperity to the land, as Solomon ruled with justice and understanding (1 Kings 4:20-21). He became more famous than all the kings of the earth, and people from all nations came to hear his wisdom (1 Kings 4:34, 10:23-24). His neighbors and foreign rulers, such as the Queen of Sheba, were amazed by his insight and the blessed state of his kingdom (1 Kings 10:1-9). The result was a golden age for Israel where the people were as numerous as the sand by the sea (1 Kings 4:20).

## Conclusion
Solomon faced the problem of governing a vast and chosen people while feeling like an inexperienced child. He was limited by his own youth and the immense pressure of his father David's legacy. However, the LORD promised to give him a wise and discerning mind because Solomon sought the welfare of the people over his own. Solomon responded by humbly asking for wisdom, and God intervened by granting him unparalleled insight and immense wealth. The result was a period of unprecedented peace and international respect for Israel. Solomon sacrificed the pursuit of personal riches and long life, but he gained both of those along with the wisdom he requested. His story proves that we can trust the LORD to equip us for any task He gives us. He is faithful to provide more than we even know to ask for.
