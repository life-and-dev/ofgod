# Solomon: the King's Prayer for Wisdom

Solomon is chosen to lead a kingdom and build a house for God. Before his reign begins, he must navigate a dangerous coup and face his own inadequacy. His request for a simple gift changes everything. It shows that God's strength is perfect in weakness.

## The Problem of a Divided Succession

King David was old and could not get warm (1 Kings 1:1). His son Adonijah exalted himself, saying "I will be king," and prepared chariots and horsemen (1 Kings 1:5). He drew away key leaders like Joab and Abiathar (1 Kings 1:7). However, David had previously sworn that Solomon would sit on the throne (1 Kings 1:13,17). The kingdom was in a state of political instability as David's health declined (1 Kings 1:15-21). Solomon was but a "little child" compared to the weight of the throne and the task of building the temple (1 Chronicles 22:5, 29:1). David had received the mandate to build a house for the LORD, but was told his son would be the one to complete it (1 Chronicles 17:11-12, 22:8-10). The problem was that Solomon faced a hostile succession attempt while lacking the experience to lead a great people or fulfill the divine architectural mandate (1 Kings 1:11-12,21).

## Solomon's Humble Condition

Solomon was in a state of humility and devotion. He loved the LORD and walked in the statutes of his father David (1 Kings 3:3). Before God spoke to him, Solomon went to Gibeon to sacrifice (1 Kings 3:4). He offered a thousand burnt offerings on the altar at the great high place (1 Kings 3:4). He did not seek a vision, but he sought to honor the LORD through worship and sacrifice (1 Kings 3:3-4).

## The Divine Calling

The LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night. God invited him to ask for anything he desired.

> At Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night, and God said, "**Ask what I shall give you.**"
> ...
> And God said to him, "Because you have asked this, and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies, but have asked for yourself understanding to discern what is right, behold, **I now do according to your word**. Behold, **I give you a wise and discerning mind**, so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you. **I give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor**, so that no other king shall compare with you, all your days. And if you will walk in My ways, keeping My statutes and My commandments, as your father David walked, then **I will lengthen your days.**"
>
> — 1 Kings 3:5,11-14 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice

Solomon had to sacrifice his own reputation and safety during Adonijah's coup (1 Kings 1:11-12,21). He risked being killed if the rebellion succeeded (1 Kings 1:12,21). He also sacrificed his own desires for power, wealth, or the death of his enemies when he asked only for wisdom (1 Kings 3:9-11). He yielded his life and his reign to the service of God's people and the construction of the temple (1 Chronicles 28:10,20).

## The Initial Response

Solomon did not rebel against the call. He acknowledged his own inadequacy, calling himself a "little child" (1 Kings 3:7). He admitted he did not know "how to go out or come in" (1 Kings 3:7). He showed a sense of being overwhelmed by the multitude of the people (1 Kings 3:8). His response was one of humble dependence rather than refusal (1 Kings 3:6-9).

## Solomon's Eventual Reaction

Solomon immediately went to Jerusalem after the dream (1 Kings 3:15). He stood before the ark of the covenant and offered burnt offerings and peace offerings (1 Kings 3:15). He made a feast for all his servants (1 Kings 3:15). He soon demonstrated the wisdom God gave him when two prostitutes came with a dispute over a baby (1 Kings 3:16). He ordered a sword to divide the living child, revealing the true mother's compassion (1 Kings 3:24-27). Beyond this, he applied himself to the massive task of building the temple (1 Kings 5:2-5). He gathered seventy thousand burden-bearers and eighty thousand stonecutters (1 Kings 5:15). He made alliances to secure cedar and cypress timber (1 Kings 5:8-10). He oversaw the construction for seven years until the house was finished in all its parts (1 Kings 6:37-38). He also built his own house and the wall around Jerusalem (1 Kings 3:1, 7:1). His life became a testament to the wisdom and resources provided by God (1 Kings 10:23-24).

## The Divine Miracle

God granted Solomon unparalleled wisdom and immense wealth, fulfilling every promise.

> And all Israel heard of the judgment that the king had rendered, and they stood in awe of the king, because they perceived that **the wisdom of God was in him to do justice**.
>
> — 1 Kings 3:28 (ESV)
>
> Thus King Solomon excelled all the kings of the earth in riches and in wisdom. And the whole earth sought the presence of Solomon to hear his wisdom, which **God had put into his mind**.
>
> — 1 Kings 10:23-24 (ESV)
>
> So Solomon sat on the throne of the LORD as king instead of David his father. And **he prospered, and all Israel obeyed him**. The LORD made Solomon **very great in the sight of all Israel, and bestowed on him such royal majesty as had not been on any king before him** in Israel.
>
> — 1 Chronicles 29:23,25 (ESV)

## The Lasting Result

The miracle resulted in a period of unprecedented peace and prosperity for Israel (1 Kings 4:20-21,25). Judah and Israel lived in safety, every man under his vine and under his fig tree (1 Kings 4:25). The reputation of the LORD spread to other nations, as seen when the Queen of Sheba visited to test Solomon with hard questions (1 Kings 10:1). She praised the LORD for His delight in Solomon (1 Kings 10:9). The temple was completed, providing a permanent place for the ark of the covenant and the presence of God (1 Kings 8:1-11). Solomon's wisdom was recorded in three thousand proverbs and over a thousand songs (1 Kings 4:32). His reign marked the golden age of the United Kingdom of Israel (1 Kings 10:23-27).

## Conclusion

Solomon faced the problem of a divided kingdom and his own inexperience. He was limited by his youth and the threat of Adonijah. God promised him an understanding mind and added riches and honor. Solomon responded with humble worship and by asking for the right thing. God intervened by granting him supernatural wisdom and peace. The result was a prosperous nation and the completion of the temple. Solomon sacrificed his own ambitions for the sake of God's people. In the end, he gained a legacy of wisdom and glory that no other king could match. This proves that when we trust the LORD with our responsibilities, He provides more than we could ask or imagine. He is faithful to His word and establishes those who walk in His ways.
