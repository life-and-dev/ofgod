# Solomon: The King Who Asked for Wisdom

A young king, newly crowned and overwhelmed by the weight of his responsibilities, receives an extraordinary offer from the LORD in a dream. God invites him to ask for anything He desires. Discover how his humble choice secured a legacy of unparalleled wisdom and prosperity for Israel.

## The Problem of an Inexperienced King

Solomon was the son of David (1 Kings 1:11). The transition of power was turbulent, as David's older son Adonijah had attempted to seize the throne (1 Kings 1:5,11-18). Solomon was young and inexperienced when he became king (1 Kings 3:7). The political environment was tense, as David's kingdom was vast and required a strong leader to maintain order (1 Kings 2:1-4). Although David had consolidated the kingdom, there were still internal threats and the immense task of building the house of the LORD (1 Kings 3:1; 1 Chronicles 22:19). The problem was that Solomon felt inadequate to govern such a numerous and chosen people (1 Kings 3:8-9). He was but a little child in his own eyes, lacking the wisdom to go out or come in as a leader (1 Kings 3:7).

## Solomon's Condition at Gibeon

Solomon loved the LORD and walked in the statutes of David (1 Kings 3:3). He went to Gibeon, the great high place, to offer sacrifices (1 Kings 3:4). He did something special to get God's attention: he offered a thousand burnt offerings on the altar (1 Kings 3:4). It was there, at Gibeon, that the LORD appeared to him in a dream by night (1 Kings 3:5).

## The Heavenly Calling

The LORD spoke to Solomon in a dream, giving him the opportunity to request whatever he wanted.

> "Ask what I shall give you." ... "Because you have asked this, and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies, but have asked for yourself understanding to discern what is right, behold, **I now do according to your word**. Behold, **I give you a wise and discerning mind**, so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you. **I give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor**, so that no other king shall compare with you, all your days. And if you will walk in My ways, keeping My statutes and My commandments, as your father David walked, then **I will lengthen your days**." — 1 Kings 3:5,11-14 (ESV)

## The Sacrifice of Personal Ambition

Solomon had to sacrifice the opportunity to ask for things that kings usually value: long life, wealth, or the death of his enemies (1 Kings 3:11). By asking for wisdom instead of personal power or riches, he risked his kingdom's security and his own prestige if he could not effectively govern (1 Kings 3:9,11). He chose to prioritize the needs of God's people over his own selfish desires (1 Kings 3:9).

## Solomon's Initial Response

Solomon responded with deep humility and gratitude (1 Kings 3:6). He acknowledged that he was but a little child who did not know how to go out or come in (1 Kings 3:7). He did not rebel or refuse, but he did express his own limitations and his dependence on the LORD'S grace (1 Kings 3:6-8).

## Solomon's Reaction: A Reign of Divine Wisdom

Solomon's response to the LORD'S invitation was to ask for an understanding mind to govern the people and to discern between good and evil (1 Kings 3:9). He recognized that the people he was called to lead were great and too many to be numbered (1 Kings 3:8). After the dream, Solomon went to Jerusalem and stood before the ark of the covenant of the LORD (1 Kings 3:15). He offered up burnt offerings and peace offerings, showing his continued devotion and thankfulness (1 Kings 3:15). He also made a feast for all his servants, celebrating the LORD'S favor (1 Kings 3:15). Solomon's wisdom was immediately put to the test when two prostitutes came to him with a dispute over a living child (1 Kings 3:16-22). Solomon proposed to divide the living child in two, which revealed the true mother's compassion (1 Kings 3:24-27). This judgment demonstrated that the wisdom of God was in him to do justice (1 Kings 3:28). Throughout his reign, Solomon used his wisdom to build the house of the LORD and his own palace (1 Kings 5:1-5, 6:1). He established peace and prosperity in Israel, such that Judah and Israel were as many as the sand by the sea (1 Kings 4:20). He spoke three thousand proverbs and his songs were a thousand and five (1 Kings 4:32). Solomon's reaction was to live out the wisdom God had granted him for the benefit of the entire nation (1 Kings 4:29-34).

## The Miracle of Understanding

The LORD fulfilled His promise by giving Solomon unparalleled wisdom, along with riches and honor (1 Kings 3:12-13).

> "Behold, **I give you a wise and discerning mind**, so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you. **I give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor**, so that no other king shall compare with you, all your days." — 1 Kings 3:12-13 (ESV)

> "And all Israel heard of the judgment that the king had rendered, and they stood in awe of the king, because they perceived that **the wisdom of God was in him to do justice**." — 1 Kings 3:28 (ESV)

## The Result: A Nation Blessed With Peace

The miracle resulted in a golden age for Israel (1 Kings 4:20). Solomon's wisdom brought peace to the land, and his fame spread to all the surrounding nations (1 Kings 4:24,31). People from all over the world came to hear his wisdom, including the Queen of Sheba (1 Kings 10:1). Israel became a center of wealth and culture, and the house of the LORD was completed in great splendor (2 Chronicles 7:11). Solomon's judgment provided justice for the lowly and established the king's authority (1 Kings 3:28). The nation lived in safety and joy, each man under his vine and under his fig tree (1 Kings 4:25).

## Conclusion

Solomon faced the problem of his own inexperience and the daunting task of leading a great nation. He was limited by his youth and the political instability following his father's death. However, when the LORD offered him anything, Solomon asked for wisdom rather than personal gain. God responded by granting him a wise heart and adding riches and honor that He had not even requested. The result was a period of unprecedented peace, justice, and prosperity for all of Israel. Solomon sacrificed his own desires for power and wealth, but he gained a legacy of divine wisdom and an enduring reputation. His story shows that when we seek God's righteousness and the welfare of His people, He is faithful to provide all that we need and more.
