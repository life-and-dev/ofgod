# Solomon: the King Who Asked for Wisdom

A young king inherits a vast empire and a divine task far beyond his abilities. Facing the pressure of his father's legacy, he makes a choice that will determine the fate of his kingdom. This single decision sets in motion events that will be remembered for thousands of years.

## The Problem of an Inexperienced Heir

Solomon was born into a royal family at a time of great transition for the nation of Israel (2 Samuel 12:24). As King David grew old and feeble, the political atmosphere became unstable and competitive (1 Kings 1:1,5). Adonijah, David's older son, attempted to seize the throne by force before David had officially named a successor (1 Kings 1:5-10). Solomon was young and lacked the military or political experience of his older brothers (1 Chronicles 22:5, 29:1). Furthermore, David had been forbidden from building a temple for the LORD because he was a man of war who had shed much blood (1 Chronicles 22:8). The responsibility for this massive and holy project was placed solely upon Solomon's shoulders (1 Chronicles 22:9-10). He had to lead a populous nation that was as numerous as the dust of the earth (1 Kings 3:8). The problem was that Solomon was a young and inexperienced king who lacked the wisdom to govern a vast nation and fulfill his father's legacy of building the temple (1 Chronicles 22:5; 1 Kings 3:7).

## Solomon's Devoted Condition

Solomon demonstrated his love for the LORD by walking in the statutes of his father David (1 Kings 3:3). He traveled to Gibeon, which was the most significant high place for worship at that time (1 Kings 3:4). Before the LORD spoke to him, Solomon offered a massive sacrifice of a thousand burnt offerings on the altar (1 Kings 3:4). This act of worship and devotion showed his heart's condition before God responded (1 Kings 3:4-5). The LORD appeared to him in a dream during the night while he was at Gibeon (1 Kings 3:5).

## The Divine Calling

God communicated directly with Solomon in a vision during the night.

> At Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night, and God said, "Ask what I shall give you." . . . And God said to him, "Because you have asked this, and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies, but have asked for yourself understanding to discern what is right, behold, **I now do according to your word.** Behold, **I give you a wise and discerning mind**, so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you. **I give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor**, so that no other king shall compare with you, all your days. And if you will walk in My ways, keeping My statutes and My commandments, as your father David walked, then **I will lengthen your days.**"
>
> — 1 Kings 3:5,11-14 (ESV)

## The Sacrifice of Personal Ambition

Solomon had to sacrifice his own personal desires and ambitions to serve the needs of his people (1 Kings 3:11). He did not ask for long life, great wealth, or victory over his enemies, which were common goals for any ancient monarch (1 Kings 3:11). He risked being seen as weak or incompetent if his request for wisdom did not result in effective leadership (1 Kings 3:9). He also committed himself to the exhausting task of building the temple and the palace (1 Kings 5:5). He sacrificed his own ease and safety for the sake of the LORD'S work (1 Kings 5:5).

## Solomon's Humble Initial Response

Solomon responded with deep humility and an admission of his own inadequacy (1 Kings 3:7). He did not express pride in his new position but instead called himself a "little child" who did not know how to lead (1 Kings 3:7). He acknowledged that the people he was called to govern were numerous and chosen by God (1 Kings 3:8). He did not doubt God's ability to help him but specifically asked for an understanding mind (1 Kings 3:9). His response was one of total and complete dependence on the LORD (1 Kings 3:9).

## Solomon's Wise Reaction

Solomon eventually responded to God's calling by applying the wisdom he received to every aspect of his reign (1 Kings 3:28). When two women brought a difficult dispute over a child, he used a wise test to reveal the true mother (1 Kings 3:16-27). He organized the administration of the kingdom with great skill and appointed capable officials (1 Kings 4:1-19). He maintained peace with neighboring nations and established trade routes (1 Kings 4:24-25, 5:12). Solomon diligently prepared the plans and gathered the resources for the construction of the temple as his father David had charged him (1 Chronicles 22:5). He made a treaty with Hiram, king of Tyre, to secure the best timber and craftsmen (1 Kings 5:1-12). He mobilized thousands of workers to quarry stones and prepare materials (1 Kings 5:13-18). He built the temple with incredible attention to detail, overlaying much of it with pure gold (1 Kings 6:20-22). During the dedication of the temple, Solomon stood before the altar and offered a humble prayer of consecration (1 Kings 8:22-53). He acknowledged that even the highest heaven could not contain God, yet he asked the LORD to dwell among His people (1 Kings 8:27). He trusted that the LORD would hear the prayers of those who turned toward the temple (1 Kings 8:29-30). Solomon's reaction was characterized by a commitment to excellence and a desire to honor the name of the LORD in everything he did (1 Kings 5:5, 8:20). He continued to offer sacrifices and lead the people in worship throughout his life (1 Kings 9:25). His life demonstrated a heart that initially sought to please God above all else (1 Kings 3:3).

## The Divine Miracle

God miraculously granted Solomon wisdom that surpassed all others and added riches and honor beyond measure. The LORD fulfilled His promises by making Solomon the most famous and wealthy king of his time.

> And the LORD gave Solomon wisdom, **as He promised him.** . . . Thus King Solomon **excelled all the kings of the earth in riches and in wisdom.** And the whole earth sought the presence of Solomon to hear his wisdom, which **God had put into his mind.** Every one of them brought his present, articles of silver and gold, garments, myrrh, spices, horses, and mules, so much year by year.
>
> — 1 Kings 5:12, 10:23-25 (ESV)

## The Result of Peace and Prosperity

The miracle resulted in a golden age of peace and prosperity for the nation of Israel (1 Kings 4:20). The people were as numerous as the sand by the sea, eating and drinking and being happy (1 Kings 4:20). Judah and Israel lived in safety, from Dan even to Beer-sheba, all the days of Solomon (1 Kings 4:25). The reputation of Solomon's wisdom reached far-off lands, drawing the Queen of Sheba to witness his greatness (1 Kings 10:1-7). She confessed that the reports she heard did not even tell half of the truth (1 Kings 10:7). The surrounding nations became peaceful and brought tribute to Israel (1 Kings 4:21, 10:25). The building of the temple provided a central place for the worship of the LORD, uniting the nation spiritually (1 Kings 8:62-66). Solomon's wisdom was also expressed in three thousand proverbs and over a thousand songs, leaving a literary legacy for future generations (1 Kings 4:32). His reign was a time of unprecedented glory that prefigured the eternal kingdom of the Messiah (Matthew 12:42).

## Conclusion

Solomon faced the problem of being a young king with the heavy burden of building the temple and governing a vast nation. He was limited by his lack of experience and the potential for political instability. However, the LORD promised him wisdom and riches. Solomon responded with humility, choosing to ask for an understanding mind rather than personal gain. God intervened by making him the wisest and wealthiest man on earth. The result was a kingdom of peace that influenced the entire world. Solomon sacrificed his own selfish ambitions, but he gained a legacy of wisdom and the honor of building God's house. His story proves that we can trust the LORD to equip us for any task when we seek Him first. He is faithful to provide exactly what we need.
