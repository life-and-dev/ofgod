# Solomon: the Wise King

A young king stands at the threshold of immense responsibility, succeeding a legendary father. In a mysterious night vision, the Almighty offers him any gift he desires. His choice reveals a heart that trusts God's provision over human power and wealth.

## The Problem of Royal Responsibility

Solomon was the son of David and Bathsheba, born into a royal house marked by both glory and internal strife (2 Samuel 12:24). As David grew old, Solomon's half-brother Adonijah attempted to seize the throne, creating a dangerous political crisis (1 Kings 1:5,11). David intervened to crown Solomon, but the young king inherited a kingdom with unresolved tensions and powerful enemies (1 Kings 1:33-35, 2:1-9). He was tasked with building the first temple for the LORD and ruling a people as numerous as the dust of the earth (1 Kings 2:2-3, 3:8). Solomon felt the weight of his inexperience and the complexity of governing a vast nation (1 Kings 3:7). He followed a father whose heart was perfect toward the LORD, setting a standard that seemed impossible to reach (1 Kings 3:3,6). The problem was that Solomon lacked the personal strength and insight to lead God's chosen people and fulfill his royal duties (1 Kings 3:7-9).

## Solomon's Humble Condition

Solomon went to Gibeon because it was the great high place for worship (1 Kings 3:4). He showed his love for the LORD by walking in the statutes of his father David (1 Kings 3:3). He did something special to get God's attention by offering a thousand burnt offerings on the altar (1 Kings 3:4). During this time of intense sacrifice and worship, the LORD responded to him in a dream (1 Kings 3:5).

## The Divine Calling

The LORD appeared to Solomon in the night and gave him a radical invitation to ask for anything (1 Kings 3:5).

> At Gibeon **the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night**, and God said, "**Ask what I shall give you**." — 1 Kings 3:5 (ESV)

After Solomon made his request, the LORD spoke again to confirm what He would do (1 Kings 3:11).

> And God said to him, "Because you have asked this, and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies, but have asked for yourself understanding to discern what is right, behold, **I now do according to your word. Behold, I give you a wise and discerning mind**, so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you. **I give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor**, so that no other king shall compare with you, all your days. And if you will walk in My ways, keeping My statutes and My commandments, as your father David walked, then **I will lengthen your days**." — 1 Kings 3:11-14 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice

Solomon had to sacrifice his own ego and the common desires of a monarch (1 Kings 3:9,11). He could have asked for long life, great wealth, or the destruction of his political rivals to secure his grip on power (1 Kings 3:11). Instead, he risked being seen as weak by admitting his own limitations and depending entirely on God's wisdom (1 Kings 3:7-8). He sacrificed the pursuit of personal glory to seek the welfare of the people of Israel (1 Kings 3:9).

## Solomon's Initial Response

Solomon did not rebel or ask for selfish gains (1 Kings 3:10). He responded with deep humility and gratitude for God's steadfast love toward his father David (1 Kings 3:6). He confessed that he was but a little child who did not know how to go out or come in (1 Kings 3:7). He acknowledged the greatness of the people he was called to lead and his own inadequacy for the task (1 Kings 3:8-9).

## Solomon's Faithful Reaction

Solomon responded to the call by asking for an understanding mind to govern the people and to discern between good and evil (1 Kings 3:9). This request pleased the LORD because it showed Solomon's heart was aligned with God's purposes (1 Kings 3:10). Upon waking from the dream, he returned to Jerusalem and stood before the ark of the covenant of the LORD (1 Kings 3:15). There he offered burnt offerings and peace offerings and made a feast for all his servants (1 Kings 3:15). He demonstrated his new wisdom almost immediately when two women came to him with a dispute over a child (1 Kings 3:16-22). He proposed a solution that revealed the true mother's heart (1 Kings 3:24-27). This caused all Israel to stand in awe of his divine wisdom (1 Kings 3:28). He then devoted himself to the massive project of building the temple, using the best resources and craftsmen (1 Kings 5:1-5). He recognized that the house he was building could not contain the Almighty, yet he trusted God to put His name there (1 Kings 8:27-29). He led the nation in a great dedication ceremony, offering thousands of sacrifices and praying a prayer of intercession (1 Kings 8:62-66). His reaction was one of active obedience and reliance on the wisdom he had received (1 Kings 4:29-34).

## The Divine Miracle

The LORD performed the miracle of transforming a young, uncertain king into the wisest man who ever lived (1 Kings 4:29). He bestowed upon him unparalleled insight and vast resources that exceeded all human expectation (1 Kings 10:23).

> And **God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding beyond measure**, and breadth of mind like the sand on the seashore, so that Solomon's wisdom surpassed the wisdom of all the people of the east and all the wisdom of Egypt. . . . Thus **King Solomon excelled all the kings of the earth in riches and in wisdom.** . . . All that Solomon had planned to do in the house of the LORD and in his own house **he successfully accomplished.** — 1 Kings 4:29-30, 10:23; 2 Chronicles 7:11 (ESV)

In this miracle, God fulfilled His promise to **give you a wise and discerning mind** and to grant him **both riches and honor** (1 Kings 3:12-13).

## The Lasting Result

The miracle resulted in a golden age of peace and prosperity for Israel (1 Kings 4:20,25). The nation grew in wealth and influence, and people from all the surrounding nations came to hear the wisdom of Solomon (1 Kings 4:34). The Queen of Sheba visited him and was amazed at the splendor of his kingdom and the depth of his insight (1 Kings 10:1-5). The temple was completed as a dwelling place for the glory of the LORD, serving as the spiritual center of the nation (1 Kings 6:37-38, 8:10-11). Solomon's family and servants were blessed by the orderly and abundant state of his house (1 Kings 10:8). Despite later failures, the immediate result was a powerful demonstration of God's blessing on a king who put wisdom first (1 Kings 10:23-25).

## Conclusion

Solomon faced the problem of leading a great nation without the experience or wisdom of his father David. He was limited by his youth and the political instability of his early reign. The LORD promised to give him whatever he asked, and Solomon chose an understanding heart. Solomon responded by worshiping the LORD and dedicating his reign to building the temple and judging with justice. God intervened by granting him supernatural wisdom, riches, and honor beyond any other king. The result was a kingdom of peace that became a light to the nations. Solomon sacrificed his personal desires for wealth and power at Gibeon, but he gained all those things and more as a gift from God. His story proves that we can trust the LORD to equip us for any task He assigns. He is faithful to provide more than we can even think to ask.
