# Solomon: the Wise King

A young king is granted any wish he desires by God. His choice will determine the fate of his kingdom and lead to a legacy of unparalleled wisdom and splendor. Discover how his humble request transformed a nation.

## The Problem of a Great People

Solomon became king of Israel following the death of his father, David (1 Kings 2:1,10-12). The political environment was fragile, with potential rivals like Adonijah having already attempted to seize the throne (1 Kings 1:5-7, 2:13-25). Solomon was young and inexperienced, feeling like a small child who did not know how to go out or come in (1 Kings 3:7). He was responsible for a people so numerous they could not be counted (1 Kings 3:8). His father David had charged him with the monumental task of building a house for the name of the LORD (1 Kings 5:5; 1 Chronicles 22:6). The kingdom was at a crossroads between the legacy of David and the challenges of a new era (1 Kings 2:1-4). The problem was that Solomon lacked the wisdom and stability to lead God's chosen people and fulfill his father's sacred charge (1 Kings 3:7-9).

## Solomon's Humble Condition

Solomon went to Gibeon to sacrifice there, for that was the great high place (1 Kings 3:4). He offered a thousand burnt offerings on that altar (1 Kings 3:4). This act of worship and sacrifice showed his devotion to the LORD (1 Kings 3:3-4). He did this before the LORD responded to him in a dream (1 Kings 3:5). Solomon loved the LORD, walking in the statutes of David his father (1 Kings 3:3). It was during this time of seeking God through sacrifice that the LORD appeared to him (1 Kings 3:5).

## The Divine Calling

God appeared to Solomon in a dream at night and offered him a choice.

> At Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night, and God said, "Ask what I shall give you." And Solomon said, "You have shown great and steadfast love to Your servant David my father, because he walked before You in faithfulness, in righteousness, and in uprightness of heart toward You. And You have kept for him this great and steadfast love and have given him a son to sit on his throne this day. And now, O LORD my God, You have made Your servant king in place of David my father, although I am but a little child. I do not know how to go out or come in. And Your servant is in the midst of Your people whom You have chosen, a great people, too many to be numbered or counted for multitude. Give Your servant therefore an understanding mind to govern Your people, that I may discern between good and evil, for who is able to govern this Your great people?"
>
> It pleased the LORD that Solomon had asked this. And God said to him, "Because you have asked this, and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies, but have asked for yourself understanding to discern what is right, behold, **I now do according to your word. Behold, I give you a wise and discerning mind**, so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you. **I give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor**, so that no other king shall compare with you, all your days. And if you will walk in My ways, keeping My statutes and My commandments, as your father David walked, then **I will lengthen your days.**"
>
>  — 1 Kings 3:5-14 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice

Solomon had to sacrifice his own desires for personal gain, such as long life, riches, or the death of his enemies (1 Kings 3:11). He sacrificed his pride by admitting his limitations as a "little child" before the LORD (1 Kings 3:7). He risked the stability of his new reign by prioritizing spiritual wisdom over military or political power (1 Kings 3:9-11). He also offered a thousand burnt offerings at Gibeon as a physical sacrifice of his resources (1 Kings 3:4).

## Solomon's Initial Response

Solomon did not rebel or refuse the LORD'S offer (1 Kings 3:6-9). Instead, he responded with humility and a sense of inadequacy (1 Kings 3:7-8). He acknowledged God's steadfast love toward his father David and his own dependence on God's grace (1 Kings 3:6-7). His immediate reaction was to seek help rather than assert his own authority (1 Kings 3:9).

## Solomon's Eventual Reaction

After Solomon awoke from his dream, he went to Jerusalem and stood before the ark of the covenant of the LORD (1 Kings 3:15). He offered up burnt offerings and peace offerings, and made a feast for all his servants (1 Kings 3:15). He began to exercise the wisdom God had given him almost immediately (1 Kings 3:16-28). When two women came to him claiming the same child, Solomon proposed to divide the living child in two with a sword (1 Kings 3:16-25). This test revealed the true mother's compassion and established Solomon's reputation for divine wisdom throughout Israel (1 Kings 3:28).

Solomon also proceeded with the monumental task of building the temple (1 Kings 5:1-5). He made a treaty with Hiram, king of Tyre, to obtain timber (1 Kings 5:6-12). He raised a labor force of thirty thousand men and seventy thousand burden-bearers (1 Kings 5:13-15). He oversaw the construction of the house of the LORD for seven years (1 Kings 6:1,38). When the temple was finished, Solomon brought in the things that David his father had dedicated (1 Kings 7:51). He assembled the elders of Israel and all the heads of the tribes to bring up the ark of the covenant of the LORD (1 Kings 8:1). Solomon stood before the altar of the LORD and prayed a great prayer of dedication (1 Kings 8:22-53). He blessed the assembly of Israel and exhorted them to be true to the LORD (1 Kings 8:54-61).

Throughout his reign, Solomon continued to seek wisdom (1 Kings 4:29-34). He spoke three thousand proverbs and his songs were one thousand and five (1 Kings 4:32). People from all nations came to hear his wisdom (1 Kings 4:34). Even the Queen of Sheba visited him and was breathless at his wisdom and the house he had built (1 Kings 10:1-5). Despite his later failures in following other gods, his initial reaction was one of profound trust and dedicated service to the LORD'S purposes (1 Kings 11:1-6, 3:3). He proved that a heart yielded to God can achieve great things for **His** glory (1 Kings 10:24).

## The Divine Miracle

God performed the miracle of granting Solomon supernatural wisdom and immense wealth, far exceeding any other king. He also filled the newly built temple with **His** presence.

> And **God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding beyond measure**, and breadth of mind like the sand on the seashore. . . **He was wiser than all other men**. . . Thus **King Solomon excelled all the kings of the earth in riches and in wisdom**. . . And when the priests came out of the Holy Place, a cloud filled the house of the LORD, so that the priests could not stand to minister because of the cloud, for the glory of the LORD filled the house of the LORD.
>
>  — 1 Kings 4:29,31, 8:10-11, 10:23 (ESV)

In this miracle, God fulfilled **His** promise to **give you a wise and discerning mind** and to **give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor** (1 Kings 3:12-13).

## The Golden Result

The miracle resulted in a period of unprecedented peace and prosperity for Israel (1 Kings 4:20-25). Judah and Israel dwelt in safety, from Dan even to Beersheba, all the days of Solomon (1 Kings 4:25). The nation became a center of wisdom and a light to the surrounding peoples (1 Kings 4:34, 10:24). The construction of the temple provided a permanent place for the worship of the LORD and the presence of **His** glory (1 Kings 8:10-11; 2 Chronicles 7:1). Solomon's wisdom brought justice to the oppressed and stability to the government (1 Kings 3:28, 4:1-19). His fame reached all the kingdoms of the earth, leading many to acknowledge the greatness of the God of Israel (1 Kings 10:1-9,24). Even though the kingdom eventually divided after his death due to his later unfaithfulness, the impact of his wise reign and the temple he built remained central to Israel's identity for centuries (1 Kings 11:11-13, 12:16-20).

## Conclusion

Solomon faced the problem of leading a vast nation with no experience. He felt limited by his youth and the weight of his father's legacy. However, he trusted God and asked for wisdom instead of selfish gain. God was faithful to **His** promise, granting him unmatched discernment, riches, and honor. The miracle of his wisdom transformed Israel into a prosperous and respected kingdom centered on the worship of the LORD in the temple. Solomon sacrificed his own ego and personal ambitions to seek God's guidance. In return, he gained a legacy that defined the golden age of Israel. His story proves that we can trust the LORD to equip us for any task when we prioritize **His** kingdom. He is faithful to give generously to those who seek **Him** with a humble heart.
