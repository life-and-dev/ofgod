# Solomon: a Wise Heart

Solomon, the young son of David, finds himself on the throne of a vast kingdom. When the LORD appears in a dream offering anything he desires, Solomon's humble request sets him on a path to legendary wisdom and the construction of God's dwelling place.

## The Problem of a Usurped Throne

King David was old and advanced in years, unable to even keep himself warm (1 Kings 1:1). His son Adonijah took advantage of this weakness and exalted himself, declaring that he would be king (1 Kings 1:5). Adonijah gathered influential supporters like Joab and Abiathar to secure his claim (1 Kings 1:7). He even began sacrificing animals and celebrating his presumed kingship while David was still alive (1 Kings 1:9,18). Solomon, whom the LORD loved and designated as the successor, was excluded from these festivities (2 Samuel 12:24-25; 1 Kings 1:10; 1 Chronicles 22:9). The nation faced the threat of a civil war and a contested throne (1 Kings 1:21). The political environment was tense with the looming death of the king and the secret schemes of his sons (1 Kings 1:11,21). The problem was that the rightful heir to David's throne was being usurped by a brother who sought power through his own strength and political maneuvering (1 Kings 1:5,13,17).

## Solomon's Humble Condition

Solomon was a young man who had just been anointed king by the order of his father David (1 Kings 1:33,39). He loved the LORD and walked in the statutes of his father (1 Kings 3:3). He went to Gibeon to offer sacrifices, demonstrating his devotion to God (1 Kings 3:4). He offered a thousand burnt offerings on the altar at the great high place (1 Kings 3:4). He did not seek a vision or a specific calling, but was simply worshiping the LORD (1 Kings 3:4-5). It was during the night at Gibeon that the LORD appeared to him in a dream (1 Kings 3:5).

## The Divine Calling

The LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream at Gibeon, offering to grant him whatever he requested.

> At Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night, and God said, “**Ask what I shall give you**.”
>
> — 1 Kings 3:5 (ESV)

Later, after Solomon asked for an understanding mind, the LORD responded with a promise of unparalleled wisdom and wealth.

> And God said to him, “Because you have asked this, and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies, but have asked for yourself understanding to discern what is right, behold, **I now do according to your word. Behold, I give you a wise and discerning mind, so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you. I give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor, so that no other king shall compare with you, all your days.** And if you will walk in My ways, keeping My statutes and My commandments, as your father David walked, then **I will lengthen your days**.”
>
> — 1 Kings 3:11-14 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice

Solomon had to sacrifice his own youthful desires and personal ambitions for the sake of the kingdom (1 Kings 3:7). He acknowledged that he was but a little child who did not know how to go out or come in (1 Kings 3:7). He risked being seen as weak or inadequate by choosing a humble heart over the military might or wealth usually sought by kings (1 Kings 3:9,11). He sacrificed the opportunity to ask for the life of his enemies or personal longevity (1 Kings 3:11).

## Solomon's Initial Response

Solomon did not rebel or refuse the LORD'S offer (1 Kings 3:6). He responded with deep humility and gratitude for the love God had shown his father David (1 Kings 3:6). He admitted his limitations and his dependence on God's guidance (1 Kings 3:7-8). He asked for an understanding mind to govern the people and to discern between good and evil (1 Kings 3:9). He placed the needs of the great people of Israel above his own (1 Kings 3:8-9).

## Solomon's Eventual Reaction

Solomon awoke and realized it was a dream, then he immediately returned to Jerusalem (1 Kings 3:15). He stood before the ark of the covenant of the LORD and offered up more burnt offerings and peace offerings (1 Kings 3:15). He made a feast for all his servants to celebrate the divine encounter (1 Kings 3:15). Solomon began to govern with the wisdom God had granted him (1 Kings 3:16). He demonstrated this wisdom in the famous judgment between two prostitutes over a living child (1 Kings 3:16-28).

He also undertook the massive task of building the house of the LORD in Jerusalem (1 Kings 5:5, 6:1). He drafted thousands of laborers and secured resources from King Hiram of Tyre (1 Kings 5:13, 7:13). Solomon followed all the instructions David had given him for the temple (1 Chronicles 28:11-12; 1 Kings 6:2). He prayed a long and powerful prayer of dedication when the temple was finished (1 Kings 8:22-53). He continued to offer sacrifices three times a year to the LORD (1 Kings 9:25). Despite his later failures with many wives and idols, his initial reaction was one of total dedication to the task God had given him (1 Kings 11:1-4, 9:25). He trusted the promise that God would give him a wise and discerning mind (1 Kings 3:12).

## The Divine Miracle

God performed the miracle of infusing Solomon with supernatural wisdom that exceeded all the people of the east and all the wisdom of Egypt.

> And God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding beyond measure, and breadth of mind like the sand on the seashore, so that Solomon's wisdom surpassed the wisdom of all the people of the east and all the wisdom of Egypt. . . . He also spoke 3,000 proverbs, and his songs were 1,005. . . . And **people of all nations came to hear the wisdom of Solomon, and from all the kings of the earth, who had heard of his wisdom.**
>
> — 1 Kings 4:29-30,32,34 (ESV)

In this miracle, the LORD fulfilled the promise to **give you a wise and discerning mind, so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you** (1 Kings 3:12). God also miraculously filled the temple with His glory during the dedication.

> And when the priests came out of the Holy Place, **a cloud filled the house of the LORD**, so that the priests could not stand to minister because of the cloud, for the glory of the LORD filled the house of the LORD.
>
> — 1 Kings 8:10-11 (ESV)

## The Lasting Result

The miracle resulted in a period of unprecedented peace and prosperity for the nation of Israel (1 Kings 4:20-21, 5:4). Judah and Israel were as many as the sand by the sea while they were eating and drinking and being happy (1 Kings 4:20). Solomon ruled over all the kingdoms from the Euphrates to the land of the Philistines and to the border of Egypt (1 Kings 4:21). His fame spread to all the surrounding nations (1 Kings 4:31). This led the Queen of Sheba to travel from the ends of the earth to test him with hard questions (1 Kings 10:1). She was breathless at the sight of his wisdom and the house he had built (1 Kings 10:4-5).

The building of the temple provided a central place of worship and a permanent dwelling for the Name of the LORD (1 Kings 8:20, 9:3). It strengthened the spiritual identity of the people who returned to their homes joyful and glad of heart (1 Kings 8:66). It also served as a witness to the surrounding nations (1 Kings 8:41-43). The wealth and honor God provided made Solomon the greatest king of his time (1 Kings 10:23).

## Conclusion

Solomon faced the problem of a contested throne and his own feelings of inadequacy as a young leader. He was limited by his youth and the political schemes of his older brother Adonijah. However, the LORD promised to give him a wise heart and unmatched riches if he would walk in His ways. Solomon responded by asking for wisdom to serve God's people rather than seeking personal gain. God intervened by granting him supernatural discernment and filling his kingdom with peace and glory. The result was a golden age for Israel and a temple that stood as a testament to God's presence. Solomon sacrificed the pursuit of personal power, but he gained a legacy of wisdom. His story proves that when we trust the LORD with our responsibilities, He is faithful to provide everything we need.
