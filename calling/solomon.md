# Solomon: the Wise Builder

A young king faces the impossible task of building a dwelling for the Creator. He must choose between his own reputation and the glory of God. His decision to seek divine guidance instead of personal gain sets the stage for a golden age.

## The Problem of an Inexperienced King
Solomon inherited the throne from his father, David, during a time of transition for Israel (1 Kings 2:12). Although David had established the kingdom, he was a man of war and was not permitted to build a house for the LORD (1 Chronicles 22:8). The people were still sacrificing at the high places because no house had yet been built for the name of the LORD (1 Kings 3:2). Solomon loved the LORD, but he was young and inexperienced (1 Kings 3:3,7). He faced the immense pressure of leading a vast nation and fulfilling his father's dream of a permanent temple (1 Kings 3:8-9; 1 Chronicles 22:19). The political environment was relatively peaceful, but the spiritual need for a central place of worship was critical (1 Kings 5:4). The problem was that Solomon lacked the wisdom and the established sacred site to unify the nation's worship under the LORD (1 Kings 3:5-9).

## Solomon's Devout Condition
Solomon showed his devotion to the LORD by going to Gibeon to sacrifice (1 Kings 3:4). He offered a thousand burnt offerings on that altar (1 Kings 3:4). This act of worship and sacrifice preceded God's response (1 Kings 3:5). He was not seeking a "calling" but was fulfilling his duties as king and worshiper (1 Kings 3:3-4). It was in the context of this massive sacrifice that the LORD appeared to him in a dream at night (1 Kings 3:5).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream at night.

> At Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night, and God said, “Ask what I shall give you.” And Solomon said, “You have shown great and steadfast love to Your servant David my father, because he walked before You in faithfulness, in righteousness, and in uprightness of heart toward You. And You have kept for him this great and steadfast love and have given him a son to sit on his throne this day, as it is now. And now, O LORD my God, You have made Your servant king in place of David my father, although I am but a little child. I do not know how to go out or come in. And Your servant is in the midst of Your people whom You have chosen, a great people, too many to be numbered or counted for multitude. Give Your servant therefore an understanding mind to govern Your people, that I may discern between good and evil, for who is able to govern this Your great people?”
>
> It pleased the LORD that Solomon had asked this. And God said to him, “Because you have asked this, and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies, but have asked for yourself understanding to discern what is right, behold, **I now do according to your word. Behold, I give you a wise and discerning mind**, so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you. **I give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor**, so that no other king shall compare with you, all your days. And if you will walk in My ways, keeping My statutes and My commandments, as your father David walked, then **I will lengthen your days**.”
>
> — 1 Kings 3:5-14 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Solomon had to sacrifice his own pride and reliance on his royal status (1 Kings 3:7). He admitted he was like a little child who did not know how to lead (1 Kings 3:7). He risked his reputation as a strong and independent ruler by publicly acknowledging his total dependence on the LORD (1 Kings 3:8-9). He also sacrificed the potential for personal wealth, long life, and the destruction of his enemies by choosing to ask for wisdom instead (1 Kings 3:11).

## Solomon's Initial Response
Solomon did not rebel or refuse the task given to him (1 Kings 3:15). Instead, he responded with humility and a deep sense of his own inadequacy (1 Kings 3:7-8). He acknowledged the LORD'S faithfulness to his father David and his own need for divine guidance (1 Kings 3:6-9). His initial reaction was to ask for help rather than asserting his own power (1 Kings 3:9).

## Solomon's Eventual Reaction
Solomon's reaction was one of immediate and ongoing obedience (1 Kings 3:15). After he awoke from the dream, he returned to Jerusalem and stood before the ark of the covenant of the LORD (1 Kings 3:15). He offered burnt offerings and peace offerings and made a feast for all his servants (1 Kings 3:15). Solomon then began the massive undertaking of building the temple, using the wisdom God had given him to manage resources and foreign relations (1 Kings 5:1-12). He entered into a treaty with Hiram, king of Tyre, to secure the cedar and cypress timber needed for the construction (1 Kings 5:10). He organized a labor force of thirty thousand men and appointed eighty thousand stonecutters in the hill country (1 Kings 5:13-15). Throughout the seven years of construction, Solomon remained focused on the details the LORD had prescribed (1 Kings 6:37-38). He also built his own palace, but the priority was the house for the name of the LORD (1 Kings 7:1; 1 Chronicles 22:19). When the temple was finished, Solomon gathered all the elders of Israel to bring the ark of the covenant into its place (1 Kings 8:1-6). He offered a prayer of dedication that acknowledged the LORD'S greatness and His promise-keeping nature (1 Kings 8:22-53). Solomon's reaction was characterized by a commitment to fulfill the vision his father David had received, trusting that the LORD would provide the wisdom and strength needed for the task (1 Chronicles 28:20). He demonstrated his trust by investing the kingdom's wealth and his own time into a project that was for the glory of God rather than his own fame (1 Kings 9:1).

## The Divine Miracle
God fulfilled His promise by granting Solomon unparalleled wisdom, which was demonstrated in his judgment and the successful completion of the temple.

> And **God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding beyond measure**, and breadth of mind like the sand on the seashore, so that Solomon's wisdom surpassed the wisdom of all the people of the east and all the wisdom of Egypt. . . . And the **LORD gave Solomon wisdom, as He promised him**. . . . Thus **Solomon finished the house of the LORD and the king's house.** All that Solomon had planned to do in the house of the LORD and in his own house **he successfully accomplished.** — 1 Kings 4:29-30, 5:12; 2 Chronicles 7:11 (ESV)

In this miracle, the LORD fulfilled the promise to grant the wisdom, riches, and honor that allowed the temple to be completed (1 Kings 3:12-13). The success of the project was a testament to God's intervention.

## The National Result
The result of the miracle was a period of unprecedented peace and prosperity for Israel (1 Kings 4:20,25). The building of the temple provided a central place of worship that unified the nation and drew people to the LORD (1 Kings 8:60). Solomon's wisdom became world-renowned, attracting rulers like the Queen of Sheba who came to see if the reports were true (1 Kings 10:1-7). She acknowledged that the LORD must love Israel to have given them such a king (1 Kings 10:9). The nation flourished, with silver and gold becoming as common as stones in Jerusalem (2 Chronicles 1:15). The neighboring nations respected Israel and brought tribute, further increasing the kingdom's strength (1 Kings 4:21). The temple stood as a physical witness to God's presence among His people (1 Kings 9:3).

## Conclusion
Solomon faced the problem of leading a vast nation and building a temple without the necessary wisdom or experience. He was limited by his youth and the heavy expectations of his father's legacy. However, the LORD promised to give him a wise and discerning heart. Solomon responded by humbly asking for wisdom and then obediently executing the plan for the temple. God intervened by granting him supernatural understanding and immense wealth. The result was a unified nation, a magnificent house for the LORD, and a kingdom witnessing to the world of God's glory. Solomon sacrificed his self-sufficiency and personal desires for the kingdom. He gained a legacy of wisdom and saw the LORD'S presence fill the temple. His story proves we can trust the LORD to equip us for any task He gives. He is faithful to His promises despite our limitations.
