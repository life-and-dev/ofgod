# Solomon: the King of Wisdom

A young and inexperienced king faces the impossible task of governing a vast and restless nation. When offered anything he desires by the Almighty, his humble choice triggers a supernatural transformation. Witness how one dream changed the course of a kingdom and brought unparalleled peace.

## The Problem of Inadequacy

Solomon was the son of David and Bathsheba during a period of transition in Israel (2 Samuel 12:24; 1 Kings 1:11). Although he was appointed by David, his brother Adonijah had already attempted to seize the throne with the support of powerful military and religious leaders (1 Kings 1:5,7). The kingdom David left behind was vast but still surrounded by potential threats and internal divisions (1 Kings 2:1-9, 5:3). Solomon was young and felt entirely inadequate for the immense responsibility of leading God's chosen people (1 Kings 3:7). No permanent house had been built for the name of the LORD, forcing the people to sacrifice at high places (1 Kings 3:2,5). The problem was that Solomon lacked the wisdom and experience to maintain the throne and provide justice for a multitude of people (1 Kings 3:7-9).

## Solomon's Condition at Gibeon

Solomon showed his love for the LORD by walking in the statutes of his father David (1 Kings 3:3). He traveled to Gibeon because it was the great high place where the tabernacle was located (1 Kings 3:4; 2 Chronicles 1:3). Before God spoke to him, Solomon offered a thousand burnt offerings on the altar to demonstrate his devotion (1 Kings 3:4). He did not seek a vision or a specific sign (1 Kings 3:5). His massive sacrifice and worship caught the attention of the LORD (1 Kings 3:5).

## The Divine Calling

The LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream at night while he was at Gibeon. God invited Solomon to make a request and then responded to his humble plea with a promise of unparalleled wisdom and wealth.

> At Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night, and God said, "**Ask what I shall give you.**"
>
> — 1 Kings 3:5 (ESV)

After Solomon asked for an understanding mind to govern the people, God replied:

> And God said to him, "Because you have asked this, and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies, but have asked for yourself understanding to discern what is right, behold, **I now do according to your word. Behold, I give you a wise and discerning mind, so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you. I give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor**, so that no other king shall compare with you, all your days. And if you will walk in My ways, keeping My statutes and My commandments, as your father David walked, then **I will lengthen your days.**"
>
> — 1 Kings 3:11-14 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice

Solomon sacrificed his own personal ambitions for power and the destruction of his enemies (1 Kings 3:11). He set aside his pride by publicly admitting he was but a "little child" who did not know how to lead (1 Kings 3:7). He risked being seen as weak by his rivals by prioritizing spiritual wisdom over military might (1 Kings 3:9, 5:4). He surrendered his right to seek his own benefit to serve the interests of God's people (1 Kings 3:8). He risked the failure of his kingdom by trusting in God's provision instead of political maneuvering (1 Kings 3:11).

## Solomon's Initial Response

Solomon responded with deep humility and gratitude for God's steadfast love toward David (1 Kings 3:6). He acknowledged that his position as king was a gift from the LORD rather than a result of his own merit (1 Kings 3:7). He expressed his dependence on God by confessing his lack of knowledge (1 Kings 3:7). He did not doubt God's ability to provide but instead asked for the specific tools needed to be faithful to his calling (1 Kings 3:9).

## Solomon's Eventual Reaction

After waking from the dream, Solomon returned to Jerusalem and stood before the ark of the covenant (1 Kings 3:15). He offered burnt offerings and peace offerings and made a feast for all his servants (1 Kings 3:15). He immediately put his new wisdom to the test when two prostitutes came to him with a dispute over a living child (1 Kings 3:16-22). Solomon proposed dividing the baby with a sword to reveal the true mother's compassion (1 Kings 3:24-27). All Israel stood in awe of the king because they saw the wisdom of God in him (1 Kings 3:28). He organized his officials and established a stable administration across the land (1 Kings 4:1-19). Solomon then used his wisdom to negotiate with Hiram the king of Tyre for the resources needed to build the temple (1 Kings 5:1-12). He organized a massive labor force to quarry stones and cut timber (1 Kings 5:13-18). He dedicated the temple with a magnificent prayer that acknowledged God's faithfulness to His promises (1 Kings 8:14-61). His reaction was a consistent pursuit of the task God had set before him (1 Kings 6:37-38).

## The Divine Miracle

The LORD performed a miracle by supernaturally infusing Solomon with wisdom that exceeded all the people of the east and all the wisdom of Egypt. He also granted Israel a period of absolute peace as He had promised.

> And God gave Solomon **wisdom and understanding beyond measure**, and breadth of mind like the sand on the seashore . . . **he was wiser than all other men** . . . and his fame was in all the surrounding nations. . . . "But now the LORD my God has given me rest on every side. **There is neither adversary nor misfortune. And so I intend to build a house for the name of the LORD my God** . . . And the LORD gave Solomon wisdom, **as He promised him**."
>
> — 1 Kings 4:29,31, 5:4-5,12 (ESV)

## The Lasting Result

The miracle resulted in a golden age of prosperity and peace for Israel (1 Kings 4:20). Judah and Israel lived in safety with each man under his vine and under his fig tree (1 Kings 4:25). Solomon's wisdom attracted leaders from all nations who came to hear him and brought gifts of gold and silver (1 Kings 10:23-25). The Queen of Sheba visited and confessed that the reality of his wisdom exceeded all reports (1 Kings 10:6-7). The completion of the temple provided a permanent place for the name of the LORD among His people (1 Kings 8:20). Solomon's fame brought glory to the God of Israel across the known world (2 Chronicles 9:22-24).

## Conclusion

Solomon faced the problem of being an inexperienced king in a divided and threatened nation. He was limited by his youth and his lack of discernment to govern a vast multitude. God promised him a wise mind and added riches and honor because he sought the welfare of the people. Solomon responded with a heart of worship and immediate application of the wisdom he received. God intervened by granting him supernatural intelligence and protecting the borders of Israel from every enemy. The result was a kingdom of unprecedented splendor and a temple that stood as a witness to God's presence. Solomon sacrificed his personal desires for long life and wealth. Yet he gained both in abundance along with the respect of the world. His story proves that when we trust the LORD with our inadequacies, He is faithful to equip us for His purpose.
