# Solomon: the Wise King

A young king is offered anything he desires by the Creator of the universe (1 Kings 3:5). He must choose between personal glory and the heavy burden of leadership (1 Kings 3:9). His decision in the middle of the night would set the course for a nation (1 Kings 3:10-14).

## The Problem of Inexperience

Solomon inherited a kingdom established by his father David (1 Kings 2:12). The people of Israel were a great multitude, too many to be numbered or counted (1 Kings 3:8). Although David had secured the borders, the responsibility of governing such a vast and chosen people was immense (2 Chronicles 1:9). Solomon was young and described himself as but a little child who did not know how to go out or come in (1 Kings 3:7). The political environment required a leader who could discern between good and evil to maintain justice (1 Kings 3:9). Without divine guidance, the young king faced the risk of failing to lead the nation in the ways of the LORD (1 Kings 2:3). The problem was that Solomon lacked the wisdom and experience necessary to govern the great people of Israel effectively (1 Kings 3:7-9).

## The Condition of Devotion

Solomon demonstrated his devotion by loving the LORD and walking in the statutes of David (1 Kings 3:3). He traveled to Gibeon, the great high place, to offer sacrifices (1 Kings 3:4). Before God responded, Solomon offered a thousand burnt offerings on the altar at Gibeon (2 Chronicles 1:6). He sought the LORD through worship and massive sacrifice rather than waiting for a sign (1 Kings 3:4). It was during that night, following his act of worship, that God appeared to him in a dream (2 Chronicles 1:7; 1 Kings 3:5).

## The Divine Calling

God communicated with Solomon through a dream in the night at Gibeon (1 Kings 3:5).

> "In that night God appeared to Solomon, and said to him, '**Ask what I shall give you**.'" — 2 Chronicles 1:7 (ESV)

Later in the same dream, the LORD confirmed His response to the request of Solomon.

> "And God said to him, 'Because you have asked this, and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies, but have asked for yourself understanding to discern what is right, behold, **I now do according to your word**. Behold, **I give you a wise and discerning mind**, so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you. **I give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor**, so that no other king shall compare with you, all your days. And if you will walk in My ways, keeping My statutes and My commandments, as your father David walked, then **I will lengthen your days**.'" — 1 Kings 3:11-14 (ESV)

## The Sacrifice of Personal Gain

Solomon sacrificed a thousand burnt offerings to demonstrate his loyalty to the LORD (1 Kings 3:4). He risked the stability of his new reign by admitting his weakness and total dependence on God (1 Kings 3:7). He gave up the opportunity to ask for personal gain, such as long life, riches, or the destruction of his enemies (1 Kings 3:11). By prioritizing the needs of the people over his own desires, he risked his personal future for the sake of the kingdom (1 Kings 3:9).

## The Initial Response of Humility

Solomon responded to God's presence with deep humility and gratitude (1 Kings 3:6). He acknowledged the steadfast love God had shown to his father David (2 Chronicles 1:8). He did not presume to be worthy of the throne but saw himself as a servant in the midst of a chosen people (1 Kings 3:8). Solomon recognized his own limitations and did not try to hide his lack of experience from the LORD (1 Kings 3:7). He simply asked for what he needed to serve God faithfully (2 Chronicles 1:10).

## The Reaction of Faithful Service

Solomon eventually responded to God's calling by applying the wisdom he received to every aspect of his leadership (1 Kings 4:29). He returned to Jerusalem and stood before the ark of the covenant to offer more sacrifices and celebrate with his servants (1 Kings 3:15). He used his discerning mind to bring justice to the people, as demonstrated by his famous ruling between two mothers (1 Kings 3:16-28). Solomon also undertook the massive task of building the first temple for the LORD, fulfilling the desire of his father David (1 Kings 5:5). He organized the labor and resources of the nation to create a house for the name of the LORD (2 Chronicles 2:1). Throughout his early reign, he continued to seek God's guidance and walk in His ways (1 Kings 3:14). He trusted that the LORD would provide the resources and peace needed to complete the temple (1 Kings 5:4). Solomon dedicated the temple with a prayer of humble petition, asking that God's eyes would be open toward the house night and day (1 Kings 8:29). His reaction was one of active obedience and reliance on the gift of wisdom he had received (1 Kings 4:29).

## The Miracle of Divine Wisdom

The LORD gave Solomon wisdom and wealth exactly as He had promised in the dream (1 Kings 4:29-30). In this miracle, the LORD fulfilled the promise that I now do according to your word by giving him a heart that could discern justice (1 Kings 3:12). He also provided the riches and honor that Solomon had not even requested (1 Kings 3:13).

> "And God gave Solomon **wisdom and understanding beyond measure**, and breadth of mind like the sand on the seashore, so that Solomon's wisdom surpassed the wisdom of all the people of the east and all the wisdom of Egypt . . . and the LORD gave Solomon wisdom, as He promised him . . . and King Solomon **excelled all the kings of the earth in riches and in wisdom**." — 1 Kings 4:29-30, 5:12, 10:23 (ESV)

The LORD fulfilled the promise to give you a wise and discerning mind and to provide both riches and honor (1 Kings 3:12-13).

## The Result of Global Recognition

The miracle resulted in a golden age of peace and prosperity for the nation of Israel (1 Kings 4:20). Judah and Israel were as numerous as the sand by the sea, and they ate and drank and were happy (1 Kings 4:20). Solomon's wisdom became famous throughout the world, and people from all nations came to hear him (1 Kings 4:34). He spoke three thousand proverbs and wrote one thousand and five songs (1 Kings 4:32). His neighbors, such as Hiram the king of Tyre, rejoiced in the wisdom God had given him and assisted in the building of the temple (1 Kings 5:7). The Queen of Sheba visited him and was breathless at the wisdom and prosperity she witnessed (1 Kings 10:4-5). The result was a nation that stood as a witness to the greatness and faithfulness of the LORD (1 Kings 10:9).

## Conclusion

Solomon faced the daunting problem of leading a massive nation with the heart of a child. He was limited by his youth and the weight of his father's legacy. However, the LORD promised to give him wisdom and even things he did not ask for. Solomon responded by humbly seeking the LORD and prioritizing the governance of God's people over his own wealth. God intervened by miraculously granting him a discerning mind and global influence. The result was a golden age of peace and the completion of the holy temple. Solomon sacrificed his personal desires and a thousand offerings, but he gained a legacy of wisdom that remains unmatched. His story proves that when we trust the LORD'S faithfulness, He provides everything we need and more.
