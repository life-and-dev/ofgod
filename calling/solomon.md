# Solomon: the Gift of Wisdom

A young king is offered anything he desires by the Creator of the universe. Instead of wealth or power, he asks for something that will define his legacy. Discover how Solomon's request for wisdom led to an era of unprecedented peace and prosperity.

## The Problem of Inexperience
Solomon's rise to power was marked by political instability and family conflict. His father, King David, was old and frail (1 Kings 1:1). David's eldest surviving son, Adonijah, attempted to seize the throne by force (1 Kings 1:5). This created a crisis for Solomon and his mother, Bathsheba (1 Kings 1:11-13). David eventually intervened and had Solomon anointed as king (1 Kings 1:32-35,39). However, Solomon was very young and inexperienced (1 Kings 3:7). He was tasked with leading a vast and numerous people whom God had chosen (1 Kings 3:8-9). The people were still sacrificing at high places because no house had yet been built for the name of the LORD (1 Kings 3:2). Solomon faced the daunting challenge of governing a great nation while lacking the wisdom to discern between good and evil (1 Kings 3:9). The problem was that Solomon was a "little child" in experience who needed divine guidance to rule Israel effectively (1 Kings 3:7).

## Solomon's Sacrifice at Gibeon
Solomon loved the LORD and walked in the statutes of his father David (1 Kings 3:3). He went to Gibeon, the great high place, to offer sacrifices to God (1 Kings 3:4). Solomon offered a thousand burnt offerings on that altar (1 Kings 3:4). He did this special act of worship and sacrifice before the LORD responded to him (1 Kings 3:4-5). It was during the night at Gibeon that the LORD appeared to him in a dream (1 Kings 3:5).

## The Divine Calling
God spoke directly to Solomon in a dream by night.

> At Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night, and God said, "**Ask what I shall give you.**" — 1 Kings 3:5 (ESV)

Later, after Solomon asked for an understanding mind, God responded:

> And God said to him, "Because you have asked this, and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies, but have asked for yourself understanding to discern what is right, behold, **I now do according to your word.** Behold, **I give you a wise and discerning mind**, so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you. **I give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor**, so that no other king shall compare with you, all your days. And if you will walk in **My** ways, keeping **My** statutes and **My** commandments, as your father David walked, then **I will lengthen your days.**" — 1 Kings 3:11-14 (ESV)

## The Choice of Wisdom
Solomon had to sacrifice his own personal desires for the sake of his people. He could have asked for long life, great wealth, or the destruction of his political enemies (1 Kings 3:11). By choosing wisdom, he risked being perceived as weak or lacking in ambition by his rivals. He sacrificed his right to pursue his own glory in order to seek the welfare of the nation (1 Kings 3:9). He placed his trust entirely in God's provision rather than in his own political or military strength (1 Kings 3:3,9).

## Solomon's Initial Response
Solomon did not rebel or refuse God's offer. Instead, he responded with humility and gratitude (1 Kings 3:6). He acknowledged his own limitations, describing himself as a "little child" who did not know how to "go out or come in" (1 Kings 3:7). He recognized that his position was a result of God's steadfast love for his father David (1 Kings 3:6). His only request was for an understanding mind to govern God's people (1 Kings 3:9).

## Solomon's Reign of Discernment
Solomon responded to God's calling by dedicating his life to the pursuit of justice and the construction of the temple. After awakening from his dream, he returned to Jerusalem and stood before the ark of the covenant (1 Kings 3:15). He offered up burnt offerings and peace offerings and made a feast for all his servants (1 Kings 3:15). Solomon demonstrated his trust in God's gift of wisdom by ruling with fairness and discernment (1 Kings 3:28). He used his God-given resources to build a magnificent house for the name of the LORD, fulfilling the promise made to his father David (1 Kings 5:5, 6:1). Solomon also organized a vast administration to manage the affairs of Israel, ensuring peace and security for the people (1 Kings 4:1,20). He composed thousands of proverbs and songs, sharing his divine insights with others (1 Kings 4:32). He welcomed people from all nations who came to hear the wisdom that God had put into his heart (1 Kings 4:34, 10:24). Solomon remained faithful to God's laws for many years, leading the nation in worship and obedience (1 Kings 8:14-21). He recognized that his success was entirely dependent on the LORD'S favor (1 Kings 8:15).

## The Miracle of Wisdom
God fulfilled His promise to Solomon by granting him supernatural wisdom and prosperity that exceeded all other kings of the earth.

> And **God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding beyond measure, and breadth of mind** like the sand on the seashore, so that Solomon's wisdom surpassed the wisdom of all the people of the east and all the wisdom of Egypt. . . and **his fame was in all the surrounding nations.** — 1 Kings 4:29-31 (ESV)

The miracle was also evident in how the people perceived his authority.

> And all Israel heard of the judgment that the king had rendered, and they stood in awe of the king, because they perceived that **the wisdom of God was in him to do justice.** — 1 Kings 3:28 (ESV)

## The Result of Prosperity
The miracle resulted in a period of unprecedented peace and prosperity for Israel. Judah and Israel were as many as the sand by the sea; they ate and drank and were happy (1 Kings 4:20). Solomon ruled over all the kingdoms from the Euphrates to the land of the Philistines and to the border of Egypt (1 Kings 4:21). The neighboring nations brought tribute and served Solomon all the days of his life (1 Kings 4:21). There was peace on all sides around him (1 Kings 4:24). Every man sat under his vine and under his fig tree, in safety (1 Kings 4:25). The reputation of Israel grew, and foreign rulers like the Queen of Sheba visited to witness Solomon's wisdom and wealth (1 Kings 10:1-7). The completion of the temple provided a permanent dwelling place for the name of the LORD (1 Kings 8:10-11). Solomon's family and servants were blessed by the abundance and order of his kingdom (1 Kings 10:8).

## Conclusion
Solomon began his reign facing the problem of political instability and his own inexperience. He was limited by his youth and the weight of governing a vast nation. Despite these challenges and the threats to his throne, he chose to trust God by asking for wisdom instead of personal gain. God was faithful to His promise and gave Solomon a discerning mind, as well as riches and honor that surpassed any other king. The result was a golden age of peace and justice for all Israel. Solomon sacrificed his own selfish ambitions for the good of his people. In return, he gained a legacy of wisdom and a kingdom of extraordinary wealth and security. His story proves that when we seek God's righteousness first, He is faithful to provide everything else we need.
