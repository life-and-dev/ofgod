# Solomon: the Wise King

Young and inexperienced, a new king faced the impossible task of governing a vast and restless nation. He was offered anything he desired by the Almighty. His choice would define the golden age of his people and leave a legacy of wisdom that still echoes today.

## The Problem of a Divided Kingdom
King David was old and advanced in years (1 Kings 1:1). His son Adonijah took advantage of this and declared himself king (1 Kings 1:5). Adonijah prepared chariots and horsemen to support his claim (1 Kings 1:5). He gained the support of powerful leaders like Joab and Abiathar the priest (1 Kings 1:7). Solomon was the chosen successor but was not invited to Adonijah's feast (1 Kings 1:10,13). Nathan the prophet and Bathsheba had to act quickly to save their lives and Solomon's throne (1 Kings 1:11-12). David eventually confirmed Solomon's appointment and had him anointed at Gihon (1 Kings 1:30,34). Despite being king, Solomon felt like a little child who did not know how to lead (1 Kings 3:7). He was responsible for a people too numerous to be counted (1 Kings 3:8). The problem Solomon faced was a high-stakes succession threat and his own overwhelming inexperience in ruling a vast nation (1 Kings 1:5, 3:7-8).

## Solomon's Condition at Gibeon
Solomon loved the LORD and walked in the statutes of David (1 Kings 3:3). He went to Gibeon to sacrifice a thousand burnt offerings on the altar (1 Kings 3:4). This act of worship was a significant demonstration of his commitment to God (1 Kings 3:3-4). Solomon did not ask for anything until the LORD appeared to him in a dream (1 Kings 3:5). His devotion through massive sacrifice preceded the divine encounter (1 Kings 3:4-5).

## The Heavenly Calling
The LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream at Gibeon and initiated a dialogue.

> At Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night, and God said, “**Ask what I shall give you.**” . . . And God said to him, “Because you have asked this, and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies, but have asked for yourself understanding to discern what is right, behold, **I now do according to your word.** Behold, **I give you a wise and discerning mind**, so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you. **I give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor**, so that no other king shall compare with you, all your days. And if you will walk in my ways, keeping my statutes and my commandments, as your father David walked, then **I will lengthen your days.**”
>
> — 1 Kings 3:5,11-14 (ESV)

## The Sacrifice of Personal Ambition
Solomon had to sacrifice his own personal desires for wealth, long life, and the death of his enemies (1 Kings 3:11). He could have asked for military power or personal glory to secure his kingdom (1 Kings 3:11). Instead, he sacrificed his own agenda to seek what was best for the people of God (1 Kings 3:9). He risked his status by admitting his weakness and total dependence on the LORD (1 Kings 3:7).

## Humility As an Initial Response
Solomon responded to the LORD with profound humility (1 Kings 3:7). He acknowledged his own limitations by calling himself a little child (1 Kings 3:7). He did not boast of his status as David's son or his new position as king (1 Kings 3:6-7). He expressed his inability to lead the people effectively without divine help (1 Kings 3:7-8). He focused on the greatness of God's chosen people rather than his own potential for greatness (1 Kings 3:8).

## Solomon's Reaction to the Call
Solomon eventually responded by asking for an understanding mind to govern the people (1 Kings 3:9). He sought the ability to discern between good and evil for the sake of justice (1 Kings 3:9). After the dream, Solomon stood before the ark of the covenant in Jerusalem (1 Kings 3:15). He offered up more burnt offerings and peace offerings to the LORD (1 Kings 3:15). He made a feast for all his servants in celebration of God's promise (1 Kings 3:15). Solomon immediately applied his new wisdom by resolving a difficult dispute between two women (1 Kings 3:16-28). He also fulfilled the calling to build a house for the name of the LORD (1 Kings 5:5). He gathered materials from Hiram, king of Tyre, and organized thousands of workers (1 Kings 5:6,13). He dedicated seven years to the construction of the Temple (1 Kings 6:38). His reaction was one of active obedience and continued worship of the LORD (1 Kings 3:15, 8:62).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD fulfilled His promise by granting Solomon unprecedented wisdom and wealth.

> And God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding beyond measure, and breadth of mind like the sand on the seashore, so that **Solomon's wisdom surpassed the wisdom of all the people of the east and all the wisdom of Egypt.** For he was **wiser than all other men** . . . and his fame was in all the surrounding nations. . . . And **all the kings of the earth** . . . **heard of his wisdom.**
>
> — 1 Kings 4:29-31,34 (ESV)

In this miracle, God fulfilled the promise to **give you a wise and discerning mind** and to give **riches and honor** such that no other king would compare (1 Kings 3:12-13). The LORD also granted Solomon peace on all sides (1 Kings 4:24). He transformed a fearful young man into the most renowned ruler of his time (1 Kings 4:31).

## The Result of Divine Wisdom
The miracle of divine wisdom resulted in a period of unparalleled peace and prosperity for Israel (1 Kings 4:20). Judah and Israel lived in safety, and every man lived under his own vine and fig tree (1 Kings 4:25). The nation became a center of wisdom that attracted people from all nations (1 Kings 4:34). Solomon's reign saw the completion of the first Temple, which became the dwelling place of the LORD's name (1 Kings 8:20). The neighboring kings brought tribute and served Solomon throughout his life (1 Kings 4:21). His wisdom settled complex legal issues and provided a stable foundation for the growing kingdom (1 Kings 3:28). The legacy of his songs and proverbs continued to instruct generations (1 Kings 4:32).

## Conclusion
Solomon faced the daunting problem of a succession crisis and his own profound inexperience in leading a massive nation. He was limited by his young age and his feeling of being like a little child. However, the LORD promised him an understanding mind, along with riches and honor. Solomon responded with humility and a request for wisdom to serve God's people. God intervened by granting him measureless understanding and national peace. The result was a golden age for Israel and the successful construction of the Temple. Solomon had to sacrifice his personal ambition and admit his weakness, but he gained the wisdom to lead a great people and a reputation that lasted for millennia. His story shows that when we trust the LORD with our limitations, He is faithful to provide more than we could ever ask.
