# Solomon: the Wise King

A young man inherits a kingdom but feels like a helpless child. He is given the chance to ask for anything in the world. His choice will determine the fate of his people and reveal the source of true greatness.

## The Problem of an Inexperienced Ruler
Solomon became king after his father David (1 Kings 2:12). The nation of Israel was a great people who were too many to be numbered (1 Kings 3:8). Solomon faced the daunting task of ruling this vast multitude in a politically complex environment (1 Kings 3:1). He had made a marriage alliance with Pharaoh king of Egypt to secure his borders (1 Kings 3:1). The people were still sacrificing at the high places because the temple had not yet been built (1 Kings 3:2). Solomon felt inadequate for the responsibility placed upon him (1 Kings 3:7). He described himself as but a little child who did not know how to go out or come in (1 Kings 3:7). He needed to discern between good and evil to govern the people fairly (1 Kings 3:9). The problem was that Solomon lacked the experience and discernment to govern the great nation he had inherited (1 Kings 3:7-9).

## Solomon's Devout Condition
Solomon showed his devotion by walking in the statutes of his father David (1 Kings 3:3). He went to Gibeon, which was the great high place, to offer sacrifices (1 Kings 3:4). He did something special to get God's attention by offering a thousand burnt offerings on that altar (1 Kings 3:4). It was during the night at Gibeon that the LORD appeared to him (1 Kings 3:5). He was in a state of worship and humility when the LORD responded to him (1 Kings 3:4-5).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream to offer him a choice that would define his reign. God promised to grant his request and provide beyond what he asked.

> At Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night, and God said, "**Ask what I shall give you.**"
>
> — 1 Kings 3:5 (ESV)

Solomon asked for wisdom, and God responded:

> And God said to him, "Because you have asked this, and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies, but have asked for yourself understanding to discern what is right, behold, **I now do according to your word**. Behold, **I give you a wise and discerning mind**, so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you. **I give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor**, so that no other king shall compare with you, all your days. And if you will walk in My ways, keeping My statutes and My commandments, as your father David walked, then **I will lengthen your days**."
>
> — 1 Kings 3:11-14 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Solomon sacrificed his own desires for personal gain or power (1 Kings 3:11). He could have asked for long life, great riches, or the death of his enemies (1 Kings 3:11). Instead, he chose to prioritize the needs of the people over his own selfish ambitions (1 Kings 3:9). He risked appearing weak to his enemies by admitting his lack of knowledge (1 Kings 3:7). He sacrificed the pursuit of earthly glory to seek divine wisdom (1 Kings 3:11).

## Solomon's Initial Response
Solomon did not rebel or refuse the LORD'S offer (1 Kings 3:6). He responded with deep humility and gratitude for the kindness shown to his father David (1 Kings 3:6). He acknowledged his own limitations and his dependence on God's grace (1 Kings 3:7-8). His only "refusal" was to seek his own benefit (1 Kings 3:9-11). He immediately recognized the weight of his calling and the greatness of the people he was to serve (1 Kings 3:8-9).

## Solomon's Faithful Reaction
Solomon's reaction was one of immediate petition followed by worship (1 Kings 3:9,15). He asked the LORD for an understanding mind to govern the people and to discern between good and evil (1 Kings 3:9). When he awoke from his dream, he went to Jerusalem and stood before the ark of the covenant of the LORD (1 Kings 3:15). He offered up burnt offerings and peace offerings and made a feast for all his servants (1 Kings 3:15). Solomon demonstrated his trust in the promised wisdom when two prostitutes came before him with a difficult case (1 Kings 3:16). One woman's son had died, and she had swapped him for the living son of the other woman (1 Kings 3:19-20). The case seemed impossible to solve as there were no witnesses (1 Kings 3:18). Solomon proposed to divide the living child in two with a sword (1 Kings 3:24-25). He trusted that the true mother's compassion would reveal her identity (1 Kings 3:26). When the real mother begged to save the child, Solomon knew she was the mother (1 Kings 3:26-27). He then ordered the living child to be given to her (1 Kings 3:27). His reaction showed that he relied on the wisdom God had promised rather than human legalism (1 Kings 3:28). He continued to build the house of the LORD and his own palace as planned (1 Kings 6, 7). He organized the kingdom with great skill and provided peace for the nation (1 Kings 4:20-25). Solomon's life became a testament to the fact that true leadership begins with a heart yielded to God (1 Kings 3:3, 4:29).

## The Divine Miracle
God gave Solomon the promised wisdom, which was immediately demonstrated through a righteous judgment (1 Kings 3:12,16-28).

> Then the king answered and said, "Give the living child to the first woman, and by no means put him to death; she is his mother." And all Israel heard of the judgment that the king had rendered, and they stood in awe of the king, because they perceived that **the wisdom of God was in him to do justice**.
>
> — 1 Kings 3:27-28 (ESV)

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the restoration of a child to his mother and the establishment of Solomon's reputation (1 Kings 3:27-28). All Israel stood in awe of the king because they saw that the wisdom of God was in him (1 Kings 3:28). This wisdom brought peace and prosperity to the entire nation (1 Kings 4:20,25). Judah and Israel were as many as the sand by the sea, eating and drinking and being happy (1 Kings 4:20). Solomon ruled over all the kingdoms from the Euphrates to the land of the Philistines (1 Kings 4:21). His wisdom surpassed the wisdom of all the people of the east and all the wisdom of Egypt (1 Kings 4:30). People from all nations came to hear the wisdom of Solomon, and kings from all over the earth sent representatives to him (1 Kings 4:34). The result was a golden age for Israel where the LORD was glorified through the wise rule of His servant (2 Chronicles 9:22-24).

## Conclusion
Solomon faced the problem of ruling a vast nation while feeling like an inexperienced child. He was limited by his youth and the immense responsibility of his position. However, the LORD promised to give him a wise and discerning mind because he chose wisdom over riches. Solomon responded by worshiping God and trusting the divine gift to solve impossible cases. God's intervention was seen in the miraculous discernment that astonished the nation. The result was a kingdom of unprecedented peace, wealth, and international respect. Solomon sacrificed his opportunity for selfish gain, and in return, he gained world-renowned wisdom, immense riches, and the honor of building God's temple. His story demonstrates that when we seek God's kingdom first, He is faithful to provide everything else we need. We can trust the LORD to equip us for any task He assigns.
