# Solomon: The Gift of Wisdom

A young king faces the impossible task of governing a vast and numerous people. He is offered a blank check by the Almighty: "Ask what I shall give you." His choice would redefine the meaning of wealth and wisdom for all of history.

## The Problem of Inexperience

Solomon the son of David established himself in his kingdom (2 Chronicles 1:1). The LORD his God was with him and made him exceedingly great (2 Chronicles 1:1). However, he was a young man following the legendary reign of his father David (1 Kings 3:6-7). He described himself as but a little child who did not know how to go out or come in (1 Kings 3:7). He was in the midst of a people whom God had chosen, a great people too many to be numbered or counted for multitude (1 Kings 3:8). The political environment involved a marriage alliance with Pharaoh king of Egypt to secure his borders (1 Kings 3:1). No house had yet been built for the name of the LORD, so the people were sacrificing at the high places (1 Kings 3:2). The problem was that Solomon lacked the personal experience and discernment required to govern such a massive and chosen nation (1 Kings 3:8-9).

## Solomon's Sacrificial Condition

Solomon loved the LORD and walked in the statutes of David his father (1 Kings 3:3). He went to Gibeon to sacrifice there because it was the great high place (1 Kings 3:4). The tent of meeting which Moses had made was there (2 Chronicles 1:3). Solomon offered a thousand burnt offerings on that altar to seek the LORD (1 Kings 3:4; 2 Chronicles 1:6). In that night, God appeared to him in a dream (1 Kings 3:5; 2 Chronicles 1:7). He did not seek a vision for his own glory (1 Kings 3:3).

## The Divine Calling

At Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night to offer him a gift (1 Kings 3:5).

> And God said, "Ask what **I shall give you**."
>
> And Solomon said, "**You** have shown great and steadfast love to **Your** servant David my father, because he walked before **You** in faithfulness, in righteousness, and in uprightness of heart toward **You**. And **You** have kept for him this great and steadfast love and have given him a son to sit on his throne this day. And now, O LORD my God, **You** have made **Your** servant king in place of David my father, although I am but a little child. I do not know how to go out or come in. And **Your** servant is in the midst of **Your** people whom **You** have chosen, a great people, too many to be numbered or counted for multitude. Give **Your** servant therefore an understanding mind to govern **Your** people, that I may discern between good and evil, for who is able to govern this **Your** great people?"
>
> It pleased the LORD that Solomon had asked this. And God said to him, "Because you have asked this, and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies, but have asked for yourself understanding to discern what is right, behold, **I now do according to your word**. Behold, **I give you a wise and discerning mind**, so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you. **I give you also what you have not asked**, both riches and honor, so that no other king shall compare with you, all your days. And if you will walk in **My** ways, keeping **My** statutes and **My** commandments, as your father David walked, then **I will lengthen your days**."
>
>  — 1 Kings 3:5-14 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice

Solomon sacrificed his own selfish desires instead of asking for long life, riches, or the death of his enemies (1 Kings 3:11). He sacrificed his personal ambition to prioritize the needs of God's people (1 Kings 3:9). He humbled himself by admitting his own limitations as a "little child" before the Almighty (1 Kings 3:7). He gave a massive offering of a thousand animals to demonstrate his devotion (1 Kings 3:4). He risked being seen as weak by neighboring nations by admitting his lack of knowledge (1 Kings 3:7-8).

## The Initial Response

Solomon did not rebel or refuse the call (1 Kings 3:6-9). He responded with profound humility and a request for help (1 Kings 3:7-9). He acknowledged God's steadfast love to his father David (1 Kings 3:6). He recognized that it was the LORD Who had made him king (1 Kings 3:7). He did not ask for power to dominate others (1 Kings 3:9). His initial response was one of total dependence on God for the wisdom he lacked (2 Chronicles 1:10).

## Solomon's Wisdom in Action

Solomon awoke and realized it was a dream (1 Kings 3:15). He did not doubt the encounter but immediately returned to Jerusalem (1 Kings 3:15). He stood before the ark of the covenant of the LORD (1 Kings 3:15). He offered up burnt offerings and peace offerings and made a feast for all his servants (1 Kings 3:15). He began to exercise the wisdom God had granted him (1 Kings 3:16). When two women brought a difficult dispute about a child, Solomon used his discernment to reveal the true mother (1 Kings 3:16-28). He organized his kingdom with great efficiency (1 Kings 4:1). He began the massive project of building the temple for the LORD (1 Kings 6:1). Solomon also spoke three thousand proverbs and his songs were one thousand and five (1 Kings 4:32). He studied and spoke of trees, beasts, birds, reptiles, and fish (1 Kings 4:33). He recognized that his wisdom was a gift to be used for the glory of God and the benefit of the nation (1 Kings 10:9). Solomon's reaction was to walk in the wisdom he received while managing the unprecedented riches God provided (1 Kings 10:14,23).

## The Miracle of an Understanding Mind

God performed a miracle of the mind by granting Solomon supernatural intelligence and insight (1 Kings 4:29).

> And **God gave Solomon wisdom and understanding beyond measure**, and breadth of mind like the sand on the seashore, so that **Solomon's wisdom surpassed the wisdom of all the people of the east and all the wisdom of Egypt**. For he was wiser than all other men . . . and his fame was in all the surrounding nations. . . . And people of all nations came to hear the wisdom of Solomon, and from all the kings of the earth, who had heard of his wisdom.
>
>  — 1 Kings 4:29-31,34 (ESV)

The LORD fulfilled **His** promise to **give you a wise and discerning mind** and to **give you also what you have not asked** (1 Kings 3:12-13). Solomon became the wealthiest and wisest king in the world (1 Kings 10:23).

## The Golden Age of Israel

The result of the miracle was a golden age of peace and prosperity for Israel (1 Kings 4:20). Judah and Israel were as many as the sand by the sea; they ate and drank and were happy (1 Kings 4:20). Solomon ruled over all the kingdoms from the Euphrates to the land of the Philistines (1 Kings 4:21). People from all nations and all the kings of the earth came to hear his wisdom (1 Kings 4:34). The Queen of Sheba visited and was breathless at the wisdom and prosperity she witnessed (1 Kings 10:1-5). She praised the LORD for **His** love for Israel in setting Solomon on the throne (1 Kings 10:9). The temple was completed, providing a permanent place for the name of the LORD (1 Kings 6:38). The nation experienced a time of rest from all their enemies (1 Kings 5:4).

## Conclusion

Solomon faced the problem of being an inexperienced "child" tasked with leading a vast nation. He was limited by his youth and the heavy responsibility of his father's legacy. However, the LORD promised him unmatched wisdom and riches because Solomon sought the good of others rather than himself. Solomon responded by offering sacrifices and governing with the discernment he received. God intervened by supernaturally expanding his mind and his kingdom. The result was a period of unprecedented joy and international fame for Israel. Solomon sacrificed his personal requests for power or long life, but he gained a reputation that has lasted for millennia. This story proves that when we trust the LORD for what we need to serve **Him**, **He** provides far beyond what we can imagine. **He** is faithful to those who seek **His** heart.
