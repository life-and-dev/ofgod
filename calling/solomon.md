# Solomon: the Gift of Wisdom

A young king faces the impossible task of ruling a vast nation. In a mysterious dream, the LORD offers him anything he desires. His unexpected choice at Gibeon would lead to a reign of unprecedented peace and unparalleled insight.

## The Problem of a Great People

Solomon became king after his father David (1 Kings 2:12). The transition was marked by internal strife and the threat of rebellion from his brother Adonijah (1 Kings 1:5, 2:13-25). David's final instructions emphasized the need for wisdom to deal with powerful enemies like Joab and Shimei (1 Kings 2:5-9). Solomon felt the weight of his father's legacy and the complexity of governing a people as numerous as the sand (1 Kings 3:8, 4:20). He described himself as a "little child" who did not know how to "go out or come in" (1 Kings 3:7). The political environment required a firm hand to establish the kingdom (1 Kings 2:46). The people were still sacrificing at high places because the temple had not yet been built (1 Kings 3:2). The problem was that the young king lacked the maturity and discernment necessary to lead God's chosen people effectively (1 Kings 3:7-9).

## Solomon's Humble Condition

Solomon showed his love for the LORD by walking in the statutes of his father David (1 Kings 3:3). He went to the great high place at Gibeon to offer worship (1 Kings 3:4). Before God responded, Solomon offered a thousand burnt offerings on the altar at Gibeon (1 Kings 3:4). It was during this time of significant sacrifice and public devotion that the LORD chose to interact with him (1 Kings 3:5). God appeared to him in a dream by night to test the desires of his heart (1 Kings 3:5). He did not seek out a vision, but his faithful worship prepared him to hear the divine voice (1 Kings 3:3-5).

## The Divine Calling

The LORD spoke to Solomon in a dream to establish his heart and offer him a unique opportunity. God communicated directly through a vision while the king slept at the great high place.

> At Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night, and God said, "**Ask what I shall give you.**"
>
> — 1 Kings 3:5 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice

Solomon sacrificed a thousand burnt offerings to the LORD at the great high place (1 Kings 3:4). Beyond these animals, he sacrificed his own personal ambition for power, wealth, or the death of his enemies (1 Kings 3:11). He risked being seen as weak by his political rivals by admitting his inadequacy (1 Kings 3:7). He surrendered his right to ask for his own benefit in favor of the needs of the nation (1 Kings 3:9). He prioritized the spiritual well-being of the people over his own comfort or long life (1 Kings 3:11).

## The Initial Response

Solomon did not rebel or refuse the LORD'S offer (1 Kings 3:6). Instead, he responded with deep humility and gratitude for God's steadfast love toward David (1 Kings 3:6). He acknowledged his own limitations and his dependence on God for success (1 Kings 3:7-8). He did not seek his own glory but rather the ability to serve the people of Israel (1 Kings 3:9). His heart was focused on the magnitude of the task and the greatness of the people he was called to govern (1 Kings 3:8-9).

## Solomon's Heartfelt Reaction

Solomon asked for an understanding mind to govern the people (1 Kings 3:9). He requested the ability to discern between good and evil so that he could do justice (1 Kings 3:9). This request pleased the LORD because Solomon did not ask for long life or riches (1 Kings 3:10-11). He accepted the responsibility of the throne as a servant of the LORD (1 Kings 3:7-9). After the dream, he went to Jerusalem and stood before the ark of the covenant (1 Kings 3:15). There he offered up burnt offerings and peace offerings and made a feast for all his servants (1 Kings 3:15). He demonstrated his commitment to the LORD through continued worship and leadership (1 Kings 3:15-28). His response was one of total reliance on divine wisdom rather than human strategy (1 Kings 3:9). He showed that he valued the spiritual heritage of his father David more than any earthly treasure (1 Kings 3:6-7).

## The Divine Miracle

God gave Solomon what he asked for and much more. He transformed a young, inexperienced man into the wisest king the world had ever seen.

> And God said to him, "Because you have asked this, and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies, but have asked for yourself understanding to discern what is right, behold, **I now do according to your word.** Behold, **I give you a wise and discerning mind**, so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you. **I give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor**, so that no other king shall compare with you, all your days."
>
> — 1 Kings 3:11-13 (ESV)

## The Prosperous Result

The miracle resulted in the famous judgment between two prostitutes, proving that the wisdom of God was in him (1 Kings 3:16-28). All Israel stood in awe of the king because of his discernment (1 Kings 3:28). God gave Solomon breadth of mind like the sand on the seashore (1 Kings 4:29). His wisdom surpassed the wisdom of all the people of the east and all the wisdom of Egypt (1 Kings 4:30). He spoke 3,000 proverbs and 1,005 songs (1 Kings 4:32). The nation lived in safety and prosperity, with every man under his vine and fig tree (1 Kings 4:20,25). People from all nations came to hear his wisdom (1 Kings 4:34). His reign became a shadow of the perfect peace that would come through the Messiah (Matthew 12:42).

## Conclusion

Solomon faced the problem of leading a vast nation without the necessary experience or insight. He was limited by his youth and the political instability of a new reign. However, when the LORD invited him to ask for anything, he chose to trust God for wisdom rather than seeking personal gain. God's promise to grant him a wise heart was miraculously fulfilled and accompanied by riches and honor. Solomon sacrificed his selfish desires and animal offerings, but he gained the respect of his people and a legacy of divine wisdom. His story demonstrates that we can trust the LORD to equip us for any task when we seek His righteousness first. He is faithful to provide more than we could ever ask or imagine.
