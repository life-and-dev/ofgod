# Solomon: Request for Wisdom

A young king stands at the threshold of a vast empire. He feels the weight of a legacy he cannot carry alone. In a dream, God offers him anything he desires. His choice will define his reign and reveal the source of true leadership.

## The Problem of an Inherited Kingdom
Solomon became king after the death of his father David (1 Kings 2:1,12). The transition was marked by internal conflict as factions competed for influence (1 Kings 1:5-53). His brother Adonijah had previously tried to seize the throne (1 Kings 1:5,50-53). Solomon was young and inexperienced compared to the mighty men who had surrounded David (1 Kings 3:7). The kingdom of Israel was large and its people were numerous (1 Kings 3:8). There were still many tasks to complete to secure the nation (1 Kings 3:1). These included the building of the house of the LORD and the royal palace (1 Kings 3:1). The people were still sacrificing at high places because no house had yet been built for the name of the LORD (1 Kings 3:2). Solomon faced the daunting challenge of governing a great nation without the seasoning or wisdom of his father (1 Kings 3:7-8). The problem was that the young king felt entirely inadequate to lead God's chosen people and maintain the peace of the realm (1 Kings 3:7-9).

## Solomon's Devout Condition
Solomon loved the LORD and walked in the statutes of his father David (1 Kings 3:3). He went to Gibeon, the great high place, to offer sacrifices (1 Kings 3:4). He offered a thousand burnt offerings on the altar there (1 Kings 3:4). He did not ask for a vision or a specific word before the LORD appeared to him (1 Kings 3:5). His act of worship and sacrifice preceded the divine encounter (1 Kings 3:4-5).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream during the night at Gibeon. God invited the king to make a request. This tested the priorities of his heart.

> At Gibeon the LORD appeared to Solomon in a dream by night, and God said, "**Ask what I shall give you.**"
>
> And Solomon said, "You have shown great and steadfast love to Your servant David my father, because he walked before You in faithfulness, in righteousness, and in uprightness of heart toward You. And You have kept for him this great and steadfast love and have given him a son to sit on his throne this day. And now, O LORD my God, You have made Your servant king in place of David my father, although I am but a little child. I do not know how to go out or come in. And Your servant is in the midst of Your people whom You have chosen, a great people, too many to be numbered or counted for multitude. **Give Your servant therefore an understanding mind to govern Your people, that I may discern between good and evil**, for who is able to govern this Your great people?" — 1 Kings 3:5-9 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Solomon had to sacrifice his own personal desires for the sake of the kingdom (1 Kings 3:11). He could have asked for long life, great wealth, or the death of his enemies (1 Kings 3:11). Instead, he risked being seen as weak by admitting his limitations (1 Kings 3:7). He gave up the pursuit of personal glory to seek the welfare of the people he was called to serve (1 Kings 3:9).

## Solomon's Initial Response
Solomon responded with profound humility and a deep sense of his own inadequacy (1 Kings 3:7). He did not boast of his status or his father's achievements (1 Kings 3:6-7). He described himself as a "little child" who did not know how to conduct the affairs of the state (1 Kings 3:7). He did not ask for anything that would benefit himself personally (1 Kings 3:9,11).

## Solomon's Wise Reaction
Solomon chose to prioritize the needs of the nation over his own comfort or power (1 Kings 3:9). He recognized that the people belonged to the LORD and that he was merely a servant (1 Kings 3:8). He asked for an understanding mind specifically to discern between good and evil in his judgments (1 Kings 3:9). After he awoke from the dream, he returned to Jerusalem and stood before the ark of the covenant of the LORD (1 Kings 3:15). He offered up burnt offerings and peace offerings and made a feast for all his servants (1 Kings 3:15). He demonstrated his trust by immediately beginning his duties as a judge (1 Kings 3:16-28). When two women brought a difficult case regarding a child, he relied on the wisdom God had promised (1 Kings 3:16-27). He trusted that the LORD would provide the necessary discernment to govern justly (1 Kings 3:28).

## The Divine Miracle
God was pleased with Solomon's request and granted him wisdom that exceeded all others (1 Kings 3:10,12). He also gave him the very things he had not asked for as a reward for his faithfulness (1 Kings 3:13).

> It pleased the LORD that Solomon had asked this. And God said to him, "Because you have asked this, and have not asked for yourself long life or riches or the life of your enemies, but have asked for yourself understanding to discern what is right, behold, **I now do according to your word. Behold, I give you a wise and discerning mind**, so that none like you has been before you and none like you shall arise after you. **I give you also what you have not asked, both riches and honor**, so that no other king shall compare with you, all your days. And if you will walk in My ways, keeping My statutes and My commandments, as your father David walked, then **I will lengthen your days.**" — 1 Kings 3:10-14 (ESV)

In this miracle, God fulfilled His promise to give Solomon an understanding mind and unparalleled wisdom (1 Kings 4:29-30). He also granted him immense prosperity and fame among all nations (1 Kings 10:23-25).

## The Global Result
The miracle resulted in a period of unprecedented peace and prosperity for Israel (1 Kings 4:20-25). Solomon's wisdom became famous throughout the known world and among all its kings (1 Kings 4:31, 10:1). This attracted leaders from distant lands (1 Kings 10:1-13). The Queen of Sheba visited Jerusalem and was breathless at the wisdom and wealth she witnessed (1 Kings 10:4-5). Solomon built a magnificent temple for the LORD (2 Chronicles 7:11). The nation of Israel reached its greatest geographical extent and cultural influence (1 Kings 4:21,24). The people lived in safety (1 Kings 4:25). The wisdom of Solomon was recorded in proverbs and songs that continue to guide people today (1 Kings 4:32). His reign became a shadow of the coming Kingdom of the Messiah (Psalm 72).

## Conclusion
Solomon faced the problem of leading a vast and chosen people while feeling as vulnerable as a child. He was limited by his youth and the heavy shadow of his father's legacy. However, when the LORD invited him to ask for anything, he responded with humility and a request for wisdom. God's promise was fulfilled through a miraculous granting of discernment, wealth, and honor. Solomon sacrificed his opportunity for personal gain to seek the righteousness of the kingdom. In return, he gained more wisdom and riches than any king before him. His story proves that we can trust the LORD to equip us for the tasks He gives us. He is faithful to provide more than we could ever ask or imagine when we seek His kingdom first.
