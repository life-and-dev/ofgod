# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A reluctant prophet attempts the impossible by trying to outrun the presence of the Almighty. His journey takes him to the depths of the ocean and into the heart of a savage empire. Discover how God's relentless mercy pursues those who rebel against His clear command.

## The Problem of Assyrian Brutality
Jonah lived during the reign of Jeroboam II when Israel was expanding its borders (2 Kings 14:25). However, the rising power of Assyria and its capital Nineveh posed a growing threat to the region (Jonah 1:2). The Ninevites were known for their extreme cruelty and violence toward their enemies (Nahum 3:1,19). This political environment made any message to Nineveh inherently dangerous and undesirable for a Hebrew prophet (Jonah 1:2-3). Jonah likely feared that if Nineveh repented, God would spare a nation that was a direct threat to his own people (Jonah 4:2). The city was filled with such great evil that it had come up before the face of the LORD (Jonah 1:2). The problem was that the most brutal empire of the day was ripe for divine judgment, yet its survival threatened the safety and future of Israel (Jonah 1:2, 4:2).

## Jonah's Humble Condition
Jonah was a recognized prophet of the LORD from Gath-hepher (2 Kings 14:25). He was already in a position of service to the King of Israel before he received this specific call (2 Kings 14:25). There is no record of Jonah performing any special ritual or prayer to gain God's attention (Jonah 1:1). He did not visit the temple or offer a specific sacrifice before the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a servant waiting for his next assignment (Jonah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came to Jonah with a clear command to confront the wickedness of a foreign city.

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, "Arise, **go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
By obeying the call, Jonah risked his reputation as a prophet in Israel by potentially saving their enemies (Jonah 4:2). He also risked his life by entering the heart of a city known for its violence (Nahum 3:1). He had to sacrifice his personal comfort, his nationalistic pride, and his own sense of justice (Jonah 1:3, 4:2). He risked being seen as a traitor to his own people if the Assyrians were spared (Jonah 4:1-2).

## Jonah's Initial Response
Jonah's initial reaction was one of direct rebellion and avoidance (Jonah 1:3). He rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and paid the fare to join a ship heading in the opposite direction of Nineveh (Jonah 1:3). He believed he could escape the reach of the Almighty by traveling across the sea (Jonah 1:3). He chose to abandon his post rather than obey a command he disliked (Jonah 1:3).

## Jonah's Reluctant Reaction
The LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea to stop Jonah's flight (Jonah 1:4). While the mariners were afraid, Jonah was fast asleep in the inner part of the ship (Jonah 1:5). After the sailors cast lots, the lot fell on Jonah, and he confessed he was fleeing from the God of heaven (Jonah 1:7-10). He told the men to hurl him into the sea so that it would quiet down for them (Jonah 1:12). Once he was in the water, a great fish swallowed him, and he remained there for three days (Jonah 1:17). From the belly of the fish, Jonah prayed and acknowledged that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:1-9). After being vomited onto dry land, Jonah received the word of the LORD a second time and finally went to Nineveh (Jonah 2:10, 3:1-3). He walked through the city and proclaimed that it would be overthrown in forty days (Jonah 3:4). Even after the city repented, Jonah reacted with anger and sat outside the city to see what would happen (Jonah 4:1,5). He complained to God about His gracious and merciful nature (Jonah 4:2). He even asked for his life to be taken because he was so displeased with the outcome (Jonah 4:3). Through the incident with a plant and a worm, Jonah eventually faced a lesson about God's compassion (Jonah 4:6-11). He learned that God's mercy extends even to those he considered unworthy (Jonah 4:11).

## The Divine Miracle
God intervened with multiple miracles to preserve Jonah and lead Nineveh to repentance (Jonah 1:17, 3:10). He controlled the elements, the creatures, and the hearts of a pagan people (Jonah 1:4, 2:10, 3:5).

> And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish **three days and three nights.** — Jonah 1:17 (ESV)

> And the LORD spoke to the fish, and it **vomited Jonah out upon the dry land.** — Jonah 2:10 (ESV)

Later, after Jonah preached, the entire city turned from their ways.

> When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them**, and **He did not do it.** — Jonah 3:10 (ESV)

In these actions, the LORD fulfilled His promise to **call out against** the city by bringing them to a state where He could spare them (Jonah 3:10).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the survival of over one hundred and twenty thousand people in Nineveh (Jonah 4:11). The entire city, from the king to the animals, fasted and called out mightily to God (Jonah 3:6-8). This massive repentance averted a sudden destruction and extended the life of the city (Jonah 3:10). For the sailors, the miracle on the sea led them to fear the LORD exceedingly and offer sacrifices (Jonah 1:16). For Jonah, the result was a direct confrontation with his own lack of mercy compared to the heart of God (Jonah 4:10-11). The nation of Israel was shown that the God of heaven is the Creator of all and has authority over every nation (Jonah 1:9, 4:11). The story became a sign for future generations, including the ministry of Jesus (Matthew 12:39-41).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a brutal enemy empire and his own struggle with God's mercy. He was limited by his prejudice and his attempt to flee from the divine presence. God promised to use him to call out against Nineveh. Jonah responded with rebellion, then with reluctant obedience, and finally with anger. God intervened through a great storm, a fish, and a city-wide repentance. The result was the salvation of a great city and a lesson on grace. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and his pride, but he gained a front-row seat to a massive miracle of repentance. This story proves that we can trust the LORD to be faithful to His character, even when we fail to represent Him well. He is a gracious God Who does not forsake His servants or the world He created.
