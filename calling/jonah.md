# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet receives a clear command to warn a brutal enemy empire of coming judgment. Instead of obeying, he boards a ship headed in the opposite direction. His attempt to escape leads him into the heart of a storm and the depths of the sea.

## The Problem of Nineveh
The city of Nineveh was a great and powerful city (Jonah 1:2). It was known for its extreme cruelty and wickedness toward other nations (Nahum 3:1). The Assyrians were a major threat to the people of Israel (2 Kings 17:3,5). Their evil had become so great that it came up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). Jonah was a prophet from Gath-hepher in the kingdom of Israel (2 Kings 14:25). He lived during the reign of Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:23,25). The people of Israel were struggling with their own spiritual failures while facing the rising power of Assyria (2 Kings 14:24). The clear problem was that the great city of Nineveh was headed for divine destruction because of its persistent evil (Jonah 1:2).

## Jonah's Reluctant Condition
Jonah was the son of Amittai and a recognized prophet in Israel (Jonah 1:1; 2 Kings 14:25). He was likely in his home country when the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1-2). Jonah did nothing special like fasting or visiting the temple to seek this specific message (Jonah 1:1). The word of the LORD simply came to him while he was performing his normal life (Jonah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came directly to Jonah.

> “**Arise, go to Nineveh**, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me.”
>
> — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

After Jonah fled and was rescued, the LORD spoke to him a second time.

> “**Arise, go to Nineveh**, that great city, and **call out against it the message that I tell you**.”
>
> — Jonah 3:2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his personal comfort and national pride (Jonah 1:3). He risked his reputation as a prophet by delivering a message to a hated enemy (Jonah 3:4). He also risked his life by entering a city known for its violence (Jonah 1:2, 3:8). Jonah chose to sacrifice his own safety and resources by paying a fare to flee from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3).

## The Initial Response
Jonah immediately rebelled against the command of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into the ship to go away from his calling (Jonah 1:3). Jonah preferred to flee to the furthest known point in the opposite direction rather than obey (Jonah 1:3).

## The Eventual Reaction
The LORD sent a great wind and a mighty tempest upon the sea (Jonah 1:4). Jonah slept in the inner part of the ship while the mariners were afraid (Jonah 1:5). After the sailors cast lots, the lot fell on Jonah (Jonah 1:7). He confessed that he was fleeing from the LORD, the God of heaven (Jonah 1:9-10). Jonah told the men to hurl him into the sea so that the tempest would quiet down (Jonah 1:12). Eventually, the sailors threw Jonah into the sea (Jonah 1:15). Inside the belly of a great fish, Jonah prayed to the LORD (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged his distress and thanked the LORD for delivering his life from the pit (Jonah 2:2,6). He vowed to sacrifice to the LORD with the voice of thanksgiving (Jonah 2:9). After the fish vomited him onto dry land, Jonah finally obeyed (Jonah 3:3). He went to Nineveh and proclaimed that the city would be overthrown in forty days (Jonah 3:4). Even after the city repented, Jonah reacted with anger because he wanted the Assyrians to be destroyed (Jonah 4:1-2). He sat east of the city to see what would happen (Jonah 4:5). The LORD used a plant, a worm, and a wind to teach Jonah about His compassion (Jonah 4:6-11).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD intervened through multiple supernatural events to ensure Jonah completed his mission and to save the city.

> But the **LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea**, and there was a mighty tempest on the sea, so that the ship threatened to break up... So they picked up Jonah and **hurled him into the sea, and the sea ceased from its raging**. . . And the **LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah**. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . And the **LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**.
>
> — Jonah 1:4,15,17, 2:10 (ESV)

The LORD also worked a miracle in the hearts of the Ninevites.

> And the **people of Nineveh believed God**. They called for a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least of them. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them**, and **He** did not do it.
>
> — Jonah 3:5,10 (ESV)

The LORD fulfilled **His** promise to **call out against it** and showed that **He** is a gracious God Who **relents from disaster** (Jonah 1:2, 4:2).

## The Lasting Result
The entire city of Nineveh repented and was spared from destruction (Jonah 3:10). More than 120,000 people were saved from the immediate judgment of God (Jonah 4:11). The mariners on the ship also feared the LORD exceedingly and offered sacrifices (Jonah 1:16). Jonah himself was rescued from certain death in the deep sea (Jonah 2:6). The miracle served as a sign of God's authority over both the natural world and all nations (Jonah 1:9). It demonstrated that God's mercy extends even to the most wicked people who turn to Him (Jonah 3:10). Jonah's story became a lasting testimony to later generations (Matthew 12:39-41).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city that deserved judgment and his own deep-seated prejudice. He was a prophet who failed his initial calling by attempting to flee from God's presence. He had no power to save himself from the storm or the sea. However, the LORD promised to deliver the message to Nineveh and preserved Jonah's life in a great fish. Jonah sacrificed his pride and comfort to eventually deliver the warning. God's intervention through the repentance of Nineveh proved His immense mercy and faithfulness. Jonah sacrificed his desire for revenge, but he gained a firsthand experience of God's grace and became an instrument of salvation for an entire city. This story shows that we can trust the LORD to be faithful even when we are reluctant or rebellious.
