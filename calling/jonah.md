# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet tries to run from a divine mission to warn a city of its doom. He discovers that no one can hide from the Creator of the sea and land. Follow his journey from the depths of the ocean to a city's transformation.

## The Wickedness of Nineveh

The word of the LORD came to Jonah (Jonah 1:1). Nineveh was an exceedingly great city (Jonah 3:3). It was a major city of the Assyrian Empire (2 Kings 19:36). Their evil had come up before God (Jonah 1:2). The city was filled with violence (Jonah 3:8). The Assyrians were known for their cruelty to their enemies (Nahum 3:1). Jonah was a prophet from Gath-hepher in Israel (2 Kings 14:25). He had previously prophesied success for King Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:25). The problem was that Nineveh's wickedness had reached a point where God's judgment was imminent (Jonah 1:2).

## Jonah's Spiritual Condition

Jonah was a prophet who served the LORD (2 Kings 14:25). He was likely in Israel when the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1). He did not perform any special sacrifice or fast before God spoke (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a chosen servant of the God of heaven (Jonah 1:9). He recognized that the LORD was the Creator of the sea and the dry land (Jonah 1:9).

## The Call to Nineveh

God spoke directly to Jonah to send him to the heart of the Assyrian Empire.

> "Arise, go to **Nineveh**, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before **Me**." — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

## The Risk of Obedience

Jonah risked his reputation as a prophet of Israel (Jonah 1:3). He likely feared that if Nineveh repented, God would spare them (Jonah 4:2). This would benefit Israel's enemies (Nahum 3:1). He sacrificed his comfort and safety to travel to a foreign and hostile land (Jonah 1:3).

## An Attempted Escape

Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into the ship (Jonah 1:3). He intended to go away from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3).

## A Journey Through the Deep

The LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea (Jonah 1:4). There was a mighty tempest so that the ship threatened to break up (Jonah 1:4). The mariners were afraid and each cried out to his god (Jonah 1:5). They hurled the cargo that was in the ship into the sea to lighten it for them (Jonah 1:5). Jonah had gone down into the inner part of the ship and had lain down and was fast asleep (Jonah 1:5). The captain came and said to him, "What do you mean, you sleeper? Arise, call out to your god!" (Jonah 1:6). The sailors cast lots and the lot fell on Jonah (Jonah 1:7). He confessed that he was fleeing from the LORD (Jonah 1:10). He told them to hurl him into the sea so it would quiet down for them (Jonah 1:12). After trying to row to land, the men eventually hurled him into the sea (Jonah 1:15). The sea ceased from its raging (Jonah 1:15). The LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah (Jonah 1:17). Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights (Jonah 1:17). He prayed to the LORD out of his distress (Jonah 2:1-2). He acknowledged that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). The LORD spoke to the fish and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land (Jonah 2:10). Jonah then obeyed the second call to go to Nineveh (Jonah 3:1-3). He walked through the city and proclaimed its coming destruction (Jonah 3:4).

## The Miracle of Mercy

God used a reluctant prophet to bring an entire city to repentance. He showed His power over nature and His compassion for the lost.

> And the people of Nineveh believed God. They called for a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least of them. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster** that **He** had said **He** would do to them, and **He** did not do it. — Jonah 3:5,10 (ESV)

## A Nation Spared

The miracle resulted in the repentance of over 120,000 people (Jonah 3:5, 4:11). The king of Nineveh issued a proclamation and published through Nineveh for everyone to turn from his evil way (Jonah 3:6-8). The disaster was averted (Jonah 3:10). Jonah's preaching was successful beyond his own desires (Jonah 4:1). God used this event to teach Jonah about His grace for all nations (Jonah 4:10-11).

## Conclusion

Nineveh was a city of violence facing divine judgment. Jonah was a prophet who tried to flee his calling because he knew God was merciful. He suffered the consequences of his rebellion in the belly of a great fish. God heard his prayer and gave him a second chance. Jonah eventually preached to Nineveh and the entire city repented. God fulfilled His promise to call out against Nineveh and then showed His character by relenting from disaster. Jonah sacrificed his pride and comfort but gained a front-row seat to one of the greatest revivals in history. His story shows that the LORD is faithful even when His servants are reluctant. He is the God Who pursues us even in the depths of the sea.
