# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet tries to run away from God's command but finds that even the depths of the ocean cannot hide him from his Creator. Discover how his rebellion led to a miraculous rescue and the salvation of a city he despised.

## The Wickedness of Nineveh
Nineveh was an exceedingly great city, yet its people were known for their profound wickedness (Jonah 1:2, 3:3). The Assyrians were a violent nation that posed a significant threat to Israel (Nahum 3:1; 2 Kings 19:35-36). Their evil had reached such a height that it had come up before the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:2). The city was filled with violence and social injustice (Jonah 3:8; Nahum 3:19). This corruption was not hidden from the eyes of the Almighty, Who saw the suffering it caused (Jonah 1:2). The problem was that Nineveh faced imminent divine judgment because of its persistent and overwhelming wickedness (Jonah 1:2, 3:4).

## Jonah's Reluctant Condition
Jonah was the son of Amittai and a prophet from Gath-hepher in Israel (2 Kings 14:25). He had previously prophesied the restoration of Israel's borders during the reign of Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:25). He did not perform any special sacrifice or prayer to get God's attention (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a servant of the LORD whom God chose to send on a specific mission (Jonah 1:1-2). His heart was filled with nationalistic pride and a deep-seated hatred for the enemies of his people (Jonah 4:2).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came directly to Jonah with a clear and urgent instruction to warn the pagan city of Nineveh.

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, "**Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his reputation as a prophet in Israel and his own personal comfort (Jonah 1:3). He risked being seen as a traitor for bringing a warning to a nation that oppressed his own people (Jonah 4:2). He also had to let go of his desire for vengeance against the Assyrians (Jonah 4:1-2). By obeying, he would be risking his safety in a violent foreign city (Jonah 1:2; Nahum 3:1).

## Jonah's Initial Response
Jonah did not initially obey the LORD but instead attempted to flee from His presence (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship bound for Tarshish, the opposite direction of Nineveh (Jonah 1:3). He thought he could escape the reach of the Creator by crossing the sea (Jonah 1:3-4). His rebellion was a deliberate act of distrust and fear (Jonah 1:3, 4:2). He chose the path of disobedience because he did not want God to show mercy to his enemies (Jonah 4:2).

## Jonah's Eventual Reaction
After his attempt to flee, Jonah found himself in a violent storm sent by the LORD (Jonah 1:4). He eventually confessed his flight to the sailors and told them to throw him into the sea to save their lives (Jonah 1:10-12). While in the belly of a great fish for three days, he prayed and committed himself to God's salvation (Jonah 2:1-9). When the fish vomited him onto dry land, the LORD gave him the command a second time (Jonah 2:10, 3:1). This time, Jonah arose and went to Nineveh as he was told (Jonah 3:3). He walked through the city and proclaimed the message of judgment (Jonah 3:4). Even though his heart was still filled with anger and he hoped for the city's destruction, he fulfilled the task God had assigned him (Jonah 3:4, 4:1). He sat outside the city to see what would happen, demonstrating his lingering reluctance even after his obedience (Jonah 4:5). Jonah trusted God with his life when he asked to be thrown into the sea, acknowledging that his fate was in the hands of the LORD (Jonah 1:12).

## The Divine Miracle
God preserved Jonah's life in the depths of the sea and moved the entire city of Nineveh to repentance through a simple message of judgment.

> And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And **Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights**. . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and **it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it**. — Jonah 1:17, 2:10, 3:10 (ESV)

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the total transformation of Nineveh as the people believed God and turned from their evil ways (Jonah 3:5-8). The king himself sat in ashes and issued a decree for a fast (Jonah 3:6-7). This repentance saved over 120,000 lives from immediate destruction (Jonah 4:11). For Jonah, it was a lesson in the vastness of God's mercy which extends even to those we consider unworthy (Jonah 4:10-11). The incident also served as a sign to future generations, including the time of Jesus (Matthew 12:39-41). The nations around Nineveh witnessed the power of the God of the Hebrews to bring a great empire to its knees in repentance (Jonah 3:5-10).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of Nineveh's wickedness and his own deep-seated prejudice. He failed initially by fleeing to Tarshish, attempting to escape God's presence. However, God was faithful and did not forsake His reluctant prophet. Through the miracle of the great fish and the repentance of Nineveh, God's sovereign purpose was achieved. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and his pride, but he gained a firsthand experience of the mercy of the LORD. His story proves that God's faithfulness is not dependent on our perfection but on His own character. We can trust Him even when He calls us to do things that challenge our understanding. God's mercy is greater than our failures and His plans will always prevail for His glory.
