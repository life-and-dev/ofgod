# Jonah: Reluctant Prophet

A prophet is sent to warn a city of impending doom but chooses to sail in the opposite direction. His attempt to hide from God leads to a stormy sea and an impossible sanctuary. Discover how God's mercy reached a reluctant messenger.

## The Problem of Great Wickedness
Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire and was known for its great wickedness (Jonah 1:2). The political environment was dominated by this powerful and cruel nation which threatened Israel (2 Kings 14:25). The people of Nineveh were trapped in their evil ways and violence (Jonah 3:8). The problem was that their evil had come up before the LORD and they faced total destruction (Jonah 1:2).

## Jonah's Condition
Jonah was a prophet of Israel and the son of Amittai (Jonah 1:1). He did nothing special like fasting or visiting the temple to get God's attention before the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a chosen messenger in his own country when **He** called him (Jonah 4:2).

## The Calling
The word of the LORD came directly to Jonah with a clear command.

> "Arise, go to **Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

Later, the word came a second time.

> "Arise, go to **Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it the message that I tell you**." — Jonah 3:2 (ESV)

## The Sacrifice
Jonah risked his national identity and his sense of justice (Jonah 4:2). He knew that if the Ninevites repented, God would show them mercy (Jonah 4:2). As a patriot, he likely hated the Assyrians and did not want them to be saved from judgment (Jonah 4:1-3). He sacrificed his comfort and safety by eventually traveling into the heart of a hostile and violent city (Jonah 3:3-4).

## Jonah's Initial Response
Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going in the opposite direction of Nineveh (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into the ship to hide (Jonah 1:3). He even fell into a deep sleep during a great storm that threatened the lives of everyone on board (Jonah 1:5). He initially refused to obey the command of the LORD (Jonah 1:3).

## Jonah's Reaction
When the storm became too great, Jonah admitted to the mariners that he was fleeing from the LORD (Jonah 1:10). He told them to hurl him into the sea so that the sea would quiet down for them (Jonah 1:12). After they threw him overboard, the sea ceased from its raging (Jonah 1:15). In the belly of the fish, Jonah prayed and recognized that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:1-9). After being vomited onto dry land, he finally obeyed the second calling (Jonah 3:3). He went through Nineveh and proclaimed the message of destruction (Jonah 3:4). Even when the people repented and God relented, Jonah struggled with anger because **He** was so gracious (Jonah 4:1-2). He sat outside the city to see what would happen, still hoping for judgment (Jonah 4:5). Through a plant, a worm, and a wind, God taught Jonah about **His** compassion for all people (Jonah 4:6-11). Jonah eventually submitted to the reality of God's universal mercy (Jonah 4:11).

## The Miracle
God used a great fish to save Jonah from drowning and to bring him back to his mission. He also worked in the hearts of the Ninevites to bring about total repentance.

> "And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And **Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights**. . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and **it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them**, and He did not do it." — Jonah 1:17, 2:10, 3:10 (ESV)

The miracle of the fish preserved Jonah's life so he could **go to Nineveh** and the miracle of repentance meant God **did not do** the disaster He planned.

## The Result
The result was the salvation of over 120,000 people in Nineveh (Jonah 4:11). The king and the people believed God and turned from their evil ways (Jonah 3:5-10). The city was spared from destruction (Jonah 3:10). The mariners on the ship also feared the LORD exceedingly and made vows to **Him** (Jonah 1:16). Jonah himself learned that God's mercy extends even to the enemies of Israel (Jonah 4:11).

## Conclusion
Nineveh faced destruction because of their great wickedness. Jonah was limited by his own prejudice and fear, choosing to flee rather than obey. However, the LORD promised to send a message to the city and used a storm and a fish to redirect **His** prophet. Jonah responded with eventual obedience after experiencing God's discipline. God intervened by preserving Jonah and softening the hearts of a violent nation. The result was a city-wide repentance and the sparing of many lives. Jonah sacrificed his pride and comfort, but he gained a deeper understanding of God's character. His story shows that we can trust the LORD'S compassion even when we fail or disagree with **His** ways. **He** is faithful to accomplish **His** purposes through even the most reluctant servants.
