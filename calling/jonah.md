# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet receives a divine command to warn a city of impending doom. Instead of obeying, he boards a ship to the opposite end of the world. A massive storm and a deep-sea creature await to redirect his path toward an impossible mission.

## The Problem of Wickedness
Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire, a nation known for its extreme cruelty and violence (Nahum 3:1). The Assyrians were a constant threat to the kingdom of Israel and were feared for their brutal military tactics (Isaiah 10:5-6). Their evil had become so great that it caught the attention of the LORD (Jonah 1:2). Jonah was a prophet from Gath-hepher in the northern kingdom of Israel (2 Kings 14:25). He lived during the reign of Jeroboam II, a time of national expansion but spiritual decay (2 Kings 14:23-24). The problem was that the people of Nineveh were steeped in wickedness and faced divine destruction unless they repented (Jonah 1:2).

## Jonah's Chosen Condition
Jonah was a recognized prophet of the LORD in Israel (2 Kings 14:25). He had previously seen the LORD'S word fulfilled regarding the borders of Israel (2 Kings 14:25). There is no record of Jonah doing anything special like fasting or praying to receive this specific word (Jonah 1:1). The word of the LORD simply came to him while he was in his home country (Jonah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came directly to Jonah with a clear command to travel to the heart of the enemy's empire.

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, "Arise, **go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me."
>
> — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his national pride and his comfort (Jonah 4:1-2). He risked his reputation as a prophet if the enemy repented and was spared (Jonah 4:2). He feared that God would show mercy to the very people who oppressed his own nation (Jonah 4:2). By going to Nineveh, he would be entering a hostile and dangerous environment where his life could be at risk (Jonah 1:2).

## The Initial Response
Jonah immediately rebelled and fled from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish, which was in the opposite direction of Nineveh (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went aboard to escape his assignment (Jonah 1:3). He chose to hide in the inner part of the ship and fell into a deep sleep during a great storm (Jonah 1:5).

## Jonah's Eventual Reaction
After being cast into the sea and swallowed by a great fish, Jonah finally cried out to the LORD (Jonah 2:1). In the belly of the fish, he prayed and acknowledged that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). When the fish vomited him onto dry land, the LORD called him a second time (Jonah 3:1). This time, Jonah arose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD (Jonah 3:3). He entered the city and proclaimed the message of judgment (Jonah 3:4). When the people repented, Jonah became angry because the LORD spared them (Jonah 4:1). He even asked the LORD to take his life (Jonah 4:3). Despite his bad attitude, he stayed to see what would happen to the city (Jonah 4:5). He had to learn about God's compassion for all people (Jonah 4:10-11).

## The Divine Miracle
God performed miracles to preserve Jonah's life and to bring an entire city to repentance.

> And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And **Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights**. . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and **it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**.
>
> — Jonah 1:17, 2:10 (ESV)

In these events, the LORD fulfilled **His** intent to have Jonah **go to Nineveh** (Jonah 1:2).

## The Saving Result
The miracle resulted in the repentance of the entire city of Nineveh, from the king to the least of them (Jonah 3:5-6). They turned from their evil way, and God did not bring the destruction He had threatened (Jonah 3:10). This event spared over one hundred and twenty thousand people (Jonah 4:11). The sailors on the ship also feared the LORD and offered sacrifices after the storm ceased (Jonah 1:16). Jonah himself experienced God's discipline and mercy firsthand (Jonah 2:7, 4:6).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of Nineveh's wickedness and his own deep-seated prejudice. He was limited by his hatred for his enemies and his desire for their destruction. However, the LORD promised that His message would be heard. Jonah responded first with rebellion and later with a reluctant obedience. God intervened by sending a storm and a fish to save and redirect His prophet. The result was a city-wide revival that demonstrated God's reach extends to all nations. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and his pride, but he gained a front-row seat to the boundless mercy of the LORD. This story proves that we can trust the LORD even when His commands challenge our biases. He is the LORD Who is gracious and merciful.
