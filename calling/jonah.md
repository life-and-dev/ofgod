# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A reluctant prophet attempts to outrun the Presence of God by boarding a ship to the opposite end of the known world. His flight triggers a supernatural storm that forces a desperate choice between his life and the survival of a pagan crew.

## The Problem of a Wicked City
The city of Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire (Jonah 1:2). It was an exceedingly great city that required a three days' journey to cross (Jonah 3:3). However, Nineveh was also a place of immense wickedness (Jonah 1:2). The people were known for their evil ways and the violence that was in their hands (Jonah 3:8). This Assyrian power was a constant threat to the security and peace of Israel (2 Kings 14:25). The spiritual and moral decay of Nineveh had become so severe that it had come up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). The problem was that the city was facing imminent divine destruction because of its persistent rebellion and cruelty (Jonah 3:4). The pagan inhabitants did not know their right hand from their left and were headed toward a disaster they did not yet perceive (Jonah 4:11).

## Jonah's Humble Condition
Jonah was the son of Amittai and a recognized prophet in Israel (Jonah 1:1; 2 Kings 14:25). He did nothing special like fasting or visiting the temple before God spoke to him (Jonah 1:1). Jonah was simply a servant of the LORD who received the word of the LORD in the course of his life (Jonah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came to Jonah with a clear command to warn the enemies of his people.

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me."
>
> — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

After Jonah's flight and rescue, the LORD spoke to him a second time.

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it the message that I tell you**."
>
> — Jonah 3:2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his personal comfort and national pride (Jonah 4:1-2). He risked his reputation as a prophet in Israel by traveling to a hostile foreign power (Jonah 3:3). He risked his life by entering a city known for its violence (Jonah 3:8). Most importantly, he had to sacrifice his desire for vengeance against the Assyrians (Jonah 4:2).

## Jonah's Initial Response
Jonah responded with immediate rebellion and flight (Jonah 1:3). He rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and paid the fare to board a ship heading in the opposite direction of Nineveh (Jonah 1:3). He believed he could escape the reach of God by traveling across the sea (Jonah 1:3).

## Jonah's Eventual Reaction
The LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea to stop Jonah's flight (Jonah 1:4). Jonah admitted to the mariners that he was fleeing from the God of heaven Who made the sea and the dry land (Jonah 1:9-10). He told them to hurl him into the sea so that the storm would quiet down for them (Jonah 1:12). After being rescued by a great fish, Jonah prayed from the belly of the fish (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged his distress and thanked the LORD for hearing his voice (Jonah 2:2). He vowed to sacrifice to the LORD with the voice of thanksgiving (Jonah 2:9). When the fish vomited him onto dry land, Jonah finally obeyed the second call (Jonah 3:3). He entered Nineveh and proclaimed that the city would be overthrown in forty days (Jonah 3:4). Even after the people repented, Jonah struggled with anger because he knew the LORD was gracious and merciful (Jonah 4:1-2). He sat outside the city to see what would become of it (Jonah 4:5).

## The Divine Miracle
God used both nature and the response of the people to preserve life and demonstrate His sovereignty (Jonah 1:17, 3:10).

> "And the LORD **appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah**. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights."
>
> — Jonah 1:17 (ESV)

> "And the LORD spoke to the fish, and **it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**."
>
> — Jonah 2:10 (ESV)

Later, the LORD spared the city when the people responded to Jonah's warning (Jonah 3:10).

> "When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster** that He had said He would do to them, and **He did not do it**."
>
> — Jonah 3:10 (ESV)

The LORD fulfilled His purpose to **call out against it** by bringing the city to repentance and sparing them from the disaster He had threatened (Jonah 1:2, 3:10).

## The Lasting Result
The entire population of Nineveh, from the king to the least of the people, believed God and called for a fast (Jonah 3:5). They turned from their evil ways and their violence (Jonah 3:8). The city was spared from destruction, saving more than 120,000 people and much cattle (Jonah 4:11). The mariners on the ship also feared the LORD exceedingly and offered sacrifices (Jonah 1:16). Jonah himself learned a profound lesson about the depth of God's compassion for all nations (Jonah 4:10-11).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city and his own prejudice against the Assyrians. He was a prophet who tried to flee from God's presence rather than obey a difficult command. The LORD promised to use Jonah to warn Nineveh. Jonah sacrificed his safety and his pride after being humbled in the belly of a fish. God's intervention through the great fish and the mass repentance of Nineveh showed His absolute faithfulness and mercy. Jonah sacrificed his rebellion, but he gained a front-row seat to one of the greatest miracles of mercy in history. This story proves that we can trust the LORD even when we disagree with His plans. He never forsakes His servants or the people He desires to save.
