# Jonah: a Second Chance

A reluctant prophet attempts to outrun the Almighty only to find himself in the dark depths of the sea. His flight from duty leads to an encounter with a great fish and an entire city on the brink of destruction.

## The Problem of Great Evil
The city of Nineveh was a place of immense wickedness and violence (Jonah 1:2). The LORD observed that their evil had come up before **Him** (Jonah 1:2). Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire, a powerful and cruel nation that threatened many people (Nahum 3:1). The political environment was one of fear as Assyria expanded its reach (2 Kings 15:19). The people of Nineveh did not know their right hand from their left in spiritual matters (Jonah 4:11). They were destined for destruction if they did not turn from their evil ways (Jonah 3:4). The problem was that Nineveh's persistent wickedness had reached a point where divine judgment was imminent (Jonah 1:2).

## Jonah's Ordinary Condition
Jonah was the son of Amittai and a prophet from Gath-hepher (Jonah 1:1; 2 Kings 14:25). He was likely performing his regular duties when the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1). Jonah did nothing special like fasting, praying, or visiting the temple to get God's attention (Jonah 1:1). **He** simply chose to speak to Jonah at a time of **His** own choosing (Jonah 1:1-2). Jonah was in a state of ordinary life before the divine call interrupted his peace (Jonah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came to Jonah directly to send him to a foreign and hostile city.

> "Arise, **go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me."
> — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his comfort and national pride to go to an enemy nation (Jonah 1:2-3). He risked his reputation as a prophet by delivering a message of doom to people he hated (Jonah 4:1-2). He sacrificed his safety by traveling to a city known for its violence (Jonah 1:2). He had to give up his own will to follow the command of the LORD (Jonah 1:3).

## The Initial Response
Jonah responded with immediate rebellion and flight (Jonah 1:3). He rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into it to go away from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). Jonah chose to go in the opposite direction of his calling (Jonah 1:3).

## Jonah's Reluctant Reaction
The LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea to stop Jonah's flight (Jonah 1:4). There was a mighty tempest so that the ship threatened to break up (Jonah 1:4). The mariners were afraid and each cried out to his god (Jonah 1:5). They cast lots to know on whose account this evil had come, and the lot fell on Jonah (Jonah 1:7). Jonah told them to pick him up and hurl him into the sea (Jonah 1:12). After he was thrown overboard, the sea ceased from its raging (Jonah 1:15). The LORD appointed a great fish to swallow Jonah (Jonah 1:17). Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights (Jonah 1:17). He prayed to the LORD his God from the belly of the fish (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). The LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land (Jonah 2:10). The word of the LORD came to Jonah a second time (Jonah 3:1). Jonah arose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD (Jonah 3:3). He called out that in forty days Nineveh would be overthrown (Jonah 3:4). Even when Jonah was angry at God's mercy, he remained under the instruction of the LORD (Jonah 4:1-4). **He** taught Jonah about compassion through a plant and a worm (Jonah 4:6-11). Jonah eventually submitted to the task despite his personal prejudice (Jonah 3:3).

## The Divine Miracle
God performed the miracle of preserving Jonah in the fish and transforming the hearts of the Ninevites.

> "And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And **Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.** . . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and **it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land.** . . . And the people of Nineveh believed God. They called for a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least of them. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them**, and **He did not do it**."
> — Jonah 1:17, 2:10, 3:5,10 (ESV)

In this miracle, God fulfilled the purpose of the call to **go to Nineveh... and call out against it** (Jonah 1:2).

## The Gracious Result
The miracle resulted in the survival of over one hundred and twenty thousand people in Nineveh (Jonah 4:11). The entire city turned from its evil way and violence (Jonah 3:10). The king of Nineveh and his nobles showed great humility before the LORD (Jonah 3:6-9). The mariners on the ship also feared the LORD exceedingly and offered a sacrifice (Jonah 1:16). Jonah learned that the LORD'S mercy extends even to the most undeserving nations (Jonah 4:10-11). The nation of Israel was shown that God is the LORD of all nations (Jonah 4:11).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city and his own prejudice. He was limited by his hatred for the Assyrians and his desire to see them destroyed. However, the LORD promised to use his voice to warn them. Jonah sacrificed his peace of mind and his pride by going to Nineveh. God intervened by rescuing Jonah from the sea and sovereignly turning a city to repentance. The result was a massive spiritual awakening in a pagan empire. Jonah sacrificed his comfort, but he gained a deeper understanding of the boundless grace of the LORD. His story proves that we can trust the LORD'S compassion even when we do not agree with it. **He** is faithful to **His** purpose even when **His** servants are reluctant.
