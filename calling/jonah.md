# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet attempts to outrun the omnipresent Creator by boarding a ship to the edge of the known world. His flight triggers a supernatural storm that forces a desperate choice. Discover how God uses a great fish and a reluctant messenger to save an entire city.

## The Problem of Impending Judgment

Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire, a nation known for its extreme cruelty and violence (Nahum 3:1). The Assyrians were a constant threat to Israel and the surrounding nations (2 Kings 18:13). Their wickedness had become so great that it caught the attention of the LORD (Jonah 1:2). Jonah was a prophet from Gath-hepher in the northern kingdom of Israel (2 Kings 14:25). He lived during a time of relative prosperity under Jeroboam II, but the shadow of Assyrian aggression loomed large (2 Kings 14:23-25). The political environment was one of deep-seated animosity between the Hebrews and the Assyrians (2 Kings 15:19). Jonah likely harbored a personal and nationalistic hatred for these people (Jonah 4:2). The problem was that the city of Nineveh was facing imminent divine judgment because of its overwhelming evil (Jonah 1:2, 3:4).

## Jonah's Active Condition

Jonah was a recognized prophet of the LORD in Israel (2 Kings 14:25). He was in a state of active service when the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1). There is no record of him performing any special sacrifice or prayer to receive this specific call (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a chosen servant of God who received a direct command (Jonah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling

The word of the LORD came directly to Jonah with a clear command to confront the wicked city.

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, "Arise, **go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

Later, after Jonah's rescue, the LORD gave him a second chance.

> Then the word of the LORD came to Jonah the second time, saying, "Arise, **go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it the message that I tell you**." — Jonah 3:1-2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice

Jonah risked his national reputation and his own sense of justice (Jonah 4:1-2). By warning Nineveh, he risked the possibility that his enemies would repent and be spared from destruction (Jonah 4:2). He sacrificed his comfort and safety to travel to a hostile foreign capital (Jonah 1:2-3). He also risked his life when he asked to be thrown into the raging sea to save the pagan mariners (Jonah 1:12).

## Jonah's Initial Response

Jonah immediately rebelled against the command of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into it to hide (Jonah 1:3). He chose to flee in the opposite direction of Nineveh to avoid fulfilling his mission (Jonah 1:3).

## Jonah's Eventual Reaction

After being swallowed by a great fish, Jonah prayed to the LORD from the belly of the fish (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged that his distress was from the LORD and vowed to fulfill his sacrifice (Jonah 2:2-9). Once he was vomited onto dry land, he obeyed the second call and went to Nineveh (Jonah 3:3). He walked through the city for a day's journey, proclaiming the message of judgment (Jonah 3:4). Even after the city repented, Jonah reacted with anger and frustration (Jonah 4:1). He sat outside the city to see what would happen, still hoping for its destruction (Jonah 4:5). He complained to the LORD that he knew God was gracious and merciful (Jonah 4:2). Jonah even asked the LORD to take his life because he was so displeased with the outcome (Jonah 4:3). The LORD then used a plant, a worm, and a scorching wind to teach Jonah about compassion (Jonah 4:6-8). Jonah eventually had to face the reality of God's concern for all people (Jonah 4:10-11). His reaction shows a struggle between his own desires and the sovereign will of God (Jonah 4:1-11). Despite his internal conflict, Jonah did the work he was called to do (Jonah 3:3-4).

## The Divine Miracle

The LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea to stop Jonah's flight and later provided a unique rescue. When Nineveh heard the message, God showed mercy as He had promised.

> But the LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea, and there was a mighty tempest on the sea, so that the ship threatened to break up . . . And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And **Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.** . . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and **it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land.** . . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it.** — Jonah 1:4,17, 2:10, 3:10 (ESV)

The miracle fulfilled the implied promise of the calling to **call out against it**, which led to their repentance and God's relenting (Jonah 1:2, 3:10).

## The Lasting Result

The result of God's miracle was the total repentance of Nineveh, from the king to the animals (Jonah 3:5-9). The entire city was saved from destruction (Jonah 3:10). The pagan mariners also came to fear the LORD and offered sacrifices to Him (Jonah 1:16). Jonah himself was humbled and forced to recognize that salvation belongs to the LORD alone (Jonah 2:9). The story became a sign for future generations, including the time of Jesus (Matthew 12:39-41). It demonstrated that God's mercy extends even to the most wicked of enemies (Jonah 4:11).

## Conclusion

Jonah faced the problem of Nineveh's wickedness and his own deep-seated prejudice. He was limited by his nationalism and his desire for vengeance over mercy. God promised to use Jonah to deliver a message of judgment. Jonah responded with rebellion and later with a begrudging obedience. God intervened with a supernatural storm and a great fish to redirect His prophet. The result was a national repentance and the preservation of a great city. Jonah sacrificed his pride and his comfortable status in Israel to witness to his enemies. In the end, he gained a deeper, though painful, understanding of the boundless grace of the LORD. His story proves that we can trust God's compassion even when it challenges our own sense of justice. He is faithful to save those who turn to Him.
