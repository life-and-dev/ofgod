# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet is sent to a wicked city but chooses to sail in the opposite direction. His flight leads to a storm and an underwater encounter that changes everything. Discover how one man's rebellion met God's relentless mercy for a city of thousands.

## The Wickedness of Nineveh
Nineveh was a great city that had become exceedingly wicked in the sight of God (Jonah 1:2). Their evil had come up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). Jonah was the son of Amittai and a prophet of the LORD (Jonah 1:1). The people of Nineveh were known for their violence and sin (Jonah 3:8). God decided to send a warning of destruction because of their great wickedness (Jonah 1:2). The problem was that a massive city faced total destruction from God because of their evil ways (Jonah 1:2).

## Jonah's Sudden Call
Jonah was simply a prophet in his own country when the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1-2). He did not perform any special prayer or sacrifice before the LORD spoke (Jonah 1:1). He was a Hebrew who feared the LORD (Jonah 1:9). God chose him to deliver a message of judgment to his enemies (Jonah 1:2).

## The Divine Calling
God spoke to Jonah and commanded him to go to the city of Nineveh to proclaim judgment.

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it**, for **their evil has come up before Me**." — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

Later, after Jonah was rescued, the LORD gave him the same command a second time.

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it the message that I tell you**." — Jonah 3:2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his personal comfort and his national pride (Jonah 1:3). He risked his reputation as a prophet by delivering a warning to a hated enemy nation (Jonah 1:2). He also sacrificed his safety by fleeing from the presence of the LORD on a ship (Jonah 1:3). He risked his life when he asked the mariners to throw him into the sea (Jonah 1:12).

## Jonah's Initial Flight
Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into the ship to go away from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He tried to hide from his calling by going in the opposite direction (Jonah 1:3).

## Jonah's Eventual Obedience
Jonah eventually responded to God's calling after being rescued from the sea (Jonah 3:1). He arose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD (Jonah 3:3). Nineveh was an exceedingly great city that required a three days' journey to cross (Jonah 3:3). Jonah began to go into the city and walked for a day (Jonah 3:4). He called out the message that the city would be overthrown in forty days (Jonah 3:4). The people of Nineveh believed God and called for a fast (Jonah 3:5). They put on sackcloth from the greatest to the least of them (Jonah 3:5). The king of Nineveh also repented and issued a decree for all people and beasts to fast and call out to God (Jonah 3:6-8). Jonah was initially displeased when God spared the city (Jonah 4:1). He sat east of the city to see what would happen to it (Jonah 4:5). The LORD used a plant and a worm to teach Jonah about His compassion (Jonah 4:6-11). Jonah learned that God pities those who do not know their right hand from their left (Jonah 4:11).

## The Divine Miracle
God used a great storm and a fish to preserve Jonah and lead him to Nineveh. He also worked a miracle of repentance in the hearts of the Ninevites.

> And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . And **the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them**, and **He did not do it**. — Jonah 1:17, 2:10, 3:10 (ESV)

God fulfilled **His** command for Jonah to **call out against it** and showed that **He** is a **gracious God and merciful** (Jonah 1:2, 4:2).

## The City's Salvation
The miracle resulted in the salvation of more than 120,000 people in Nineveh (Jonah 4:11). The city was not overthrown as God had threatened because of their repentance (Jonah 3:10). The sailors on the ship also came to fear the LORD and offered sacrifices (Jonah 1:16). Jonah himself was humbled and taught a lesson about God's universal mercy (Jonah 4:10-11). The story serves as a sign to future generations of God's power to save even the most wicked (Matthew 12:41).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city and his own prejudice. He failed by attempting to flee from God's presence. However, the LORD promised that Nineveh would be called to account. Jonah eventually obeyed after a miraculous rescue. God spared Nineveh because of their repentance. Jonah sacrificed his pride and comfort but he gained a front-row seat to one of the greatest revivals in history. This story proves that we can trust the LORD even when we disagree with His commands. He is the God Who is slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love. He does not forsake His servants even when they run away.
