# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet receives a command to warn a brutal enemy nation of impending judgment. Instead of obeying, he boards a ship to the opposite side of the world. Discover how God's persistent mercy reached both a runaway messenger and a wicked city.

## The Problem of Nineveh's Wickedness
The word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai with a message of judgment (Jonah 1:1). Nineveh was an exceedingly great city, yet its evil had come up before God (Jonah 1:2, 3:3). The political environment was dominated by the Assyrian Empire, known for its extreme cruelty and violence against its neighbors (Nahum 3:1-3,19). Israel lived in constant fear of this rising power (2 Kings 15:19). The inhabitants of Nineveh were trapped in their own wickedness and did not know their right hand from their left (Jonah 4:11). The problem was that the city was ripe for divine destruction unless they repented of their evil ways (Jonah 1:2, 3:10).

## Jonah's Avoiding Condition
Jonah was a prophet of the LORD from Gath-hepher (2 Kings 14:25). He was already established in his ministry when the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1). He did not perform any special sacrifice or prayer to receive this specific instruction (Jonah 1:1). He was simply in his normal state of life when the LORD called him to action (Jonah 1:1-2). His condition was one of immediate resistance to the difficult task assigned to him (Jonah 1:3).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke directly to Jonah, giving him a clear and urgent mission to preach against the capital of Assyria.

> "Arise, **go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

After Jonah fled, the LORD spoke to him a second time with the same command.

> "Arise, **go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it the message that I tell you**." — Jonah 3:2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his national pride and his comfort (Jonah 1:3, 4:1-2). He risked his reputation as a prophet if the message of judgment did not come to pass (Jonah 4:2). He risked his life by entering the heart of a hostile and violent empire (Jonah 3:3-4). He also risked losing the satisfaction of seeing his enemies destroyed (Jonah 4:1-3). He had to give up his own sense of justice to serve God's mercy (Jonah 4:10-11).

## Jonah's Initial Response
Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to the furthest possible destination (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into the ship to hide (Jonah 1:3). He chose to rebel against the clear command of God because he knew the LORD was gracious and merciful (Jonah 4:2). He preferred the depths of the sea to the streets of Nineveh (Jonah 1:12,15).

## Jonah's Eventual Reaction
After being swallowed by a great fish, Jonah prayed to the LORD from the belly of the fish (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged that his life was fainting away and remembered the LORD (Jonah 2:7). He vowed to sacrifice with the voice of thanksgiving and to pay what he had vowed (Jonah 2:9). When the LORD spoke to the fish and it vomited him out, Jonah arose and went to Nineveh (Jonah 3:3). He walked through the city for a day's journey and proclaimed the message (Jonah 3:4). He cried out that the city would be overthrown in forty days (Jonah 3:4). Even when he was angry at the result, he still sat outside the city to see what would happen (Jonah 4:5). He eventually had to listen to God's lesson through a plant and a worm (Jonah 4:6-11). He submitted to the LORD'S sovereign will despite his personal frustrations (Jonah 3:3). He learned that salvation belongs to the LORD alone (Jonah 2:9).

## The Divine Miracle
God performed a series of miracles to redirect His prophet and save the city of Nineveh. He hurled a great wind, prepared a fish, and moved the hearts of an entire population.

> "And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land. . . And the people of Nineveh believed God. They called for a fast and put on sackcloth. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it**." — Jonah 1:17, 2:10, 3:5,10 (ESV)

In these events, God fulfilled His word to **call out against it** and showed that the message **that I tell you** had the power to transform a nation (Jonah 1:2, 3:2).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the total repentance of Nineveh, from the king to the least of the citizens (Jonah 3:6-9). The entire city was spared from destruction (Jonah 3:10). This event served as a sign to future generations, including the time of Jesus (Matthew 12:39-41). Jonah's family and nation saw that the LORD'S mercy extends even to the most unlikely people (Jonah 4:11). The mariners on the ship also came to fear the LORD and offered sacrifices (Jonah 1:16). The result was a profound demonstration of God's sovereignty over nature and human hearts (Jonah 1:15, 3:10).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city and his own reluctant heart. He was limited by his prejudice and his attempt to run from the presence of the LORD. However, God promised a message for Nineveh and was faithful to ensure it was delivered. Jonah responded with initial rebellion but eventually with obedience after God's miraculous intervention. The result was the salvation of over one hundred and twenty thousand people. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and his own will, but he gained a first-hand experience of the depths of God's compassion. This story proves that we can trust the LORD to pursue us even in our failures and to fulfill His purposes for all nations. He is the God Who never gives up on His servants or the lost.
