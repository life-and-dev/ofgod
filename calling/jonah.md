# Jonah: Go to Nineveh

A reluctant prophet attempts to outrun a divine mandate by boarding a ship to the edge of the known world. His flight triggers a violent storm and a bizarre encounter that forces him to confront his destiny in the most unlikely of places.

## The Wickedness of Nineveh
The city of Nineveh was a great and powerful metropolis, but its inhabitants were known for their profound wickedness (Jonah 1:2). Their evil had become so great that it came up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire, a nation often at odds with Israel (2 Kings 19:36). The people lived in a state of violence and moral decay (Jonah 3:8). Jonah, a prophet from Gath-hepher, lived during a time of national expansion under Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:25). However, the spiritual rot of Nineveh posed a threat that God chose to address through a warning of impending judgment (Jonah 1:2). The problem was that a massive city of over one hundred and twenty thousand people was on the verge of divine destruction due to their persistent evil (Jonah 4:11).

## Jonah's Humble Condition
Jonah was a recognized prophet of the LORD in Israel (2 Kings 14:25). He was the son of Amittai (Jonah 1:1). Jonah did nothing special like fasting or praying to receive this specific call (Jonah 1:1). The word of the LORD simply came to him while he was in his own country (Jonah 4:2). He was a man with established prejudices against the Assyrians, whom he knew to be a cruel and violent people (Jonah 3:8).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came to Jonah with a direct command to confront the wickedness of Nineveh.

> "Arise, go to **Nineveh**, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me."
>
>  — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

Later, after Jonah tried to flee, the LORD spoke to him a second time.

> "Arise, go to **Nineveh**, that great city, and **call out against it the message that I tell you**."
>
>  — Jonah 3:2 (ESV)

## A Prophet's Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah risked his reputation as a prophet by delivering a message of doom to a foreign enemy (Jonah 3:4). He had to sacrifice his comfort and safety to travel to a city known for its violence (Jonah 3:8). Furthermore, he sacrificed his personal desire to see his enemies destroyed, fearing that God's mercy would spare them (Jonah 4:2). He eventually risked his life by being thrown into a raging sea to save the mariners (Jonah 1:12).

## The Initial Response
Jonah immediately rebelled against the command of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). Instead of going east to Nineveh, he rose to flee to Tarshish in the opposite direction (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and paid a fare to board a ship, attempting to hide from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He chose to sleep in the inner part of the ship while a divine storm threatened the lives of everyone on board (Jonah 1:5).

## The Heart of the Sea
After the lot fell on him and he confessed his flight from the LORD, Jonah accepted his responsibility (Jonah 1:12). He told the mariners to hurl him into the sea so that the tempest would quiet down for them (Jonah 1:12). After being swallowed by a great fish, Jonah prayed from its belly (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged that he had been driven away from God's sight but promised to look again toward the holy temple (Jonah 2:4). He recognized that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). When the fish vomited him onto dry land, Jonah finally obeyed the second call (Jonah 3:3). He entered Nineveh and proclaimed that the city would be overthrown in forty days (Jonah 3:4). Even when he became angry at God's mercy, he remained in communication with the LORD (Jonah 4:2). He sat east of the city to see what would happen (Jonah 4:5). Through a plant and a worm, he learned a lesson about God's compassion (Jonah 4:10-11).

## The Divine Miracle
God intervened with supernatural power to save Jonah and the city of Nineveh. First, the LORD spared Jonah's life in the midst of the sea.

> And the **LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah**. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . And the **LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**.
>
>  — Jonah 1:17, 2:10 (ESV)

Later, God fulfilled the purpose of the call by bringing a whole city to repentance.

> When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it**.
>
>  — Jonah 3:10 (ESV)

God fulfilled **His** word to **call out against it** by ensuring the message reached everyone from the king to the least (Jonah 3:5).

## Repentance and Its Result
The entire city of Nineveh believed God and called for a fast (Jonah 3:5). The king himself covered himself with sackcloth and issued a decree for total repentance (Jonah 3:6-9). Because they turned from their evil ways, God spared the city from destruction (Jonah 3:10). The mariners on the ship also feared the LORD exceedingly and offered sacrifices (Jonah 1:16). Jonah's family and nation were potentially spared from future Assyrian aggression for a time. The miracle served as a sign for future generations (Matthew 12:41).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city and his own prejudice. He was a disobedient prophet who tried to flee from the presence of the LORD. However, the LORD promised to use him to deliver a message of warning. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and safety, and eventually his pride. God's intervention through a great fish and the repentance of Nineveh proved His mercy. Jonah sacrificed his flight, but he gained a witness of God's power over nature and His compassion for all people. This story demonstrates that we can trust the LORD even when we struggle with His commands. He is the LORD Who relents from disaster.
