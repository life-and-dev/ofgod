# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet receives a command to warn a brutal enemy city of impending doom. Instead of obeying, he boards a ship to the opposite end of the world. Discover how God's persistent mercy follows a runaway messenger into the depths of the sea.

## The Wickedness of Nineveh
Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire and was known for its extreme cruelty and violence (Nahum 3:1). Their evil had become so great that it came up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). The political environment was one of constant warfare and oppression as the Assyrians expanded their power (Nahum 3:1,7). They were a threat to all neighboring nations, including Israel (2 Kings 15:19). The people of Nineveh were spiritually blind and did not know their right hand from their left (Jonah 4:11). The city was a place of lies and pillage (Nahum 3:1). The problem was that the wickedness of Nineveh had reached a point where divine judgment was inevitable unless they repented (Jonah 1:2, 3:4).

## Jonah's Prophetic Condition
Jonah was a prophet of the LORD from Gath-hepher (2 Kings 14:25). He had previously prophesied the restoration of Israel's borders during the reign of Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:25). He did not perform any special fast or sacrifice before the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1). He was a servant of God who had already seen the LORD'S faithfulness to his own nation (2 Kings 14:25). He was simply a man called to speak on behalf of the LORD (Jonah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came to Jonah directly, giving him a clear command to confront the wickedness of a foreign city.

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

After his rescue from the sea, the LORD called him a second time.

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it the message that I tell you**." — Jonah 3:2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his comfort and nationalistic pride (Jonah 4:1-2). He risked his reputation as a prophet of Israel by traveling to a hated enemy (Jonah 1:2). He feared that if Nineveh repented, God would show them mercy, which he did not want (Jonah 4:2). He risked his life by entering a city known for its brutality (Nahum 3:1). He sacrificed his own sense of justice to serve God's mercy (Jonah 4:2-3).

## Jonah's Initial Response
Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship, paying the fare to escape his calling (Jonah 1:3). He chose to go in the opposite direction of Nineveh (Jonah 1:3). He showed a heart of rebellion and distrust toward God's plan (Jonah 1:3). He even fell into a deep sleep during a great storm, indifferent to the danger he brought upon the sailors (Jonah 1:5).

## Jonah's Eventual Reaction
After the sailors threw Jonah into the sea to still the storm, he was swallowed by a great fish (Jonah 1:15-17). From the belly of the fish, Jonah prayed to the LORD his God (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged his distress and cried out for help (Jonah 2:2). He recognized that his salvation came only from the LORD (Jonah 2:9). After three days and nights, the fish vomited Jonah out upon the dry land (Jonah 2:10). When the word of the LORD came to him the second time, Jonah arose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD (Jonah 3:3). He entered the city and proclaimed that in forty days Nineveh would be overthrown (Jonah 3:4). Even though he still struggled with anger when God showed mercy, he completed the task he was given (Jonah 4:1). He learned that the LORD is a gracious God and merciful, slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love (Jonah 4:2). He eventually obeyed the command to preach despite his personal bias (Jonah 3:3).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD performed a miracle by preserving Jonah in the sea and by turning the hearts of an entire city (Jonah 1:17, 3:10).

> And **the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah**. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and **it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them**, and **He did not do it**. — Jonah 1:17, 2:10, 3:10 (ESV)

The miracle of repentance was the fulfillment of God's word that Jonah would **call out against it** the message that **He** would provide (Jonah 1:2, 3:2).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the survival of over one hundred and twenty thousand people in Nineveh (Jonah 4:11). The king and the people believed God and called for a fast, turning from their evil ways and violence (Jonah 3:5-8). This mass repentance prevented the immediate destruction of the city (Jonah 3:10). Jonah himself experienced God's power to deliver even from the heart of the seas (Jonah 2:3). The sailors on the ship also feared the LORD exceedingly and offered sacrifices after the storm ceased (Jonah 1:16). The story of Jonah became a sign to future generations, including the generation of Jesus (Matthew 12:39-41). It demonstrated that God's mercy extends beyond the borders of Israel to all who repent (Jonah 4:11).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city that deserved judgment. He was limited by his own prejudice and a desire to see his enemies destroyed. He initially rebelled against God's calling by fleeing across the sea. However, the LORD promised to send him to Nineveh and preserved him through a great fish when he failed. Jonah eventually responded with obedience and preached the message of warning. God intervened by changing the hearts of the Ninevites and relenting from the disaster. The result was the salvation of a city and a testimony of God's universal mercy. Jonah sacrificed his pride and comfort, but he gained a front-row seat to one of the greatest revivals in history. His story proves that we can trust the LORD to be faithful to **His** character, even when we are unfaithful to **Him**. **He** is the God **Who** always pursues us.
