# Jonah: The Reluctant Prophet

A man receives a divine command to preach to his nation's most brutal enemies. Instead of obeying, he boards a ship headed in the opposite direction. Discover how a massive storm and an even larger fish brought him back to his mission.

## The Brutal Power of Nineveh
Nineveh was an exceedingly great city and the capital of the Assyrian Empire (Jonah 1:2, 3:3). The Assyrians were known for their extreme violence and cruelty toward their enemies (Nahum 3:1,19). Their evil had come up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). The political environment was one of fear as Assyria expanded its power across the region (2 Kings 15:19). The city was so large that it took three days to walk across it (Jonah 3:3). The problem was that the wickedness of the Ninevites was so great that God had determined to overthrow the city (Jonah 1:2, 3:4).

## Jonah's Silent Condition
Jonah was the son of Amittai and a prophet from Gath-hepher (Jonah 1:1; 2 Kings 14:25). He lived during the reign of Jeroboam II when Israel was experiencing a period of expansion and prosperity (2 Kings 14:23-25). Jonah did not perform any special fast or prayer to get God's attention before he was called (Jonah 1:1). The word of the LORD simply came to him while he was in his country (Jonah 1:1, 4:2). He was a Hebrew who feared the LORD, the God of heaven, Who made the sea and the dry land (Jonah 1:9).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke directly to Jonah to send him on a mission of judgment and warning to the city of Nineveh.

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, "Arise, **go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

Later, after Jonah's rescue, the LORD called him a second time.

> Then the word of the LORD came to Jonah the second time, saying, "**Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it the message that I tell you.**" — Jonah 3:1-2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his personal comfort and national pride to obey God (Jonah 1:3). He risked his life by traveling to a hostile and violent city (Jonah 1:2). He also risked his reputation as a prophet of Israel by offering a chance of repentance to their enemies (Jonah 4:2). Jonah preferred to die rather than see Nineveh spared (Jonah 4:3). He sacrificed his freedom when he was cast into the sea (Jonah 1:15).

## Jonah's Initial Response
Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going in the opposite direction of Nineveh (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into the ship to go away from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). Jonah ignored the command and tried to escape his responsibility (Jonah 1:3). He fell into a deep sleep while a great storm threatened the ship (Jonah 1:5).

## Jonah's Eventual Reaction
When the storm became life-threatening, Jonah admitted that he was fleeing from the LORD (Jonah 1:10). He told the mariners to pick him up and hurl him into the sea so that the tempest would quiet down for them (Jonah 1:12). After being swallowed by a great fish, Jonah prayed to the LORD his God from the belly of the fish (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). After the fish vomited him onto dry land, Jonah obeyed the second call (Jonah 3:3). He went into Nineveh and proclaimed that the city would be overthrown in forty days (Jonah 3:4). When the Ninevites repented, Jonah became angry and sat outside the city to see what would happen (Jonah 4:1,5). He struggled with God's mercy but remained a witness to His power (Jonah 4:10-11).

## The Divine Miracle
God used a great fish to save Jonah from drowning and used Jonah's simple message to trigger a massive revival. He demonstrated His sovereignty over nature and the human heart.

> And the LORD **appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah**. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and it **vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**. . . And the people of Nineveh believed God. They called for a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least of them. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it**. — Jonah 1:17, 2:10, 3:5,10 (ESV)

In this miracle, God fulfilled His promise to **call out against** the city and delivered the **message that I tell you** (Jonah 1:2, 3:2).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the repentance of more than 120,000 people in Nineveh (Jonah 4:11). The king of Nineveh and all his subjects turned from their evil ways and violence (Jonah 3:6-8). The city was spared from immediate destruction (Jonah 3:10). The sailors on the ship also feared the LORD exceedingly and offered sacrifices (Jonah 1:16). Jonah's story became a sign for future generations (Matthew 12:39-41). It revealed that God's mercy extends to all nations, not just Israel (Jonah 4:11).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of Nineveh's extreme wickedness and his own deep-seated prejudice. He was limited by his desire for vengeance and his attempt to run away from God's presence. However, the LORD promised to use him to bring a message to the great city. Jonah responded first with rebellion and later with reluctant obedience. God intervened by preserving Jonah in the fish and softening the hearts of the Ninevites. The result was a national repentance and the salvation of thousands. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and his pride, but he gained a firsthand experience of God's limitless mercy. His story proves that we can trust the LORD to be faithful even when we are not. He never forsakes His servants, even when they try to flee from Him.
