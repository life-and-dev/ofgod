# Jonah: The Reluctant Prophet

A prophet receives a command to warn a brutal enemy city of impending doom. Instead of obeying, he flees in the opposite direction, only to find that no one can hide from the Almighty. Witness a journey from the depths of the sea to the gates of Nineveh.

## The Problem of a Wicked City

Nineveh was an exceedingly great city, yet its inhabitants were known for their profound wickedness (Jonah 1:2, 3:3). The Assyrian Empire, with Nineveh as its capital, was a cruel and powerful threat to the surrounding nations, including Israel (Nahum 3:1-7,19). Their evil had become so great that it had come up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). The political environment was marked by violence and oppression (Jonah 3:8). The people were spiritually blind, not knowing their right hand from their left (Jonah 4:11). Israel viewed the Assyrians as a merciless enemy that deserved nothing but destruction (Nahum 3:1). The problem was that the city's persistent sin had invited divine judgment, and they were only forty days away from total overthrow (Jonah 1:2, 3:4).

## Jonah's Simple Condition

Jonah was the son of Amittai and a prophet of the LORD (Jonah 1:1; 2 Kings 14:25). He was likely living in Gath-hepher in the northern kingdom of Israel (2 Kings 14:25). He did nothing special like fasting or praying to get God's attention before the call (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a chosen servant of God when the word of the LORD first came to him (Jonah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling

The word of the LORD came to Jonah directly, commanding him to confront the wickedness of a foreign city.

> "Arise, **go to Nineveh**, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me."
>
> — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

After Jonah's initial rebellion, the word of the LORD came to him a second time.

> "Arise, **go to Nineveh**, that great city, and **call out against it the message that I tell you**."
>
> — Jonah 3:2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice

Jonah had to sacrifice his personal comfort and nationalistic pride (Jonah 1:3). He risked his reputation as a prophet in Israel by going to a hated enemy (2 Kings 14:25). He sacrificed his safety, as Nineveh was a violent and dangerous city (Jonah 3:8). He even risked his life by asking to be thrown into the sea to save the sailors (Jonah 1:12).

## Jonah's Initial Response

Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into it to go with them, away from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He even fell into a deep sleep during the storm, trying to ignore the consequences of his flight (Jonah 1:5).

## Jonah's Faithful Reaction

After being swallowed by a great fish, Jonah eventually prayed to the LORD from the belly of the fish (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). When the fish vomited him onto dry land, he obeyed the second call (Jonah 3:3). He arose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD (Jonah 3:3).

He entered the city and began to cry out the message of judgment (Jonah 3:4). He proclaimed that in forty days, Nineveh would be overthrown (Jonah 3:4). Even though he still struggled with anger when God showed mercy, he had fulfilled the task of warning the city (Jonah 4:1-2). He sat outside the city to see what would happen, still wrestling with God's compassion (Jonah 4:5). Through the lesson of a plant, the LORD challenged Jonah's lack of pity for the lost (Jonah 4:10-11). Ultimately, Jonah's story became a sign for future generations (Matthew 12:39-41).

## The Divine Miracle

The LORD performed several miracles to preserve Jonah and bring Nineveh to repentance. He prepared a great fish to save His prophet from drowning.

> And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.
>
> — Jonah 1:17 (ESV)

> And the LORD spoke to the fish, and **it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**.
>
> — Jonah 2:10 (ESV)

The greatest miracle was the massive repentance of the pagan city.

> When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it**.
>
> — Jonah 3:10 (ESV)

## The Lasting Result

The miracle resulted in the sparing of more than 120,000 people and many cattle (Jonah 4:11). The people of Nineveh believed God, called for a fast, and put on sackcloth (Jonah 3:5). The king of Nineveh issued a decree for everyone to turn from their evil ways and violence (Jonah 3:7-8). This delayed the judgment of Nineveh for generations.

For Jonah, the experience taught him that God's mercy extends even to the most undeserving enemies (Jonah 4:10-11). The sailors on the ship also came to fear the LORD, offered a sacrifice, and made vows (Jonah 1:16). Jonah's life served as a testament that no one can escape God's presence or His sovereign plan (Psalm 139:7-12).

## Conclusion

Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city destined for destruction. He was a reluctant prophet who tried to run away from God's command because of his own prejudices.

However, the LORD pursued him with a storm and a fish, proving that His purpose would stand. Jonah sacrificed his pride and comfort to deliver a message he didn't want to give. God's mercy was miraculously extended to a repentant Nineveh, sparing an entire nation.

Jonah sacrificed his desire for Nineveh's destruction, but he gained a first-hand encounter with the overwhelming grace of God. His story reminds us that we can trust the LORD'S heart for the lost, even when we do not understand His ways. He is faithful to use even our failures to accomplish His divine will.
