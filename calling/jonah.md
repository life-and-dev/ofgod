# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet receives a divine command to warn a brutal enemy city of impending doom. Instead of obeying, he attempts to outrun the Almighty by sea. This decision leads to a terrifying storm and an encounter that defies natural explanation.

## The Problem of Nineveh
Jonah the son of Amittai lived during the reign of Jeroboam II in Israel (2 Kings 14:25). During this time, the Assyrian Empire was a dominant and cruel power (Nahum 3:1-3). Nineveh was a great city within Assyria, but it was known for its extreme violence and wickedness (Jonah 1:2, 3:8). The Israelites viewed the Assyrians as a significant threat to their national security and survival (2 Kings 15:19). The moral decay of Nineveh had reached a point where divine intervention was necessary (Jonah 1:2). The problem was that the people of Nineveh were heading toward total destruction because of their great evil (Jonah 1:2).

## Jonah's Unlikely Condition
Jonah was already established as a prophet of the LORD who had seen his previous prophecies fulfilled (2 Kings 14:25). He did nothing special to get God's attention for this specific task (Jonah 1:1). He did not seek this specific calling through prayer or fasting (Jonah 1:1-2). Instead, the word of the LORD simply came to him while he was in his own country (Jonah 1:1, 4:2). He was a man who feared the LORD, the God of heaven (Jonah 1:9). However, he also possessed a strong personal prejudice against the enemies of Israel (Jonah 4:1-2).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke directly to Jonah to give him a clear and urgent message for the city of Nineveh.

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it, for **their evil has come up before Me**."
>
> — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

Later, when Jonah finally reached the city, he delivered the specific message from the LORD.

> Jonah began to go into the city, going a day's journey. And he called out, "**Yet forty days, and Nineveh shall be overthrown!**"
>
> — Jonah 3:4 (ESV)

## The Required Sacrifice
By obeying the LORD, Jonah risked his reputation as a prophet if Nineveh repented and God relented (Jonah 4:2). He also risked his life by entering a city known for its brutality toward its enemies (Nahum 3:1). Furthermore, Jonah had to sacrifice his own desire to see the enemies of Israel destroyed (Jonah 4:1-3). He valued his nationalistic pride and personal comfort above the lives of the people in Nineveh (Jonah 4:10-11).

## An Initial Response of Flight
Jonah immediately rebelled against the command of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He rose to flee to Tarshish to get away from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and paid the fare for a ship heading in the opposite direction of Nineveh (Jonah 1:3). He believed that he could escape his responsibility by distancing himself from the land of Israel (Jonah 1:3,10).

## A Reaction in the Deep
The LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea which created a mighty tempest (Jonah 1:4). While the mariners were afraid and crying out to their gods, Jonah was fast asleep in the inner part of the ship (Jonah 1:5). After the sailors cast lots to find the cause of the trouble, the lot fell on Jonah (Jonah 1:7). He confessed that he was fleeing from the LORD and told them to hurl him into the sea to quiet the storm (Jonah 1:9-12). The men eventually threw Jonah into the raging waters (Jonah 1:15). Inside the belly of a great fish, Jonah finally turned back to God (Jonah 1:17). He prayed a prayer of thanksgiving and recognized that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:1-9). After the fish vomited him onto dry land, he obeyed the second call (Jonah 3:1-3). He walked through Nineveh and proclaimed the message of judgment (Jonah 3:4).

## A Miraculous Repentance
God used a series of miraculous events to preserve Jonah and bring about the repentance of an entire city.

> And the LORD **appointed a great fish** to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.
>
> — Jonah 1:17 (ESV)
>
> When God saw what they did, **how they turned from their evil way**, God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and **He did not do it**.
>
> — Jonah 3:10 (ESV)

## The Result of Divine Mercy
The repentance of Nineveh was complete and immediate, starting from the king down to the animals (Jonah 3:5-9). This resulted in the preservation of over one hundred and twenty thousand people who would have otherwise perished (Jonah 4:11). The mariners on the ship also came to fear the LORD exceedingly and offered sacrifices to Him (Jonah 1:16). For Jonah, the miracle resulted in a profound lesson about the mercy and sovereignty of God (Jonah 4:10-11). He learned that God's compassion extends beyond the borders of Israel to all of His creation (Jonah 4:11).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city that threatened his nation. His limitations were his prejudice and his attempt to flee from God's presence. Despite his rebellion and his anger at God's mercy, the LORD was faithful to His purpose. God used a storm and a great fish to redirect His reluctant prophet. Jonah eventually responded with obedience, and the result was the salvation of a great city. Jonah risked his safety and reputation, but he gained a first-hand experience of the boundless grace of the LORD. This story demonstrates that God is faithful to His word and merciful even to those who initially refuse Him.
