# Jonah: Running From God

A prophet tries to run from an omniscient God by boarding a ship to the edge of the world. He soon discovers that the sea itself obeys the voice he is trying to ignore. His attempt to escape leads to an underwater encounter that changes his destination.

## The Wickedness of Nineveh
Nineveh was a great city known for its wickedness (Jonah 1:2). The people of Assyria were enemies of Israel and were notorious for their violence and cruelty (Jonah 1:2). Jonah was a prophet of the LORD from Gath-hepher (2 Kings 14:25). The political environment was tense as the Assyrian Empire was a growing threat to the surrounding nations (Jonah 1:2). The wickedness of Nineveh had come up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). The problem was that the city was facing divine judgment unless someone warned them to repent (Jonah 1:2, 3:4).

## Jonah's Established Condition
Jonah was in his own country when the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1, 4:2). He was already established as a prophet who had seen God's word fulfilled in Israel (2 Kings 14:25). He did nothing special like fasting or praying to get God's attention (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a servant of the LORD whom God chose to send on a difficult mission to a pagan nation (Jonah 1:2).

## The Call to Nineveh
The LORD spoke directly to Jonah to send him to a foreign city with a message of warning.

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before **Me**." — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

Later, after Jonah's attempt to flee, the LORD spoke to him again.

> Then the word of the LORD came to Jonah the second time, saying, "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it the message that I tell you**." — Jonah 3:1-2 (ESV)

## The Sacrifice of Pride
Jonah had to sacrifice his national pride and his personal comfort (Jonah 4:1-2). He risked his reputation as a prophet if the LORD relented from the promised judgment (Jonah 4:2). He also risked his life by traveling to a violent and hostile foreign city (Jonah 1:2). He sacrificed his desire for justice against his enemies to obey a command of mercy (Jonah 4:2).

## Jonah's Flight to Tarshish
Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into the ship to go away from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He chose to rebel and flee in the opposite direction of Nineveh (Jonah 1:3).

## Jonah's Eventual Obedience
After being swallowed by a great fish, Jonah prayed to the LORD his God from the belly of the fish (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged that the LORD had cast him into the deep (Jonah 2:3). He confessed that he had been driven away from the sight of the LORD but still looked toward His holy temple (Jonah 2:4). Jonah vowed to sacrifice to the LORD with the voice of thanksgiving (Jonah 2:9). He declared that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). After the fish vomited him onto dry land, Jonah obeyed the second call of the LORD (Jonah 3:3). He arose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD (Jonah 3:3). Jonah began to go into the city and cried out the message of judgment (Jonah 3:4). He faithfully delivered the word that the city would be overthrown in forty days (Jonah 3:4). Even though he still struggled with anger when God showed mercy, he completed the task he was given (Jonah 4:1).

## The Miracle of the Fish and Repentance
The LORD used a storm and a great fish to redirect His prophet (Jonah 1:4,17). He moved the hearts of an entire city to repent (Jonah 3:5).

> And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and **it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**. — Jonah 1:17, 2:10 (ESV)

> When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it**. — Jonah 3:10 (ESV)

In these events, the LORD fulfilled His intention to call out against the city and deal with their evil while also preserving the messenger He had sent (Jonah 1:2, 3:2).

## The Result of Divine Mercy
The miracle resulted in the total repentance of Nineveh from the king to the animals (Jonah 3:5-8). The people believed God and called for a fast (Jonah 3:5). God relented from the disaster and spared more than 120,000 people who did not know their right hand from their left (Jonah 3:10, 4:11). Jonah was humbled by the experience of God's sovereignty over nature and nations (Jonah 2:9, 4:10-11). The preservation of the city served as a witness to God's mercy even toward the Gentiles (Jonah 4:11).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city and his own rebellious heart. He was limited by his prejudice and his attempt to flee from the presence of the LORD. However, God promised to use him to deliver a message of judgment. Jonah initially refused but eventually submitted after divine intervention in the belly of a fish. God relented from disaster when Nineveh repented, showing His great compassion. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and his pride, but he gained a firsthand experience of the relentless mercy of the LORD. This story proves that we cannot escape God's calling and that He is faithful to pursue us even in our rebellion. We can trust the LORD to accomplish His purposes for all nations through His abounding steadfast love.
