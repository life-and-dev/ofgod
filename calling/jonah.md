# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet receives a divine command to warn a wicked city. Instead of obeying, he attempts to outrun the presence of the Almighty by boarding a ship to a distant land. His journey leads him into the depths of the ocean and an unexpected encounter.

## The Problem of Wickedness
Nineveh was an exceedingly great city, but its people were consumed by evil and violence (Jonah 1:2, 3:8). The city was a major capital of the Assyrian Empire, which was a cruel enemy of the people of Israel (2 Kings 19:36). Jonah lived during the reign of Jeroboam II, a time when Israel was expanding its borders but facing the rising threat of Assyria (2 Kings 14:23,25). The wickedness of the Ninevites had become so great that it had come up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). The people of the city did not know their right hand from their left in spiritual matters (Jonah 4:11). The problem was that the people of Nineveh faced total destruction because their profound evil had come up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2).

## Jonah's Rebellious Condition
The word of the LORD came to Jonah, the son of Amittai (Jonah 1:1). Jonah was a prophet of the LORD from Gath-hepher (2 Kings 14:25). He had previously prophesied concerning the restoration of the territory of Israel (2 Kings 14:25). There is no record that Jonah performed any special religious acts like fasting or visiting the temple before God called him (Jonah 1:1-2). He was simply in his own country when the divine word reached him (Jonah 4:2).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came to Jonah with a direct and clear command.

> “Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me.” — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

After Jonah’s first attempt to flee, the LORD spoke to him a second time.

> “Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it the message that I tell you**.” — Jonah 3:2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his personal comfort and his safety to travel to a hostile foreign city (Jonah 1:3). He risked his reputation as a prophet by delivering a message of doom to a people he despised (Jonah 3:4). He also had to sacrifice his own sense of justice, as he preferred to see Nineveh destroyed rather than forgiven (Jonah 4:2). By obeying, he risked being seen as a friend to the enemies of Israel. He eventually sacrificed his own will to the compassion of the LORD (Jonah 4:10-11).

## The Initial Response
Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into the ship to go away from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). Jonah deliberately chose to rebel against the divine command because he knew the LORD was gracious and merciful (Jonah 4:2). He preferred to flee rather than see his enemies receive God's mercy (Jonah 4:2).

## The Eventual Reaction
The LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea to stop Jonah's flight (Jonah 1:4). Jonah was found sleeping during the storm while the pagan mariners prayed (Jonah 1:5). When he was identified by lot, Jonah confessed his rebellion and told the men to hurl him into the sea (Jonah 1:9-12). While in the belly of the fish, Jonah prayed and acknowledged that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:1,9). After being vomited onto dry land, he finally obeyed the second call and went to Nineveh (Jonah 3:3). He proclaimed the message of judgment throughout the city (Jonah 3:4). His reaction was a journey from deliberate rebellion to eventual submission to the word of the LORD (Jonah 1:3, 3:3).

## The Divine Miracle
God orchestrated several miracles to ensure His message reached Nineveh and to teach Jonah about His character. He appointed a great fish to save Jonah and later caused a whole city to repent.

> “And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and it **vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**.” — Jonah 1:17, 2:10 (ESV)

> “When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them**, and He did not do it.” — Jonah 3:10 (ESV)

The LORD fulfilled His intent to **call out against it** and deliver the **message that I tell you** to Nineveh (Jonah 1:2, 3:2).

## The Resulting Repentance
The people of Nineveh believed the word of God and proclaimed a fast (Jonah 3:5). The king himself covered himself with sackcloth and sat in ashes (Jonah 3:6). Because they turned from their evil way, the LORD relented and did not destroy the city (Jonah 3:10). This saved more than 120,000 people from death (Jonah 4:11). The mariners on the ship also came to fear the LORD and offered sacrifices to Him (Jonah 1:16). Jonah himself received a profound lesson on the universal mercy of the LORD (Jonah 4:10-11). The result was the preservation of a great city and a demonstration of God's love for all nations.

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city and his own rebellious heart. He was a prophet who tried to limit God's mercy to his own people. However, the LORD promised to bring a message of warning to Nineveh. Jonah initially sacrificed his obedience and tried to flee, but God intervened through a great storm and a fish. Jonah eventually responded by preaching the message he was given. God's miracle of repentance saved an entire nation from disaster. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and pride, but he gained a first hand experience of the boundless compassion of the LORD. His story proves that we can trust the LORD to be faithful to His purposes even when we fail. He is a gracious God Who relents from disaster.
