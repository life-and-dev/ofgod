# Jonah: The Reluctant Prophet

A prophet is commanded to preach to a city he despises. He flees, only to be caught in a storm and swallowed by a great fish. Discover how God's persistent calling and mercy reach even the most rebellious hearts.

## The Wickedness of Nineveh
Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire, a nation known for its extreme cruelty and violence toward its enemies (Jonah 1:2; Nahum 3:1). Their wickedness had come up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). For the people of Israel, Nineveh represented a constant threat and a symbol of oppressive pagan power (Nahum 3:1-7). Jonah, as a prophet from Gath-hepher in Galilee, likely shared the common Israelite sentiment of fear and hatred toward these brutal oppressors (2 Kings 14:25). The city was so vast that it took three days to walk through it (Jonah 3:3). The problem was that the great city of Nineveh was on the brink of divine destruction due to its overwhelming evil (Jonah 1:2, 3:4).

## Jonah's Prophet Condition
Jonah was a prophet of the LORD who had previously served during the reign of Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:25). There is no record of Jonah performing any specific fast or sacrifice before the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a servant of God in Israel when he received his sudden and difficult assignment (Jonah 1:1-2). He did nothing to initiate this particular encounter or to seek out this new mission (Jonah 1:1).

## The Call to Nineveh
God spoke directly to Jonah, commanding him to go to the heart of the enemy's empire to deliver a message of warning.

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, "**Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

## The Price of Obedience
Jonah had to sacrifice his personal comfort, his safety, and his national pride (Jonah 1:2-3). Entering Nineveh as a lone Hebrew prophet meant risking his life among a violent people (Jonah 1:2). He also risked his reputation as a prophet if God showed mercy to the hated Assyrians (Jonah 4:2). By obeying, he would be helping the very enemies who threatened his own nation (Jonah 4:2). He had to give up his desire for vengeance to fulfill God's purpose (Jonah 4:1-3).

## Jonah's Flight to Tarshish
Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into it to go with them to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He did this specifically to get away from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He refused the direct command to go to Nineveh (Jonah 1:2-3).

## Jonah's Prayer and Obedience
God hurled a great wind upon the sea which caused a mighty tempest (Jonah 1:4). The mariners were afraid and threw the cargo into the sea (Jonah 1:5). Jonah was fast asleep in the inner part of the ship (Jonah 1:5). The captain woke him and they cast lots to see who was responsible for the evil (Jonah 1:6-7). The lot fell on Jonah (Jonah 1:7). He told them he was a Hebrew who feared the LORD, the God of heaven, Who made the sea and the dry land (Jonah 1:9). He told the men to pick him up and hurl him into the sea so that the sea would quiet down for them (Jonah 1:12). Eventually, the men did as he said (Jonah 1:15). While inside the great fish for three days and three nights, Jonah prayed to the LORD his God (Jonah 1:17, 2:1). He acknowledged that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). After the fish vomited him out upon the dry land, the word of the LORD came to Jonah a second time (Jonah 2:10, 3:1). Jonah arose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD (Jonah 3:3). He called out that in forty days Nineveh would be overthrown (Jonah 3:4). Even after the city repented, Jonah struggled with his emotions and sat outside the city to see what would happen (Jonah 4:1,5). God used a plant, a worm, and a scorching east wind to teach Jonah about His compassion (Jonah 4:6-11). Jonah eventually submitted to God's will despite his initial rebellion and his personal desire for Nineveh's destruction (Jonah 3:3, 4:11).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD provided a great fish to swallow Jonah and preserve his life in the depths of the sea. Later, God worked a miracle of mass repentance in the heart of the pagan city.

> And the LORD **appointed a great fish** to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and it **vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**. — Jonah 1:17, 2:10 (ESV)

> When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it**. — Jonah 3:10 (ESV)

## The Result of Repentance
The entire city of Nineveh, from the greatest to the least, believed God and repented with fasting and sackcloth (Jonah 3:5). The king of Nineveh issued a decree for everyone to call urgently out to God and turn from their evil ways (Jonah 3:7-8). As a result, God spared the city and its one hundred and twenty thousand inhabitants (Jonah 3:10, 4:11). The mariners on the ship also feared the LORD exceedingly, offered a sacrifice, and made vows (Jonah 1:16). Jonah himself was humbled and became a living sign of God's power and mercy (Matthew 12:39-41). The miracle demonstrated that God's grace is not limited to Israel but is available to all who repent (Jonah 4:11).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of being called to preach to his nation's most brutal enemies. His limitation was his own prejudice and bitterness, which led him to flee from God's presence. Despite his rebellion and attempt to escape by sea, God did not forsake him. He provided a great fish to save Jonah and a second chance to fulfill his calling. When Jonah finally obeyed, God performed a massive miracle of repentance in Nineveh. The result was the salvation of an entire city and the sailors who witnessed the storm. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and pride, but he gained a front-row seat to the overwhelming mercy of God. His story proves that we can trust the LORD'S faithfulness even when we fail, for His compassion is far greater than our rebellion.
