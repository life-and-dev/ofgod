# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet receives a command to warn a brutal enemy empire of impending doom. Instead of obeying, he flees to the ends of the earth. His attempt to escape leads to a terrifying storm and a miraculous rescue that no one expected.

## The Problem of Nineveh's Wickedness

Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire and was known for its extreme violence and cruelty (Nahum 3:1). Their evil had come up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). The Assyrians were a major threat to Israel and were feared by all surrounding nations (2 Kings 15:19). Jonah was the son of Amittai and a prophet from Gath-hepher (Jonah 1:1; 2 Kings 14:25). He lived during the reign of Jeroboam II when Israel was expanding its borders (2 Kings 14:23-25). The people of Nineveh were pagans who did not know the LORD (Jonah 4:11). The problem was that the wickedness of this great city had reached a point where divine judgment was certain unless they repented (Jonah 1:2, 3:4).

## Jonah's Prophetic Condition

Jonah was a prophet of the LORD who had already seen his prophecies fulfilled in Israel (2 Kings 14:25). He did not perform any special fast or sacrifice before the LORD spoke to him (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a servant of God in his homeland when the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling

God spoke directly to Jonah to warn him of the coming judgment on Nineveh.

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, "Arise, **go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice

Jonah had to sacrifice his national pride and his personal sense of justice (Jonah 4:1-2). He risked his reputation as a prophet of Israel by going to a hated enemy (Jonah 4:2). He also risked his life by entering a city known for its brutality to deliver a message of destruction (Jonah 3:3-4). He sacrificed his comfort to undertake a long and dangerous journey to a foreign land (Jonah 3:3).

## Jonah's Initial Rebellion

Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into it, to go with them to Tarshish, away from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He deliberately chose to go in the opposite direction of Nineveh (Jonah 1:3).

## Jonah's Eventual Reaction

The LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea, and there was a mighty tempest (Jonah 1:4). Jonah was asleep in the inner part of the ship (Jonah 1:5). The mariners were afraid and cried out to their gods (Jonah 1:5). When Jonah was confronted by the shipmaster, he confessed that he was fleeing from the LORD, the God of heaven, **Who** made the sea and the dry land (Jonah 1:6-9). He told the men to throw him into the sea so that it would quiet down for them (Jonah 1:12). After they threw him in, the sea ceased from its raging (Jonah 1:15).

While inside the great fish, Jonah prayed to the LORD (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged that **His** holy temple was where he looked (Jonah 2:4). He promised to sacrifice to the LORD with the voice of thanksgiving and to pay what he had vowed (Jonah 2:9). He declared that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9).

After being vomited out upon the dry land, the word of the LORD came to Jonah a second time (Jonah 3:1-2). This time, Jonah arose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD (Jonah 3:3). He walked into the city and cried out that in forty days Nineveh would be overthrown (Jonah 3:4). Even after the city repented, Jonah reacted with anger because **He** is a gracious God and merciful (Jonah 4:1-2). He sat outside the city to see what would become of it (Jonah 4:5). He learned a final lesson about God's compassion through a plant that withered (Jonah 4:6-11).

## The Divine Miracle

God prepared a great fish to save Jonah from drowning and later caused an entire pagan city to repent at a single warning.

> And **the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah.** And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . And **the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land.** . . And the people of Nineveh believed God. They called for a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least of them. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it.** — Jonah 1:17, 2:10, 3:5,10 (ESV)

In this miracle, God fulfilled **His** intention to **call out against it** to bring about a change (Jonah 1:2). **He** delivered Jonah from the depths of the sea to complete **His** mission.

## The National Result

The miracle resulted in the survival of over one hundred and twenty thousand people in Nineveh (Jonah 4:11). The king and the people turned from their evil ways and from the violence that was in their hands (Jonah 3:8). The sailors on the ship also feared the LORD exceedingly, offered a sacrifice, and made vows (Jonah 1:16). Jonah himself was preserved and learned the depth of God's mercy for all nations (Jonah 4:11). The repentance of Nineveh stood as a witness against the unfaithfulness of Israel (Matthew 12:41).

## Conclusion

Jonah faced the problem of a wicked enemy city and his own prejudice against them. He was limited by his nationalism and his desire for vengeance. However, the LORD promised to judge Nineveh and called Jonah to be **His** messenger. Jonah responded first with rebellion and later with a reluctant but effective obedience. God intervened by preserving Jonah in a fish and softening the hearts of the Ninevites. The result was a massive national repentance and the sparing of many lives. Jonah sacrificed his pride and his hatred, but he gained a firsthand experience of the sovereign mercy of the LORD. His story proves that we can trust the LORD to be faithful to **His** character even when we fail. **He** is the God **Who** pursues the rebellious and offers grace to the repentant.
