# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet is given a mission to warn a city of its impending doom, but he chooses to flee in the opposite direction. His journey involves a violent storm and a descent into the depths of the sea. Discover how God's mercy prevails despite man's rebellion.

## The Wickedness of Nineveh
The word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai (Jonah 1:1). Nineveh was an exceedingly great city, three days' journey in breadth (Jonah 3:3). However, its evil had come up before God (Jonah 1:2). The people of Nineveh were known for their violence and evil ways (Jonah 3:8). They were a threat to Israel and lived in opposition to God's righteousness (Jonah 1:2). The problem was that the city was ripe for judgment due to its overwhelming wickedness (Jonah 1:2).

## Jonah's Sudden Assignment
Jonah was a prophet from Gath-hepher (2 Kings 14:25). He was already established in his role as a messenger for the LORD (2 Kings 14:25). He did not perform any special sacrifice or prayer to receive this new mission (Jonah 1:1-2). The LORD simply spoke to him and gave him a clear command (Jonah 1:1). He was in a position of spiritual responsibility when the call came (Jonah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD gave Jonah a direct and specific command to go to the heart of the Assyrian empire (Jonah 1:1-2).

> "Arise, **go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before **Me**." — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

After Jonah was vomited onto the dry land, the LORD spoke to him a second time (Jonah 3:1-2).

> "Arise, **go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it the message that I tell you**." — Jonah 3:2 (ESV)

## The Risk of the Mission
Jonah had to sacrifice his personal comfort and national pride (Jonah 1:3). He risked his life by entering a city known for its brutality toward its enemies (Jonah 3:8). He also risked his reputation as a prophet if God chose to show mercy to his enemies (Jonah 4:2). He feared that God's grace would extend to those he believed deserved destruction (Jonah 4:2).

## Jonah's Flight to Tarshish
Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into it to go with them (Jonah 1:3). He deliberately disobeyed the direct command of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He thought he could escape the reach of **His** authority (Jonah 1:3).

## Jonah's Eventual Obedience
The LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea (Jonah 1:4). The mariners were afraid and cried out to their gods (Jonah 1:5). Jonah was asleep in the inner part of the ship (Jonah 1:5). When the lot fell on him, he confessed that he was a Hebrew who feared the LORD (Jonah 1:9). He told them to throw him into the sea so that it would quiet down for them (Jonah 1:12). The LORD appointed a great fish to swallow Jonah (Jonah 1:17). He was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights (Jonah 1:17). Jonah prayed to the LORD his God from the belly of the fish (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). The LORD spoke to the fish and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land (Jonah 2:10). Then he arose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD (Jonah 3:3). He began to go into the city and called out the warning (Jonah 3:4). He waited to see what would happen to the city (Jonah 4:5). Even when he was angry with God's mercy, he remained under **His** sovereign care (Jonah 4:6-11).

## The Preservation of Jonah
God used a violent storm and a great fish to bring Jonah back to his path (Jonah 1:4,17). **He** showed **His** power over nature to ensure Jonah fulfilled his task (Jonah 1:4).

> And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And **Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights**. . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and **it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**. — Jonah 1:17, 2:10 (ESV)

The LORD also provided a plant to shelter him (Jonah 4:6).

> Now the LORD God appointed a plant and made it come up over Jonah, that it might be a shade over his head, to **save him from his discomfort**. — Jonah 4:6 (ESV)

## The Results of the Miracle
The people of Nineveh believed God and called for a fast (Jonah 3:5). The king of Nineveh issued a proclamation for everyone to turn from their evil way (Jonah 3:7-8). God saw what they did and relented of the disaster that **He** had said **He** would do to them (Jonah 3:10). The entire city was spared from destruction (Jonah 3:10). This miracle affected both the people of Nineveh and Jonah's understanding of God's heart (Jonah 3:10, 4:11). This event showed that God's mercy is available to all who repent (Jonah 3:10; 4:11).

## Conclusion
Jonah was a prophet who tried to flee from God's presence because he disagreed with **His** mercy toward his enemies. He faced a life-threatening storm and was swallowed by a great fish due to his rebellion. However, the LORD was faithful and preserved Jonah to complete his mission. Jonah eventually obeyed and warned Nineveh, leading to a massive repentance that saved thousands of lives. While Jonah sacrificed his comfort and pride, he gained a deeper understanding of God's compassion. His story demonstrates that the LORD'S purposes will prevail even when **His** servants fail. God did not forsake Jonah but used his limitations to bring about a great miracle of salvation.
