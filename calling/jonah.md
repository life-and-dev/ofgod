# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A reluctant prophet attempts to outrun the Almighty by boarding a ship headed in the opposite direction. His flight leads him into the heart of a terrifying storm and the belly of a leviathan. Discover why he ran and how God's mercy reached the depths.

## The Problem of Assyrian Cruelty
Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire, a nation known for its extreme cruelty and violence (Nahum 3:1). They were a constant threat to Israel and other neighboring nations (2 Kings 18:13). The city was so large that it took three days to walk across it (Jonah 3:3). The people of Nineveh were trapped in their own wickedness and faced imminent divine judgment (Jonah 1:2). Jonah, a prophet from Gath-hepher, lived during the reign of Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:25). He likely shared the common Israelite hatred for the oppressive Assyrians (Nahum 3:1). The political environment was one of fear and hostility toward the growing Assyrian power (Jonah 1:1-2). The problem was that the great city of Nineveh had reached a pinnacle of evil that required divine intervention and judgment (Jonah 1:2).

## Jonah's Simple Condition
Jonah was a recognized prophet of the LORD (2 Kings 14:25). He did nothing special to get God's attention before the call (Jonah 1:1). He was not seeking a new mission when the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1). There is no record of him praying or fasting before God spoke to him (Jonah 1:1-2). He was simply a servant of God in Israel when he received the command to go to a foreign and hostile nation (Jonah 1:2).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came to Jonah twice, first as a command and later as a renewed mission after his failure.

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it the message that I tell you**." — Jonah 3:2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his comfort and his nationalistic pride (Jonah 1:3). He risked his life by traveling to the heart of a violent enemy empire (Nahum 3:1). He also risked his reputation as a prophet in Israel by offering a chance of repentance to their enemies (Jonah 4:2). If he succeeded, he would be saving the very people who threatened his own nation (Jonah 4:2). He sacrificed his own sense of justice for the sake of God's mercy (Jonah 4:2).

## The Initial Response
Jonah immediately rebelled and fled in the opposite direction (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish, away from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into the ship (Jonah 1:3). He tried to hide from God by sleeping in the inner part of the ship during a great storm (Jonah 1:5). He refused to pray even when the captain asked him to (Jonah 1:6).

## Jonah's Eventual Reaction
After being cast into the sea and swallowed by a great fish, Jonah finally turned to the LORD (Jonah 1:15,17). He prayed from the belly of the fish, acknowledging that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:1-9). When the fish vomited him onto dry land, he obeyed the second call and went to Nineveh (Jonah 2:10, 3:3). He walked into the city and proclaimed that it would be overthrown in forty days (Jonah 3:4). Even after the city repented, Jonah struggled with his emotions (Jonah 4:1). He became angry because the LORD was gracious and merciful (Jonah 4:2). He sat outside the city to see what would happen to it (Jonah 4:5). He had to learn through the lesson of a plant that God cares for all people (Jonah 4:10-11). Despite his personal failures and bad attitude, he completed the mission he was given (Jonah 3:3).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD performed a series of miracles to save both His prophet and the city. He appointed a great fish to rescue Jonah and moved the hearts of an entire pagan city to repent.

> And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and **it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land.** . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them**, and **He did not do it.** — Jonah 1:17, 2:10, 3:10 (ESV)

The LORD fulfilled His command for Jonah to **call out against it** by preserving him and then showing that the message reached its intended goal (Jonah 1:2, 3:10).

## The Widespread Result
The entire city of Nineveh, from the king to the animals, fasted and repented (Jonah 3:5-9). The LORD spared the city from destruction (Jonah 3:10). The sailors on the ship also feared the LORD and offered sacrifices (Jonah 1:16). Jonah himself was humbled and taught a profound lesson about the scope of God's mercy (Jonah 4:11). The result was the temporary salvation of a vast population of over one hundred and twenty thousand people (Jonah 4:11). It served as a witness to the power of God over all nations (Jonah 3:10).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city and his own prejudice. He was limited by his hatred for his enemies and his attempt to flee from God. However, the LORD promised to use him to bring a message of judgment. Jonah responded with initial rebellion followed by a prayer of desperation and eventual obedience. God intervened by rescuing him from the sea and turning the hearts of the Ninevites. The result was a city spared and a prophet corrected. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and pride, but he gained a first-hand experience of the limitless grace of God. His story proves that we can trust the LORD to be faithful even when we are unfaithful. He is a God Who relentlessly pursues both the lost and His wayward servants.
