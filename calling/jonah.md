# Jonah: The Reluctant Prophet

A prophet is given a mission to a city he despises. He attempts to flee God's presence, only to find himself in the depths of the ocean. Discover how a divine rescue leads to one of the greatest mass repentances in history.

## Nineveh's Wickedness

The city of Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire (Jonah 1:2). Its people were known for their extreme wickedness and violence (Jonah 1:2; Nahum 3:1). The political environment was one of imperial expansion and brutal oppression of neighboring nations (Nahum 3:1-3,19). This empire posed a significant threat to the security and existence of Israel (2 Kings 15:19,29). Jonah, a prophet from Gath-hepher, lived during the reign of Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:25). The problem was that Nineveh's evil had reached a point where divine judgment was impending unless they repented (Jonah 1:2).

## Jonah's Condition

Jonah was a recognized prophet in Israel who had previously seen his prophecies of national expansion fulfilled (2 Kings 14:25). He did not perform any special prayer, fast, or sacrifice to receive this specific call from the LORD (Jonah 1:1). The word of the LORD simply came to him as he was about his work (Jonah 1:1). He was in a state of nationalistic pride and prejudice against the enemies of his people (Jonah 4:2).

## The Divine Calling

The LORD spoke directly to Jonah, giving him a specific and difficult command to warn the city of Nineveh (Jonah 1:1-2).

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, "Arise, **go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

Later, after Jonah's rescue, the LORD spoke to him a second time (Jonah 3:1).

> Then the word of the LORD came to Jonah the second time, saying, "Arise, **go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it the message that I tell you**." — Jonah 3:1-2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice

Jonah had to sacrifice his reputation as a patriot and his personal desire for Nineveh's destruction (Jonah 4:2). By warning Nineveh, he risked seeing his nation's enemies spared and strengthened (Jonah 4:1-3). He also risked his life by traveling to a city known for its brutality (Jonah 1:2). He sacrificed his comfort and safety to obey a command that went against his own conscience and prejudices (Jonah 1:3).

## Jonah's Initial Response

Jonah immediately rebelled and tried to flee from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish, paying the fare to escape his assignment (Jonah 1:3). He chose to travel in the opposite direction of Nineveh (Jonah 1:3). He remained silent and fell asleep in the hold of the ship even as a great storm threatened to destroy the vessel (Jonah 1:5).

## Jonah's Reaction

When the sailors confronted him about the storm, Jonah confessed that he was fleeing from the LORD (Jonah 1:9-10). He trusted God with his life by instructing the sailors to throw him into the sea to save themselves (Jonah 1:12). From the belly of the great fish, Jonah prayed and acknowledged that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:1-9). He repented of his rebellion and vowed to fulfill what he had promised (Jonah 2:9). After being vomited onto dry land, Jonah obeyed the second call and traveled to Nineveh (Jonah 3:3). He walked through the city and proclaimed that in forty days Nineveh would be overthrown (Jonah 3:4). When the people repented and God spared the city, Jonah was exceedingly angry and prayed to die (Jonah 4:1-3). He sat outside the city to see what would happen, still hoping for its destruction (Jonah 4:5). He responded to God's lesson of the plant with continued anger (Jonah 4:9). Ultimately, he had to accept God's right to show mercy to whom He chooses (Jonah 4:10-11).

## The Divine Miracle

The LORD sovereignly controlled the elements and a great fish to preserve Jonah and bring Nineveh to repentance (Jonah 1:17, 2:10).

> And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land. — Jonah 1:17, 2:10 (ESV)

God also fulfilled His command that Jonah would **go to Nineveh** and **call out against it** (Jonah 3:3-5).

> So **Jonah arose and went to Nineveh**, according to the word of the LORD. . . And the people of Nineveh believed God. They called for a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least of them. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it.** — Jonah 3:3,5,10 (ESV)

## The Global Result

The miracle resulted in the survival of over one hundred and twenty thousand people and much cattle (Jonah 4:11). The entire city of Nineveh turned from their evil ways and violence (Jonah 3:10). This repentance delayed the judgment of Assyria for several generations (Nahum 3:1). Jonah himself was humbled and taught a profound lesson about God's universal compassion (Jonah 4:10-11). The sailors on the ship also came to fear the LORD, offering sacrifices and making vows (Jonah 1:16). This event served as a sign to future generations, as Jesus later used Jonah's three days in the fish as a prophecy of his own resurrection (Matthew 12:39-41).

## Conclusion

Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city that he wanted destroyed. He was limited by his prejudice and his initial flight from God. However, the LORD called him to be a messenger of warning. Jonah eventually sacrificed his desire for vengeance and risked his life in the sea. God intervened by preserving him in a fish and working a miracle of repentance in Nineveh. The result was the salvation of a city and a demonstration of God's mercy to all nations. Jonah sacrificed his pride and comfort, but he gained the honor of being a witness to God's grace. His story proves that we can trust the LORD even when His ways challenge our views. He is faithful to use even reluctant servants to accomplish His merciful purposes.
