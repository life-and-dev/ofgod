# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet receives a terrifying mission to warn a brutal enemy city of impending doom. Instead of obeying, he boards a ship to the opposite end of the world. His attempt to escape leads to a supernatural encounter in the depths of the ocean.

## The Wickedness of Nineveh
Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire and was known for its extreme violence and cruelty toward its enemies (Nahum 3:1-3). The city was an exceedingly great city that required a three days' journey to cross (Jonah 3:3). The evil of its people had become so great that it came up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). As an Israeli prophet, Jonah likely viewed the Assyrians as a direct threat to his own nation (2 Kings 14:25). The political environment was one of constant warfare and oppression by the Assyrians. The problem was that the people of Nineveh were living in such wickedness that God determined to overthrow the city within forty days (Jonah 1:2, 3:4).

## Jonah's Spiritual Condition
Jonah was the son of Amittai and a prophet from Gath-hepher (2 Kings 14:25). He was a Hebrew who feared the LORD, the God of heaven, Who made the sea and the dry land (Jonah 1:9). Before God spoke to him, there is no record of Jonah performing any special sacrifice, fast, or prayer to seek a new mission (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a servant of God living in Israel when the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
God spoke directly to Jonah, giving him a clear command to preach against the city of Nineveh.

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

Later, after Jonah's rescue, the LORD spoke to him again with the same instructions.

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it the message that I tell you**." — Jonah 3:2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his nationalistic pride and his desire for justice against Israel's enemies (Jonah 4:2). By warning Nineveh, he risked the survival of his own people, as a repentant Assyria would remain a threat (Nahum 1:1). He also risked his reputation as a prophet if God relented from the promised destruction (Jonah 4:2). He sacrificed his comfort and safety to travel to a city known for its violence (Jonah 1:2, 3:3).

## Jonah's Initial Response
Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into the ship to go away from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). Jonah deliberately refused God's command because he knew God was gracious and might spare the city (Jonah 4:2). He preferred the destruction of his enemies over their salvation (Jonah 4:1-2).

## Jonah's Ultimate Reaction
After being swallowed by a great fish, Jonah prayed to the LORD his God from the belly of the fish (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged that his life was fainting away and remembered the LORD (Jonah 2:7). He vowed to sacrifice to God with the voice of thanksgiving and to pay what he had vowed (Jonah 2:9). After the fish vomited him onto dry land, Jonah obeyed the second call (Jonah 3:3). He went to Nineveh and proclaimed the message of judgment (Jonah 3:4). Even though he was initially angry when God spared the city, he had fulfilled the task (Jonah 4:1). He sat east of the city to see what would happen (Jonah 4:5). Through a plant and a worm, he learned about God's compassion for all people (Jonah 4:6-11).

## The Divine Miracle
God used a series of supernatural events to bring Jonah to repentance and save Nineveh. He controlled the wind, the sea, a great fish, a plant, and a worm to accomplish His purpose.

> "But the LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea, and there was a mighty tempest on the sea, so that the ship threatened to break up. . . And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. . . And **the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land.** . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it.**" — Jonah 1:4,17, 2:10, 3:10 (ESV)

The miracle of the fish preserved Jonah's life so he could **call out against it** as originally commanded (Jonah 1:2). The miracle of repentance in Nineveh meant God **relented from disaster** just as Jonah feared and God intended (Jonah 4:2).

## The Result of Obedience
The miracle resulted in the total repentance of Nineveh (Jonah 3:5). The people believed God, called for a fast, and put on sackcloth (Jonah 3:5). Even the king of Nineveh arose from his throne and sat in ashes (Jonah 3:6). He issued a decree for everyone to turn from their evil ways and violence (Jonah 3:7-8). God spared more than 120,000 people and much cattle from destruction (Jonah 4:11). Jonah himself was humbled and taught a lesson about the depth of God's mercy (Jonah 4:10-11). The neighbors and surrounding nations saw that the God of Israel has power over both the sea and the hearts of men (Jonah 1:16).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of Nineveh's extreme wickedness and his own desire to see them destroyed. He was limited by his prejudice and his attempt to flee from the presence of the LORD. God promised to use him to warn the city and despite Jonah's rebellion, God's purpose stood. He intervened with a storm and a great fish to rescue His reluctant prophet. Jonah eventually responded with obedience and Nineveh was spared through a massive revival. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and his pride, but he gained a front-row seat to one of the greatest miracles of mercy in history. His story proves that we can trust the LORD'S faithfulness even when we fail. He is the God Who pursues us even in our rebellion.
