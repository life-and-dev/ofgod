# Jonah: a Second Chance

A prophet attempts to flee from the presence of the Almighty by boarding a ship to the edge of the world. A violent storm and a deep-sea encounter force him to face the mission he feared. Discover how God's mercy extends even to the most undeserving.

## The Problem of Nineveh's Wickedness

The city of Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire, a nation known for its extreme cruelty and violence (Nahum 3:1). Their evil had become so great that it came up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). The political environment was one of constant threat to Israel, as the Assyrians were a dominant and aggressive power in the ancient Near East (2 Kings 15:19). The people of Nineveh did not know their right hand from their left in a spiritual sense (Jonah 4:11). They were trapped in a cycle of plunder, lies, and witchcraft (Nahum 3:1,4). The problem was that the massive city was facing imminent divine destruction because of its persistent and overwhelming wickedness (Jonah 3:4).

## Jonah's Chosen Condition

Jonah, the son of Amittai, was a prophet from Gath-hepher who had previously prophesied favor for Israel (2 Kings 14:25). He did not perform any special act of worship, fasting, or prayer to receive this new commission (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a servant of the LORD living in Israel when the divine word came to him (Jonah 1:1). He was not seeking a mission to a foreign and hostile nation when God intervened in his life (Jonah 1:1-2).

## The Divine Calling

The LORD spoke to Jonah directly to send him on a mission to warn the enemies of his people.

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, "Arise, **go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice

Jonah had to sacrifice his personal comfort and his nationalistic pride (Jonah 1:3, 4:1-2). By going to Nineveh, he risked his reputation as a prophet if the enemies of Israel were spared (Jonah 4:2). He also faced the physical danger of entering a city known for its violence (Nahum 3:1). He eventually sacrificed his own safety by asking to be thrown into the sea to save the sailors (Jonah 1:12).

## Jonah's Initial Response

Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa, found a ship, and paid the fare to go as far away as possible (Jonah 1:3). He chose deliberate rebellion instead of obedience (Jonah 1:3). He showed a lack of trust in God's plan and a deep-seated hatred for the people he was sent to warn (Jonah 4:2). He even fell into a deep sleep during the storm, trying to ignore the chaos his disobedience caused (Jonah 1:5).

## Jonah's Eventual Reaction

After being thrown into the sea, Jonah prayed to the LORD from the belly of the fish (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged that his life was fainting away and remembered the LORD (Jonah 2:7). He promised to pay what he had vowed and declared that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). When the LORD spoke to him a second time, Jonah arose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD (Jonah 3:1-3). He walked into the heart of the great city and proclaimed the message of judgment (Jonah 3:4). Even after the city repented, Jonah reacted with anger and frustration (Jonah 4:1). He sat outside the city to see what would happen to it (Jonah 4:5). He complained to **Him** about **His** mercy and even asked to die because the Ninevites were spared (Jonah 4:2-3). Despite his lingering bitterness, he had completed the task he was given (Jonah 3:10, 4:1). He learned through the experience of a plant and a worm that the LORD'S compassion is far greater than his own (Jonah 4:6-11). He eventually accepted that he could not escape the presence or the purposes of the Almighty (Psalm 139:7-10). His story shows a man who struggled with the character of the God he served but could not deny **His** sovereignty (Jonah 1:9, 4:2).

## The Divine Miracle

The LORD performed several miracles to preserve Jonah and to bring an entire city to repentance (Jonah 1:17, 3:10). **He** controlled the wind, the sea, a great fish, a plant, and a worm to accomplish **His** will (Jonah 1:4,15,17, 4:6-7).

> And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . And **the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land.** . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it.** — Jonah 1:17, 2:10, 3:10 (ESV)

In these actions, the LORD fulfilled the purpose of the call to **go to Nineveh** and **call out against it** by ensuring the message was delivered and the city was spared (Jonah 3:4,10). **He** demonstrated that **He** is a gracious God and merciful, slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love (Jonah 4:2).

## The Lasting Result

The miracle resulted in the survival of over one hundred and twenty thousand people in Nineveh (Jonah 4:11). The king and the people of the city turned from their evil ways and cried mightily to God (Jonah 3:7-8). This mass repentance is one of the greatest recorded in the Bible (Matthew 12:41). The sailors on the ship also feared the LORD exceedingly, offered a sacrifice, and made vows after the storm ceased (Jonah 1:16). For Jonah, the result was a profound lesson in the universality of God's mercy (Jonah 4:10-11). Jesus later used the "sign of the prophet Jonah" as a shadow of his own death and resurrection (Matthew 12:39-40). The story remains a witness to all nations that the LORD desires repentance rather than destruction (Ezekiel 33:11).

## Conclusion

Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city and his own prejudice. He was a prophet limited by nationalism and a desire for vengeance. However, the LORD promised to warn the city and sovereignly pursued Jonah when he fled. Jonah sacrificed his pride and his life in the sea, but he gained a firsthand experience of God's incredible grace. The LORD intervened with a great fish and a redirected storm to bring **His** servant back to the mission. The result was the salvation of a city and a lasting testimony. Jonah's story proves that we cannot outrun God's calling or **His** mercy. **He** is faithful to **His** purposes even when **His** servants fail.
