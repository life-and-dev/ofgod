# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet receives a terrifying mission to warn an enemy empire of their imminent destruction. Instead of obeying, he boards a ship to the edge of the world. His attempt to escape leads him into the depths of a storm and an impossible sanctuary.

## The Problem of Wickedness
Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire (Jonah 1:2). The Assyrians were known for their extreme cruelty and violence toward other nations (Nahum 3:1). They were a direct threat to the people of Israel (2 Kings 15:19,29). The wickedness of the city had become so great that it came up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). The people were living in spiritual darkness and moral decay (Jonah 4:11). They did not know their right hand from their left (Jonah 4:11). The problem was that a city of more than one hundred and twenty thousand people was facing imminent divine judgment because of their overwhelming evil (Jonah 1:2).

## Jonah's Sudden Condition
Jonah was a prophet of the LORD from Gath-hepher (2 Kings 14:25). He had previously served during the reign of Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:25). Jonah did not perform any special ceremony or prayer to receive this specific word (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a servant of God when the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came to Jonah with a direct command to confront the great city.

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, “**Arise, go to Nineveh**, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me.” — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

Later, after Jonah's rescue, the LORD called him a second time.

> Then the word of the LORD came to Jonah the second time, saying, “**Arise, go to Nineveh**, that great city, and **call out against it the message that I tell you**.” — Jonah 3:1-2 (ESV)

## The Required Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his personal comfort and national pride (Jonah 1:3, 4:1-2). He risked his reputation as a prophet among his own people by offering a chance for repentance to their enemies (Jonah 4:2). He also risked his life by entering a city known for its violence (Nahum 3:1).

## The Initial Response
Jonah immediately rebelled against the command of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into the ship to go with them (Jonah 1:3). He believed he could hide from the Creator of the sea and the dry land (Jonah 1:9).

## The Reluctant Reaction
The LORD sent a mighty tempest on the sea to stop Jonah's flight (Jonah 1:4). The mariners were afraid and cast lots to find the cause of the storm (Jonah 1:5,7). The lot fell on Jonah (Jonah 1:7). He confessed that he was fleeing from the LORD (Jonah 1:10). He told the sailors to throw him into the sea so that the storm would quiet down for them (Jonah 1:12). After they threw him overboard, the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow Jonah (Jonah 1:17). He was in the belly of the fish for three days and three nights (Jonah 1:17). In his distress, Jonah prayed to the LORD his God from the belly of the fish (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). The LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land (Jonah 2:10). Jonah then obeyed the second call (Jonah 3:3). He went to Nineveh and cried out that in forty days the city would be overthrown (Jonah 3:4). He walked through the city proclaiming the message of judgment (Jonah 3:4).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD demonstrated His power over nature and the hearts of men.

> And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . And **the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it**. — Jonah 1:17, 2:10, 3:10 (ESV)

The LORD fulfilled His intent to **go to Nineveh** and **call out against it** through His reluctant prophet (Jonah 1:2). He also fulfilled His promise of mercy when the city repented (Jonah 3:10).

## The Remarkable Result
The people of Nineveh believed God (Jonah 3:5). They called for a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least of them (Jonah 3:5). Even the king of Nineveh arose from his throne and covered himself with sackcloth (Jonah 3:6). He issued a proclamation for everyone to turn from his evil way and from the violence that is in his hands (Jonah 3:7-8). Because of their repentance, God spared the entire city (Jonah 3:10). The sailors on the ship also feared the LORD exceedingly and offered a sacrifice (Jonah 1:16). Jonah himself learned the depth of God's compassion for all nations (Jonah 4:10-11).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city and his own prejudice against them. He was a disobedient prophet who tried to flee from his calling. However, the LORD promised to use him to deliver a message of warning. Jonah sacrificed his own will and safety to finally enter Nineveh after a miraculous rescue from the sea. God's intervention through the great fish and the subsequent repentance of the city showed His sovereignty. The result was the salvation of over one hundred thousand people and a testimony of God's mercy. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and pride, but he gained a firsthand experience of the relentless grace of God. This story proves that the LORD is faithful to His purpose even when His servants fail.
