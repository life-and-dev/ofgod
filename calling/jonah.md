# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet is sent to the capital of a brutal empire with a message of doom. Instead of obeying, he flees to the opposite end of the world. Witness how God pursues His reluctant servant through a storm and a great fish.

## The Cruelty of Nineveh
Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire, known for its extreme violence and cruelty toward other nations (Nahum 3:1,19). The city was exceedingly great but its evil had come up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2, 3:3). The Assyrians were a constant threat to Israel and were infamous for their brutal warfare and oppression (2 Kings 18:13,19). Jonah, a prophet from Gath-hepher in Israel, lived during the reign of Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:25). He knew that God is gracious and merciful, and he likely feared that Nineveh would be spared if they repented (Jonah 4:2). The problem was that a wicked and violent city faced imminent divine judgment unless they heard a warning from a prophet who did not want to go (Jonah 1:2).

## Jonah's Simple Condition
Jonah was a prophet of the LORD who had previously seen God's word fulfilled in Israel (2 Kings 14:25). He did not perform any special ritual or seek a new mission before the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a servant of God in his homeland when he received the unexpected command to go to Nineveh (Jonah 1:1-2).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came to Jonah twice, first with a command to go and later to reiterate the mission after his rescue. God spoke directly to His prophet to ensure the message reached the wicked city.

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, "Arise, **go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

After Jonah's experience in the fish, the LORD spoke to him again.

> Then the word of the LORD came to Jonah the second time, saying, "Arise, **go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it the message that I tell you**." — Jonah 3:1-2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah risked his reputation and his national pride by going to an enemy city (Jonah 4:1-2). He sacrificed his comfort and safety to travel to a place known for its cruelty (Nahum 3:1). He also risked being seen as a traitor to his own people by offering a chance of repentance to their oppressors (Jonah 4:2). Ultimately, he had to sacrifice his own will and his desire for vengeance to obey the LORD (Jonah 4:3,9).

## Jonah's Initial Response
Jonah immediately rebelled by fleeing in the opposite direction (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and paid the fare for a ship bound for Tarshish to escape the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He chose to desert his calling and hide in the inner part of a ship while a great storm threatened the lives of the mariners (Jonah 1:4-5). His refusal was a deliberate act of disobedience driven by his disapproval of God's mercy (Jonah 4:2).

## Jonah's Faithful Reaction
After the storm and his time in the fish, Jonah eventually responded to the LORD'S second call with obedience (Jonah 3:3). He arose and went to Nineveh, which was an exceedingly great city requiring a three-day journey to cross (Jonah 3:3). Jonah began to go into the city, going a day's journey, and he cried out the message God gave him (Jonah 3:4). He proclaimed that in forty days Nineveh would be overthrown (Jonah 3:4). Despite his earlier reluctance, he spoke the truth of the coming judgment to the people (Jonah 3:4). However, his internal reaction remained troubled (Jonah 4:1). When the people repented and God spared the city, Jonah became exceedingly angry and prayed to the LORD to take his life (Jonah 4:1-3). He sat outside the city to see what would happen, still hoping for its destruction (Jonah 4:5). God used a plant, a worm, and a scorching wind to teach Jonah about His compassion for the lost (Jonah 4:6-11). Jonah's journey shows a reluctant prophet who obeyed the outward command while struggling to align his heart with the LORD'S mercy (Jonah 4:9-11). He witnessed the power of God's word to bring a whole nation to its knees (Jonah 3:5-9).

## The Divine Miracle
God sovereignly directed the natural world to preserve Jonah and to bring about the repentance of Nineveh. He appointed a great fish to save His prophet and moved the hearts of an entire city.

> And **the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah**. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. — Jonah 1:17 (ESV)

> And **the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**. — Jonah 2:10 (ESV)

> When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it**. — Jonah 3:10 (ESV)

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the total repentance of Nineveh, from the king to the animals (Jonah 3:5-8). The people believed God, called for a fast, and put on sackcloth (Jonah 3:5). The king issued a proclamation for everyone to turn from their evil ways and violence (Jonah 3:7-8). As a result, God saw their turning and did not bring the disaster He had threatened (Jonah 3:10). The city was spared from immediate destruction, and a violent people were temporarily humbled before the LORD (Jonah 3:10). For Jonah, the experience revealed his own lack of compassion and God's right to show mercy to all nations (Jonah 4:10-11). The account served as a sign to later generations of the power of repentance and God's concern for the Gentiles (Matthew 12:41).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of delivering a message to a cruel enemy. He was limited by his own prejudice and a failure to trust God's mercy for those he hated. Despite his flight to Tarshish and his anger at God's grace, the LORD remained faithful to His purpose. God intervened with a storm and a great fish to redirect His servant and then spared a city of over a hundred and twenty thousand people. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and his pride, but he gained a front-row seat to the greatest revival in history. This story proves that the LORD is faithful even when we are reluctant. He does not forsake His servants in their failure but continues to pursue them with His grace.
