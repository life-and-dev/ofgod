# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet receives a mission to warn his nation's greatest enemies. Instead of obeying, he boards a ship to flee as far as possible from God's presence. His journey leads to a storm, a sacrifice, and an encounter in the depths of the ocean.

## The Wickedness of Nineveh
The city of Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire (Jonah 1:2). It was a great city but filled with extreme wickedness and violence (Jonah 1:2). The Assyrians were notorious for their cruelty toward their enemies, including the people of Israel (Nahum 3:1,19). This political environment made Nineveh a place of terror for surrounding nations (Nahum 3:1). The LORD observed that their evil had come up before **Him** (Jonah 1:2). The spiritual state of the city was one of total rebellion against God's laws (Jonah 1:2). There was an urgent need for a prophetic warning to avert divine judgment (Jonah 1:2). The problem was that Nineveh faced imminent destruction because of its overwhelming corruption (Jonah 1:2, 3:4).

## Jonah's Prophetic Condition
Jonah was the son of Amittai and a recognized prophet of the LORD (Jonah 1:1; 2 Kings 14:25). He lived during the reign of Jeroboam II in the Northern Kingdom of Israel (2 Kings 14:25). Jonah did not perform any special fast or ritual to attract God's attention (Jonah 1:1). The word of the LORD simply came to him as part of his prophetic office (Jonah 1:1). He was a servant of the LORD who was already in a position to hear from **Him** (Jonah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke directly to Jonah, giving him a specific mission to the enemies of his people.

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, “**Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me.” — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

## The Risk of Mercy
Jonah risked his reputation as a prophet in Israel by going to a Gentile nation (Jonah 1:2-3). He feared that if Nineveh repented, God would show mercy to Israel's enemies (Jonah 4:2). This could be seen as a betrayal of his own people (Jonah 4:2). He also risked his life by entering a city known for its violence (Jonah 1:2; Nahum 3:1). He had to sacrifice his comfort and nationalistic pride to obey God's command (Jonah 1:3; 4:2).

## A Prophet on the Run
Jonah immediately rebelled against the command of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He rose to flee to Tarshish to escape the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going in the opposite direction of Nineveh (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went into the ship to hide from God's calling (Jonah 1:3). Jonah's response was one of deliberate refusal and distrust of God's plan (Jonah 1:3).

## From the Sea to the City
God did not allow Jonah to escape **His** presence (Jonah 1:4). The LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea, causing a mighty tempest that threatened to break the ship (Jonah 1:4). While the mariners prayed to their gods and threw cargo overboard, Jonah was fast asleep in the inner part of the ship (Jonah 1:5). The captain woke him and urged him to call out to his God (Jonah 1:6). The sailors cast lots to find the cause of the storm, and the lot fell on Jonah (Jonah 1:7). He confessed that he was a Hebrew who feared the LORD, the God of heaven, **Who** made the sea and the dry land (Jonah 1:9). He admitted he was fleeing from the LORD (Jonah 1:10). As the storm grew worse, Jonah told the men to hurl him into the sea so it would quiet down for them (Jonah 1:12). The men initially tried to row to land but failed (Jonah 1:13). Finally, they cried out to the LORD and threw Jonah into the raging sea (Jonah 1:14-15). The sea immediately ceased from its raging, leading the sailors to fear the LORD exceedingly (Jonah 1:15-16). God provided a great fish to swallow Jonah, where he remained for three days and three nights (Jonah 1:17). From the belly of the fish, Jonah prayed and acknowledged that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:1,9). After the fish vomited him onto dry land, the word of the LORD came to him a second time (Jonah 2:10; 3:1). Jonah eventually responded by going to Nineveh and proclaiming God's message (Jonah 3:3-4).

## The Deliverance and the Relenting
God miraculously preserved Jonah in the fish and then brought about a national repentance in Nineveh.

> And the **LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah**. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . And the **LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster** that **He** had said **He** would do to them, and **He** did not do it. — Jonah 1:17, 2:10, 3:10 (ESV)

In this miracle, God fulfilled **His** word to Jonah by sparing him so he could **go to Nineveh and call out against it** (Jonah 3:2-3). The LORD also demonstrated **His** power by changing the hearts of the Ninevites (Jonah 3:5).

## A Nation Spared
The miracle resulted in the total repentance of the city of Nineveh (Jonah 3:5). From the king to the animals, everyone fasted and put on sackcloth (Jonah 3:6-8). The people turned from their evil ways and from the violence in their hands (Jonah 3:8). Because of their response, God relented from the destruction **He** had planned (Jonah 3:10). This event saved over one hundred and twenty thousand people who did not know their right hand from their left (Jonah 4:11). For Jonah, the result was a deeper understanding of God's compassion for all nations (Jonah 4:10-11). The sailors on the ship also came to worship the LORD because of the miracle they witnessed (Jonah 1:16). The survival of Nineveh during this period delayed their judgment for generations (Nahum 1:1).

## Conclusion
Nineveh was a city of violence facing divine judgment. Jonah was a reluctant prophet who failed by trying to flee from God's presence. Despite his rebellion and the impossibility of his situation inside a great fish, God provided a way of escape. The LORD promised to use Jonah to warn Nineveh, and **He** fulfilled that promise through a series of miracles. Jonah's response moved an entire nation to repentance, sparing them from disaster. Jonah sacrificed his pride and comfort, but he gained a front-row seat to one of the greatest revivals in history. His story demonstrates that we can trust the LORD even when we fail. God is faithful to **His** purposes and full of mercy toward all who turn to **Him**.
