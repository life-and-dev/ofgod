# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet of the LORD is given a mission to the most feared city of his time. Instead of obeying, he flees to the opposite end of the world. Discover how a divine storm and a great fish brought this rebellious man to fulfill a mission of mercy.

## The Problem of Assyrian Wickedness
The city of Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire, a nation known for its extreme cruelty and violence towards its enemies (Nahum 3:1). Their evil had become so great that it had come up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). The political environment was one of constant warfare and expansion, with the Assyrians posing a direct threat to the survival of Israel and the surrounding nations (2 Kings 15:19). The people of Nineveh were trapped in a cycle of violence and idolatry that provoked the wrath of God (Jonah 3:8). The problem was that a great city of more than one hundred and twenty thousand people was on the verge of total destruction because of their unrepentant wickedness (Jonah 4:11).

## Jonah's Sudden Call
Jonah was the son of Amittai and a prophet from Gath-hepher in the kingdom of Israel (2 Kings 14:25). He was likely living in a time of national prosperity under King Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:23-25). Jonah did absolutely nothing special to get God's attention before the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1). There is no record of him praying, fasting, or offering sacrifices at the moment of his calling (Jonah 1:1,2). He was simply a prophet who received a sudden and direct command from the LORD (Jonah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke directly to Jonah with a clear command and a reason for the mission.

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it, for their evil has come up before Me.**" — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his personal comfort and his nationalistic pride to obey the LORD (Jonah 4:1,2). He risked his reputation as a prophet in Israel by traveling to a pagan and hostile nation (Jonah 1:2). He also risked his life by entering Nineveh, a city famous for its brutality against foreigners (Nahum 3:1). By going, he would be offering a chance of mercy to the very enemies who threatened his own people (Jonah 4:2).

## The Initial Response
Jonah immediately rebelled against the command of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He rose to flee to Tarshish, which was in the opposite direction of Nineveh (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and paid the fare to board a ship, attempting to escape from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). Jonah believed that by crossing the sea, he could avoid the responsibility that **He** had placed upon him (Jonah 1:3,4).

## Jonah's Eventual Reaction
The LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea, creating a mighty tempest that threatened to break the ship (Jonah 1:4). Jonah was found sleeping in the inner part of the ship while the mariners cried out to their gods (Jonah 1:5). After the lot fell on him, Jonah confessed that he was a Hebrew who feared the LORD, the God of heaven, **Who** made the sea and the dry land (Jonah 1:9). He told the men to hurl him into the sea so that the storm would quiet down for them (Jonah 1:12). After being swallowed by a great fish, Jonah prayed to the LORD from the belly of the fish for three days and three nights (Jonah 1:17, 2:1). He acknowledged that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). When the fish vomited him onto dry land, the word of the LORD came to Jonah a second time (Jonah 2:10, 3:1). This time, Jonah arose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD (Jonah 3:3). He began to go into the city and proclaimed that in forty days Nineveh would be overthrown (Jonah 3:4). Even though he still struggled with his own desire for judgment, he fulfilled the task that the LORD had assigned to him (Jonah 4:1).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD performed a miracle of preservation and a greater miracle of national repentance. **He** prepared a great fish to save Jonah from drowning and moved the hearts of the Ninevites to believe **His** word.

> "And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And **Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.** . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them**, and He **did not do it.**" — Jonah 1:17, 3:10 (ESV)

In these miracles, the LORD fulfilled **His** intention to **call out against** the city and deal with the **evil** that had **come up before** **Him** (Jonah 1:2).

## The Lasting Result
The entire city of Nineveh, from the king to the least of the people, believed God and turned from their evil ways (Jonah 3:5,6). They called for a fast and put on sackcloth as a sign of their repentance (Jonah 3:5). Because of their reaction, God spared the city from the promised destruction (Jonah 3:10). This event demonstrated that the mercy of the LORD extends even to the Gentiles and the most wicked of nations (Jonah 4:11). Jonah himself was humbled and forced to confront the depth of the compassion of the LORD (Jonah 4:10,11). The mariners on the ship also came to fear the LORD exceedingly and offered sacrifices to **Him** (Jonah 1:16).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced a wicked city deserving judgment and his own refusal to be an instrument of mercy. He was limited by prejudice and his attempt to flee from the presence of the LORD. However, the LORD promised to use Jonah to call out against Nineveh. Jonah initially rebelled but was brought to obedience through a miraculous intervention at sea. God intervened by preserving Jonah in the fish and then by transforming the hearts of an entire city. The result was the salvation of over one hundred and twenty thousand people and a powerful testimony of God's grace. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and his pride, but he gained a first-hand experience of the sovereign mercy of the LORD. His story proves that we cannot escape our calling because the LORD is faithful to **His** purpose even when we are not.
