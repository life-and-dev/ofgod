# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet receives a command to warn a brutal enemy city of impending doom. Instead of obeying, he boards a ship headed in the opposite direction. Discover how a massive storm and a great fish brought him back to his divine calling.

## The Problem of Nineveh's Wickedness
The city of Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire (Jonah 1:2; 2 Kings 19:36). It was known for its extreme cruelty and violence toward other nations (Nahum 3:1-3). The LORD observed that their evil had come up before **Him** (Jonah 1:2). Nineveh was an exceedingly great city that required a three days' journey to cross (Jonah 3:3). The people were living in spiritual darkness and did not know their right hand from their left (Jonah 4:11). Israel viewed the Assyrians as a significant threat to their national security (2 Kings 15:19). The problem was that the wickedness of Nineveh had reached a point where divine judgment was imminent unless they repented (Jonah 1:2, 3:4).

## Jonah's Condition as a Prophet
Jonah was the son of Amittai and a recognized prophet in Israel (Jonah 1:1; 2 Kings 14:25). He lived during the reign of Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:25). He did not perform any special fast or sacrifice to receive a word from the LORD (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a servant of the God of heaven Who made the sea and the dry land (Jonah 1:9). The word of the LORD came to him directly while he was in his own country (Jonah 1:1, 4:2).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD gave Jonah a clear command to preach against Nineveh. When Jonah initially fled, the LORD spoke to him a second time with the same mission.

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it the message that I tell you.**" — Jonah 3:2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah risked his safety by entering a city known for its brutality (Jonah 1:2; Nahum 3:1). He also sacrificed his reputation among his own people by offering a chance of mercy to their enemies (Jonah 4:2). He had to give up his personal comfort and his desire for vengeance against the Assyrians (Jonah 4:1-3). He risked his life on the sea when he requested to be thrown overboard (Jonah 1:12).

## Jonah's Initial Response
Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into the ship to go away from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). Jonah tried to hide from his calling by falling into a deep sleep in the inner part of the ship (Jonah 1:5). He initially refused to obey the command because he knew the LORD was gracious and merciful (Jonah 4:2).

## Jonah's Faithful Reaction
After the LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea, Jonah confessed that he was fleeing from the God of heaven (Jonah 1:4,9-10). He told the mariners to pick him up and hurl him into the sea to quiet the storm (Jonah 1:12). While in the belly of the fish, Jonah prayed to the LORD his God (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). When the word of the LORD came to him a second time, Jonah arose and went to Nineveh (Jonah 3:3). He began to go into the city and called out the message of judgment (Jonah 3:4). He proclaimed that Nineveh would be overthrown in forty days (Jonah 3:4). Although he was displeased when the city repented, he remained at his post to see what would become of the city (Jonah 4:1,5). He eventually accepted the LORD'S correction regarding His compassion for the lost (Jonah 4:10-11). He proved that even a reluctant servant can be used by God when they finally submit to **His** word (Jonah 3:3).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD used a great storm and a sea creature to preserve Jonah and then brought about a national revival in Nineveh.

> And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it.** — Jonah 1:17, 2:10, 3:10 (ESV)

## The Lasting Result
The entire city of Nineveh believed God and called for a fast (Jonah 3:5). The king of Nineveh issued a proclamation for everyone to turn from their evil ways and violence (Jonah 3:6-8). Because of their repentance, the LORD spared the city from destruction (Jonah 3:10). This event served as a sign to future generations of God's willingness to forgive those who repent (Matthew 12:41). Jonah himself learned a profound lesson about the depth of God's mercy and the reach of **His** grace (Jonah 4:10-11). The mariners on the ship also feared the LORD exceedingly and offered sacrifices (Jonah 1:16).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city that deserved judgment but was offered mercy. He was limited by his own prejudice and fear, leading him to flee from God's presence. Despite Jonah's rebellion and his stay in the belly of a fish, God remained faithful to His promise of salvation. Jonah eventually obeyed and witnessed the miraculous repentance of an entire nation. God intervened through nature and the hearts of men to ensure His word prevailed. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and his pride to deliver a message he did not want to give. In the end, he gained a first-hand experience of God's sovereign mercy which is far greater than any human failure. His story proves that we can trust the LORD to complete His work even through the most reluctant of servants.
