# Jonah: Running From Mercy

A prophet is given a mission he despises and attempts to outrun the Presence of the Almighty. His journey into the depths of the sea leads to an unexpected encounter that changes his destination and the fate of a great city.

## The Problem of Assyrian Wickedness
Nineveh was a great city of Assyria, known for its extreme wickedness and violence (Jonah 1:2). The Assyrians were a major threat to Israel, and Jonah, as a prophet of the LORD, likely viewed them with patriotic disdain (2 Kings 14:25). The LORD saw that their evil had come up before **Him** (Jonah 1:2). The political environment was one of imperial expansion and brutal warfare (Nahum 3:1). Jonah's own heart was filled with prejudice and a desire for judgment rather than mercy for his enemies (Jonah 4:2). The problem was that a massive city of more than one hundred and twenty thousand people was on the brink of divine destruction due to their pervasive evil (Jonah 1:2, 4:11).

## Jonah's Prophetic Condition
Jonah was a prophet from Gath-hepher who had previously served during the reign of Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:25). He did not perform any special fast or prayer to seek this specific assignment (Jonah 1:1). The word of the LORD simply came to him as he was going about his life in Israel (Jonah 1:1). He was a recognized messenger of God who was suddenly called to a task that conflicted with his personal desires and national loyalty (Jonah 1:1-2).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came to Jonah with a specific command to warn the wicked city.

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, "Arise, **go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his national pride and his personal reputation as a prophet of Israel (Jonah 1:1-3). By warning Nineveh, he risked the survival of his own nation's enemy (Jonah 4:2). He also sacrificed his physical safety and comfort by fleeing to Joppa and boarding a ship for Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He risked his life in the storm and eventually in the belly of the fish to escape his calling (Jonah 1:12,17).

## Jonah's Initial Response
Jonah immediately rebelled against the command of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship, paying the fare to go as far away as possible from Nineveh (Jonah 1:3). He preferred a life as a fugitive on the sea over being a messenger of mercy to the Assyrians (Jonah 1:3).

## Jonah's Reluctant Reaction
After being rescued from the fish and receiving the call a second time, Jonah eventually obeyed (Jonah 3:1-3). He arose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD (Jonah 3:3). Nineveh was an exceedingly great city, three days' journey in breadth (Jonah 3:3). Jonah began to go into the city, going a day's journey, and he called out the message of judgment (Jonah 3:4). He told the people that in forty days Nineveh would be overthrown (Jonah 3:4). Even though he still hoped for their destruction, he performed the task God assigned to him (Jonah 4:1-2). When the city repented, Jonah was greatly displeased and angry (Jonah 4:1). He prayed to the LORD and confessed that he had fled because he knew God was gracious and merciful (Jonah 4:2). He even asked the LORD to take his life because he was so frustrated by God's compassion for his enemies (Jonah 4:3). Jonah then went out of the city and sat under a booth to see what would become of Nineveh (Jonah 4:5). He struggled with God's right to show mercy to those he deemed unworthy (Jonah 4:9-11). Despite his personal failures and bad attitude, he ultimately delivered the word that led to a citywide transformation (Jonah 3:5-10). His reaction showed a complex mix of external obedience and internal resistance (Jonah 3:3, 4:1).

## The Divine Miracle
God performed a series of miracles to redirect Jonah and then to save Nineveh. He sent a great wind on the sea and prepared a great fish to swallow Jonah (Jonah 1:4,17). Most significantly, **He** moved the hearts of the Ninevites to repent from the greatest to the least (Jonah 3:5).

> And the people of Nineveh believed God. They called for a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least of them. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it.** — Jonah 3:5,10 (ESV)

God fulfilled **His** purpose to **call out against** the city, which resulted in their salvation rather than their destruction (Jonah 1:2).

## The Global Result
The miracle resulted in the preservation of more than one hundred and twenty thousand people and much cattle (Jonah 4:11). The King of Nineveh and all his subjects turned from their evil ways and violence (Jonah 3:6-9). The city was spared from the imminent overthrow that Jonah had prophesied (Jonah 3:10). For Israel, this served as a witness that God's mercy extends to all nations who repent (Luke 11:30-32). Jonah himself received a profound lesson on the depth of God's compassion for the lost (Jonah 4:10-11). The story became a sign for future generations of God's power over death and His desire for the repentance of all people (Matthew 12:39-41).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city destined for judgment and his own heart full of prejudice. He was limited by his narrow nationalism and his attempt to outrun the Almighty. God promised to bring a message to Nineveh, and despite Jonah's flight and anger, that promise was kept. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and safety during his rebellion, yet God preserved him. God intervened through a storm, a fish, and a citywide revival. The result was the salvation of a great empire and a permanent record of God's universal mercy. Jonah gained his life back and a deeper understanding of God's character, even if he struggled to accept it. This story proves that we can trust the LORD'S faithfulness even when we are unfaithful. He is the God of the second chance Who never forsakes His purpose.
