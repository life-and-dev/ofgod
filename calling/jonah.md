# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A reluctant prophet attempts to escape the presence of the LORD after receiving a challenging assignment. His flight leads into the depths of the sea and the belly of a great beast. This journey reveals the reach of divine authority and the depths of His mercy toward all nations.

## The Problem of Nineveh
Jonah lived during the reign of Jeroboam II in Israel (2 Kings 14:25). He saw the LORD restore the borders of Israel from Lebo-hamath to the Sea of the Arabah (2 Kings 14:25). The LORD had not yet said that He would blot out the name of Israel (2 Kings 14:27). The city of Nineveh was a great and powerful capital of Assyria (Jonah 1:2). The people of Nineveh were known for their extreme wickedness and violence (Jonah 1:2, 3:8). Their evil had come up before the sight of the LORD (Jonah 1:2). The Assyrians were a significant military threat to the surrounding nations including Israel (2 Kings 18:13). The problem was that Nineveh faced imminent divine destruction because of their great wickedness (Jonah 1:2, 3:4).

## Jonah's Prophetic Condition
Jonah was the son of Amittai and a prophet from Gath-hepher (2 Kings 14:25). He was a servant of the LORD who had already spoken prophetic words concerning Israel's restoration (2 Kings 14:25). Jonah did not perform any special fast or sacrifice to receive this new word from the LORD (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a recognized prophet in the land of Israel (2 Kings 14:25). He identified himself as a Hebrew who feared the LORD (Jonah 1:9).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came to Jonah with a direct command to confront the evil of a foreign city (Jonah 1:1).

> "Arise, **go to Nineveh**, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me."
>
> — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

After Jonah's rescue from the sea. The LORD spoke to him a second time (Jonah 3:1).

> "Arise, **go to Nineveh**, that great city, and **call out against it the message that I tell you**."
>
> — Jonah 3:2 (ESV)

## The Cost of Obedience
Jonah had to sacrifice his personal comfort and his nationalistic pride (Jonah 1:3, 4:1-2). He risked his reputation as a prophet in Israel by traveling to a pagan enemy (Jonah 1:2). He feared that God would show mercy to the Assyrians who were enemies of his people (Jonah 4:2). He preferred the destruction of Nineveh over their repentance (Jonah 4:1-3). He risked his life by traveling to a city known for its violence (Jonah 1:2, 3:8).

## The Flight From God
Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went into the inner part of the ship to hide (Jonah 1:3,5). He attempted to go away from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). Jonah chose to disobey the direct command of God because he knew the LORD was gracious (Jonah 4:2).

## Jonah's Eventual Reaction
The LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea which caused a mighty tempest (Jonah 1:4). The mariners were afraid and cast lots to find the cause of the storm (Jonah 1:5,7). The lot fell on Jonah (Jonah 1:7). He admitted that he was fleeing from the LORD (Jonah 1:10). He told the men to hurl him into the sea so that it would quiet down for them (Jonah 1:12). The men eventually hurled Jonah into the sea and the raging ceased (Jonah 1:15). Jonah was inside the great fish (Jonah 1:17). He prayed to the LORD his God (Jonah 2:1). He called out of his distress and the LORD answered him (Jonah 2:2). He acknowledged that the LORD had cast him into the deep (Jonah 2:3). He promised to sacrifice with the voice of thanksgiving (Jonah 2:9). He declared that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). The fish vomited him onto dry land (Jonah 2:10). Jonah obeyed the second call (Jonah 2:10, 3:3). He entered Nineveh and proclaimed the message of judgment (Jonah 3:4). He walked through the city for a day (Jonah 3:4). He told the people that Nineveh would be overthrown in forty days (Jonah 3:4). The people repented (Jonah 3:5). Jonah became exceedingly angry (Jonah 4:1). He sat east of the city to see what would happen (Jonah 4:5). He complained to God about His mercy (Jonah 4:2). He asked the LORD to take his life (Jonah 4:3). He eventually listened as the LORD taught him about compassion (Jonah 4:10-11). He witnessed the power of God to save an entire city (Jonah 3:10). Jonah learned that God's concern extends even to those who do not know their right hand from their left (Jonah 4:11).

## The Miraculous Deliverance
God used nature to preserve Jonah and to demonstrate His sovereignty over all creation (Jonah 1:17, 4:6). He provided a way of escape from the depths of the sea (Jonah 2:10). The LORD later showed mercy to the repentant city (Jonah 3:10). God also provided comfort to Jonah even in his anger (Jonah 4:6).

> "And **the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah**. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights."
>
> — Jonah 1:17 (ESV)

> "And **the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**."
>
> — Jonah 2:10 (ESV)

> "When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them**, and He did not do it."
>
> — Jonah 3:10 (ESV)

> "Now the LORD God **appointed a plant and made it come up over Jonah, that it might be a shade over his head**, to save him from his discomfort."
>
> — Jonah 4:6 (ESV)

## The Result of Repentance
The miracle resulted in the total repentance of Nineveh (Jonah 3:5). The people believed God and called for a fast (Jonah 3:5). The king of Nineveh covered himself with sackcloth and sat in ashes (Jonah 3:6). He issued a decree for every person and beast to turn from their evil way (Jonah 3:7-8). God spared the city of more than 120,000 people (Jonah 4:11). The mariners on the ship also feared the LORD exceedingly (Jonah 1:16). They offered a sacrifice and made vows to the LORD (Jonah 1:16). Jonah himself was restored to his prophetic ministry and became a sign to future generations (Matthew 12:39-41). The result was a widespread recognition of the power and mercy of the God of Israel among pagan nations (Jonah 1:16, 3:5).

## Conclusion
Nineveh faced total destruction because of their great wickedness. Jonah was a prophet who failed by attempting to flee from God's presence. He was limited by his own anger and prejudice against the Assyrians. However, the LORD promised to send a message of warning and was faithful to preserve His messenger. God intervened through a great fish and a miraculous plant. The result was the salvation of a great city and a ship of mariners. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and reputation, but he gained a front row seat to one of the greatest revivals in history. This story proves that we can trust the LORD to be faithful even when we are unfaithful. He is the God Who pursues the lost and the rebellious alike.
