# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A reluctant prophet attempts to flee from a divine assignment by boarding a ship headed in the opposite direction. His journey takes an unexpected turn when a violent storm and a giant sea creature intervene to redirect him toward the very city he sought to avoid.

## The Problem of Wickedness
The city of Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire and was known for its extreme wickedness (Jonah 1:2). Their evil had come up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). The Assyrians were a cruel people and a threat to Israel (Nahum 3:1,19). Jonah, a prophet from Gath-hepher, lived during a time of national expansion under Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:25). He likely harbored deep resentment toward the enemies of his people (Jonah 4:2). The problem was that a great city faced imminent divine judgment due to its overwhelming sin, yet the one sent to warn them desired their destruction rather than their repentance (Jonah 1:2, 4:2).

## The Prophet's Condition
Jonah was the son of Amittai (Jonah 1:1). He was an established prophet of the LORD (2 Kings 14:25). Before the LORD spoke to him, there is no record of Jonah performing any special ritual, prayer, or sacrifice to seek a new word (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a servant of God when the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1).

## A Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came to Jonah with a direct command to confront the wickedness of Nineveh (Jonah 1:1-2).

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me."
> — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

After Jonah's initial flight and rescue, the LORD spoke to him a second time to reaffirm his assignment (Jonah 3:1).

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it the message that I tell you**."
> — Jonah 3:2 (ESV)

## The Price of Pride
Jonah had to sacrifice his nationalistic pride and his personal comfort (Jonah 4:1-2). He risked his reputation as a prophet by delivering a message of judgment that might be overturned if the people repented (Jonah 4:2). He also risked his life by traveling to the heart of a violent and hostile empire (Jonah 1:2).

## A Flight From God
Jonah immediately rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into it, intending to go away from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). Jonah deliberately chose to disobey the direct command of God (Jonah 1:3).

## From the Deep to the City
The LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea, and there was a mighty tempest (Jonah 1:4). After being cast into the sea by the sailors, Jonah was swallowed by a great fish (Jonah 1:17). Inside the fish, Jonah prayed to the LORD his God (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). After the fish vomited him out, Jonah obeyed the second call (Jonah 3:3). He went to Nineveh and began to call out the message of judgment (Jonah 3:4). Even after the city repented, Jonah reacted with great anger and wished to die (Jonah 4:1,3). He sat outside the city to see what would become of it (Jonah 4:5). Eventually, he had to listen as the LORD taught him a lesson about compassion (Jonah 4:10-11).

## The Miracle of Mercy
The LORD used a great fish to preserve Jonah and the repentance of an entire city to demonstrate His mercy (Jonah 1:17, 3:10).

> And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.
> — Jonah 1:17 (ESV)

> And the LORD spoke to the fish, and **it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**.
> — Jonah 2:10 (ESV)

God fulfilled His purpose to call out against Nineveh and ensure Jonah delivered the message He commanded (Jonah 1:2, 3:2). When the people repented, God showed the depth of His mercy (Jonah 3:10).

> When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them**, and **He did not do it**.
> — Jonah 3:10 (ESV)

## A City Spared
The entire population of Nineveh, from the greatest to the least, believed God and repented (Jonah 3:5). The king of Nineveh issued a decree for everyone to turn from their evil ways (Jonah 3:7-8). God spared the city and its more than one hundred and twenty thousand inhabitants (Jonah 4:11). Jonah's life was preserved through the fish, and he became a sign to the Ninevites (Matthew 12:40-41). The event demonstrated that God's mercy extends even to the most wicked nations when they repent (Jonah 4:11).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city and his own prejudice. He was a prophet who tried to outrun the presence of the LORD. Despite his blatant rebellion and later his petulant anger, the LORD did not forsake him or the people of Nineveh. God used a storm, a fish, and a plant to guide His stubborn servant. Jonah sacrificed his pride and his hatred to see his enemies saved. While he gained a deeper understanding of God's heart, he also experienced the frustration of his own limitations. This story proves that God is faithful to His word and compassionate toward all, even when His servants are unfaithful or unwilling.
