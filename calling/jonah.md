# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet is swallowed by a giant creature while fleeing a mission. This survival led to history's greatest mass repentance. Discover how God's persistent grace can reach even the most defiant runners.

## The Wickedness of Nineveh
Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire and was known for its extreme cruelty and violence (Nahum 3:1). The city was an exceedingly great city that required a three days' journey to traverse (Jonah 3:3). The people were spiritually blind and did not know their right hand from their left (Jonah 4:11). Their evil had become so great that it had come up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). The Assyrians were a major threat to Israel and were often used as an instrument of judgment against God's people (Isaiah 10:5-6). The problem was that Nineveh was a wicked city facing imminent divine destruction unless they turned from their evil ways (Jonah 1:2, 3:4).

## Jonah's Prophetic Condition
Jonah was the son of Amittai and a recognized prophet of the LORD (Jonah 1:1; 2 Kings 14:25). There is no record of Jonah performing any special prayer or sacrifice before the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1). He was simply in his own country when he received the command (Jonah 4:2). God initiated the contact without any prior action from Jonah to seek His attention (Jonah 1:1-2).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came directly to Jonah (Jonah 1:1).

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before **Me**." — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

After Jonah's initial rebellion, the LORD spoke to him a second time (Jonah 3:1).

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it the message that I tell you**." — Jonah 3:2 (ESV)

## The Required Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his personal comfort and nationalistic pride (Jonah 1:3, 4:2). He risked his life by traveling to a hostile and violent foreign capital (Jonah 3:3-4). He also risked his reputation as a prophet because he feared that God would show mercy to Israel's enemies if they repented (Jonah 4:2). He would rather have died than see the Assyrians receive God's compassion (Jonah 4:3,8).

## The Flight of Jonah
Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship to carry him in the opposite direction of Nineveh (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into the ship to hide (Jonah 1:3). He fell into a deep sleep while a great storm threatened the vessel (Jonah 1:4-5). He initially refused to obey the command to preach to his enemies (Jonah 1:3, 4:2).

## The Reaction of Jonah
After being cast into the sea by the mariners, Jonah prayed to the LORD from the belly of the fish (Jonah 1:15, 2:1). He acknowledged that the LORD had cast him into the deep and that his life was fainting away (Jonah 2:3,7). He vowed to sacrifice to the LORD with the voice of thanksgiving and to pay what he had vowed (Jonah 2:9). When the fish vomited him out on dry land, he finally obeyed the second calling (Jonah 2:10, 3:3). He entered Nineveh and proclaimed the message of judgment (Jonah 3:4). He spent forty days waiting to see if the city would be destroyed (Jonah 4:5). Even when he was angry with God's mercy, he remained in dialogue with the LORD (Jonah 4:2-4,9). He eventually listened to the LORD'S lesson regarding the plant and the worm (Jonah 4:10-11). His ultimate response was one of submission to God's sovereign will despite his personal feelings (Jonah 3:3).

## The Divine Miracle
God miraculously prepared a great fish to save Jonah (Jonah 1:17) and later caused a whole city to repent (Jonah 3:5-10). He commanded the fish to deliver the prophet back to dry land (Jonah 2:10).

> "And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And **Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights**. . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and **it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**." — Jonah 1:17, 2:10 (ESV)

The LORD fulfilled His purpose to have Jonah **call out against it** and bring the city to repentance (Jonah 3:3-10).

> "When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them**, and **He did not do it**." — Jonah 3:10 (ESV)

## The Lasting Result
The result was the repentance of over 120,000 people in Nineveh (Jonah 4:11). The king and the citizens fasted and put on sackcloth to seek God's mercy (Jonah 3:5-8). The city was spared from the promised destruction (Jonah 3:10). This event served as a sign to future generations of the power of repentance (Matthew 12:39-41). Jonah himself learned a profound lesson about the depth of God's compassion for all nations (Jonah 4:10-11). The neighbors of Nineveh witnessed a sovereign God Who offers grace to any who turn from violence (Jonah 3:8-10).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of Nineveh's wickedness and his own deep-seated prejudice. He was limited by his desire for vengeance and his attempt to flee from God's presence. However, the LORD promised to use him to deliver a message of judgment. Jonah initially rebelled but God intervened through a great storm and a fish. Jonah finally responded with obedience and the result was a city-wide transformation. He sacrificed his comfort and pride, but he gained a firsthand experience of God's infinite mercy. His story proves that we can trust the LORD'S faithfulness even when we fail. He is the God Who pursues the runner and offers grace to the repentant.
