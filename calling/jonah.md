# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet receives a command to warn a wicked city of its impending doom. Instead of obeying, he flees in the opposite direction. This triggers a series of supernatural events that challenge his understanding of God's mercy and his own deep-seated prejudices.

## The Wickedness of Nineveh
Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire (Jonah 1:2, 3:3). It was a nation known for its extreme cruelty and violence toward its neighbors (Nahum 3:1). Their evil had reached such a height that it had come up before the LORD for judgment (Jonah 1:2). As an Israelite prophet, Jonah likely viewed the Ninevites as irredeemable enemies of his people (Jonah 4:2). The political environment was one of constant threat (2 Kings 14:25). Assyria was a rising power that eventually conquered the northern kingdom of Israel (2 Kings 17:5-6). Jonah's internal struggle was compounded by his knowledge that God is gracious and might spare the very people he wished to see destroyed (Jonah 4:2). The core problem was that the massive city of Nineveh was on the brink of divine destruction due to its overwhelming wickedness (Jonah 1:2).

## Jonah's Sudden Call
Jonah was a prophet of the LORD and the son of Amittai (Jonah 1:1). There is no record of Jonah performing any special sacrifice, prayer, or ritual to initiate God's communication (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a chosen servant who received the word of the LORD in the course of his daily life (Jonah 1:1). His status as a prophet meant he already had a relationship with God (Jonah 1:1, 4:2). However, he did nothing specific to earn this particular assignment (Jonah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

> Then the word of the LORD came to Jonah the second time, saying, "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it the message that I tell you**." — Jonah 3:1-2 (ESV)

## The Personal Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his own comfort, safety, and nationalistic pride (Jonah 1:3). Traveling to Nineveh meant entering the heart of a hostile and violent empire (Jonah 1:2). He also risked his reputation as a prophet if God relented from the promised judgment (Jonah 4:2). Jonah effectively sacrificed his desire for personal and national justice to fulfill the command of the LORD (Jonah 4:1-3).

## The Initial Response
Jonah immediately rebelled by attempting to flee from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship bound for Tarshish. This was in the opposite direction of Nineveh (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into the ship. He hoped to escape his divine commission (Jonah 1:3). His flight was a deliberate act of distrust and disobedience (Jonah 1:3).

## Jonah's Eventual Reaction
After the mariners cast lots and identified him as the cause of the storm, Jonah confessed his faith in the LORD (Jonah 1:7-9). **He** is the God of heaven **Who** made the sea and the dry land (Jonah 1:9). He instructed the sailors to hurl him into the sea to quiet the tempest. This showed a willingness to sacrifice his life for the sake of the innocent crew (Jonah 1:12). While in the belly of the fish, Jonah prayed a prayer of thanksgiving and deliverance (Jonah 2:1-9). He acknowledged that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). After being vomited onto dry land, he finally obeyed the second call and went to Nineveh (Jonah 3:3). He walked through the city for a day and proclaimed the message of coming destruction (Jonah 3:4). When the people repented, Jonah's reaction was one of extreme displeasure and anger (Jonah 4:1). He complained to the LORD that **He** was too merciful and gracious (Jonah 4:2). He sat outside the city in a booth and waited to see if God would still destroy it (Jonah 4:5). Even after God provided a plant for shade and then took it away, Jonah remained stubbornly focused on his own discomfort (Jonah 4:6-9). He still desired judgment for his enemies (Jonah 4:9). His journey shows a heart that struggled to align with the compassion of **His** Creator (Jonah 4:10-11).

## The Divine Miracle
God intervened with several supernatural acts to direct Jonah and save Nineveh. First, **He** hurled a great wind and then appointed a great fish to swallow Jonah (Jonah 1:4,17). Later, **He** caused an entire city to repent at a single warning (Jonah 3:5-10).

> "And the LORD **appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah**. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . . And the LORD **spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**." — Jonah 1:17, 2:10 (ESV)

> "When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them**, and He did not do it." — Jonah 3:10 (ESV)

By preserving Jonah and bringing Nineveh to repentance, the LORD fulfilled **His** intent to **call out against it** and deliver the **message that I tell you** (Jonah 1:2, 3:2).

## The National Result
The miracle resulted in the total repentance of Nineveh from the king to the animals (Jonah 3:6-8). More than 120,000 persons were spared from destruction (Jonah 4:11). This event served as a sign to future generations of God's willingness to forgive even the most wicked nations when they turn to **Him** (Matthew 12:41). For Jonah, the experience forced him to confront his own lack of compassion and the vastness of God's mercy (Jonah 4:10-11). The sailors on the ship also came to fear the LORD exceedingly and offered sacrifices to **Him** (Jonah 1:16).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of delivering a message of doom to a cruel and powerful enemy. He was limited by his own prejudice, fear, and rebellion. He failed to trust the plan of the LORD. However, the LORD promised to use him to warn Nineveh. Jonah initially fled but eventually responded after God's miraculous intervention in the sea and the fish. The result was the salvation of a great city and a profound lesson in divine grace. Jonah sacrificed his safety and his national pride, but he gained a witness to the power of repentance. He saw the character of a God **Who** is slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love. His story proves that we can trust the LORD'S faithfulness even when we are unfaithful. **He** never forsakes **His** purpose or **His** servants despite their failures.
