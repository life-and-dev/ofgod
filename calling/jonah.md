# Jonah: Reluctant Prophet

A prophet attempts to flee from a divine assignment by boarding a ship to the edge of the known world. His rebellion triggers a supernatural storm that forces him to face certain death in the open sea. Discover how God uses a great fish to rescue His reluctant servant.

## The Wickedness of Nineveh
The city of Nineveh was an exceedingly great city (Jonah 3:3). It was a nation known for its extreme cruelty and violence against its neighbors (Jonah 1:2). Their wickedness had come up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). The Assyrians were a significant threat to the people of Israel and were dreaded for their brutal military tactics (Nahum 3:1,19). Jonah was a prophet from Gath-hepher in the territory of Zebulun (2 Kings 14:25). He lived during the reign of Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:23-25). This was a time when Israel was expanding its borders (2 Kings 14:25). Despite Israel's prosperity, the rising power of Nineveh represented a dark cloud of impending judgment (Jonah 1:2). The problem was that Nineveh's massive scale of evil required divine intervention to prevent further suffering (Jonah 1:2).

## Jonah's Prophetic Condition
Jonah was a recognized prophet in Israel. He had previously seen his prophecies of national expansion fulfilled (2 Kings 14:25). He did not perform any special fast or prayer to receive this new word from the LORD (Jonah 1:1). The word of the LORD came to him unexpectedly while he was in his homeland (Jonah 1:1). He was a man already familiar with hearing God's voice and serving as His messenger (Jonah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came directly to Jonah to warn a pagan city of their coming destruction.

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, "**Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

## The Risk of Obedience
Jonah risked his reputation as a prophet of Israel by being sent to a foreign, pagan enemy (Jonah 1:2). He had to sacrifice his comfort and safety to travel to a city known for its violence (Jonah 1:2). By obeying, he also risked the security of his own nation if Nineveh were to repent and be spared from judgment (Jonah 4:2). He eventually had to sacrifice his life by being thrown into the raging sea to save the sailors (Jonah 1:12).

## Jonah's Initial Response
Jonah immediately rebelled against the LORD'S command (Jonah 1:3). Instead of going east to Nineveh, he rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into the ship to go with them (Jonah 1:3). He thought he could escape the reach of God by traveling to the opposite end of the world (Jonah 1:3).

## Jonah's Eventual Reaction
When the LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea, Jonah was found sleeping in the inner part of the ship (Jonah 1:4-5). Even after the sailors cast lots and the lot fell on him, he remained honest about his identity (Jonah 1:7-9). He confessed that he was a Hebrew who feared the LORD, the God of heaven, Who made the sea and the dry land (Jonah 1:9). Recognizing that the storm was his fault, he told the sailors to throw him into the sea so that it would quiet down for them (Jonah 1:12). He was willing to die to save the lives of the pagan sailors (Jonah 1:12,15). While inside the belly of the great fish for three days and three nights, Jonah prayed to the LORD his God (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged that he had been cast into the deep and that the waters closed over him (Jonah 2:3,5). He remembered the LORD and vowed to sacrifice with the voice of thanksgiving (Jonah 2:7,9). He declared that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). After being vomited out onto the dry land, the word of the LORD came to him a second time (Jonah 2:10, 3:1). This time Jonah arose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD (Jonah 3:3). He walked into the city and cried out the message of judgment (Jonah 3:4). Even though he still struggled with his own desire for Nineveh's destruction, he obeyed the divine command (Jonah 4:1-2).

## The Divine Miracle
God sovereignly directed the natural world to preserve His prophet and bring an entire city to repentance.

> And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . Then Jonah prayed to the LORD his God from the belly of the fish. . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and **it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**. . . Then the word of the LORD came to Jonah the second time, saying, "**Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it the message that I tell you.**" So Jonah arose and went to Nineveh, according to the word of the LORD. . . And the people of Nineveh believed God. They called for a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least of them. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it**. — Jonah 1:17, 2:1,10, 3:1-3,5,10 (ESV)

## The Great Result
The miracle of the great fish preserved Jonah's life and gave him a second chance to obey (Jonah 2:10). His preaching in Nineveh led to a city-wide repentance that included the king and all the inhabitants (Jonah 3:6-8). Because they turned from their evil ways, God spared the entire city of more than one hundred and twenty thousand people (Jonah 4:11). This delayed the judgment of Assyria for generations (Jonah 3:10). The sailors on the ship also came to fear the LORD exceedingly. They offered a sacrifice and made vows (Jonah 1:16). Jonah himself learned a profound lesson about the depth of God's mercy toward all nations (Jonah 4:10-11). His story became a sign for future generations. It was even referenced by Jesus (Matthew 12:39-41).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked enemy city and his own prejudice against them. He failed by attempting to flee from God's presence and was met with a life-threatening storm. However, God did not forsake His reluctant prophet. Through the miracle of the great fish, the LORD rescued Jonah and recommissioned him for the task. Jonah responded with obedience, and his message sparked a massive repentance in Nineveh. He sacrificed his comfort and his own desires. Yet he gained his life and witnessed one of the greatest revivals in history. The result was the salvation of an entire city and a testimony of God's sovereign mercy. This story demonstrates that God's purpose will stand despite our limitations and failures. We can trust that the LORD is gracious and merciful, even when He calls us to difficult places.
