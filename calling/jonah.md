# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet receives a divine mandate to warn a brutal enemy city of impending doom. Instead of obeying, he attempts to outrun the Presence of the Creator by taking to the sea. His flight leads to an encounter that challenges the limits of mercy.

## The Problem of a Violent Enemy
Nineveh was an exceedingly great city with a reputation for extreme wickedness (Jonah 1:2, 3:3). The evil of the people had come up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). As the capital of the Assyrian Empire, it represented a violent threat to the people of Israel (2 Kings 15:19). The political environment was characterized by Assyrian expansion and cruelty toward neighboring nations (Nahum 3:1). Jonah likely feared that if Nineveh repented, God would spare a nation that was a mortal enemy to his own people (Jonah 4:2). He preferred the destruction of the city over the possibility of their salvation (Jonah 4:2). The problem was that a massive city of more than one hundred and twenty thousand people was on the brink of divine destruction due to their persistent violence (Jonah 3:8, 4:11).

## Jonah's Prophetic Condition
Jonah was the son of Amittai and a recognized prophet of the LORD (Jonah 1:1; 2 Kings 14:25). He lived during a time of national recovery for Israel (2 Kings 14:23-27). There is no record of Jonah praying or seeking a special mission before the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a servant of God available to receive a divine message (Jonah 1:1). He was in a state of ordinary prophetic duty when he received the unexpected command to go to Nineveh (Jonah 1:2).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came directly to Jonah on two separate occasions. The first was a command to warn the city, and the second was a renewal of that same mission after his rebellion.

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me."
>
> — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

After Jonah was rescued from the sea, the LORD spoke to him again.

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it the message that I tell you**."
>
> — Jonah 3:2 (ESV)

## The Sacrifice of Pride
Jonah had to sacrifice his nationalistic pride and his desire for vengeance against the enemies of Israel (Jonah 4:2). He risked his reputation as a prophet if his prophecy of destruction did not come to pass (Jonah 4:1-3). He also risked his personal safety by entering a city known for its violence to deliver a message of judgment (Jonah 3:4,8). He essentially had to sacrifice his own sense of justice to serve God's mercy (Jonah 4:10-11).

## Jonah's Initial Response
Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship, paying the fare to go as far away as possible (Jonah 1:3). He chose to rebel because he knew that the LORD is a gracious and merciful God (Jonah 4:2). He preferred to drown in a storm rather than see his enemies spared (Jonah 1:12). He was asleep in the inner part of the ship while the storm raged (Jonah 1:5).

## Jonah's Reluctant Reaction
After the mariners cast lots and the lot fell on Jonah, he confessed that he was fleeing from the LORD (Jonah 1:7-10). He told the men to hurl him into the sea so that the tempest would quiet down for them (Jonah 1:12). Once he was swallowed by the great fish, Jonah prayed to the LORD his God from the belly of the fish (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged that his life was fainting away and remembered the LORD (Jonah 2:7). He vowed to sacrifice to the LORD and declared that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). When the fish vomited him out upon the dry land, Jonah obeyed the second call (Jonah 2:10, 3:3). He arose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD (Jonah 3:3). Jonah began to go into the city, going a day's journey, and called out the warning (Jonah 3:4). Even after the city repented, Jonah struggled with anger and sat outside the city to see what would happen (Jonah 4:1,5). He eventually had to accept the lesson of the plant and the worm, which God used to teach him about divine compassion (Jonah 4:6-11). His reaction was a journey from deliberate rebellion to reluctant obedience and finally to a direct confrontation with the heart of God (Jonah 1:3, 3:3, 4:1).

## The Miracle of the Fish and Repentance
God used a great fish to preserve Jonah and then moved the hearts of an entire city to repent. These miracles demonstrated His sovereignty over both nature and human hearts.

> "And **the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah**. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . And **the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it**." — Jonah 1:17, 2:10, 3:10 (ESV)

In these actions, the LORD fulfilled the intent to **call out against it** (Jonah 1:2) by bringing the city to repentance and sparing them.

## The Result of Divine Mercy
The miracle resulted in the total repentance of Nineveh, from the king to the animals (Jonah 3:5-8). The people believed God and called for a fast (Jonah 3:5). God saw their actions and relented of the disaster, sparing over one hundred and twenty thousand people who did not know their right hand from their left (Jonah 3:10, 4:11). This event served as a sign to future generations, including the generation of Jesus, of the power of repentance (Matthew 12:41). Jonah himself was humbled and forced to confront the reality that God's mercy extends beyond the borders of Israel (Jonah 4:10-11). The neighbors and surrounding nations witnessed a God Who is slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love (Jonah 4:2).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of Nineveh's wickedness and his own deep-seated prejudice. He was limited by nationalistic pride and his attempt to escape the presence of the LORD. Despite his deliberate rebellion and flight to Tarshish, the LORD used him to deliver a message of warning. Jonah eventually responded with obedience after being miraculously preserved in the belly of a fish. God intervened by bringing the entire city to repentance and sparing them from destruction. The result was the salvation of a massive population and a powerful lesson in divine mercy. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and his reputation as a prophet of doom, but he gained a front-row seat to the boundless grace of God. This story proves that we can trust the LORD to be faithful even when we are unfaithful. He is the God Who pursues us with His mercy.
