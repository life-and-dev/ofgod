# Jonah: a Reluctant Prophet

A prophet receives a terrifying command to warn a brutal enemy empire. Instead of obeying, he flees across the sea in the opposite direction. Discover how God uses a storm and a great fish to bring a rebellious servant back to **His** mission of mercy.

## The Wickedness of Nineveh
The city of Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire, a nation known for its extreme cruelty and violence toward its neighbors (Nahum 3:1,19). They were a constant threat to the people of Israel and Judah (2 Kings 18:13). The wickedness of Nineveh had become so great that it reached the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:2). Their evil deeds were a stench in the nostrils of God (Jonah 1:2). Jonah was a prophet from Gath-hepher in the kingdom of Israel (2 Kings 14:25). He lived during a time when Israel was expanding its borders under King Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:25). The Israelites hated the Assyrians for their oppression and longed for their destruction (Nahum 3:19). The problem was that the people of Nineveh were exceedingly wicked and their evil required divine judgment (Jonah 1:2).

## Jonah's Prophetic Condition
Jonah was a recognized prophet of God who had previously spoken successful prophecies concerning Israel's borders (2 Kings 14:25). He did not perform any specific prayer, fast, or sacrifice to initiate this particular encounter (Jonah 1:1). The word of the LORD simply came to him as he was going about his life (Jonah 1:1). He was in a position of status as a prophet when God interrupted his life with a difficult assignment (Jonah 1:1, 2).

## The Divine Calling
God spoke directly to Jonah, giving him a clear but challenging command to confront the world's most feared city.

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

Later, after Jonah's failure, the LORD spoke to him again with the same mission.

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it the message that I tell you**." — Jonah 3:2 (ESV)

## The Risk of Obedience
Jonah risked his national reputation and his life by going to Nineveh (Jonah 3:3, 4). As an Israelite prophet, preaching to the enemy could be seen as treason by his own people (Nahum 3:19). He also faced the danger of being tortured or killed by the notoriously violent Assyrians (Nahum 3:1). By obeying, he had to sacrifice his personal desire to see his enemies destroyed without warning (Jonah 4:2).

## A Flight From Presence
Jonah immediately rebelled against God's command (Jonah 1:3). He did not argue or refuse with words, but he rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and paid the fare for a ship going in the exact opposite direction of Nineveh (Jonah 1:3). He hoped to escape his divine responsibility by crossing the sea (Jonah 1:3).

## A Heart of Resentment
After being rescued from the sea by a great fish, Jonah eventually obeyed the LORD (Jonah 3:3). He traveled to Nineveh, which was an exceedingly great city that took three days to walk through (Jonah 3:3). He began to cry out the message of judgment, warning that in forty days Nineveh would be overthrown (Jonah 3:4). However, his internal reaction remained one of resentment (Jonah 4:1). He was exceedingly displeased and angry that God showed mercy to the Assyrians (Jonah 4:1). He confessed that he had fled originally because he knew God was gracious, merciful, slow to anger, and abounding in steadfast love (Jonah 4:2). Jonah sat outside the city to see what would become of it, still hoping for its destruction (Jonah 4:5). He even asked **Him** to take his life because he preferred death over seeing his enemies forgiven (Jonah 4:3). Despite his physical obedience in preaching, his heart struggled to align with the compassion of **His** character (Jonah 4:10, 11). He reacted with bitterness when a plant that provided him shade withered, yet he had no pity for the thousands of people in Nineveh (Jonah 4:9, 11). His story shows a man who performed the task but failed to trust the goodness of **His** mercy for others (Jonah 4:1).

## The Miracle of Repentance
God used a great fish to save Jonah from drowning and later caused the entire city of Nineveh to repent in sackcloth and ashes (Jonah 1:17, 3:5). **He** spared the city, demonstrating that **His** word of judgment was intended to lead to mercy (Jonah 3:10).

> "And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights." — Jonah 1:17 (ESV)

> "**And the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land.**" — Jonah 2:10 (ESV)

> "When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it**." — Jonah 3:10 (ESV)

## A City Spared
The miracle resulted in the total repentance of the city of Nineveh, from the king to the animals (Jonah 3:5, 9). Over one hundred and twenty thousand people were spared from destruction (Jonah 4:11). This event served as a sign to later generations and even to Jesus Christ himself, who compared his own burial and resurrection to Jonah's time in the fish (Matthew 12:39, 41). Jonah learned the hard lesson that salvation belongs to the LORD alone (Jonah 2:9). The Assyrians received a temporary reprieve from judgment, and the name of the LORD was made known even in a pagan empire (Jonah 3:10).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of God's call to preach to a cruel enemy. His limitation was his own prejudice and anger, leading him to flee from the presence of the LORD. Despite his rebellion and attempt to hide in a ship, God did not forsake him. **He** sent a storm and a fish to redirect **His** servant. When Jonah finally preached, the city repented, and God intervened by relenting from disaster. The result was the preservation of thousands of lives and a testimony of divine mercy. Jonah had to sacrifice his comfort and his pride, but he gained a firsthand experience of the depths of **His** grace. His story proves that God is faithful to **His** mission and will use even a reluctant, failing prophet to accomplish **His** purposes for the world.
