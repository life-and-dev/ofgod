# Jonah: the Reluctant Messenger

A prophet is commanded to preach to a brutal enemy nation. Instead of obeying, he boards a ship to the opposite end of the world. Witness how the LORD uses a great storm and a massive fish to redirect **His** runaway servant.

## The Evil of Nineveh
The word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai (Jonah 1:1). Nineveh was an exceedingly great city, but its inhabitants were known for their wickedness (Jonah 1:2, 3:3). The LORD observed that their evil had come up before **Him** (Jonah 1:2). Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire, a dominant and violent power that threatened the surrounding nations (Nahum 3:1). The people of Israel viewed the Ninevites as cruel and irredeemable enemies (Nahum 3:19). The political environment was one of constant fear and tension due to Assyrian expansion (2 Kings 14:25, 17:3-6). The problem was that the city of Nineveh had reached such a level of corruption and violence that God was prepared to overthrow it unless they repented (Jonah 1:2, 3:4).

## Jonah's Position as Prophet
Jonah was a recognized prophet of the LORD who had previously spoken words of restoration for Israel (2 Kings 14:25). He was a Hebrew who feared the LORD, the God of heaven, Who made the sea and the dry land (Jonah 1:9). Jonah did not perform any special fast or ritual to gain God's attention before the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a servant of God living in his own country when he received the divine command (Jonah 4:2).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke directly to Jonah, giving him a clear and urgent mission to the enemies of his people.

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

## The Risk of Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his national pride and personal reputation by preaching to a hated enemy (Jonah 4:2). He risked his safety by entering a city known for its violence and cruelty (Nahum 3:1). He also risked being seen as a traitor to his own people if the Ninevites actually repented and were spared (Jonah 4:1-2). Jonah preferred the destruction of his enemies over their salvation (Jonah 4:2).

## Jonah's Initial Flight
Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish, paying the fare to go away from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He chose to travel in the exact opposite direction of Nineveh (Jonah 1:3). Jonah distrusted the mercy of God toward a foreign nation (Jonah 4:2).

## Jonah's Eventual Submission
The LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea, causing a mighty tempest that threatened to break the ship (Jonah 1:4). Jonah was found sleeping in the inner part of the ship while the mariners were afraid and cried out to their gods (Jonah 1:5). After the lot fell on Jonah, he confessed that he was fleeing from the LORD (Jonah 1:7-10). He told the men to hurl him into the sea so that the sea would quiet down for them (Jonah 1:11-12). The men eventually hurled Jonah into the sea, and the raging of the sea ceased (Jonah 1:15). The LORD appointed a great fish to swallow Jonah, and he remained in its belly for three days and three nights (Jonah 1:17). From the belly of the fish, Jonah prayed and acknowledged that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:1-9). After the fish vomited him onto dry land, the word of the LORD came to Jonah a second time (Jonah 2:10, 3:1). This time, Jonah arose and went to Nineveh as commanded (Jonah 3:3). He walked into the city and proclaimed that in forty days Nineveh would be overthrown (Jonah 3:4). Even after the city repented, Jonah remained outside the city, watching and waiting to see if God would still destroy it (Jonah 4:5).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD used nature and the hearts of men to fulfill **His** purpose and preserve **His** servant. **He** demonstrated **His** sovereignty over both the sea and the people of Nineveh.

> "And the LORD **appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah**. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it**." — Jonah 1:17, 3:10 (ESV)

The LORD fulfilled the command to **call out against it** and showed that **His** mercy extends even to the most wicked when they repent (Jonah 3:10).

## The Broad Result
The miracle resulted in the total repentance of Nineveh, from the king to the animals (Jonah 3:5-9). The entire population of more than 120,000 people was spared from destruction (Jonah 4:11). The mariners on the ship also feared the LORD exceedingly and offered sacrifices to **Him** (Jonah 1:16). Jonah himself learned a hard lesson about the depth of God's compassion for all **His** creatures (Jonah 4:10-11). His story became a sign to future generations, ultimately being referenced by Jesus as a parallel to his own death and resurrection (Matthew 12:39-41). The result was a witness of God's grace that transcended national boundaries.

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city that the LORD intended to judge. He was limited by his own prejudice and desire for vengeance. However, the LORD promised to call out against Nineveh and redirected Jonah through miraculous events. Jonah responded by fleeing, then repenting in the fish, and finally preaching the message. The LORD intervened by calming the storm, preserving Jonah, and turning the hearts of the Ninevites. The result was the salvation of a great city and a demonstration of God's universal compassion. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and pride, but he gained a front-row seat to one of history's greatest revivals. His story proves that we can trust the LORD to be gracious and merciful, even when we fail to reflect **His** character. **He** is the God Who never gives up on **His** servants or **His** creation.
