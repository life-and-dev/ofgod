# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet sent to warn a city of impending doom chooses to flee to the world's end. His escape attempt results in a terrifying storm and a miraculous rescue from the sea. Discover how God's mercy extends to the most reluctant messenger.

## The Wickedness of Nineveh
The word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai (Jonah 1:1). Nineveh was a great city, but its evil had come up before God (Jonah 1:2). The Neo-Assyrian Empire, with Nineveh as a major city, was known for its extreme cruelty and violence against its enemies (Nahum 3:1). They were a constant threat to the Northern Kingdom of Israel where Jonah served (2 Kings 14:25). The people of Nineveh did not know God and lived in deep spiritual darkness (Jonah 4:11). The problem was that the city's wickedness had reached a point where divine judgment was imminent (Jonah 1:2, 3:4).

## A Prophet in Israel
Jonah was a prophet of the LORD from Gath-hepher in Galilee (2 Kings 14:25). He lived during the reign of Jeroboam II, a time of territorial expansion for Israel (2 Kings 14:23,25). The Bible does not mention Jonah praying or seeking a special mission before the LORD spoke (Jonah 1:1). He was simply a servant of God in Israel when he received the command (2 Kings 14:25).

## The Divine Calling
God spoke directly to Jonah to warn him of the coming judgment and to give him a mission.

> "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his personal comfort and his nationalistic pride (Jonah 4:1-2). By going to Nineveh, he risked being seen as a traitor to his own people, the Israelites, who suffered under Assyrian aggression (Nahum 3:1,19). He also risked his life by entering a city known for its violence (Jonah 1:2). Most significantly, he had to sacrifice his desire to see his enemies destroyed (Jonah 4:2).

## Jonah's Flight
Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into it to go away from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He chose to travel in the opposite direction of Nineveh (Jonah 1:2-3).

## The Prophet's Turn
The LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea, and there was a mighty tempest (Jonah 1:4). The mariners were afraid and cast lots to find the cause of the storm, and the lot fell on Jonah (Jonah 1:5,7). Jonah admitted he was fleeing from the LORD and told them to hurl him into the sea so it would quiet down (Jonah 1:9-12). When they did, the sea ceased from its raging (Jonah 1:15). God appointed a great fish to swallow Jonah, and he was in its belly for three days and three nights (Jonah 1:17). Inside the fish, Jonah prayed to the LORD and acknowledged that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:1,9). The LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land (Jonah 2:10). The word of the LORD came to Jonah a second time (Jonah 3:1). This time, Jonah arose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD (Jonah 3:3). He cried out that in forty days Nineveh would be overthrown (Jonah 3:4). Even after the city repented, Jonah reacted with anger because he knew God was gracious and merciful (Jonah 4:1-2). He sat outside the city to see what would happen (Jonah 4:5). God used a plant, a worm, and a scorching east wind to teach Jonah about His compassion for the lost (Jonah 4:6-11). Jonah eventually learned that God's mercy extends to all who repent (Jonah 4:11).

## The Divine Miracle
God performed miracles to rescue Jonah and the city. He controlled the storm and the sea creatures to bring His messenger back to the task.

> "But the LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea. . . and the sea ceased from its raging. . . And the LORD **appointed a great fish to swallow Jonah**. . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and **it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them**, and He did not do it." — Jonah 1:4,15,17, 2:10, 3:10 (ESV)

In these miracles, God fulfilled His word to **call out against it** and showed that He is the LORD of both the sea and the dry land (Jonah 1:2,9).

## The Global Result
The miracle resulted in the total repentance of Nineveh (Jonah 3:5). From the king to the least of them, the people put on sackcloth and called out mightily to God (Jonah 3:5-8). God spared the city and its more than 120,000 inhabitants (Jonah 3:10, 4:11). The mariners on the ship also feared the LORD exceedingly, offered a sacrifice, and made vows (Jonah 1:16). For Jonah, the experience proved that he could not escape God's presence and that God's mercy is far wider than human prejudice (Jonah 2:9, 4:11).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of Nineveh's wickedness and his own deep-seated hatred for his enemies. He was limited by his prejudice and his attempt to flee from God's command. Despite his rebellion and his anger at God's mercy, the LORD was faithful to His purpose. God intervened with a storm and a great fish to redirect His prophet and eventually spared an entire city. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and his reputation as a prophet of judgment, but he gained a firsthand experience of the depths of God's grace. His story serves as a powerful reminder that we can trust the LORD'S compassion even when we fail. He is a God Who relentlessly pursues both the lost and His own reluctant servants.
