# Jonah: The Reluctant Prophet

A prophet of the Most High receives a command to preach to his nation's most brutal enemies. Instead of obeying, he flees in the opposite direction across the sea. His attempt to escape the presence of God leads to a storm that threatens everyone on board.

## The Problem of Impending Judgment
The city of Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire, known for its extreme cruelty and violence (Nahum 3:1-3,19). Their evil had come up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). The political environment was one of constant warfare and expansion (2 Kings 15:19). Israel had suffered under the threat of Assyrian power for years (2 Kings 15:29). The prophet Jonah was a servant of the LORD from Gath-hepher (2 Kings 14:25). He lived during a time when Israel was expanding its borders under King Jeroboam II (2 Kings 14:23-25). The people of Nineveh were pagans who did not know the God of Israel (Jonah 4:11). They were a direct threat to the safety and existence of the northern kingdom of Israel (Hosea 11:5). The problem was that a wicked and powerful nation faced imminent divine judgment unless they received a warning they did not deserve (Jonah 1:2, 3:4,10).

## Jonah's Prophetic Condition
Jonah was a recognized prophet of the LORD in Israel (2 Kings 14:25). He was likely in his home country when the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1). Jonah did nothing special like fasting or praying to receive this specific calling (Jonah 1:1). The LORD initiated the communication without any prior request from the prophet (Jonah 1:1-2). He was simply a servant of God available to hear His voice (Jonah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came directly to Jonah.

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it**, for **their evil has come up before Me**." — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

## The Personal Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his personal comfort and his nationalistic pride (Jonah 4:1-2). He risked his reputation as a prophet in Israel by traveling to a foreign and hostile land (Jonah 1:2). He risked his life by entering the capital of a brutal empire that was an enemy to his people (Nahum 3:1). He had to give up his desire to see Nineveh destroyed (Jonah 4:2).

## The Initial Response
Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into it (Jonah 1:3). He chose to travel in the exact opposite direction of Nineveh (Jonah 1:3). He attempted to hide from his calling by going into the inner part of the ship and falling fast asleep (Jonah 1:5).

## Jonah's Complex Reaction
Jonah's reaction changed only after the LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea (Jonah 1:4). The mariners were afraid and cried out to their own gods (Jonah 1:5). When the captain woke him, Jonah eventually confessed that he was a Hebrew who feared the LORD, the God of heaven (Jonah 1:6-9). He admitted that the storm was his fault (Jonah 1:12). He told the sailors to pick him up and hurl him into the sea so that the sea would quiet down for them (Jonah 1:12). After being swallowed by a great fish, Jonah prayed to the LORD from the belly of the fish (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged that his life was fainting away and remembered the LORD (Jonah 2:7). He vowed to sacrifice to the LORD with the voice of thanksgiving (Jonah 2:9). He declared that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). When the fish vomited him out upon the dry land, the word of the LORD came to him a second time (Jonah 2:10, 3:1). This time, Jonah arose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD (Jonah 3:3). He entered the city and proclaimed the message of judgment (Jonah 3:4). Even after the city repented, Jonah reacted with anger because he knew the LORD was a gracious God and merciful (Jonah 4:1-2). He sat outside the city to see what would become of it (Jonah 4:5). He had to be taught a final lesson through a plant, a worm, and a scorching east wind (Jonah 4:6-8). The LORD questioned his right to be angry (Jonah 4:9). Jonah eventually had to accept the sovereignty and compassion of God for all people (Jonah 4:10-11).

## The Divine Miracle
God used the elements and a great creature to preserve Jonah and then brought about a massive spiritual awakening in Nineveh.

> And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights. . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and **it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land.** . . And the people of Nineveh believed God. They called for a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least of them. . . When God saw what they did, **how they turned from their evil way, God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it.** — Jonah 1:17, 2:10, 3:5,10 (ESV)

In this miracle, the LORD fulfilled **His** purpose to **call out against** the city and show that **their evil has come up before Me** (Jonah 1:2). He also demonstrated **His** mercy by sparing those who repented (Jonah 3:10).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the entire city of Nineveh repenting and being spared from destruction (Jonah 3:10). The king of Nineveh himself issued a decree for everyone to turn from their evil ways and from the violence that was in their hands (Jonah 3:6-8). The sailors who witnessed the storm and its sudden end feared the LORD exceedingly (Jonah 1:16). They offered a sacrifice to the LORD and made vows (Jonah 1:16). Jonah himself was humbled and became a sign to future generations (Luke 11:29-30). The survival of Nineveh postponed its judgment for over a century (Nahum 1:1). The story serves as a permanent record of God's concern for the Gentiles and His desire for all to reach repentance (Jonah 4:11).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city and his own prejudice against them. He was limited by his desire for vengeance and his attempt to flee from God's presence. However, the LORD promised to bring a message of judgment to Nineveh. Jonah initially rebelled, but God intervened through a storm and a great fish. After his rescue, Jonah obeyed and preached, leading to the repentance of the entire city. The miracle proved God's faithfulness to His word and His mercy toward even the most violent enemies. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and his pride, though he did so reluctantly. In the end, he gained a deeper understanding of God's character and witnessed one of the greatest revivals in history. His story shows that we cannot escape God's calling. He is the LORD Who pursues us with grace even in our rebellion.
