# Jonah: Running From Mercy

A prophet attempts to escape a divine assignment by fleeing to the edge of the known world. His journey leads him into the depths of the ocean and the belly of a great fish. Discover how God's persistent pursuit transforms a reluctant messenger into a vessel of salvation.

## The Wickedness of Nineveh
The word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai (Jonah 1:1). Nineveh was an exceedingly great city, three days' journey in breadth (Jonah 3:3). However, the city was filled with evil and violence (Jonah 1:2, 3:8). The wickedness of the people had come up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). As a major power, Nineveh was a threat to Israel and known for its cruelty (Nahum 3:1,19). The political environment was one of impending judgment due to their persistent sin (Jonah 3:4). The problem was that the people of Nineveh were headed for total destruction unless they turned from their evil ways (Jonah 3:4,10). Jonah, as a Hebrew prophet, hated the Assyrians and did not want to see them spared (Jonah 4:1-2). He feared that if he preached to them, God would show **His** characteristic mercy (Jonah 4:2). The conflict lay between God's desire for repentance and Jonah's desire for justice against his enemies (Jonah 4:2-3).

## Jonah's Condition
Jonah was the son of Amittai and a recognized prophet in Israel (Jonah 1:1; 2 Kings 14:25). He was likely at home or in a place of ministry when the LORD spoke to him (Jonah 1:1). There is no record that he was fasting, praying, or performing any special religious duty to receive this message (Jonah 1:1-2). God chose to speak to him simply because he was **His** servant (Jonah 1:1). He was in a state of regular prophetic service when the call to go to Nineveh arrived (Jonah 1:1-2).

## The Divine Calling
God spoke directly to Jonah, giving him a clear and urgent command to confront the wickedness of Nineveh.

> "Arise, **go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me."
>
> — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

After Jonah's initial disobedience and subsequent rescue, the LORD spoke to him a second time.

> Then the word of the LORD came to Jonah the second time, saying, "Arise, **go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it the message that I tell you**."
>
> — Jonah 3:1-2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his personal comfort and national pride (Jonah 1:3, 4:1-2). By going to Nineveh, he risked his life entering a city known for its violence (Jonah 3:8). He also risked his reputation as a prophet if the people repented and the threatened judgment did not occur (Jonah 4:2). He had to abandon his own prejudice against the Assyrians to obey the LORD (Jonah 4:2-3). Jonah sacrificed his right to choose his own mission and his own enemies (Jonah 1:3).

## Jonah's Initial Response
Jonah rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into it (Jonah 1:3). He chose to go in the opposite direction of Nineveh to escape his assignment (Jonah 1:3). He remained asleep in the inner part of the ship while a great storm threatened to break it apart (Jonah 1:4-5). He initially refused to obey God's clear command (Jonah 1:3).

## Jonah's Reaction
After being cast into the sea and rescued by a great fish, Jonah prayed to the LORD from the belly of the fish (Jonah 2:1). He acknowledged that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). When the fish vomited him onto dry land, the LORD called him again (Jonah 3:1-2). This time, Jonah arose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD (Jonah 3:3). He began to go into the city, going a day's journey (Jonah 3:4). He called out the message God gave him (Jonah 3:4). He warned them that in forty days Nineveh would be overthrown (Jonah 3:4). Even after the city repented, Jonah reacted with great anger (Jonah 4:1). He went out of the city and sat under a booth to see what would happen (Jonah 4:5). God used a plant, a worm, and a scorching wind to teach him about compassion (Jonah 4:6-8). Jonah argued with God about **his** right to be angry (Jonah 4:9). Eventually, he had to accept God's sovereignty and mercy toward the ignorant (Jonah 4:10-11). His reaction was a mix of reluctant obedience and struggling with God's grace (Jonah 3:3, 4:1-4). He demonstrated that even a prophet can struggle to align his heart with the heart of the LORD (Jonah 4:2,11).

## The Divine Miracle
God performed multiple miracles to preserve Jonah and bring about the repentance of Nineveh. He controlled the elements, the animals, and the hearts of men.

> And the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah. And **Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights**. . . And the LORD spoke to the fish, and it vomited Jonah out upon the dry land. . . And the people of Nineveh believed God. They called for a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least of them. . . When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it**. — Jonah 1:17, 2:10, 3:5,10 (ESV)

In these miracles, the LORD fulfilled the command to **call out against it** by ensuring Jonah reached the city and that **the message that I tell you** produced the intended result (Jonah 3:2-4).

## The Result
The miracle resulted in the survival of over one hundred and twenty thousand people who did not know their right hand from their left (Jonah 4:11). The king and the people of Nineveh turned from their evil ways and their violence (Jonah 3:8-10). The city was spared from the promised destruction (Jonah 3:10). For Jonah, the experience revealed the depth of God's patience and the universality of **His** mercy (Jonah 4:2,11). It served as a sign to future generations of God's power to save even the most wicked (Matthew 12:39-41). The sailors on the ship also came to fear the LORD exceedingly and offered sacrifices (Jonah 1:16). The result was a widespread acknowledgment of the LORD'S power and compassion across different nations (Jonah 1:16, 3:5).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of Nineveh's wickedness and his own deep-seated prejudice. He was limited by his desire for vengeance and his attempt to flee from the presence of the LORD. Despite his rebellion and anger, God promised that his message would be delivered. Jonah eventually obeyed after a miraculous intervention in the depths of the sea. God showed **His** faithfulness by sparing a repenting city and patiently teaching **His** reluctant prophet. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and his pride, but he gained a firsthand experience of the Boundless Mercy of the LORD. His story proves that we can trust God even when we do not understand **His** compassion for our enemies. He is the God Who pursues the lost and the wayward alike.
