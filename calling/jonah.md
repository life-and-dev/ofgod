# Jonah: the Reluctant Messenger

A prophet is called to preach to a city he hates. He decides to flee to the furthest edge of the world to escape God's presence. Discover how a man's attempt to hide in the sea led to an encounter with God's overwhelming mercy.

## The Bitterness of Israel
Jeroboam the son of Joash reigned over Israel for forty-one years (2 Kings 14:23). He did what was evil in the sight of the LORD (2 Kings 14:24). The LORD saw that the affliction of Israel was very bitter (2 Kings 14:26). There was none left, bond or free, and there was none to help Israel (2 Kings 14:26). The Assyrian empire, with its capital Nineveh, was a rising threat (Jonah 1:2). Nineveh was an exceedingly great city (Jonah 1:2, 3:3). The evil of Nineveh had come up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). The problem was that a city of immense wickedness faced divine judgment while the prophet chosen to warn them wanted them destroyed rather than saved (Jonah 4:2).

## Jonah's Sudden Call
Jonah was the son of Amittai (Jonah 1:1). He was a prophet from Gath-hepher (2 Kings 14:25). Jonah did not perform any special religious ceremony or fast before the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1). He was likely in his home country when He addressed him (Jonah 1:1-2). Jonah had previously spoken words of restoration for Israel's borders (2 Kings 14:25). He called Jonah without any recorded preceding sacrifice or prayer from the prophet (Jonah 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came to Jonah twice. The first time Jonah fled. The second time he obeyed.

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, "**Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me." — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

> Then the word of the LORD came to Jonah the second time, saying, "**Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it the message that I tell you**." — Jonah 3:1-2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jonah had to sacrifice his personal reputation and his nationalistic comfort (Jonah 4:2). By warning Nineveh, he risked being seen as a traitor to Israel since Assyria was a cruel enemy (Jonah 1:2). He also sacrificed his own peace of mind by choosing to flee rather than obey (Jonah 1:3). He risked his life on a ship during a great storm (Jonah 1:4). He was willing to be thrown into the sea to save the mariners (Jonah 1:12).

## Jonah's Initial Response
Jonah's initial response was one of direct rebellion (Jonah 1:3). He rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and found a ship going to Tarshish (Jonah 1:3). He paid the fare and went down into it (Jonah 1:3). He tried to go away from the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He did not argue or make excuses (Jonah 1:3). He simply ran in the opposite direction (Jonah 1:3).

## Jonah's Eventual Reaction
The LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea (Jonah 1:4). There was a mighty tempest so that the ship threatened to break up (Jonah 1:4). The mariners were afraid and each cried out to his god (Jonah 1:5). Jonah had gone down into the inner part of the ship and was fast asleep (Jonah 1:5). The crew cast lots to know on whose account this evil had come upon them (Jonah 1:7). The lot fell on Jonah (Jonah 1:7). He told them he was a Hebrew and feared the LORD, the God of heaven, Who made the sea and the dry land (Jonah 1:9). He told them to hurl him into the sea so the sea would quiet down (Jonah 1:12). They eventually hurled him into the sea and the sea ceased from its raging (Jonah 1:15).

The LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah (Jonah 1:17). He was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights (Jonah 1:17). Jonah prayed to the LORD his God from the belly of the fish (Jonah 2:1). He called out to the LORD out of his distress (Jonah 2:2). He acknowledged that He had cast him into the deep (Jonah 2:3). He promised to sacrifice with the voice of thanksgiving (Jonah 2:9). He declared that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). After the fish vomited him out, Jonah arose and went to Nineveh according to the word of the LORD (Jonah 3:3). He moved Jonah to preach the message (Jonah 3:2-3). He began to go into the city and called out that Nineveh shall be overthrown in forty days (Jonah 3:4). He waited to see what would become of the city (Jonah 4:5).

## The Divine Miracle
God preserved Jonah in the depths of the sea and moved an entire city to repentance.

> And the LORD spoke to the fish, and it **vomited Jonah out upon the dry land**. — Jonah 2:10 (ESV)

> When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it**. — Jonah 3:10 (ESV)

## The Lasting Result
The people of Nineveh believed God (Jonah 3:5). They called for a fast and put on sackcloth from the greatest to the least of them (Jonah 3:5). Even the king of Nineveh sat in ashes and issued a proclamation for repentance (Jonah 3:6-8). The miracle of the city's survival affected more than 120,000 persons (Jonah 4:11). Jonah was exceedingly displeased and angry because the LORD is a gracious and merciful God (Jonah 4:1-2). God used a plant and a worm to teach Jonah about His pity (Jonah 4:6-10). The prophet learned that the LORD cares for all nations and even much cattle (Jonah 4:11).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city and his own rebellious heart. He was a prophet who tried to flee from the presence of the LORD rather than see his enemies saved. Despite his flight and his later anger, God remained faithful to His plan and His servant. God provided a great fish to save Jonah and a miraculous repentance to save Nineveh. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and his reputation, but he gained a firsthand experience of the depths of God's grace. His story proves that we can trust the LORD even when we fail or disagree with Him. He is the God Who pursues us with mercy and accomplishes His purpose despite our limitations.
