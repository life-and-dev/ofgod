# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet is given a mission to a city famous for its cruelty and wickedness. Instead of obeying, he boards a ship heading in the opposite direction, triggering a supernatural storm that threatens to destroy everyone on board.

## The Evil of Nineveh
Jonah was the son of Amittai (Jonah 1:1). He lived during a time when Nineveh was an exceedingly great city (Jonah 3:3). However, the city was notorious for its extreme wickedness and violence (Jonah 1:2, 3:8). The LORD observed that their evil had come up before Him (Jonah 1:2). Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire, which was a constant threat and enemy to the people of Israel (Nahum 3:1-4). The political environment was one of fear and impending judgment due to the widespread corruption and cruelty of the Ninevites (Jonah 3:8-9). The problem was that the city of Nineveh faced imminent divine destruction because of their great evil, yet they remained unaware of their spiritual peril (Jonah 1:2).

## Jonah's Condition
Jonah was a prophet of the LORD (Jonah 1:1). He was in a state of ordinary life when the word of the LORD came to him (Jonah 1:1). There is no record that Jonah performed any special religious acts like fasting, praying, or sacrificing to get God's attention before he was called (Jonah 1:1). He was simply chosen by God for this specific task (Jonah 1:1).

## The Prophetic Calling
The LORD communicated directly with Jonah through His word. The word of the LORD came to him twice to ensure the message was delivered to Nineveh (Jonah 1:1, 3:1).

> “Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me.” — Jonah 1:2 (ESV)

After Jonah's flight and rescue, the LORD spoke to him again.

> “Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and **call out against it the message that I tell you**.” — Jonah 3:2 (ESV)

## A Sacrifice of Comfort and Pride
Jonah sacrificed his comfort and safety by being commanded to enter a hostile city (Jonah 1:3). He risked his reputation as a prophet if the predicted judgment did not occur (Jonah 4:2). Furthermore, he had to sacrifice his nationalistic pride and his desire for vengeance against Israel's enemies (Jonah 4:1-2). By following God's call, he would also be seen as a traitor or a fool by his own people if the enemy was spared (Jonah 4:2).

## Jonah's Flight
Jonah initially rebelled and refused to obey the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the LORD to avoid the mission (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and boarded a ship going to Tarshish, paying the fare to get as far away as possible (Jonah 1:3). His refusal was rooted in his knowledge that God is merciful and might spare the Ninevites (Jonah 4:2).

## A Reluctant Obedience
Eventually, Jonah responded to God's calling after a series of divine interventions (Jonah 1:4). When the LORD hurled a great wind upon the sea, Jonah was identified as the cause and was thrown overboard at his own request (Jonah 1:4,12,15). Inside the belly of a great fish, Jonah prayed and recommitted himself to his vow (Jonah 2:1,9). After being vomited onto dry land, he obeyed the second call and traveled to Nineveh (Jonah 3:3). He walked through the city for a day's journey, proclaiming the message of judgment (Jonah 3:4). Even though he obeyed, his heart remained troubled and angry when God showed mercy (Jonah 4:1). He sat outside the city to watch its fate, still hoping for its destruction (Jonah 4:5). The LORD used a plant, a worm, and a wind to teach Jonah about compassion (Jonah 4:6-11). Despite his personal failures and bad attitude, Jonah completed the task he was given (Jonah 3:3-4).

## The Miracle of Mercy
God performed a miracle by changing the hearts of the Ninevites and sparing the city (Jonah 3:10).

> And the people of Nineveh believed God. They called for a fast and put on sackcloth, from the greatest of them to the least of them. — Jonah 3:5 (ESV)

> When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them, and He did not do it.** — Jonah 3:10 (ESV)

In this miracle, the LORD fulfilled the purpose of the calling by bringing Nineveh to repentance and demonstrating His sovereignty over all nations (Jonah 4:11).

## The Results of the Word
The result of God's miracle was the preservation of more than 120,000 people and their livestock (Jonah 4:11). The entire city, from the king to the animals, turned from their evil ways and sought God's mercy (Jonah 3:6-9). This event stands as the greatest city-wide revival recorded in the Bible (Jonah 3:5). It showed that God's grace extends even to the most wicked enemies of His people (Jonah 4:11). For Jonah, the result was a deeper, albeit painful, understanding of God's character and his own lack of compassion (Jonah 4:10-11).

## Conclusion
Jonah faced the problem of a wicked city destined for destruction. His own limitations were his stubbornness, his prejudice, and his attempt to flee from the presence of the LORD. God's promise was that the message would be delivered, and He used a storm and a great fish to bring His prophet back on track. Jonah eventually responded with obedience, leading to the miraculous repentance of Nineveh. The result was the salvation of a great city and a lasting lesson on God's mercy. Jonah sacrificed his comfort and his pride, but he gained the privilege of being an instrument of God's grace. The story proves that the LORD is faithful to His purpose and will not forsake His servants, even when they fail or rebel.
