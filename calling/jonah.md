# Jonah: the Reluctant Prophet

A prophet attempts to outrun the Almighty by boarding a ship to the edge of the world. His rebellion triggers a supernatural storm and a terrifying encounter in the ocean depths. Discover how Jonah's flight from God led to an unexpected rescue and a city's salvation.

## The Problem of a Violent Nation

Nineveh was the capital of the Assyrian Empire, a nation known for its extreme cruelty and violence toward its neighbors (Nahum 3:1). The evil of the city had become so great that it came up before the LORD (Jonah 1:2). Israel had long suffered under the threat of Assyrian expansion, and the people of Nineveh were considered sworn enemies of God's people (Nahum 3:19). The political environment was one of constant warfare and oppression as the Assyrians dominated the ancient Near East (Nahum 2:11-12). Their military power was unmatched, and their reputation for brutality was widespread among the nations (Nahum 3:1-3). Jonah was a prophet in Israel during the reign of Jeroboam II, a time when the nation was recovering territory but still faced the looming shadow of Assyrian power (2 Kings 14:25). The problem was that Nineveh's persistent wickedness required a divine warning of impending destruction (Jonah 1:2).

## Jonah's Humble Condition

Jonah was a recognized prophet in Israel, the son of Amittai from Gath-hepher (Jonah 1:1; 2 Kings 14:25). He did nothing special like fasting or making a sacrifice to get God's attention before the word of the LORD first came to him (Jonah 1:1). The word of the LORD simply came to him while he was in his home country (Jonah 1:1). He was a man with a deep understanding of God's character, which influenced his later reluctance to obey (Jonah 4:2).

## The Divine Calling

The word of the LORD came to Jonah with a direct command to confront the wickedness of a foreign city.

> Now the word of the LORD came to Jonah the son of Amittai, saying, "**Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it**, for their evil has come up before Me."
> — Jonah 1:1-2 (ESV)

After Jonah's rescue from the sea, the LORD repeated His call to ensure the message would be delivered.

> Then the word of the LORD came to Jonah the second time, saying, "**Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it the message that I tell you.**"
> — Jonah 3:1-2 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice

Jonah risked his reputation as a prophet in Israel by going to a hated enemy nation (Jonah 4:2). He sacrificed his comfort and safety to travel to a city known for its violence (Jonah 3:3). Most significantly, he had to sacrifice his personal desire for Nineveh's destruction to remain obedient to God's command to offer them a chance to repent (Jonah 4:1-2).

## Jonah's Initial Response

Jonah immediately rebelled against the LORD'S command (Jonah 1:3). Instead of going east to Nineveh, he rose to flee to Tarshish in the opposite direction to escape the presence of the LORD (Jonah 1:3). He went down to Joppa and paid the fare for a ship, choosing to hide in the hold of the vessel rather than face his calling (Jonah 1:3-5). He even slept through a divine storm, showing his initial indifference to the consequences of his flight (Jonah 1:5).

## Jonah's Faithful Reaction

After being swallowed by a great fish, Jonah eventually surrendered to the LORD'S will while in the depths of the sea (Jonah 2:1). He prayed from the belly of the fish, acknowledging that salvation belongs to the LORD (Jonah 2:9). When the fish vomited him onto dry land, Jonah obeyed the second call and went to Nineveh (Jonah 3:3). He walked into the heart of the great city and delivered the short, piercing message God had given him (Jonah 3:4).

Even after the city repented, Jonah struggled with his internal reaction to God's mercy (Jonah 4:1). He sat outside the city in a booth, watching and hoping for its destruction (Jonah 4:5). He expressed deep anger when the LORD spared the people he loathed (Jonah 4:2). However, Jonah's life remained a testimony because he ultimately recorded his own failures and God's compassionate response (Jonah 4:10-11). He proved that even a reluctant and angry prophet must eventually bow to the sovereignty and kindness of the LORD (Jonah 2:9). His reaction showed that God can use even a flawed servant to accomplish a massive spiritual transformation (Jonah 3:10).

## The Divine Miracle

The LORD performed the miracle of preservation by appointing a great fish to save Jonah from drowning (Jonah 1:17).

> And **the LORD appointed a great fish to swallow up Jonah**. And Jonah was in the belly of the fish three days and three nights.
> — Jonah 1:17 (ESV)

This miracle ensured that the prophet would survive his rebellion to fulfill the command to go to Nineveh (Jonah 2:10). The LORD also performed the miracle of a national repentance in Nineveh, turning the hearts of an entire city (Jonah 3:5-10).

> When God saw what they did, how they turned from their evil way, **God relented of the disaster that He had said He would do to them**, and He did not do it.
> — Jonah 3:10 (ESV)

## The Lasting Result

The miracle of Nineveh's repentance resulted in the preservation of over 120,000 people and much cattle (Jonah 4:11). The king and the entire population turned from their violence and evil ways (Jonah 3:8). This event served as a sign to later generations, including the time of Jesus, who cited the "sign of Jonah" as a parallel to his own death and resurrection (Matthew 12:39-41). Jonah's family and nation saw that the LORD'S mercy extended even to the most undeserving enemies (Jonah 4:11). For Jonah personally, he learned that God's compassion is far greater than human prejudice (Jonah 4:10-11).

## Conclusion

Jonah faced the problem of a wicked enemy city and his own deep-seated hatred for its people. He was a prophet who tried to flee from his calling, but the LORD pursued him with a storm and a great fish. Jonah sacrificed his pride and his hatred to deliver God's message. The LORD promised to warn Nineveh and miraculously brought about a total city-wide repentance while sparing His rebellious prophet. Jonah gained a firsthand experience of God's limitless mercy, which outweighed the loss of his personal comfort and his desire for vengeance. His story proves that God is faithful to His purpose even when His servants are weak or unwilling. We can trust that the LORD will not forsake us, and He will use our lives to manifest His grace to the world.
