# Joseph: the Beloved Dreamer

A young man receives vivid dreams of greatness but is soon betrayed by his own brothers and sold into a foreign land. His journey from a pit to a palace reveals how a patient trust in God's timing can turn a personal tragedy into a nation's salvation.

## The Jealousy of the Brothers
Jacob lived in the land of Canaan where he favored his son Joseph above all his other children (Genesis 37:1,3). He gave the boy a special robe of many colors as a sign of this preference (Genesis 37:3). This favoritism caused Joseph's brothers to hate him and speak roughly to him (Genesis 37:4). They were already irritated by his habit of bringing bad reports about them to their father (Genesis 37:2). The tension in the household grew as Joseph shared his ambitious dreams (Genesis 37:5,8). The problem was that the intense jealousy of the brothers threatened the unity of the family and the very life of the young dreamer (Genesis 37:11,18-20).

## Joseph's Favored Condition
Joseph was a seventeen-year-old shepherd living in the comfort of his father's house in Hebron (Genesis 37:2,14). He was the son of Jacob's old age and was greatly loved by his father (Genesis 37:3). He did not perform any special fast or prayer before the dreams came to him (Genesis 37:5). He was simply a boy serving his family and wearing the mark of his father's favor (Genesis 37:2-3).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD communicated Joseph's future exaltation through two symbolic dreams that involved his family members.

> He said to them, "Hear this dream that I have dreamed: Behold, we were binding sheaves in the field, and behold, **my sheaf arose and stood upright**. And behold, **your sheaves gathered around it and bowed down to my sheaf**."
>
> Then he dreamed another dream and told it to his brothers and said, "Behold, I have dreamed another dream. Behold, the sun, the moon, and **eleven stars were bowing down to me**."
>
> — Genesis 37:6-7, 9 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Joseph had to sacrifice his home, his family connections, and his freedom (Genesis 37:28). He lost the protection of his father and was forced into slavery in a foreign culture (Genesis 37:36). He risked his life multiple times, first in the pit and later in an Egyptian prison (Genesis 37:24, 39:20). He also had to sacrifice his own right to seek revenge against those who had wronged him (Genesis 45:4-5).

## Joseph's Initial Response
The Bible does not record any rebellion or doubt from Joseph when he received the dreams (Genesis 37:5-10). He shared them openly, which showed a degree of youthful naivety but also a lack of fear (Genesis 37:5,9). However, his brothers and even his father rebuked him for the seemingly arrogant nature of the messages (Genesis 37:8,10). During his years of suffering, Joseph remained faithful to the LORD despite the apparent failure of his dreams to manifest (Genesis 39:2,21).

## Joseph's Patient Reaction
Joseph responded to his circumstances with integrity and diligence regardless of his location (Genesis 39:2-4,21-23). He served Potiphar faithfully until he was falsely accused and imprisoned (Genesis 39:6-20). In prison, he interpreted the dreams of his fellow inmates, always giving the credit to God (Genesis 40:8). After two years of being forgotten, he was called to interpret Pharaoh's dreams and again declared that only God could give an answer of peace (Genesis 41:1,14,16). He accepted his promotion to governor of Egypt as an opportunity to prepare for the coming famine (Genesis 41:40-41,48). When his brothers finally came to buy grain, Joseph tested their character rather than seeking immediate retribution (Genesis 42:7-25). He eventually revealed his identity and comforted them, acknowledging that God's hand had been directing his path all along (Genesis 45:1-8). He showed his trust by providing for his family and inviting them to settle in Egypt (Genesis 45:10-11). He lived his final days in peace, confident that the LORD would eventually bring his people back to the promised land (Genesis 50:24-25).

## The Divine Miracle
God sovereignly orchestrated the events of Joseph's life to fulfill the dreams and preserve many people through a severe famine.

> Now Joseph was governor over the land. He was the one who sold to all the people of the land. And **Joseph's brothers came and bowed themselves before him with their faces to the ground**. . . And Joseph remembered the dreams that he had dreamed of them. . . Then his brothers also came and **fell down before him and said, "Behold, we are your servants."** . . . As for you, you meant evil against me, but **God meant it for good, to bring it about that many people should be kept alive, as they are today**. — Genesis 42:6, 9, 50:18, 20 (ESV)

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the salvation of the house of Israel and the people of Egypt from starvation (Genesis 41:56-57, 47:12). Joseph's family was reunited and settled in the fertile land of Goshen (Genesis 46:29, 47:27). The nation of Israel was preserved and began to multiply greatly in Egypt (Genesis 47:27). Joseph's brothers were forgiven and their fear was replaced with God's provision (Genesis 50:21). The character of Joseph became a testament to how God can use suffering to accomplish His glorious purposes (Genesis 50:20). His story remains a powerful example of forgiveness and divine providence for all generations (Hebrews 11:22).

## Conclusion
Joseph faced the problem of extreme family jealousy and years of unjust suffering in Egypt. He was a young boy with no power to fulfill his own dreams of greatness. However, the LORD was faithful and was with him in the pit, the prison, and the palace. God's promise was miraculously fulfilled when his family bowed before him, not for his own pride, but for their survival. Joseph sacrificed his youthful comfort and his freedom, but he gained the position of a savior for his nation. His life proves that we can trust the LORD even in our darkest moments. He is the God Who turns evil into good and Who never forsakes those who wait on Him.
