# Joseph: From Pit to Palace

A favored son is betrayed by his brothers and sold into slavery in a foreign land. His journey from a deep pit to the heights of Egyptian power reveals a divine plan. Witness how God transforms betrayal into a global rescue.

## The Problem of Brotherly Hatred

Joseph was the favored son of Jacob and Rachel (Genesis 37:3). He lived in the land of Canaan with his eleven brothers (Genesis 37:1). The political environment was one of intense family rivalry (Genesis 37:4). His brothers hated him because their father loved him more than all of them (Genesis 37:4). They could not speak peacefully to him (Genesis 37:4). Their hatred intensified when Joseph shared dreams of his future authority over them (Genesis 37:5-8). Eventually, they conspired to kill him when he came to check on them in the fields (Genesis 37:18-20). They stripped him of his special robe and threw him into an empty pit (Genesis 37:23-24). Instead of killing him, they sold him to Ishmaelite traders for twenty shekels of silver (Genesis 37:28). The problem was that Joseph was a young man betrayed by his own family and sold into foreign slavery (Genesis 37:28,36).

## Joseph's Favored Condition

Joseph was seventeen years old and was shepherding the flock with his brothers (Genesis 37:2). He was the son of Jacob's old age (Genesis 37:3). Jacob loved Joseph more than all his other sons and made him a robe of many colors (Genesis 37:3). Joseph did not perform any special religious acts or sacrifices to receive his dreams (Genesis 37:5). He was simply a chosen recipient of God's revelation while living in his father's house (Genesis 37:5,9).

## The Prophetic Calling

The LORD communicated His future purpose for Joseph through two symbolic dreams.

> Now Joseph had a dream, and when he told it to his brothers they hated him even more. He said to them, "Hear this dream that I have dreamed: Behold, we were binding sheaves in the field, and behold, my sheaf arose and stood upright. And behold, **your sheaves gathered around it and bowed down to my sheaf**." . . . Then he dreamed another dream and told it to his brothers and said, "Behold, I have dreamed another dream. Behold, **the sun, the moon, and eleven stars were bowing down to me**." — Genesis 37:5-7,9 (ESV)

## The Sacrifice of Home

Joseph sacrificed his position as the favored son and his life of comfort (Genesis 37:23-28). He lost his identity in his father's house and was forced to live as a slave in Egypt (Genesis 39:1). He risked his life by maintaining his integrity in Potiphar's house (Genesis 39:7-20). He also sacrificed years of his youth in an Egyptian prison for a crime he did not commit (Genesis 39:20).

## Joseph's Initial Response

Joseph did not rebel against the dreams, but he shared them with an initial lack of discretion (Genesis 37:5,9). His openness about his future authority provoked his brothers to further hatred (Genesis 37:8). He did not refuse the calling but was initially naive about the consequences of God's revelation (Genesis 37:10).

## Joseph's Faithful Reaction

Joseph responded to his trials with consistent faithfulness to the LORD (Genesis 39:2,21). While a slave in Potiphar's house, he refused the advances of Potiphar's wife, stating he could not do such great wickedness and sin against God (Genesis 39:9). Even when he was unjustly imprisoned, he remained diligent and the LORD gave him favor with the keeper of the prison (Genesis 39:21-23). He used his God-given gift to interpret the dreams of Pharaoh's cupbearer and baker, giving all credit to God (Genesis 40:8). When eventually summoned before Pharaoh, he again declared that it was God Who would give Pharaoh a favorable answer (Genesis 41:16). He successfully interpreted Pharaoh's dreams of the coming famine and provided a plan for survival (Genesis 41:25-36). Pharaoh recognized that the Spirit of God was in Joseph and appointed him as ruler over all Egypt (Genesis 41:38-41). Joseph managed the nation's resources during the years of plenty and the years of famine (Genesis 41:47-49,54). When his brothers eventually came to Egypt to buy food, Joseph tested them but ultimately showed them mercy (Genesis 42-45). He recognized that God had sent him ahead to preserve life (Genesis 45:5). His reaction was one of total trust in God's sovereignty over every hardship (Genesis 50:20).

## The Divine Miracle

The LORD performed the miracle of elevating a foreign slave to the second highest position in Egypt. He gave Joseph the wisdom to interpret dreams and manage a global crisis so that his family would eventually come to him.

> And Pharaoh said to Joseph . . . "**You shall be over my house, and all my people shall order themselves as you command.** Only as regards the throne will I be greater than you." . . . Now Joseph was governor over the land. . . And Joseph's brothers came and **bowed themselves before him with their faces to the ground.** — Genesis 41:39-40, 42:6 (ESV)

In this miracle, the LORD fulfilled the promise that Joseph's family would **bow down to me** (Genesis 37:9, 42:6). He used the famine to bring his brothers to Egypt to fulfill the prophetic dreams (Genesis 42:6).

## The Global Result

The miracle resulted in the preservation of the nation of Egypt and the surrounding lands during a severe famine (Genesis 41:57). Joseph's entire family, seventy people in all, moved to Egypt and were provided for in the land of Goshen (Genesis 46:27, 47:11). This move protected the lineage of the promised Messiah (Genesis 49:10). The brothers were reconciled to Joseph and were humbled by his mercy (Genesis 45:14-15). The Egyptians saw the wisdom of Joseph and were saved from starvation (Genesis 41:55-56). The result was the formation of the nation of Israel within the safety of Egypt (Exodus 1:7).

## Conclusion

Joseph faced the problem of betrayal and unjust suffering. He was limited by his status as a slave and a prisoner in a foreign land. However, the LORD gave him dreams of authority and remained with him throughout his trials. Joseph responded with unwavering integrity and faith in God's character. God intervened by granting him the interpretation of Pharaoh's dreams and elevating him to power. The result was the salvation of many nations and the preservation of God's covenant family. Joseph sacrificed his freedom and his home, but he gained the honor of being a deliverer for his people. His story proves that we can trust the LORD to work all things together for good. He is faithful to His promises, even when we are in the pit.
