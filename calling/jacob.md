# Jacob: a New Identity

A man who spent his life deceiving others finds himself wrestling with a mysterious stranger in the dark. This encounter will transform him from a fugitive into the father of a nation. Discover how God's faithfulness prevailed over Jacob's manipulative past.

## The Problem of a Deceptive Heart

Jacob was the younger of twin brothers born to Isaac and Rebekah (Genesis 25:24-26). Even before birth, he struggled with his brother Esau (Genesis 25:22). His name meant "he takes by the heel" or "cheater" (Genesis 25:26). Esau was a skillful hunter and his father's favorite, while Jacob was a quiet man staying in tents and favored by his mother (Genesis 25:27-28). The family lived as sojourners in Canaan, surrounded by local inhabitants (Genesis 26:1). Jacob manipulated Esau into selling his birthright for a bowl of stew (Genesis 25:29-34). Later, he deceived his blind father into giving him the blessing intended for the firstborn (Genesis 27:18-29). This caused Esau to hate Jacob and plan his murder (Genesis 27:41). Jacob was forced to flee for his life to Haran (Genesis 27:43-45). The problem was that Jacob's deceptive nature had fractured his family and left him a lonely fugitive in fear of his brother's revenge (Genesis 27:41-43).

## Jacob's Fugitive Condition

Jacob was a lonely traveler sleeping on the ground with a stone for a pillow when God first called him (Genesis 28:11). He was fleeing from the consequences of his own deceit (Genesis 28:10). He did not perform any special action like fasting, praying, or offering sacrifices to get God's attention (Genesis 28:11-12). He was simply resting on his journey when the LORD chose to manifest **Himself** in a dream (Genesis 28:12-13). Jacob was unaware of God's presence in that place until the dream occurred (Genesis 28:16).

## The Divine Calling

God communicated with Jacob through a dream of a ladder reaching to heaven and later through a physical wrestling match.

> And he dreamed, and behold, there was a ladder set up on the earth, and the top of it reached to heaven. And behold, the angels of God were ascending and descending on it! And behold, the LORD stood above it and said, "I am the LORD, the God of Abraham your father and the God of Isaac. **The land on which you lie I will give to you and to your offspring.** Your offspring shall be as the dust of the earth, and you shall spread abroad to the west and to the east and to the north and to the south, and in you and your offspring shall all the families of the earth be blessed. Behold, **I am with you and will keep you wherever you go, and will bring you back to this land.** For I will not leave you until I have done what I have promised you." — Genesis 28:12-15 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice

Jacob had to sacrifice the comfort and security of his father's house (Genesis 28:10). He risked his life by traveling through dangerous territories alone (Genesis 28:11). Later, he sacrificed his pride by humbling himself before the brother he had cheated (Genesis 32:3-5, 33:3). He risked everything he had built in Haran, including his wives, children, and livestock, when he chose to return to Canaan as the LORD commanded (Genesis 31:17-18, 32:11).

## Jacob's Hesitant Response

After the dream at Bethel, Jacob was afraid and recognized the awesome nature of the place (Genesis 28:17). However, his trust was initially conditional (Genesis 28:20). He made a vow saying that if God would be with him, keep him, and give him bread and clothing, then the LORD would be his God (Genesis 28:20-21). He was still trying to bargain with the Almighty (Genesis 28:20-22).

## Jacob's Eventual Surrender

Jacob eventually obeyed God's command to return to the land of his kindred (Genesis 31:3,17). When he heard that Esau was coming to meet him with four hundred men, he was greatly afraid and distressed (Genesis 32:6-7). He divided his people and flocks into two camps, hoping at least one would escape (Genesis 32:7-8). For the first time, he turned to God in desperate prayer, acknowledging his unworthiness of the steadfast love and faithfulness shown to him (Genesis 32:9-12). That night, Jacob sent his family across the stream and remained alone (Genesis 32:22-24). A man wrestled with him until the breaking of the day (Genesis 32:24). Even when his hip was put out of joint, Jacob refused to let go, saying he would not stop until he received a blessing (Genesis 32:25-26). This showed a shift from relying on his own cunning to desperately clinging to God's favor (Genesis 32:26). After his name was changed to Israel, Jacob went ahead of his family and bowed to the ground seven times as he approached Esau (Genesis 33:3). He no longer tried to trick his brother but sought reconciliation through humility and generous gifts (Genesis 32:13-21, 33:10-11). He attributed all his prosperity to the grace of God (Genesis 33:5,11). Jacob eventually returned to Bethel, purged his household of foreign gods, and built an altar as the LORD had commanded (Genesis 35:1-7). He demonstrated a lifelong commitment to the God **Who** had met him in his distress (Genesis 35:3).

## The Divine Miracle

The LORD miraculously changed Esau's heart from hatred to brotherly love and preserved Jacob's entire family (Genesis 33:4). **He** also blessed Jacob's efforts in Haran despite Laban's deception (Genesis 31:7,42). Through this, God fulfilled **His** promise to be with him and keep him (Genesis 28:15). Also, God spoke to Laban in a dream to protect Jacob (Genesis 31:24).

> But God came to Laban the Aramean in a dream by night and said to him, "Be careful **not to say anything to Jacob, either good or bad**." — Genesis 31:24 (ESV)

Finally, Jacob returned safely to his father's land (Genesis 33:18).

> And Jacob came safely to the city of Shechem, which is in the land of Canaan, on his way from Paddan-aram, and **he encamped before the city.** — Genesis 33:18 (ESV)

This demonstrated that **He** would keep Jacob and bring him back just as **He** promised (Genesis 28:15).

## The National Result

The miracle resulted in the reconciliation of the two brothers and the safe return of Jacob to Canaan (Genesis 33:4,18). Jacob became the father of the twelve tribes of Israel, forming the foundation of the nation of God's people (Genesis 35:22-26). His family was preserved through his time with Laban and his encounter with Esau (Genesis 32:11, 33:5). The nations around them saw the hand of God upon Jacob, especially when a terror from God fell upon the cities around them so they did not pursue the sons of Jacob (Genesis 35:5). Jacob's transformation from a deceiver to a man who relied on God served as a testimony to his descendants (Genesis 48:15-16). The legacy of his trust in God's promise continued through his son Joseph and eventually to the entire nation (Genesis 49:1-28).

## Conclusion

Jacob faced the problem of his own deceitful character and the deadly threat from his brother Esau. He was a man limited by his tendency to manipulate and control his circumstances through trickery. However, the LORD promised to be with him and bring him back to his land. Jacob responded by eventually surrendering his pride and wrestling for God's blessing. God intervened by softening Esau's heart and protecting Jacob from Laban's schemes. The result was the birth of the nation of Israel and a restored family. Jacob sacrificed his home and his reputation as a "cheater," but he gained the identity of "Israel" and the honor of being a forefather of the Messiah. His story proves that we can trust the LORD to transform our character and fulfill **His** promises despite our past failures.
