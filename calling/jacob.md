# Jacob: The Deceiver Who Trusted God

A man fleeing for his life after deceiving his father has a dream that changes everything. He encounters God at Bethel and receives a promise. This is the story of how a deceiver became a patriarch.

## The Problem of Family Rivalry

Jacob was the son of Isaac and Rebekah (Genesis 25:19-20). He was born grasping his brother Esau's heel (Genesis 25:26). The political environment was one of family rivalry and tribal tension (Genesis 25:23, 27:41). Jacob had manipulated Esau into selling his birthright for a bowl of stew (Genesis 25:29-34). Later, he and his mother conspired to deceive Isaac into giving Jacob the blessing intended for Esau (Genesis 27:1-29). When Esau discovered the deception, he purposed to kill Jacob once their father died (Genesis 27:41). Rebekah urged Jacob to flee to her brother Laban in Haran for safety (Genesis 27:42-43). The problem was that Jacob was a fugitive from his own brother's murderous wrath (Genesis 27:41-45). He was leaving behind the protection of his family with no guarantee of safety or a future (Genesis 28:10-11).

## Jacob's Vulnerable Condition

Jacob was alone and vulnerable in a certain place when the sun had set (Genesis 28:11). He took a stone for a pillow and lay down to sleep (Genesis 28:11). He did nothing special to seek God's favor or attention (Genesis 28:11-12). He was not praying or offering a sacrifice at the time (Genesis 28:11). He was simply a traveler in flight when the LORD appeared to him in a dream (Genesis 28:10-13).

## The Heavenly Calling

The LORD spoke to Jacob through a dream of a ladder reaching to heaven with angels ascending and descending.

> "And behold, the LORD stood above it and said, 'I am the LORD, the God of Abraham your father and the God of Isaac. **The land on which you lie I will give to you and to your offspring.** Your offspring shall be like the dust of the earth, and you shall spread abroad to the west and to the east and to the north and to the south, and **in you and your offspring shall all the families of the earth be blessed.** Behold, **I am with you and will keep you wherever you go, and will bring you back to this land.** For **I will not leave you** until I have done what I have promised you.'" — Genesis 28:13-15 (ESV)

## The Sacrifice of Home

Jacob had to sacrifice his identity as a resident of his father's house (Genesis 28:10). He risked losing the inheritance he had just stolen if he could never return (Genesis 27:43-45). He lived as a servant in Laban's house for twenty years (Genesis 31:38). He sacrificed his freedom to choose his own path as he was bound by God's promise (Genesis 28:20-22).

## Jacob's Initial Response

Jacob awoke from his sleep and was afraid (Genesis 28:16-17). He acknowledged that the LORD was in that place and he did not know it (Genesis 28:16). He made a conditional vow to God (Genesis 28:20). He said that if God would be with him and keep him, then the LORD would be his God (Genesis 28:20-21).

## Jacob's Gradual Reaction

Jacob eventually responded by submitting to God's lead despite his own tendencies to manipulate (Genesis 31:3-5). After twenty years of labor in Haran, the LORD told him to return to the land of his kindred (Genesis 31:3). Jacob gathered his wives, children, and livestock to depart secretly from Laban (Genesis 31:17-21). When Laban pursued him, God warned Laban not to harm Jacob (Genesis 31:24). Jacob later prepared to meet Esau, fearing for his life and the lives of his family (Genesis 32:7-8). He prayed to God, reminding Him of His promise to be with him and to do him good (Genesis 32:9-12). He sent messengers and gifts to Esau to appease him (Genesis 32:3-5, 13-21). That night, Jacob wrestled with a man until the breaking of the day (Genesis 32:24). He refused to let go until he received a blessing (Genesis 32:26). The man changed his name from Jacob to Israel because he had striven with God and with men and had prevailed (Genesis 32:28). Jacob built an altar at Bethel as God commanded him (Genesis 35:1). He told his household to put away foreign gods and purify themselves (Genesis 35:2). He returned to the place where God had first appeared to him (Genesis 35:6-7). Jacob's response was a gradual shift from self-reliance to a deep dependence on the LORD's faithfulness (Genesis 32:10).

## The Divine Miracle

God miraculously preserved Jacob and increased his wealth despite Laban's attempts to cheat him. He also softened Esau's heart so that he welcomed Jacob back in peace.

> "Then the LORD said to Jacob, '**Return to the land of your fathers and to your kindred, and I will be with you.**'" — Genesis 31:3 (ESV)

> "So Jacob arose and set his sons and his wives on camels. He drove away all his livestock and all his property that he had gained..." — Genesis 31:17-18 (ESV)

> "**But Esau ran to meet him and embraced him and fell on his neck and kissed him, and they wept.**" — Genesis 33:4 (ESV)

> "And God said to him . . . '**The land that I gave to Abraham and Isaac I will give to you, and I will give the land to your offspring after you.**'" — Genesis 35:11-12 (ESV)

In these events, God fulfilled the promise to **be with you**, to **bring you back to this land**, and to **give the land to your offspring** (Genesis 28:13-15).

## The Global Result

The miracle resulted in the reconciliation of Jacob and Esau (Genesis 33:4). Jacob safely returned to the land of Canaan with great wealth and a large family (Genesis 33:18). His twelve sons became the patriarchs of the twelve tribes of Israel (Genesis 35:22-26). The blessing promised to Abraham and Isaac was passed to him and his descendants (Genesis 35:12). His transformation from Jacob to Israel marked the beginning of a nation set apart for God's purposes (Genesis 32:28). The neighbors and surrounding nations saw that the hand of God was upon him (Genesis 35:5).

## Conclusion

Jacob faced the problem of a broken relationship with his brother and the threat of death. He was a deceiver who relied on his own wits rather than God's promises. However, the LORD appeared to him and promised to be with him and bring him back to his land. Jacob responded by eventually surrendering his life to God's direction. God intervened by protecting him from Laban and reconciling him with Esau. The result was the establishment of the nation of Israel and the fulfillment of the covenant. Jacob sacrificed his home and his comfort, but he gained the blessing of God and the legacy of being a forefather of the Messiah. His story shows that God is faithful even to those with significant flaws. He can transform a deceiver into a prince who trusts in Him alone.
