# Elisha: the Successor to Elijah

A simple farmer is busy plowing his fields when a famous prophet suddenly throws a cloak over his shoulders. This mysterious gesture marks the beginning of a journey that will take him from the farm to the palaces of kings. Discover the secret behind his extraordinary power.

## The Problem of a Spiritual Succession
Elisha was a farmer from Abel-meholah (1 Kings 19:16,19). The political environment was one of spiritual conflict between the prophets of the LORD and the house of Ahab (1 Kings 18; 19:1-2). Elijah, the great prophet, was weary and felt alone (1 Kings 19:10). The nation of Israel was plagued by idolatry, especially the worship of Baal (1 Kings 18:18,21). The problem was that the ministry of the LORD needed to continue and expand to confront the persistent rebellion of the kings and the people (1 Kings 19:15-18).

## Elisha's Working Condition
Elisha was plowing with twelve yoke of oxen (1 Kings 19:19). He was a man of some means, as he had twelve yoke of oxen (1 Kings 19:19). He did not do anything special like praying or visiting the temple before Elijah found him (1 Kings 19:19). He was simply working in his field when the call came (1 Kings 19:19).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke to Elijah on Mount Horeb and told him to anoint Elisha.

> And the LORD said to him, "Go, return on your way to the wilderness of Damascus. And when you arrive, you shall anoint Hazael to be king over Syria. And Jehu the son of Nimshi you shall anoint to be king over Israel, and **Elisha the son of Shaphat of Abel-meholah you shall anoint to be prophet in your place**. And the one who escapes from the sword of Hazael shall Jehu put to death, and the one who escapes from the sword of Jehu shall Elisha put to death. Yet **I** will leave seven thousand in Israel, all the knees that have not bowed to Baal, and every mouth that has not kissed him." — 1 Kings 19:15-18 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Elisha had to sacrifice his livelihood and his family ties (1 Kings 19:20). He left his twelve yoke of oxen and his parents (1 Kings 19:20-21). He risked his future security to follow a hunted prophet (1 Kings 19:1-2). He sacrificed the comfort of a settled life for the uncertainty of a prophetic ministry (1 Kings 19:21).

## Elisha's Initial Response
Elisha did not rebel against the call of God (1 Kings 19:20). He asked to kiss his father and mother goodbye (1 Kings 19:20). Elijah gave him permission to return briefly, saying, "Go back again, for what have I done to you?" (1 Kings 19:20).

## Elisha's Devoted Reaction
Elisha returned from following Elijah and took the yoke of oxen and sacrificed them (1 Kings 19:21). He boiled their flesh using the equipment from the oxen and gave it to the people to eat (1 Kings 19:21). Then he arose and went after Elijah and assisted him (1 Kings 19:21). He served Elijah faithfully for years (2 Kings 3:11). When Elijah was about to be taken up to heaven, Elisha refused to leave him (2 Kings 2:2,4,6). He said three times, "As the LORD lives, and as you yourself live, I will not leave you" (2 Kings 2:2,4,6). He asked for a double portion of Elijah's spirit (2 Kings 2:9). After Elijah was taken up in a whirlwind, Elisha took up the cloak that had fallen from him (2 Kings 2:13). He struck the waters of the Jordan and they parted, confirming his succession (2 Kings 2:14). His reaction was one of total abandonment of his past and persistent commitment to his calling (1 Kings 19:21; 2 Kings 2:2).

## The Divine Miracle
God granted Elisha a double portion of Elijah's spirit and performed many miracles through him (2 Kings 2:15). He demonstrated His power by allowing Elisha to part the Jordan River (2 Kings 2:14).

> Then he took the cloak of Elijah that had fallen from him and struck the water, saying, "Where is the LORD, the God of Elijah?" And when he had struck the water, **the water was parted to the one side and to the other, and Elisha went over.** . . Now the sons of the prophets who were at Jericho saw him opposite them, and they said, "**The spirit of Elijah rests on Elisha.**" — 2 Kings 2:14-15 (ESV)

In these events, God fulfilled His promise to **anoint... Elisha... to be prophet in your place** (1 Kings 19:16).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the continuation of the prophetic voice in Israel (2 Kings 2:15). Elisha became a counselor to kings and a source of help for the common people (2 Kings 3:12; 4:1). He healed Naaman the Syrian of leprosy, showing God's power to other nations (2 Kings 5:14-15). He protected Israel from the Aramean army by revealing their secret plans (2 Kings 6:8-12). His ministry demonstrated that the LORD is the true God Who cares for both the nation and the individual (2 Kings 4:27-37).

## Conclusion
Elisha faced the problem of a nation turning away from God and the need for a successor to Elijah. He was a farmer with no previous prophetic training. However, the LORD called him to be a prophet in Elijah's place. Elisha responded by immediately leaving everything and serving Elijah with devotion. God intervened by granting him a double portion of the Spirit and confirming his authority with signs. The result was a powerful ministry that spanned decades and impacted both Israel and its neighbors. Elisha sacrificed his farm and family to follow the LORD, and in return, he gained an eternal legacy as one of the greatest prophets in history. His story proves that God can use anyone who is willing to leave their oxen behind. He is faithful to empower those He calls.
