# Gideon: Deliver Israel

A humble farmer hides in a winepress to thresh wheat, fearing the invaders who have stripped his land bare. Suddenly, a divine visitor addresses him as a "mighty man of valor" and commands him to save his nation. This journey reveals how God's strength is perfected in human weakness.

## The Oppression of Israel

Israel had fallen into a cycle of disobedience and was delivered into the hand of Midian for seven years (Judges 6:1). The Midianites and their allies would swarm the land like locusts during harvest time (Judges 6:3,5). They devoured the produce of the earth and left no sustenance for the Israelites (Judges 6:4). The people were forced to live in dens and caves to survive the relentless oppression (Judges 6:2). This constant raiding caused the people of Israel to be brought very low (Judges 6:6). They lived in a state of perpetual fear and poverty because they could not protect their food or their livestock from the overwhelming numbers of the invaders (Judges 6:4-5). The core problem was that the nation was completely defenseless against a massive coalition that laid waste to their land and threatened their very existence (Judges 6:5).

## Gideon's Humble Condition

Gideon was a man gripped by the same fear that paralyzed his entire nation (Judges 6:11). He was beating out wheat in a winepress to hide it from the Midianite raiders when the angel of the LORD appeared (Judges 6:11). Gideon did not perform any special acts of worship or repentance to gain God's attention (Judges 6:12). Instead, he was simply trying to survive while mourning the apparent absence of God's power (Judges 6:13). He viewed his clan as the weakest in his tribe (Judges 6:15).

## The Necessary Sacrifice

Before he could lead the nation, Gideon had to purge his own household of idolatry (Judges 6:25). The LORD commanded him to pull down his father's altar to Baal and cut down the Asherah pole beside it (Judges 6:25). By doing this, Gideon risked the wrath of his family and the men of his town (Judges 6:28-30). He sacrificed his safety and his reputation in his community to demonstrate his exclusive loyalty to the LORD (Judges 6:27). He also had to sacrifice his reliance on a large army (Judges 7:2-7).

## The Initial Response

Gideon's first reaction was one of deep skepticism and self-doubt (Judges 6:13). He questioned why Israel was suffering if the LORD was truly with them (Judges 6:13). He insisted that he was the least in his father's house and therefore incapable of saving Israel (Judges 6:15). Even after receiving a sign of fire from the rock, he continued to ask for further proof of God's presence (Judges 6:17,36-40). He put out a fleece twice to test if God would really do what He said (Judges 6:37,39).

## Gideon's Eventual Reaction

Despite his fears, Gideon eventually chose to trust the LORD completely (Judges 6:27). He obeyed the command to destroy the altar of Baal under the cover of night because he was afraid of his family (Judges 6:27). When the Midianite army gathered, Gideon blew the trumpet and called the tribes to follow him (Judges 6:34-35). He showed remarkable faith by accepting the drastic reduction of his army as commanded (Judges 7:4-8). He led his three hundred men to the outskirts of the Midianite camp with nothing but trumpets, jars, and torches (Judges 7:16). Gideon divided the men into three companies to surround the host (Judges 7:16). He told his men to watch him and do exactly as he did when they reached the camp (Judges 7:17). He trusted the word of the LORD that the victory was already won before the battle even began (Judges 7:15). Gideon worshiped after hearing the interpretation of a dream that confirmed God's promise (Judges 7:15). He returned to the camp of Israel and called his men to arise for the battle (Judges 7:15).

## The Divine Miracle

God achieved the victory through a supernatural intervention that turned the enemy against themselves.
> When they blew the 300 trumpets, **the LORD set every man's sword against his comrade and against all the army**. And the **army fled** as far as Beth-shittah toward Zererah, as far as the border of Abel-meholah, by Tabbath. — Judges 7:22 (ESV)
By causing the massive host to destroy one another, the LORD fulfilled His promise that Gideon would **strike the Midianites as one man** (Judges 6:16). He used the small band of three hundred to **save Israel from the hand of Midian** as He had spoken (Judges 6:14).

## The Lasting Result

The miracle resulted in the total defeat of the Midianites and their princes (Judges 7:25). The oppression that had plagued Israel for seven years was finally broken (Judges 8:28). The nation was liberated from the fear of raiding parties and could once again enjoy the produce of their land (Judges 6:4, 8:28). Gideon's family was restored to a position of leadership and honor in Israel (Judges 8:22). The surrounding nations saw that the LORD fought for His people, which brought a period of peace that lasted for forty years (Judges 8:28). Gideon's faith, though it started small, became a beacon of hope for his neighbors and his entire tribe.

## Conclusion

Gideon faced the overwhelming problem of Midianite oppression with a deep sense of personal limitation. He was a fearful man from a weak clan who failed to see God's hand at work in his life. However, the LORD made a promise to be with him and to strike the enemy down. Gideon responded by sacrificing his idols and his reliance on military strength. God's intervention through the miracle of the three hundred men proved His absolute faithfulness. Gideon sacrificed his comfort and his large army, but he gained the salvation of his nation and a legacy of divine trust. This story reminds us that we can always trust the LORD. He never forsakes those He calls, regardless of their failures.
