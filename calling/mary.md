# Mary: Unmarried Mother

A young woman in a small town receives a visitor who will change history. Her "yes" brings Jesus into the world but puts her life and reputation on the line. Her trust in God's plan defies all human logic and tradition.

## The Problem of a Scandalous Promise
Mary lived in Nazareth, a town of little repute in the region of Galilee (John 1:46; Luke 1:26). She was betrothed to a man named Joseph (Luke 1:27). The political environment was dominated by the Roman Empire's occupation of Israel (Luke 2:1). During this time, Herod the Great ruled as a client king for Rome (Luke 1:5). Under the Law of Moses, a betrothed woman found to be with child by another man faced public disgrace or death by stoning (Deuteronomy 22:23-24). Joseph, her betrothed, could have had her publicly shamed or quietly divorced (Matthew 1:19). The nation of Israel was also waiting in spiritual darkness for the promised Messiah to arrive (Luke 1:78-79). There had been no prophetic word from God for four hundred years (Malachi 4:5-6). The problem was that Mary faced potential execution and social ruin for a pregnancy that appeared scandalous to her community (Matthew 1:19).

## Mary's Humble Condition
Mary was a young virgin living in the humble town of Nazareth (Luke 1:26-27). She was engaged to Joseph of the house of David (Luke 1:27). She did not perform any special religious acts for God's attention (Luke 1:28). She was simply a recipient of God's favor (Luke 1:28,30). The angel Gabriel appeared to her while she was in Nazareth (Luke 1:26).

## The Divine Calling
God sent the angel Gabriel to Nazareth to deliver a message to Mary.

> And he came to her and said, “Greetings, O favored one, the Lord is with you!” But she was greatly troubled at the saying, and tried to discern what sort of greeting this might be. And the angel said to her, “Do not be afraid, Mary, for you have found favor with God. And behold, **you will conceive in your womb and bear a son, and you shall call his name Jesus**. He will be great and will be called [the Son of the Most High](https://son.ofgod.info). And the Lord God will give to him the throne of his father David, and he will reign over the house of Jacob forever, and of his kingdom there will be no end.”
>
> And Mary said to the angel, “How will this be, since I am a virgin?”
>
> And the angel answered her, “**The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you; therefore the child to be born will be called holy, the Son of God.** And behold, your relative Elizabeth in her old age has also conceived a son, and this is the sixth month with her who was called barren. For nothing will be impossible with God.” And Mary said, “Behold, I am the servant of the Lord; let it be to me according to your word.” And the angel departed from her. — Luke 1:28-38 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Mary sacrificed her reputation and her future security in her community (Luke 1:38). She risked being rejected by Joseph, her betrothed, which would have left her a social outcast (Matthew 1:19). She faced the potential penalty of death for perceived adultery under the Law (Deuteronomy 22:23-24). She gave up her own plans for a quiet life in Nazareth to become the mother of the Messiah (Luke 1:38). She risked the misunderstanding and judgment of her family and neighbors (Matthew 1:19).

## Mary's Initial Response
Mary did not rebel or refuse the calling of God (Luke 1:38). She was initially troubled by the angel's greeting and tried to discern its meaning (Luke 1:29). She did, however, ask a question to understand how the promise would be fulfilled (Luke 1:34). She showed a desire for clarity rather than a heart of unbelief (Luke 1:34). Her response was one of humble inquiry rather than stubborn resistance (Luke 1:34,38).

## Mary's Eventual Reaction
Mary responded with total submission to God's will by calling herself the servant of the Lord (Luke 1:38). She immediately went to visit her relative Elizabeth in the hill country of Judah (Luke 1:39). Upon entering the house, she heard Elizabeth's confirmation of the blessing (Luke 1:41-45). Mary then broke into a song of praise, known as the Magnificat, which glorified God for His mercy and faithfulness (Luke 1:46-55). She stayed with Elizabeth for about three months before returning home (Luke 1:56). When she returned, she remained faithful to the promise even as Joseph contemplated a quiet divorce (Matthew 1:19-20). She trusted that God would intervene with Joseph as He had with her (Matthew 1:20). Mary continued to believe the word of the Lord despite the physical and social changes she experienced (Luke 1:45). She traveled to Bethlehem while heavily pregnant to comply with the Roman census, trusting God's timing for the birth (Luke 2:4-5). She kept all these things and pondered them in her heart as the events unfolded (Luke 2:19,51). Her reaction was characterized by enduring faith and quiet reflection on the works of the Lord (Luke 2:19).

## The Divine Miracle
God performed the miracle of the virgin birth, fulfilling the ancient prophecy and His promise to Mary.

> [“Behold, the virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and they shall call his name Immanuel”](https://prophecies.ofgod.info/trinitarian/isaiah-7-14) (which means, God with us). When Joseph woke from sleep, he did as the angel of the Lord commanded him: he took his wife, but knew her not until **she had given birth to a son. And he called his name Jesus.** — Matthew 1:23-25 (ESV)

In this miracle, God fulfilled His promise that Mary would conceive in her womb and bear a son (Luke 1:31). He also ensured that the child would be called [the Son of God](https://son.ofgod.info) by the power of the Most High (Luke 1:35).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the birth of Jesus Christ, the Savior of the world (Luke 2:11). Joseph was encouraged by an angel in a dream to take Mary as his wife, preserving her reputation and providing a father for the child (Matthew 1:20-24). The shepherds and the wise men came to worship the newborn King, bringing joy and confirmation to Mary (Luke 2:16-17; Matthew 2:11). The birth of Jesus brought the light of salvation to Israel and a blessing to all nations (Luke 2:30-32). Mary herself was called blessed by all generations (Luke 1:48).

## Conclusion
Mary faced the problem of a potentially life-threatening and scandalous pregnancy in a strict society. She was a simple young woman with no social standing or power to protect herself. However, when the Lord called her to be the mother of the Son of God, she responded with immediate faith and surrender. God's promise was miraculously fulfilled through the virgin birth, and He protected Mary's life and reputation through Joseph's obedience. Mary sacrificed her comfort, her social standing, and her safety to follow God's call. In return, she gained the honor of being the mother of the Messiah and saw the fulfillment of God's plan for the redemption of the world. Her story proves that we can trust the LORD with our very lives. He is faithful to His word and never forsakes those who believe in Him.
