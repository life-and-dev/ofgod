# Hannah: The Vow of a Mother

A heartbroken woman stands in the temple, her lips moving in silent desperation. She makes a staggering promise to the Almighty: if **He** gives her a son, she will give him back to **Him** forever. This is the story of how Hannah trusted God with her greatest desire.

## The Problem of Barrenness and Mockery
Hannah lived in the hill country of Ephraim during a time of spiritual decline in Israel (1 Samuel 1:1). Her husband, Elkanah, had two wives, Hannah and Peninnah (1 Samuel 1:2). While Peninnah had children, the LORD had closed Hannah's womb (1 Samuel 1:2,5). Year after year, Peninnah would cruelly provoke Hannah to irritate her because of her barrenness (1 Samuel 1:6-7). This ongoing harassment caused Hannah to weep and refuse to eat even during the holy festivals (1 Samuel 1:7-8). She felt deeply humiliated and forgotten in a society that valued childbearing as a sign of divine favor (1 Samuel 1:6). Her husband's attempts to comfort her were insufficient for her grieving soul (1 Samuel 1:8). The problem was Hannah's intense personal suffering and social reproach due to her inability to bear a child (1 Samuel 1:10-11).

## Hannah's Humble Condition
Hannah was in deep distress and bitterness of soul when she went to the temple in Shiloh (1 Samuel 1:10). She did something special to get God's attention by praying fervently and making a radical vow (1 Samuel 1:11). She promised that if the LORD would look on her affliction and give her a son, she would dedicate him to the LORD for all the days of his life (1 Samuel 1:11). She was pouring out her soul before the LORD in such a way that the priest Eli initially thought she was drunk (1 Samuel 1:12-15).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD responded to Hannah's desperate prayer through Eli the priest, who delivered a divine assurance that her petition would be heard.

> Then Eli answered, "Go in peace, and the **God of Israel grant your petition that you have made to Him**." — 1 Samuel 1:17 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Hannah had to sacrifice the very child she had spent years longing for (1 Samuel 1:11). She vowed to give him up to the service of the LORD as soon as he was weaned, meaning she would not get to raise him in her own home (1 Samuel 1:22). She risked losing the comfort and joy of her firstborn son to fulfill her promise to God (1 Samuel 1:28). She also sacrificed her reputation by appearing "drunk" while praying in her extreme anguish (1 Samuel 1:13-14).

## The Initial Response
Hannah did not rebel against God, but she did initially struggle with intense sorrow and a sense of being forgotten (1 Samuel 1:10). She did not refuse the call or the promise, but her heart was heavy with grief before the word of peace came (1 Samuel 1:15). Once she received the word from Eli, her countenance changed and she was no longer sad (1 Samuel 1:18).

## Hannah's Faithful Reaction
Hannah's reaction was one of immediate faith and worship (1 Samuel 1:18-19). After hearing Eli's words, she went her way, ate, and her face was no longer sad, showing she trusted the promise before it was fulfilled (1 Samuel 1:18). The family rose early in the morning and worshiped before the LORD before returning to their home in Ramah (1 Samuel 1:19). When the child, Samuel, was born, she did not forget her vow (1 Samuel 1:22). She waited until he was weaned and then brought him to the house of the LORD in Shiloh (1 Samuel 1:24). She brought him with a bull, an ephah of flour, and a skin of wine as an offering (1 Samuel 1:24). She presented the child to Eli, reminding him of her prayer (1 Samuel 1:26-27). She declared that because she had asked for him from the LORD, he would be lent to the LORD for as long as he lived (1 Samuel 1:28). She then broke into a magnificent prayer of thanksgiving that celebrated God's power to lift up the humble (1 Samuel 2:1-10). She trusted God to care for her young son in the temple far from her side (1 Samuel 2:11). She visited him every year, bringing him a little robe she had made, continuing her devotion (1 Samuel 2:19). Her reaction proved her absolute trust in the faithfulness of the LORD (1 Samuel 2:1-2).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD performed the miracle of opening Hannah's womb and remembering her specific request.

> And Elkanah knew Hannah his wife, and **the LORD remembered her**. And in due time **Hannah conceived and bore a son, and she called his name Samuel**, for she said, "I have asked for him from the LORD." — 1 Samuel 1:19-20 (ESV)

This miracle fulfilled the promise that the God of Israel would grant her petition (1 Samuel 1:17). **He** removed her reproach and gave her the son she had sought through tears (1 Samuel 1:20).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the birth of Samuel, who became one of Israel's greatest prophets and judges (1 Samuel 3:19-20). He led the nation back to the LORD and anointed the first kings of Israel (1 Samuel 7:3, 10:1, 16:13). For Hannah, her faithfulness was rewarded with even more children, as the LORD gave her three more sons and two daughters (1 Samuel 2:21). Her story became a testimony of hope for all who are afflicted and lowly (1 Samuel 2:8). Her song of praise influenced later biblical writings, including the Magnificat of Mary (Luke 1:46-55). The entire nation of Israel was blessed through the leadership of the son she had willingly given to God (1 Samuel 3:20).

## Conclusion
Hannah faced the problem of barrenness and cruel mockery with a broken heart. She was a woman with no power to change her circumstances, but she turned to the LORD with a radical vow. She sacrificed her right to raise her son to honor her promise to God. The LORD promised to grant her petition and miraculously gave her Samuel. Hannah responded with joy and kept her word, surrendering her child to the temple. In return for her sacrifice, she gained the honor of being the mother of a great prophet and was blessed with many more children. Her life proves that when we trust God with our most precious treasures, **He** is faithful to provide and multiply **His** blessings. We can always trust that the LORD will not forsake those who pour out their souls to **Him**.
