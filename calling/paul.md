# Paul: From Persecutor to Apostle

A zealous Pharisee travels to Damascus to arrest followers of the Way, only to be stopped by a blinding light and a voice from heaven. This unexpected encounter transforms a violent persecutor into the greatest messenger of the Gospel. It shows how no one is beyond God's reach.

## The Problem of a Violent Aggressor
Saul of Tarsus lived during a time of great upheaval for the early church in Jerusalem (Acts 8:1). The religious leaders viewed the followers of Jesus as a dangerous sect that threatened Jewish tradition and law (Acts 6:13-14). Saul was a Pharisee, deeply committed to the traditions of his fathers and intensely zealous for God (Philippians 3:5-6; Galatians 1:14). He was present at the stoning of Stephen, the first Christian martyr, and approved of his execution (Acts 7:58,8:1). Following this event, a great persecution arose against the church, and Saul became a leading figure in trying to destroy it (Acts 8:1,3). He went from house to house, dragging off men and women and committing them to prison (Acts 8:3). His reputation for cruelty spread among the believers, who lived in constant fear of his arrival (Acts 9:13,21). The political and religious environment was one of hostility and violence toward anyone who confessed Jesus as the Messiah (Acts 9:1-2). The problem was that Saul was a violent aggressor who sought to eradicate the church and silence the message of Jesus forever (1 Timothy 1:13).

## Saul's Blind Condition
Saul was still breathing threats and murder against the disciples of the Lord when he approached the high priest (Acts 9:1). He was not seeking God or repenting of his actions (Acts 9:1). Instead, he was actively pursuing further authority to arrest Christians in Damascus (Acts 9:2). He did nothing special to earn God's attention or favor (Acts 9:1-3). He was a self-righteous persecutor who believed he was serving God by killing **His** people (John 16:2; Acts 26:9). He was in a state of spiritual blindness and rebellion (Acts 26:14).

## The Divine Calling
Jesus appeared to Saul in a blinding light on the road to Damascus. Jesus spoke directly to him from heaven to stop his persecution and appoint him as a witness.

> And when we had all fallen to the ground, I heard a voice saying to me in the Hebrew language, "Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me? It is hard for you to kick against the goads." And I said, "Who are you, Lord?" And the Lord said, "**i am Jesus whom you are persecuting.** But rise and stand upon your feet, for **i have appeared to you for this purpose, to appoint you as a servant and witness** to the things in which you have seen me and to those in which i will appear to you, delivering you from your people and from the Gentiles. To whom **i am sending you to open their eyes, so that they may turn from darkness to light** and from the power of Satan to God, that they may receive forgiveness of sins and a place among those who are sanctified by faith in me." — Acts 26:14-18 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Saul had to sacrifice his high standing and reputation within the Jewish community (Philippians 3:7-8). He gave up his career as a prominent Pharisee and the power that came with it (Acts 26:10-12). He risked his personal safety and became a target for the very people he once led (Acts 9:23). He left behind his previous identity and all the earthly advantages he had gained through his zeal for the law (Philippians 3:4-6). He traded a life of influence for a life of suffering (Acts 9:16).

## Saul's Initial Response
Saul was blinded by the glory of the light and had to be led by the hand into Damascus (Acts 9:8). For three days he was without sight and neither ate nor drank (Acts 9:9). He spent this time in prayer, waiting for further instruction from the Lord (Acts 9:11). He did not rebel against the vision but submitted to the authority of the one who spoke to him (Acts 26:19). His heart was immediately humbled by the encounter (Acts 9:6).

## Paul's Faithful Reaction
When a disciple named Ananias came to him, Saul received his sight and was baptized (Acts 9:17-18). He did not wait long before he began to proclaim Jesus in the synagogues, saying that he is the Son of God (Acts 9:20). All who heard him were amazed because they knew his past as a persecutor (Acts 9:21). Saul increased all the more in strength and confounded the Jews who lived in Damascus by proving that Jesus was the Christ (Acts 9:22). He chose to follow the heavenly vision regardless of the danger (Acts 26:19). He later went to Jerusalem to join the disciples, though they were initially afraid of him (Acts 9:26). Barnabas took him and brought him to the apostles, declaring how Saul had seen the Lord and preached boldly in Damascus (Acts 9:27). Saul then moved in and out among them in Jerusalem, preaching boldly in the name of the Lord (Acts 9:28). He spoke and disputed against the Hellenists, who then sought to kill him (Acts 9:29). When the brothers learned of this, they brought him down to Caesarea and sent him off to Tarsus (Acts 9:30). He completely abandoned his mission of destruction to take up the mission of the Gospel (Galatians 1:23). His life became a testament to the power of God's grace to change a heart (1 Timothy 1:14-16). He suffered many things for the sake of the name of Jesus, including beatings, imprisonment, and shipwrecks (2 Corinthians 11:23-27). Through it all, he remained faithful to the calling he received on the road (2 Timothy 4:7). He traveled extensively to plant churches and strengthen the believers (Acts 13-20). He never turned back from his commitment to the Lord (Philippians 3:13-14).

## The Divine Miracle
God performed the miracle of transforming Saul's heart and providing him with supernatural protection and guidance.

> "So Ananias departed and entered the house. And laying his hands on him he said, 'Brother Saul, the Lord Jesus who appeared to you on the road by which you came has sent me so that you may regain your sight and be filled with the Holy Spirit.' And immediately something like scales fell from his eyes, and **he regained his sight**. Then he rose and was baptized." — Acts 9:17-18 (ESV)

Later, when his enemies plotted to kill him, God provided a way of escape.

> "But their plot became known to Saul. They were watching the gates day and night in order to kill him, but **his disciples took him by night and let him down through an opening in the wall, lowering him in a basket.**" — Acts 9:24-25 (ESV)

These miracles fulfilled the promise that the Lord would **deliver him from his people** and the Gentiles (Acts 26:17). God's grace was sufficient for him in every trial (2 Corinthians 12:9).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle of Paul's conversion resulted in the rapid expansion of the church among the Gentiles (Acts 13:47-48). He traveled throughout the Roman Empire, planting churches in cities like Ephesus, Corinth, and Philippi (Acts 14-20). The church in Judea, Galilee, and Samaria had peace and was being built up (Acts 9:31). Walking in the fear of the Lord and in the comfort of the Holy Spirit, it multiplied (Acts 9:31). Paul wrote many letters that became a significant part of the New Testament, providing foundational doctrine for all believers (2 Peter 3:15-16). His life demonstrated that God can use even the worst of sinners to accomplish **His** purposes (1 Timothy 1:15-16). His ministry brought the light of the Gospel to nations that had been in darkness for centuries (Acts 26:18,23). Many thousands of people were saved through his preaching and the power of the Holy Spirit working through him (Acts 19:10,20).

## Conclusion
Paul was a violent persecutor who was limited by his own hatred and self-righteousness. He sought to destroy the church, but the Lord had a different plan for his life. Despite his past failures, Jesus called him to be a servant and a witness. Paul responded with immediate obedience and sacrificed his status and safety to follow the Messiah. God intervened by changing his heart and protecting him from those who sought his life. The result was a legacy of faith that continues to impact the world today. Paul sacrificed his earthly reputation but gained an eternal inheritance and the joy of knowing Christ. His story proves that **the LORD** is faithful and can use anyone who submits to **Him**. We can trust that **He** will never forsake **His** own, no matter how far they have wandered.
