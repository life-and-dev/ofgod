# Paul: From Persecutor to Apostle

Saul of Tarsus, the most feared enemy of the early church, was on a mission to eliminate every follower of Jesus. A blinding light on the road to Damascus suddenly halts his journey and leaves him in total darkness. Discover how this encounter changed the world forever.

## The Problem of Violent Opposition
The early Christian community in Jerusalem faced intense persecution after the stoning of Stephen (Acts 7:58-60, 8:1). Saul was a primary leader of this movement, ravaging the church and dragging off men and women to prison (Acts 8:3). He was a Pharisee, extremely zealous for the traditions of his fathers (Galatians 1:14). The political environment was tense, with the Jewish authorities in Jerusalem seeking to suppress the growing followers of "the Way" (Acts 9:2). Saul received authority from the high priests to arrest any who called on the name of Jesus (Acts 9:14). He even traveled to foreign cities to persecute them (Acts 26:11). The believers lived in constant fear of arrest and death (Acts 9:1,13). The problem was that the young church was under a systemic and violent threat of annihilation from within its own religious community (Acts 8:1, 9:1-2).

## Saul's Blind Condition
Saul was breathing threats and murder against the disciples of the Lord (Acts 9:1). He was on his way to Damascus with letters of authority from the high priest (Acts 9:2). He did absolutely nothing to seek God's mercy or to get His attention before the encounter (Acts 9:1-3). He was convinced that his violent opposition to Jesus was a service to God (Acts 26:9). He was in a state of active rebellion against the Messiah when Jesus chose to intervene (Acts 9:3-4).

## The Heavenly Calling
Jesus spoke directly to Saul in a blinding light on the road to Damascus (Acts 9:3-5).

> "And when we had all fallen to the ground, I heard a voice saying to me in the Hebrew language, 'Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting me? It is hard for you to kick against the goads.' And I said, 'who are you, Lord?' And the Lord said, 'i am Jesus whom you are persecuting. But rise and stand upon your feet, for i have appeared to you for this purpose, **to appoint you as a servant and witness** to the things in which you have seen me and to those in which i will appear to you, **delivering you from your people and from the Gentiles**; to whom i am sending you **to open their eyes, so that they may turn from darkness to light** and from the power of Satan to God, that they may receive forgiveness of sins and a place among those who are sanctified by faith in me.'" — Acts 26:14-18 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Paul had to sacrifice his prestigious position as a Pharisee and his standing in the Jewish community (Philippians 3:4-7). He gave up his reputation, his safety, and the comfort of his former life (Acts 9:23-25). He risked being seen as a traitor by his colleagues and an impostor by the Christians (Acts 9:21,26). He sacrificed his own righteousness based on the Law to gain the righteousness that comes through faith (Philippians 3:8-9). He willingly embraced a life of suffering and hardship (2 Corinthians 11:23-27).

## Saul's Initial Response
Saul fell to the ground in terror and was blinded by the light (Acts 9:4,8-9). He did not argue or resist the voice from heaven (Acts 9:6). Instead, he asked, "who are you, Lord?" and then "What shall I do, Lord?" (Acts 22:10). He spent three days in Damascus without sight, neither eating nor drinking (Acts 9:9). He spent this time in prayer, waiting for the instruction he was promised (Acts 9:11).

## Paul's Faithful Reaction
After Ananias visited him and his sight was restored, Paul was baptized immediately (Acts 9:18). He did not consult with any human being but began to proclaim Jesus in the synagogues (Galatians 1:16-17; Acts 9:20). He declared that Jesus is [the Son of God](https://son.ofgod.info), confounding the Jews who lived in Damascus (Acts 9:20-22). When a plot was made to kill him, he escaped by being lowered in a basket through an opening in the wall (Acts 9:23-25). He traveled to Jerusalem to join the disciples, though they were initially afraid of him (Acts 9:26). He proved his transformation by speaking and arguing boldly in the name of the Lord (Acts 9:27-29). Paul eventually traveled throughout the Roman Empire, establishing churches and appointing leaders (Acts 13-21). He endured shipwrecks, beatings, and imprisonments without wavering in his trust (2 Corinthians 11:23-28). He wrote many letters to encourage and instruct the growing body of believers (2 Peter 3:15-16). His reaction was one of total and lifelong surrender to the service of the Lord (Acts 20:24).

## The Divine Miracle
God performed a miracle by transforming His greatest enemy into His most effective witness (Acts 9:20-22). He restored Paul's sight and protected him from those who sought his life (Acts 9:18,25).

> "And immediately something like scales fell from his eyes, and he regained his sight. Then he rose and was baptized . . . and immediately **he proclaimed Jesus in the synagogues, saying, 'he is the Son of God.'** . . . and Saul increased all the more in strength, and confounded the Jews who lived in Damascus by proving that Jesus was the Christ. . . . but their plot became known to Saul. They were watching the gates day and night in order to kill him, but his disciples took him by night and **let him down through an opening in the wall, lowering him in a basket.**" — Acts 9:18-25 (ESV)

In this miracle, Jesus fulfilled the promise **to appoint you as a servant and witness** and to begin **delivering you from your people** (Acts 26:16-17). The power of God turned a heart of stone into a heart of fire for the Gospel (Acts 9:20).

## The Global Result
The miracle resulted in the explosive growth of the church among the Gentiles (Acts 13:46-49). Paul's missionary journeys took the Gospel to Cyprus, Asia Minor, Greece, and eventually Rome (Acts 13-28). His letters form a significant portion of the New Testament, providing the theological foundation for the church throughout the ages (2 Peter 3:15-16). His transformation silenced many critics and encouraged countless believers to stand firm in their faith (Galatians 1:23-24). The neighbors and former colleagues of Paul saw the power of God to change a life completely (Acts 9:21). The result was a global movement that continues to this day, bringing the light of Jesus to every nation (Acts 13:47).

## Conclusion
Paul faced the problem of his own zealous hatred and the mission to destroy the church. He was limited by his Pharisaic pride and his violent past. However, Jesus promised to make him a servant and a witness to the Gentiles. Paul responded by abandoning his status and risking his life for the name of the Lord. God intervened by miraculously converting him and preserving him through countless dangers. The result was the spread of the Gospel to the ends of the earth. Paul sacrificed his religious reputation and earthly safety, but he gained an eternal crown of righteousness and the joy of knowing the Lord. His story proves that we can trust the LORD to use anyone, regardless of their past. He is faithful to His word and never forsakes His own.
