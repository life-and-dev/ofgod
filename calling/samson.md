# Samson: Final Strength

A man of supernatural strength is born to a barren woman to begin the deliverance of his people. His life is marked by personal failures and a tragic betrayal leading to his capture. Discover how his final prayer reveals the unwavering faithfulness of God in the midst of ruins.

## The Philistine Oppression
The people of Israel again did what was evil in the sight of the LORD (Judges 13:1). As a result, the LORD gave them into the hand of the Philistines for forty years (Judges 13:1). The Philistines ruled over Israel during this time (Judges 14:4). The Israelites were unable to free themselves from this heavy yoke (Judges 15:11). Even the men of Judah acknowledged the Philistine dominion, choosing to bind their own countryman rather than face the wrath of their oppressors (Judges 15:11). The people were in a state of spiritual and political decline, having turned away from their God (Judges 13:1). The problem was that Israel was suffering under forty years of Philistine rule because of their repeated disobedience (Judges 13:1).

## The Barren Condition
Manoah was a man of Zorah from the tribe of the Danites (Judges 13:2). His wife was barren and had no children (Judges 13:2). There is no record that she or Manoah did anything special like fasting or visiting a temple to get God's attention (Judges 13:2-3). She was simply a barren woman living in a time of national crisis (Judges 13:2). Despite her lack of prior ritualistic action, the angel of the LORD appeared to her to announce a miraculous birth (Judges 13:3).

## The Divine Calling
The angel of the LORD appeared to Manoah's wife to deliver a specific promise and a set of instructions for her unborn son (Judges 13:3).

> And the angel of the LORD appeared to the woman and said to her, "Behold, you are barren and have not borne children, but **you shall conceive and bear a son**. Therefore be careful and drink no wine or strong drink, and eat nothing unclean, for behold, **you shall conceive and bear a son**. No razor shall come upon his head, for the child shall be a Nazirite to God from the womb, and **he shall begin to save Israel from the hand of the Philistines**." — Judges 13:3-5 (ESV)

## The Nazirite Sacrifice
Samson was called to be a Nazirite to God from the womb to the day of his death (Judges 13:5,7). This required a lifelong sacrifice of certain freedoms, including the prohibition against cutting his hair, drinking wine, or touching anything unclean (Judges 13:4-5, 14:8-9). He risked losing the source of his strength if he failed to keep his Nazirite vow (Judges 16:17,20). Eventually, he sacrificed his own life to fulfill the purpose for which **He** had called him (Judges 16:30).

## The Initial Response
Samson did not initially rebel against the calling, but he frequently ignored the holiness of his vow (Judges 14:1,8-9). He insisted on marrying a Philistine woman despite his parents' objections (Judges 14:1-3). He touched the carcass of a lion, violating the Nazirite rule against touching the dead (Judges 14:8-9). Later, he allowed his lust for Delilah to overcome his caution, eventually revealing the secret of his strength (Judges 16:15-17). He did not realize that the LORD had left him until his hair was shaved and he was captured (Judges 16:20).

## Samson's Final Reaction
After being blinded and imprisoned, Samson was brought out to entertain the Philistine lords in the temple of Dagon (Judges 16:21,25). His hair had begun to grow again during his time in prison (Judges 16:22). He asked a young man to lead him to the pillars that supported the house so he could lean against them (Judges 16:26). In this moment of total humiliation and weakness, Samson turned back to the LORD in prayer (Judges 16:28). He acknowledged his dependence on God rather than his own power (Judges 16:28). He asked the LORD for strength one last time to avenge his eyes and fulfill his mission (Judges 16:28). He grasped the two middle pillars and leaned his weight against them (Judges 16:29). He was willing to die with his enemies to see God's judgment executed (Judges 16:30). He bowed with all his strength, trusting that the LORD would answer his final plea (Judges 16:30). His reaction was a transition from self-reliance to a desperate and final trust in the faithfulness of the LORD (Judges 16:28-30).

## The Miraculous Deliverance
God answered Samson's prayer and provided the strength necessary to bring down the temple of the Philistines (Judges 16:28,30).

> Then Samson called to the LORD and said, "O Lord GOD, please remember me and please strengthen me only this once, O God, that I may be avenged on the Philistines for my two eyes." And Samson grasped the two middle pillars on which the house rested, and he leaned his weight against them, his right hand on the one and his left hand on the other. And Samson said, "Let me die with the Philistines." Then **he bowed with all his strength, and the house fell upon the lords and upon all the people who were in it.** So the dead whom he killed at his death were more than those whom he had killed during his life. — Judges 16:28-30 (ESV)

Through this miracle, the LORD fulfilled the promise that Samson would **begin to save Israel from the hand of the Philistines** (Judges 13:5).

## The Result of the Collapse
The miracle resulted in the death of the Philistine lords and thousands of their people (Judges 16:27,30). It severely weakened the Philistine grip on Israel by removing their central leadership in a single event (Judges 16:30). Samson's family came down and buried him in the tomb of his father (Judges 16:31). He had judged Israel for twenty years (Judges 16:31). His death initiated the decline of Philistine power that would continue through the times of Samuel and David (1 Samuel 7:13). Samson is remembered in the "Hall of Faith" as one who through faith gained strength out of weakness (Hebrews 11:32-34).

## Conclusion
Samson was born into a nation oppressed by the Philistines due to their own rebellion. He was limited by his own impulsive desires and failed to maintain the holiness of his Nazirite calling. Despite his many personal failures and his ultimate capture, the LORD'S promise to use him did not fail. In his final moments, Samson turned to God, and the LORD provided the strength to strike a decisive blow against the enemy. He sacrificed his sight, his freedom, and ultimately his life, but he gained the honor of fulfilling his divine purpose. The result was the beginning of Israel's deliverance and a testimony of God's grace. This story proves that even when we fail, we can trust the LORD to be faithful to **His** word. **He** never forsakes those who call upon **Him** in their time of need.
