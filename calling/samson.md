# Samson: Strength in Weakness

A man blessed with supernatural strength is called to deliver his nation from forty years of oppression. His secret lies in a sacred vow that he repeatedly tests. Discover how God used a flawed champion to begin the salvation of his people.

## The Problem of Oppression
The people of Israel again did what was evil in the sight of the LORD (Judges 13:1). As a result, the LORD gave them into the hand of the Philistines for forty years (Judges 13:1). The Philistines ruled over Israel during this time (Judges 14:4). Even the men of Judah acknowledged this subjection (Judges 15:11). They asked Samson if he did not know that the Philistines were rulers over them (Judges 15:11). The Israelites lived in fear and subjection to their neighbors (Judges 15:11-12). The problem was that Israel was trapped under the heavy hand of the Philistines because of their own rebellion against God (Judges 13:1).

## Samson's Unlikely Condition
Manoah of Zorah had a wife who was barren and had no children (Judges 13:2). She did nothing special like fasting or praying at the temple to get God's attention (Judges 13:3). The angel of the LORD appeared to her unexpectedly while she was in the field (Judges 13:3,9). She was simply a woman facing the personal sorrow of barrenness when God chose to intervene (Judges 13:2-3).

## The Heavenly Calling
The angel of the LORD appeared to the woman and later to Manoah to announce the birth and mission of their son (Judges 13:3,11).

> "Behold, you are barren and have not borne children, but you shall conceive and bear a son. Therefore be careful and drink no wine or strong drink, and eat nothing unclean, for behold, you shall conceive and bear a son. **No razor shall come upon his head**, for the child shall be a Nazirite to God from the womb, and **he shall begin to save Israel from the hand of the Philistines.**"
>
> — Judges 13:3-5 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Samson had to sacrifice the normal freedoms of an Israelite by living as a Nazirite from birth (Judges 13:5,7). He was forbidden from drinking wine or strong drink and from cutting his hair (Judges 13:4-5). He risked his life and safety by engaging in a lonely war against the Philistine rulers (Judges 14:19). He sacrificed his reputation among his own people who eventually bound him to hand him over to the enemy (Judges 15:12-13).

## Samson's Initial Response
The Bible does not record Samson's first reaction to his calling as a child. However, as a young man, he chose to marry a daughter of the Philistines (Judges 14:1-2). He ignored the protests of his parents who wanted him to marry within his own people (Judges 14:3). He insisted that she was right in his eyes (Judges 14:3). He did not initially show a heart for delivering Israel but rather sought his own desires among the oppressors (Judges 14:1-3).

## Samson's Eventual Reaction
Samson eventually responded to the stirring of the Spirit of the LORD (Judges 13:25). When a young lion roared at him, the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon him and he tore it in pieces (Judges 14:5-6). He later went down to Ashkelon and struck down thirty men to pay a debt from a riddle (Judges 14:19). After the Philistines burned his wife and father-in-law, he struck them hip and thigh with a great blow (Judges 15:7-8). He allowed the men of Judah to bind him with new ropes so he could be brought to the Philistines (Judges 15:12-13). When the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon him again, the ropes melted off and he killed a thousand men with the jawbone of a donkey (Judges 15:14-15). He judged Israel for twenty years in the days of the Philistines (Judges 15:20). Even after he failed and lost his strength to Delilah, he called upon the LORD in his final moments (Judges 16:17-21,28). He asked God to remember him and strengthen him one last time (Judges 16:28). He chose to sacrifice his own life to fulfill his mission (Judges 16:30).

## The Divine Miracle
God granted Samson supernatural strength through the Holy Spirit to defeat the Philistines (Judges 14:6). In his final act, the LORD answered his prayer despite his previous failures (Judges 16:28-30).

> Then Samson called to the LORD and said, "O Lord GOD, please remember me and please strengthen me only this once, O God, that I may be avenged on the Philistines for my two eyes." And Samson grasped the two middle pillars on which the house rested. . . And Samson said, "Let me die with the Philistines." Then **he bowed with all his strength, and the house fell upon the lords and upon all the people who were in it.** So **the dead whom he killed at his death were more than those whom he had killed during his life.**
>
> — Judges 16:28-30 (ESV)

In this miracle, the LORD fulfilled the promise that **he shall begin to save Israel from the hand of the Philistines** (Judges 13:5).

## The Mighty Result
The miracle resulted in a devastating blow to the Philistine leadership (Judges 16:30). All the lords of the Philistines and about three thousand men and women died when the temple of Dagon collapsed (Judges 16:27,30). This victory broke the morale of the oppressors and weakened their hold on Israel (Judges 13:5). Samson's family was able to recover his body and bury him in his father's tomb (Judges 16:31). His twenty-year judgeship provided a season of resistance and leadership for his nation (Judges 15:20). He demonstrated that the God of Israel was far more powerful than the Philistine god Dagon (Judges 16:23-24,30).

## Conclusion
Samson faced the problem of national oppression and his own lack of self-control. He was limited by his pride and his attraction to the women of the Philistines. However, the LORD promised that he would begin to save Israel. Samson often failed to live up to his holy calling, yet the LORD did not forsake him. When he humbled himself in the prison of his enemies, God's power returned to him. He sacrificed his sight, his freedom, and ultimately his life to strike his final blow. He gained a place in the history of faith and fulfilled the purpose for which he was born. This story proves that we can trust the LORD to use us despite our weaknesses. He is the LORD Who is faithful to His promises even when His servants are flawed.
