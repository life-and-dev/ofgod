# Samson: a Flawed Deliverer

A child is promised to a barren couple with a divine mandate to never cut his hair. His supernatural strength becomes a legend, yet his greatest victory comes only after he has lost everything. Discover how God used a flawed hero to begin Israel's deliverance.

## Problem
The people of Israel again did what was evil in the sight of the LORD (Judges 13:1). Consequently, the LORD gave them into the hand of the Philistines for forty years (Judges 13:1). This foreign nation oppressed and ruled over the Israelites during this long period (Judges 13:1). In those days, a man named Manoah from the tribe of the Danites lived in the town of Zorah (Judges 13:2). His wife was barren and had no children (Judges 13:2). The Philistines continued to exert their power over the land without challenge (Judges 14:4). Israel lacked a leader to stand against their enemies and restore their freedom (Judges 13:5). The problem was that Israel was under the heavy hand of Philistine oppression because of their persistent disobedience to God (Judges 13:1).

## Condition
Manoah's wife was in a state of barrenness and had no children (Judges 13:2). She did not perform any special religious acts like fasting or visiting a temple to get God's attention (Judges 13:3). There is no record that she prayed specifically for a child before the encounter (Judges 13:3). The angel of the LORD appeared to her while she was simply going about her life (Judges 13:3). God initiated the contact based on His own timing and purpose for Israel (Judges 13:5).

## Calling
The angel of the LORD appeared to Manoah's wife and gave her specific instructions for the child who would be born.

> Behold, you are barren and have not borne children, but **you shall conceive and bear a son**. Therefore be careful and drink no wine or strong drink, and eat nothing unclean, for behold, you shall conceive and bear a son. **No razor shall come upon his head**, for the child shall be a Nazirite to God from the womb, and **he shall begin to save Israel from the hand of the Philistines**. — Judges 13:3-5 (ESV)

## Sacrifice
Samson had to sacrifice the normal life of a typical Israelite to maintain his Nazirite vow from birth (Judges 13:5). He risked his position as a leader of Israel by his frequent associations with Philistine women (Judges 14:1, 16:1,4). He eventually sacrificed his sight and his freedom after he revealed the secret of his strength to Delilah (Judges 16:21). Finally, he sacrificed his own life to fulfill God's purpose of striking the Philistines (Judges 16:30).

## Initial Response
Samson did not initially rebel against his calling, but he often ignored the spiritual implications of his Nazirite vow (Judges 13:24, 14:1-3). He insisted on marrying a Philistine woman despite his parents' objections (Judges 14:1-3). He touched the carcass of a dead lion which was unclean for a Nazirite (Judges 14:8-9). He also participated in a feast where wine was likely served (Judges 14:10). Samson displayed a pattern of following his own desires rather than strictly adhering to God's standards for his consecrated life (Judges 16:1,4).

## Reaction
Samson eventually responded to his calling by using his God-given strength to confront the Philistines on multiple occasions (Judges 14:19). He killed thirty men of Ashkelon to pay a debt (Judges 14:19). He burned the Philistine grain fields by tying torches to the tails of three hundred foxes (Judges 15:4-5). When the Philistines came to arrest him, he struck them hip and thigh with a great blow (Judges 15:8). Later, he killed a thousand Philistines with the jawbone of a donkey (Judges 15:15). Samson judged Israel for twenty years during the days of the Philistines (Judges 15:20). Despite his personal failures and his capture by Delilah, his final reaction was a return to God in prayer (Judges 16:28). He acknowledged that his strength came from the LORD alone (Judges 16:28). He willingly took his place between the pillars of the Philistine temple (Judges 16:29). He accepted that his death would be the means through which God would fulfill the promise of saving Israel (Judges 16:30). He chose to trust God one last time to empower him for a final act of deliverance (Judges 16:28).

## Miracle
God answered Samson's final prayer and restored his supernatural strength to bring judgment upon the Philistines (Judges 16:28-30).

> Then Samson called to the LORD and said, "O Lord GOD, please remember me and please strengthen me only this once, O God, that I may be avenged on the Philistines for my two eyes." And Samson grasped the two middle pillars on which the house rested, and he leaned his weight against them, his right hand on the one and his left hand on the other. And Samson said, "Let me die with the Philistines." Then **he bowed with all his strength, and the house fell upon the lords and upon all the people who were in it**. So the dead whom **he killed at his death were more than those whom he had killed during his life**. — Judges 16:28-30 (ESV)

## Result
The miracle resulted in the death of the lords of the Philistines and thousands of their people (Judges 16:27,30). This massive blow crippled the Philistine leadership and their religious center (Judges 16:30). Samson's family came down and recovered his body for a proper burial in his father's tomb (Judges 16:31). His life and death proved that God's Spirit was the source of his might (Judges 13:25). The deliverance of Israel from Philistine hand was initiated just as the angel had prophesied (Judges 13:5). Samson's story became a lasting testimony to God's ability to use even a broken vessel to achieve His sovereign ends (Hebrews 11:32).

## Conclusion
Israel faced forty years of Philistine oppression because of their persistent evil. Samson was chosen before birth to begin their deliverance, despite his parents' barrenness. He often failed to uphold his Nazirite calling and lost his sight and freedom due to his own compromises. However, God's promise did not depend on Samson's perfection. When Samson turned back to the LORD in his final hour, God provided the strength to finish the task. Samson sacrificed his eyes, his freedom, and ultimately his life. In return, he gained a place in the hall of faith and fulfilled the divine purpose of striking his enemies. His story demonstrates that God remains faithful to His word even when His servants are weak. God's power prevailed through his final sacrifice to give Israel a new hope.
