# Peter: Walking on Water

In a terrifying storm, one man dares the impossible by stepping out of a boat onto crashing waves. This story reveals how doubt cannot separate a trusting heart from the Savior's reach. It invites us to discover the power of trust.

## The Storm's Threat
The disciples were in a boat in the middle of the Sea of Galilee (Matthew 14:24). The boat was tossed by waves because the wind was against them (Matthew 14:24). It was now the fourth watch of the night (Matthew 14:25). They were strained at the oars because the wind was contrary to them (Mark 6:48). When they saw Jesus walking on the sea, they were terrified (Matthew 14:26). They believed they were seeing a ghost and cried out in fear (Matthew 14:26). Their hearts were hardened because they failed to understand the miracle of the loaves (Mark 6:52). They were facing a life-threatening storm far from the safety of the shore (Matthew 14:24). The disciples felt abandoned by their Lord in the darkness (Matthew 14:24-26).

## Peter's Humble Condition
The disciples were in a state of distress and fear as they battled the storm (Matthew 14:26). Peter did nothing special to get Jesus' attention before the event (Matthew 14:24). He was simply one of the disciples struggling to keep the boat afloat (Matthew 14:24). However, when he heard Jesus speak, he sought a way to confirm the identity of the one walking on the water (Matthew 14:27-28). He asked for a command to come to Jesus as a test of faith (Matthew 14:28).

## The Divine Call
Jesus spoke to Peter from the water to call him into a deeper level of trust.

> But immediately Jesus spoke to them, saying, “Take heart; it is i. Do not be afraid.” And Peter answered him, “Lord, if it is you, command me to come to you on the water.” He said, “**Come.**” — Matthew 14:27-29 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Peter had to sacrifice his sense of safety and the physical security of the boat (Matthew 14:29). He risked his life by stepping onto the deep water during a violent storm (Matthew 14:29-30). He also risked the ridicule of his fellow disciples if he failed to walk (Matthew 14:26,28). He gave up his reliance on natural laws to trust the simple word of his Lord (Matthew 14:28-29).

## Fearful Initial Response
Peter's initial response to the sight of Jesus was fear, like the other disciples in the boat (Matthew 14:26). He cried out in terror because he thought he was seeing a ghost (Matthew 14:26). Even after Jesus spoke, Peter showed a need for proof by saying, "Lord, if it is you" (Matthew 14:28). He did not immediately recognize the presence and power of God in the midst of the storm (Matthew 14:26-28).

## A Step of Faith
Peter eventually responded by stepping over the side of the boat and walking on the water toward Jesus (Matthew 14:29). He demonstrated a unique level of trust among the disciples by being the only one to leave the vessel (Matthew 14:29). However, when he saw the strength of the wind, he became afraid and began to sink (Matthew 14:30). He cried out, "Lord, save me!" (Matthew 14:30). His reaction was a mixture of bold faith and human frailty (Matthew 14:29-30). He looked away from Jesus and focused on the dangerous circumstances (Matthew 14:30). Yet, in his moment of failure, he still knew whom to call upon for help (Matthew 14:30). Peter showed that even a small amount of faith is enough to move toward Jesus (Matthew 14:29). He was willing to be vulnerable in front of his peers to reach his Lord (Matthew 14:28-29).

## The Miraculous Rescue
Jesus reached out and saved Peter from the depths of the sea.

> So Peter got out of the boat and **walked on the water and came to Jesus**. But when he saw the wind, he was afraid, and beginning to sink he cried out, “Lord, save me.” Jesus immediately reached out his hand and **took hold of him**, saying to him, “O you of little faith, why did you doubt?” And when they got into the boat, the wind ceased. — Matthew 14:29-32 (ESV)

In this miracle, Jesus fulfilled his command for Peter to **come** (Matthew 14:29). He demonstrated that his power was greater than the waves and the wind (Matthew 14:32).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the safety of Peter and all the disciples in the boat (Matthew 14:32). When the wind ceased, those in the boat worshiped Jesus as the Savior (Matthew 14:33). They confessed, "Truly you are the Son of God" (Matthew 14:33). This event strengthened the faith of the apostles and revealed Jesus' authority over all creation (Matthew 14:33). Peter learned that as long as he kept his eyes on his Lord, he could overcome any storm (Matthew 14:30-31). The story has encouraged millions of believers to trust God even when their faith is failing (Matthew 14:31).

## Conclusion
Peter faced the terrifying problem of a storm and his own fear of death. He was a man with obvious limitations, struggling with doubt even after seeing a miracle. However, when Jesus called him to come, Peter responded with a brave step of faith. God's promise was fulfilled as Peter walked on the water, and **His** faithfulness was proven when he reached out to save the sinking disciple. Peter sacrificed his safety in the boat but gained a deeper revelation of the Son of God and the power of divine trust. This miracle shows that we can trust the Lord in every storm. he is always within reach, even when we fail.
