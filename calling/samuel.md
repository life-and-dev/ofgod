# Samuel: A Voice in the Night

In a time when the word of the LORD was rare and the temple was filled with corruption, a young boy heard a mysterious voice calling his name. What he heard would change the course of Israel's history forever.

## The Problem of a Corrupt Priesthood
The word of the LORD was rare in those days; there was no frequent vision (1 Samuel 3:1). Eli the priest was very old, and his sons, Hophni and Phinehas, were worthless men who did not know the LORD (1 Samuel 2:12, 22). They treated the offering of the LORD with contempt and lay with the women who served at the entrance to the tent of meeting (1 Samuel 2:17, 22). The people of Israel were being led astray by a priesthood that exploited their sacrifices for personal gain (1 Samuel 2:13-16). Eli knew of his sons' wickedness but failed to restrain them, merely offering weak rebukes (1 Samuel 2:23-25). This spiritual decay threatened the very heart of the nation's worship and their relationship with God (1 Samuel 2:17). The problem was that the spiritual leadership of Israel was fundamentally broken and the voice of God had fallen silent (1 Samuel 2:12, 3:1).

## Samuel's Ministering Condition
Samuel was a young boy who had been dedicated to the LORD by his mother, Hannah (1 Samuel 1:28). He was ministering to the LORD in the presence of Eli (1 Samuel 3:1). He did not yet know the LORD, and the word of the LORD had not yet been revealed to him (1 Samuel 3:7). He did nothing special, such as fasting or visiting a distant mountain, to seek a vision (1 Samuel 3:1). He was simply lying down in the temple of the LORD, where the ark of God was, performing his duties (1 Samuel 3:3).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD called Samuel by name in the middle of the night (1 Samuel 3:4). After Samuel mistakenly ran to Eli three times, Eli realized the LORD was calling the boy and told him how to respond (1 Samuel 3:8-9).

> And the LORD came and stood, calling as at other times, “Samuel! Samuel!” And Samuel said, “Speak, for your servant hears.”
>
> Then the LORD said to Samuel, “Behold, **I am about to do a thing in Israel** at which the two ears of everyone who hears it will tingle. On that day **I will fulfill against Eli all that I have spoken concerning his house**, from beginning to end. And I declare to him that I am about to punish his house forever, for the iniquity that he knew, because his sons were blaspheming God, and he did not restrain them. Therefore I swear to the house of Eli that **the iniquity of Eli's house shall not be atoned for by sacrifice or offering forever**.”
>
> — 1 Samuel 3:10-14 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Samuel had to sacrifice his sense of security and his comfortable relationship with Eli, his mentor and father figure (1 Samuel 3:15, 18). He was forced to deliver a message of absolute and final judgment to the very man who had raised him in the temple (1 Samuel 3:18). He risked the potential wrath of Eli and the powerful, corrupt sons of Eli by speaking the truth (1 Samuel 3:15). He also risked his own future as a priest by declaring the end of the current priestly line (1 Samuel 3:13-14).

## Samuel's Initial Response
When the LORD first called him, Samuel did not recognize the voice (1 Samuel 3:4). He thought Eli was calling him and ran to him, saying, “Here I am, for you called me” (1 Samuel 3:5). He repeated this two more times, showing his willingness to serve but his lack of spiritual experience (1 Samuel 3:6, 8). He did not yet understand that the LORD was speaking directly to him (1 Samuel 3:7).

## Samuel's Faithful Reaction
After Eli instructed him, Samuel returned to his place and waited for the LORD (1 Samuel 3:9). When the LORD called again, Samuel responded as instructed, showing his submission to God's authority (1 Samuel 3:10). He listened to the heavy message of judgment without interruption (1 Samuel 3:11-14). He lay until morning, then opened the doors of the house of the LORD, though he was afraid to tell the vision to Eli (1 Samuel 3:15). However, when Eli insisted on hearing everything, Samuel did not hide a single word from him (1 Samuel 3:17-18). He showed courage by being honest with his superior about the terrible news (1 Samuel 3:18). He accepted his role as a messenger of God, even when the message was painful (1 Samuel 3:18). This transparency proved his integrity and his priority of God's word over human favor (1 Samuel 3:18).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD continued to be with Samuel, confirming his words and revealing Himself to him (1 Samuel 3:19, 21).

> “And the ark of God was captured, and **the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, died**. ... And as soon as he mentioned the ark of God, **Eli fell over backward from his seat ... and his neck was broken and he died**.” — 1 Samuel 4:11, 18 (ESV)
>
> “And Samuel grew, and **the LORD was with him and let none of his words fall to the ground**. And all Israel from Dan to Beersheba knew that **Samuel was established as a prophet of the LORD**.” — 1 Samuel 3:19-20 (ESV)

The miracle was that God took a young, inexperienced boy and made his every word come true, establishing him as the primary voice of God in the land (1 Samuel 3:19-20). He fulfilled His promise of judgment and began a new era for His people (1 Samuel 3:12, 4:11).

## The Lasting Result
Samuel became the last judge and one of the greatest prophets in the history of Israel (1 Samuel 7:15). Through his leadership, the people turned away from idols and back to the LORD (1 Samuel 7:3-4). He anointed both Saul and David as kings, facilitating the transition from judges to a monarchy (1 Samuel 10:1, 16:13). His faithfulness ensured that the word of God was no longer rare in the land (1 Samuel 3:21). His legacy was a nation that, for a time, was restored to its spiritual foundation (1 Samuel 7:13).

## Conclusion
Samuel grew up in a corrupt environment where spiritual leadership had failed. He was limited by his youth and his lack of personal experience with the LORD. However, when God promised to judge the house of Eli and establish a new word, Samuel responded with total honesty and courage. God's intervention established Samuel as a prophet whose words never failed. The result was a national spiritual revival and the establishment of the kingdom of Israel. Samuel sacrificed his comfort and the favor of his mentor to remain faithful to the truth. In the end, he gained a lifetime of walking with God and the honor of leading an entire nation back to its Creator. His story proves that He will never forsake those who trust Him, regardless of their age or the darkness surrounding them.
