# Samuel: A Voice in the Night

A young boy sleeping in the temple hears a mysterious voice calling his name. The message he receives is a heavy burden of judgment for his mentor. His willingness to listen and speak the truth changes the future of a nation.

## The Problem of a Silent Word
The word of the LORD was rare in those days and there was no frequent vision (1 Samuel 3:1). Eli the priest was old and his eyes had begun to grow dim (1 Samuel 3:2). Eli's sons were worthless men who did not know the LORD (1 Samuel 2:12). They treated the offering of the LORD with contempt (1 Samuel 2:17). They also lay with the women who were serving at the entrance to the tent of meeting (1 Samuel 2:22). The spiritual leadership of Israel was corrupt and failing (1 Samuel 2:22-25). A man of God had already prophesied judgment against Eli's house (1 Samuel 2:27-36). The political and spiritual environment was one of decline as Israel lacked clear divine guidance (1 Samuel 3:1). The problem was that the priesthood was disgraced and the people were without a faithful prophet (1 Samuel 3:1).

## Samuel's Humble Condition
Samuel was lying down in the temple of the LORD where the ark of God was (1 Samuel 3:3). The lamp of God had not yet gone out (1 Samuel 3:3). Samuel did not yet know the LORD and the word of the LORD had not yet been revealed to him (1 Samuel 3:7). He did nothing special to get God's attention like fasting or sacrificing (1 Samuel 3:3-4). He was simply performing his humble service and resting in the presence of God (1 Samuel 3:3).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD came and stood and called to Samuel in the night.

> And the LORD came and stood, calling as at other times, "Samuel! Samuel!" And Samuel said, "Speak, for Your servant hears." Then the LORD said to Samuel, "Behold, **I am about to do a thing in Israel at which the two ears of everyone who hears it will tingle. On that day I will fulfill against Eli all that I have spoken concerning his house, from beginning to end.** And I declare to him that I am about to punish his house forever, for the iniquity that he knew, because his sons were blaspheming God, and he did not restrain them. Therefore I swear to the house of Eli that the iniquity of Eli's house shall not be atoned for by sacrifice or offering forever." — 1 Samuel 3:10-14 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Samuel had to sacrifice his comfort and the approval of Eli, who was like a father to him (1 Samuel 3:15). He risked losing Eli's favor by delivering a message of absolute judgment (1 Samuel 3:15). He had to give up his innocence as a child to take on the heavy responsibility of a prophet (1 Samuel 3:20). He risked the stability of his current position in the temple by speaking against the high priest's family (1 Samuel 3:15).

## Samuel's Initial Response
Samuel was afraid to tell the vision to Eli (1 Samuel 3:15). He did not rebel or refuse the calling, but he hesitated to share the difficult news (1 Samuel 3:15). He lay until morning, likely troubled by the weight of the LORD'S words (1 Samuel 3:15). He showed no sign of pride in receiving a divine revelation but instead felt the gravity of the message (1 Samuel 3:15).

## Samuel's Eventual Reaction
When Eli called him and insisted on hearing every word, Samuel told him everything and hid nothing from him (1 Samuel 3:16-18). He submitted to the LORD'S command to be a truthful messenger despite his fear (1 Samuel 3:18). As Samuel grew, he continued to listen to the LORD (1 Samuel 3:19). The LORD was with him and let none of his words fall to the ground (1 Samuel 3:19). Samuel remained faithful to his calling throughout his life, eventually anointing the first kings of Israel (1 Samuel 10:1, 16:13). He consistently called the people to repent and return to the LORD (1 Samuel 7:3). When the people demanded a king, Samuel felt rejected but still brought their request to the LORD (1 Samuel 8:6). He continued to pray for the nation even when they chose a path different from God's ideal (1 Samuel 12:23). He demonstrated a life of total devotion and obedience to the voice of the LORD (1 Samuel 15:22).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD fulfilled the words spoken to Samuel and established him as a prophet before all Israel.

> And Samuel grew, and **the LORD was with him and let none of his words fall to the ground**. And all Israel from Dan to Beersheba knew that **Samuel was established as a prophet of the LORD**. — 1 Samuel 3:19-20 (ESV)

The judgment was also carried out precisely as God had declared.

> And the ark of God was captured, and **the two sons of Eli, Hophni and Phinehas, died**. . . As soon as he mentioned the ark of God, Eli fell over backward from his seat by the side of the gate, and his neck was broken and **he died**. — 1 Samuel 4:11,18 (ESV)

In these events, God fulfilled **His** promise to **do a thing in Israel** and to **fulfill against Eli all that I have spoken** (1 Samuel 3:11-12).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the restoration of the word of the LORD to Israel (1 Samuel 3:21). Samuel became a trusted judge and prophet who led the nation for many years (1 Samuel 7:15). His leadership brought a period of spiritual renewal and victory over the Philistines (1 Samuel 7:10-13). The people recognized that God was once again speaking to **His** people (1 Samuel 3:20). Samuel's faithfulness provided the foundation for the transition to the monarchy, ensuring that the kings would be guided by the word of the LORD (1 Samuel 12:23-25). The spiritual darkness of the era of Judges was replaced by a new era of prophetic guidance (1 Samuel 3:19).

## Conclusion
Samuel faced the problem of a silent God and a corrupt priesthood. He was just a child with no power or experience. However, when the LORD called him, he responded with a willing heart and eventually the courage to speak the truth. God's promise to judge Eli's house and reveal **Himself** was perfectly fulfilled. Samuel sacrificed his own peace and security to deliver a hard message, but he gained the honor of being God's voice to a nation. This story proves that the LORD is faithful to **His** word and can use even the youngest servant to accomplish **His** divine purpose. We can trust that **He** will speak and act for the good of **His** people despite human failure.
