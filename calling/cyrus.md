# Cyrus: the Chosen King

An unexpected decree from a foreign king changes the destiny of an exiled nation. Long before his birth, God called him by name to rebuild a city he did not know. Witness how the LORD uses a pagan ruler to fulfill His divine promises.

## The Exile and the Desolation of Jerusalem
The people of Judah had been carried away into exile in Babylon because of their unfaithfulness (1 Chronicles 9:1). Jerusalem was in ruins and the temple of the LORD had been burned with fire (2 Chronicles 36:19). The survivors of the sword were servants to the king of Babylon until the establishment of the kingdom of Persia (2 Chronicles 36:20). This was to fulfill the word of the LORD by the mouth of Jeremiah, until the land had enjoyed its Sabbaths (2 Chronicles 36:21). The people lived as captives in a foreign land for seventy years (Jeremiah 25:11-12). They were without a temple, without a king, and without a home (Psalm 137:1-4). The political environment was dominated by the successive rise and fall of great empires (Daniel 2:37-40). The problem was that the Jewish people were stuck in a long exile with no human hope of returning to their land or rebuilding their sacred temple (Ezra 1:1).

## The State of a Pagan King
Cyrus was the king of Persia and a powerful conqueror who ruled over many nations (Ezra 1:2). He did not know the LORD personally before God began to use him (Isaiah 45:4). He did not fast or pray to the God of Israel to receive a vision (Isaiah 45:5). God called him by name and surnamed him even though Cyrus did not recognize Him (Isaiah 45:4). He was in a position of worldly power and military success when the LORD stirred up his spirit (Ezra 1:1).

## The Divine Calling
God communicated His purpose for Cyrus through the prophet Isaiah more than a hundred years before he was born. Later, the LORD stirred up the spirit of Cyrus to issue a proclamation.

> "Who says of Cyrus, '**He is My shepherd, and he shall fulfill all My purpose**'; saying of Jerusalem, 'She shall be built,' and of the temple, 'Your foundation shall be laid.' Thus says the LORD to **His anointed, to Cyrus, whose right hand I have grasped, to subdue nations before him** and to loose the belts of kings, to open doors before him that gates may not be closed: '**I will go before you and level the exalted places, I will break in pieces the doors of bronze and cut through the bars of iron, I will give you the treasures of darkness and the hoards in secret places**, that you may know that it is I, the LORD, the God of Israel, Who call you by your name. For the sake of My servant Jacob, and Israel My chosen, **I call you by your name; I surname you, though you do not know Me**.'"
>
> — Isaiah 44:28-45:4 (ESV)

Later, the LORD fulfilled this word by speaking to Cyrus directly in his heart.

> "In the first year of Cyrus king of Persia, that the word of the LORD by the mouth of Jeremiah might be fulfilled, the LORD stirred up the spirit of Cyrus king of Persia, so that he made a proclamation throughout all his kingdom and also put it in writing: 'Thus says Cyrus king of Persia: "**The LORD, the God of heaven, has given me all the kingdoms of the earth, and He has charged me to build Him a house at Jerusalem, which is in Judah**."'"
>
> — Ezra 1:1-2 (ESV)

## The Risk of Releasing Captives
Cyrus had to sacrifice a significant portion of his labor force by allowing the Jews to return to their land (Ezra 1:3). He also risked losing the wealth of his kingdom by returning the holy vessels of the temple (Ezra 1:7-8). He commanded that his own subjects provide silver, gold, goods, and beasts for the returning exiles (Ezra 1:4). He risked political instability by allowing a conquered people to rebuild their own city and fortified temple (Ezra 4:12-13).

## Cyrus's Immediate Response
The Bible does not record any rebellion or hesitation from Cyrus (Ezra 1:1). He immediately issued a written proclamation throughout all his kingdom (Ezra 1:1). He acknowledged that the LORD, the God of heaven, had given him his power (Ezra 1:2). He did not try to keep the Jews as slaves but encouraged them to go up to Jerusalem (Ezra 1:3). He was fully compliant with the divine stirring of his spirit (Ezra 1:5).

## The King's Obedience
Cyrus acted decisively to fulfill the charge he received from God (Ezra 1:2). He brought out the vessels of the house of the LORD which Nebuchadnezzar had carried away from Jerusalem (Ezra 1:7). He had them counted out to Sheshbazzar the prince of Judah (Ezra 1:8). These included thousands of basins, censers, and bowls of gold and silver (Ezra 1:9-11). He permitted anyone of the people of God to return to their homeland (Ezra 1:3). He made provision for those who stayed behind to support the mission with voluntary offerings (Ezra 1:4). Cyrus even provided the resources for the rebuilding from the royal treasury (Ezra 6:4-5). He ensured that the decree was officially recorded in the archives of the kingdom (Ezra 6:1-2). His reaction was one of total cooperation with the plan of God for Israel (Ezra 1:1). He proved that even the heart of a king is like a stream of water in the hand of the LORD (Proverbs 21:1).

## The Prophetic Miracle
God performed a miracle by sovereignly directing the heart of a pagan king to favor His people. He fulfilled the specific prophecies made by Isaiah and Jeremiah many years earlier.

> "In the first year of Cyrus king of Persia, that the word of the LORD by the mouth of Jeremiah might be fulfilled, **the LORD stirred up the spirit of Cyrus king of Persia, so that he made a proclamation throughout all his kingdom**. . . Thus says Cyrus king of Persia: '**The LORD, the God of heaven, has given me all the kingdoms of the earth, and He has charged me to build Him a house at Jerusalem**' . . . Also **Cyrus the king brought out the vessels of the house of the LORD**, which Nebuchadnezzar had carried away from Jerusalem and placed in the house of his gods." — Ezra 1:1-2,7 (ESV)

In this miracle, God showed that **He has grasped** the right hand of Cyrus to **subdue nations before him** so that the temple foundation **shall be laid** (Isaiah 44:28, 45:1). The God of Israel proved that **he is My shepherd** by using Cyrus to lead His people back to their pasture (Isaiah 44:28).

## The National Restoration
The miracle resulted in the return of nearly fifty thousand people to the land of Judah (Ezra 2:64-65). They rebuilt the altar of the God of Israel and restored the daily sacrifices (Ezra 3:2). The foundation of the second temple was laid with great shouting and weeping (Ezra 3:11-13). The surrounding nations saw that the LORD was with His people (Ezra 1:4). This restoration allowed the Jewish nation to survive and eventually bring forth the Messiah (Matthew 1:12-17). Cyrus became a legendary figure of mercy and divine providence in Jewish history (Isaiah 45:13). His decree ended the seventy years of desolation and proved that God never forgets His covenant (Jeremiah 29:10-14).

## Conclusion
Cyrus was a powerful king who ruled a vast empire while the people of God were in hopeless exile. He did not know the LORD, yet God had called him by name through a prophet long before his birth. The problem was an abandoned temple and a scattered nation, but God promised that Cyrus would be His shepherd. Cyrus responded by immediately obeying the divine stirring in his heart and releasing the captives with great wealth. God intervened by giving Cyrus victory over nations and moving him to rebuild Jerusalem. The result was the restoration of the Jewish people and the temple. Cyrus sacrificed his own labor force and treasure, but he gained the honor of being God's anointed instrument of salvation. His life demonstrates that the LORD is faithful to His word regardless of who is on the throne.
