# Elijah: Fire From Heaven

A lone prophet stands against four hundred and fifty false prophets on a mountain top. With the nation watching and a drought at stake, he challenges them to a trial by fire. Discover how one man's absolute trust in the LORD'S word brought a nation back to God.

## The Problem of National Apostasy
Israel had fallen into deep apostasy under King Ahab, who did more to provoke the LORD to anger than all the kings who were before him (1 Kings 16:33). He married Jezebel, who killed the prophets of the LORD and established the worship of Baal (1 Kings 18:4,13,19). Because of this wickedness, Elijah had prophesied a severe drought that lasted for three years (1 Kings 17:1, 18:1). The people were limping between two different opinions, unsure whether to follow the LORD or Baal (1 Kings 18:21). The political environment was hostile to any who remained faithful to the LORD (1 Kings 18:4,10). The problem was that the nation was dying of thirst both physically and spiritually while being led by a king and queen who served idols (1 Kings 18:2,18).

## Elijah's Humble Condition
Elijah had been in hiding, first at the brook Cherith and then with a widow in Zarephath (1 Kings 17:2-10). He was a man of like nature to us, but he prayed fervently (James 5:17). He did not perform any special ceremony to get God's attention before the word of the LORD came to him in the third year (1 Kings 18:1). He simply remained obedient and waited for the LORD'S timing (1 Kings 17:5,10, 18:1). He was a man who lived in total dependence on the provision of the LORD (1 Kings 17:4,9).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came to Elijah with a specific command to confront the king and a promise of restoration.

> After many days the word of the LORD came to Elijah, in the third year, saying, "Go, show yourself to Ahab, and **I will send rain upon the earth.**" — 1 Kings 18:1 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Elijah risked his life by showing himself to Ahab, who had been searching every nation and kingdom to find and kill him (1 Kings 18:10). He also risked his reputation and his life on Mount Carmel; if fire did not fall, the people and the king would have surely executed him as a false prophet and the cause of the drought (1 Kings 18:17,24). He sacrificed his safety and his anonymity to fulfill the command of the LORD (1 Kings 18:2,18).

## Elijah's Initial Response
Elijah did not hesitate or complain about the danger. The Bible simply states that he went to show himself to Ahab (1 Kings 18:2). He boldly confronted the king, telling him that he was the one who had troubled Israel by forsaking the commandments of the LORD (1 Kings 18:18). He did not fear the king's wrath (1 Kings 18:18).

## Elijah's Faithful Reaction
Elijah challenged Ahab to gather all Israel and the four hundred and fifty prophets of Baal to Mount Carmel (1 Kings 18:19). He then stood before all the people and challenged their indecision (1 Kings 18:21). He proposed a contest where two bulls would be prepared, and the God Who answered by fire would be acknowledged as the true God (1 Kings 18:23-24). He allowed the prophets of Baal to go first, even mocking their silent god when no answer came (1 Kings 18:26-27). When it was his turn, he repaired the altar of the LORD and made a trench around it (1 Kings 18:30-32). He ordered that the sacrifice and the wood be drenched with twelve jars of water until the trench was full (1 Kings 18:33-35). He then prayed a simple prayer, asking the LORD to let it be known that He is God in Israel and that Elijah had done all these things at His word (1 Kings 18:36-37). He trusted so completely that he made the miracle humanly impossible by soaking the altar in water (1 Kings 18:34-35). He demonstrated that his faith was not in his own ability but in the power of the living God (1 Kings 18:37).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD answered Elijah's prayer with an immediate display of fire and later a great rain to fulfill His promise.

> Then the fire of the LORD fell and consumed the burnt offering and the wood and the stones and the dust, and licked up the water that was in the trench. And when all the people saw it, they fell on their faces and said, "The LORD, He is God; the LORD, He is God." . . . And in a little while the heavens grew black with clouds and wind, and **there was a great rain.** — 1 Kings 18:38-39,45 (ESV)

## The Lasting Result
The people of Israel turned back to God and acknowledged that the LORD is the only true God (1 Kings 18:39). The false prophets of Baal were seized and executed at the brook Kishon (1 Kings 18:40). The three-year drought ended with a great rain as the LORD had promised (1 Kings 18:45). King Ahab witnessed the power of the LORD and the return of the rain, although Jezebel remained unrepentant (1 Kings 18:45-46, 19:1-2). The nation was given a chance for spiritual and physical renewal (1 Kings 18:39,45).

## Conclusion
Elijah faced a nation blinded by idolatry and a king who sought his life. He was limited by being only one man against many, yet he was empowered by the word of the LORD. God promised to send rain if Elijah confronted Ahab. Elijah responded with fearless obedience and a public demonstration of God's supremacy. God intervened by sending fire from heaven and then an abundance of rain. The result was the destruction of Baal worship and the restoration of the people's faith. Elijah sacrificed his safety and comfort to stand for the truth. In the end, he gained a national revival and a testimony of God's absolute faithfulness. His story proves that one person standing with the LORD is always a majority.
