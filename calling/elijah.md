# Elijah: Provision in Zarephath

A prophet is sent to a starving widow in a foreign land during a deadly famine. He must trust God to provide food from an empty jar, while the widow must trust him with her last meal. Their shared faith leads to a miracle of daily survival.

## The Problem of Drought and Famine
The land of Israel was suffering from a severe drought and famine because of the people's idolatry (1 Kings 17:1). The brook Cherith, where Elijah had been hiding, dried up because there was no rain in the land (1 Kings 17:7). King Ahab and Jezebel were actively seeking to kill the prophets of the LORD (1 Kings 18:4,10). The drought affected not only Israel but also the surrounding regions like Sidon (1 Kings 17:12). People were dying of hunger and thirst throughout the region (1 Kings 17:12). The problem was the absolute lack of water and food, which threatened the lives of both the prophet and the people he was sent to (1 Kings 17:7,12).

## Elijah's Humble Condition
Elijah was a man of God who had been living in isolation by a brook (1 Kings 17:3,5). He was dependent on ravens for food and the brook for water (1 Kings 17:4,6). He did not perform any special ritual or fast to get God's attention before this command (1 Kings 17:7-8). He was simply waiting in a place of death as the water vanished (1 Kings 17:7). He was in a state of total dependence on the LORD'S next instruction (1 Kings 17:8).

## The Divine Calling
God spoke directly to Elijah to give him a new place of refuge and a promise of provision.

> Then the word of the LORD came to him, “**Arise, go to Zarephath, which belongs to Sidon, and dwell there. Behold, I have commanded a widow there to feed you**.”
>
> — 1 Kings 17:8-9 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Elijah had to sacrifice his relative safety in the wilderness to travel to a foreign city (1 Kings 17:9). Zarephath was in the territory of Sidon, the home of the wicked Queen Jezebel (1 Kings 16:31). By going there, he was entering the heart of the enemy's territory while being a wanted man (1 Kings 18:10). He also had to sacrifice his pride by relying on a poor widow who had nothing herself (1 Kings 17:12).

## The Initial Response
Elijah did not hesitate or argue with the LORD'S command (1 Kings 17:10). He arose and went to Zarephath as he was told (1 Kings 17:10). He did not complain about the danger of the journey or the poverty of his host (1 Kings 17:10).

## Elijah's Eventual Reaction
Elijah came to the gate of the city and found a widow gathering sticks (1 Kings 17:10). He called to her and asked for a little water in a vessel (1 Kings 17:10). As she was going to get it, he asked for a morsel of bread in her hand (1 Kings 17:11). When she explained that she only had a handful of flour and a little oil to make a final meal for herself and her son, Elijah told her not to fear (1 Kings 17:12-13). He instructed her to make a little cake for him first and then for herself and her son (1 Kings 17:13). He gave her the promise of the LORD that the flour and oil would not fail (1 Kings 17:14). Elijah trusted the word of the LORD even when faced with the widow's extreme poverty (1 Kings 17:14). He stayed in her house and ate for many days (1 Kings 17:15). When the widow's son later fell ill and died, Elijah took the child to his own room (1 Kings 17:17-19). He cried out to the LORD and stretched himself upon the child three times (1 Kings 17:20-21). He asked the LORD to let the child's life come into him again (1 Kings 17:21). He trusted that the God Who provided food could also provide life (1 Kings 17:21).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD miraculously multiplied the food as **He** had promised through **His** servant.

> And she went and did as Elijah said. And she and **he and her household ate for many days**. The **jar of flour was not spent, neither did the jug of oil become empty**, according to the word of the LORD that He spoke by Elijah.
>
> — 1 Kings 17:15-16 (ESV)

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the survival of Elijah, the widow, and her son during the famine (1 Kings 17:15). It demonstrated that the LORD'S power extends beyond the borders of Israel and into the heart of pagan lands (1 Kings 17:9,24). The widow came to know that Elijah was a man of God and that the word of the LORD in his mouth was truth (1 Kings 17:24). Her son was later restored to life, which further confirmed God's faithfulness (1 Kings 17:22-23). The story became a testimony of God's grace to the Gentiles, even mentioned by Jesus centuries later (Luke 4:25-26). It encouraged the faithful in Israel that the LORD had not forsaken **His** people or **His** word (1 Kings 17:24).

## Conclusion
Elijah faced the problem of a drying brook and a nationwide famine. He was a lone prophet in a hostile world, yet he trusted the instruction of the LORD. Despite the danger of entering Sidon and the poverty of the widow, he obeyed. God fulfilled **His** promise by providing a never-ending supply of flour and oil. Elijah sacrificed his isolation and safety, but he gained a home and witnessed God's power over life and death. The widow sacrificed her last meal, but she gained her life and her son's life. This story proves that we can trust the LORD with our very survival. **He** is faithful to provide for **His** servants in the most unlikely ways.
