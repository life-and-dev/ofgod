# Noah: Lifetime project

A man is told to build a massive ship in a world that has never seen rain. His obedience will determine the survival of the human race and every animal on earth. Discover how one man's trust in God saved the world from total destruction.

## The Corruption of the Earth
The earth was corrupt in God's sight and was filled with violence (Genesis 6:11). The LORD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth (Genesis 6:5). Every intention of the thoughts of man's heart was only evil continually (Genesis 6:5). The earth was corrupt because all flesh had corrupted their way on the earth (Genesis 6:12). God was sorry that **He** had made man on the earth (Genesis 6:6). It grieved **Him** to **His** heart (Genesis 6:6). The LORD decided to blot out man whom **He** had created from the face of the land (Genesis 6:7). This included man and animals and creeping things and birds of the heavens (Genesis 6:7). The problem was that humanity had become so wicked that a global judgment was necessary (Genesis 6:13).

## Noah's Righteous Condition
Noah found favor in the eyes of the LORD (Genesis 6:8). He was a righteous man and blameless in his generation (Genesis 6:9). Noah walked with God (Genesis 6:9). He did not perform any special religious ritual or fast before the LORD spoke to him (Genesis 6:8-9). He simply lived a righteous life amidst universal corruption (Genesis 6:9). He was a father to three sons named Shem, Ham, and Japheth (Genesis 6:10).

## The Divine Calling
God spoke directly to Noah to warn him of the coming flood and to give him instructions for salvation.

> And God said to Noah, "I have determined to make an end of all flesh, for the earth is filled with violence through them. Behold, I will destroy them with the earth. **Make yourself an ark of gopher wood. Make rooms in the ark, and cover it inside and out with pitch.** This is how you are to make it: the length of the ark three hundred cubits, its breadth fifty cubits, and its height thirty cubits. Make a roof for the ark, and finish it to a cubit above, and set the door of the ark in its side. Make it with lower, second, and third decks. For behold, I will bring a flood of waters upon the earth to destroy all flesh in which is the breath of life under heaven. Everything that is on the earth shall die. But I will establish My covenant with you, and you shall come into the ark, you and your sons, your wife, and your sons' wives with you. And **of every living thing of all flesh, two of every sort shall you bring into the ark to keep them alive with you.** They shall be male and female. Of the birds according to their kinds, and of the animals according to their kinds, of every creeping thing of the ground, according to its kind, two of every sort shall come in to you to keep them alive. Also **take with you every sort of food that is eaten, and store it up**. It shall serve as food for you and for them." — Genesis 6:13-21 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Noah had to sacrifice his reputation and many years of his life to build the ark (Genesis 6:22). He likely faced ridicule from his violent and corrupt neighbors (Genesis 6:11-12). He spent decades working on a project that seemed nonsensical to those around him (Genesis 5:32, 7:6). He risked the safety of his family by trusting in a promise of judgment that had no precedent (Hebrews 11:7). He gave up his normal life to become a preacher of righteousness (2 Peter 2:5).

## Noah's Initial Response
The Bible does not record any rebellion or refusal from Noah (Genesis 6:22). He did all that God commanded him (Genesis 6:22). He did not argue with God about the destruction of the world (Genesis 6:13,22). He did not question the specific measurements of the ark (Genesis 6:14-16,22). His response was one of immediate and silent obedience (Genesis 6:22).

## Noah's Faithful Reaction
Noah began the monumental task of building the ark according to God's specifications (Genesis 6:22). He gathered the gopher wood and applied the pitch as instructed (Genesis 6:14). He organized the three decks and set the door in the side (Genesis 6:16). He gathered his wife, his three sons, and their wives (Genesis 7:7). He collected food for his family and for all the animals (Genesis 6:21). Noah gathered the animals as the LORD commanded him (Genesis 7:8-9). He entered the ark seven days before the rain began (Genesis 7:10). He trusted God enough to enter the vessel before a single drop of rain fell (Genesis 7:7,10). He remained in the ark for over a year while the world was submerged (Genesis 8:14). He showed his trust by waiting for God's command to leave the ark (Genesis 8:15-16). He built an altar and offered burnt offerings to the LORD immediately after exiting (Genesis 8:20). He demonstrated that his trust was in the word of the LORD rather than in his own eyes (Hebrews 11:7).

## The Divine Miracle
God sent the flood to judge the earth but sovereignly preserved Noah and his family. He remembered them in the midst of the storm and brought them safely to a new world.

> And the waters prevailed so mightily on the earth that all the high mountains under the whole heaven were covered. . . **He blotted out every living thing that was on the face of the ground, man and animals and creeping things and birds of the heavens. They were blotted out from the earth. Only Noah was left, and those who were with him in the ark.** . . But God remembered Noah and all the beasts and all the livestock that were with him in the ark. And **God made a wind blow over the earth, and the waters subsided.** . . Then God said to Noah, "Go out from the ark, you and your wife, and your sons and your sons' wives with you. **Bring out with you every living thing that is with you of all flesh . . . that they may swarm on the earth, and be fruitful and multiply on the earth.**" — Genesis 7:19,23, 8:1,15-17 (ESV)

## The Lasting Result

The miracle resulted in the survival of the human race and all animal life (Genesis 8:17). Noah and his family became the new ancestors of all people on earth (Genesis 9:1,19). God established a covenant with Noah and every living creature never to destroy the earth with a flood again (Genesis 9:9-11). He set the rainbow in the clouds as a sign of this everlasting covenant (Genesis 9:12-17). The world was given a fresh start free from the extreme violence of the pre-flood era (Genesis 9:1-7). Noah's obedience made him an heir of the righteousness that comes by faith (Hebrews 11:7). His story remains a testament to God's judgment of sin and His merciful provision for salvation (2 Peter 2:5).

## Conclusion
Noah lived in a world filled with violence and corruption that grieved the heart of God. Despite the impossibility of the task and the potential for public ridicule, he chose to trust God's warning. He spent years building an ark for a flood he could not yet see. God was faithful to His word and preserved Noah's family while the rest of the world perished. Noah sacrificed his time, labor, and social standing to follow God's calling. In return, he gained his life, the lives of his family, and the honor of being the father of a new world. His story proves that even when the whole world is against us, we can trust in the LORD'S provision and faithfulness. He is the God Who keeps His promises even through the greatest storms.
