# The Faithful Builder of the Ark: Noah's Obedience

## Introduction
A man is called to build a massive wooden vessel in a world that has never seen rain. He must prepare for a coming disaster that no one else believes in. This journey explores how trust in a divine promise can save what matters most.

## The Corruption of the World
The earth had become deeply corrupt in the sight of God (Genesis 6:11). Every intention of the thoughts of man's heart was only evil continually (Genesis 6:5). The LORD saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth (Genesis 6:5). He was sorry that He had made man on the earth and it grieved Him to His heart (Genesis 6:6). Violence filled the earth because of the actions of all flesh (Genesis 6:11,13). Mankind had completely turned away from the ways of the LORD (Genesis 6:12). This corruption was so pervasive that it threatened to destroy the goodness of God's creation (Genesis 6:12). The people lived in rebellion and ignored the righteous requirements of their Maker (Genesis 6:5). The core problem was that the entire world was filled with violence and total moral depravity (Genesis 6:11).

## Noah's Righteous Condition
Noah lived as a righteous man in the midst of a crooked generation (Genesis 6:9). He was blameless in his generation and he walked with God (Genesis 6:9). Noah did not do anything special like fasting or visiting a temple to get God's attention (Genesis 6:8). Instead, he simply found favor in the eyes of the LORD because of his faithful walk (Genesis 6:8). He remained untainted by the surrounding wickedness (Genesis 6:9).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke directly to Noah and revealed His plan to judge the world and save Noah's family. The communication was direct and detailed.
> And God said to Noah, "**I have determined to make an end of all flesh**, for the earth is filled with violence through them. Behold, **I will destroy them with the earth**. **Make yourself an ark** of gopher wood. Make rooms in the ark, and cover it inside and out with pitch. This is how you are to make it: the length of the ark 300 cubits, its breadth 50 cubits, and its height 30 cubits. Make a roof for the ark, and finish it to a cubit above, and set the door of the ark in its side. Make it with lower, second, and third decks. For behold, **I will bring a flood of waters upon the earth to destroy all flesh** in which is the breath of life under heaven. Everything that is on the earth shall die. But **I will establish My covenant with you**, and **you shall come into the ark**, you, your sons, your wife, and your sons' wives with you. And of every living thing of all flesh, **you shall bring two of every sort into the ark to keep them alive with you**. They shall be male and female. Of the birds according to their kinds, and of the animals according to their kinds, of every creeping thing of the ground, according to its kind, two of every sort shall come in to you **to keep them alive**. Also take with you every sort of food that is eaten, and store it up. It shall serve as food for you and for them." — Genesis 6:13-21 (ESV)

## The Sacrifice of Trust
Noah had to sacrifice his reputation and his resources to build the ark (Genesis 6:14,22). He also sacrificed many decades of his life to complete the massive project (Genesis 6:3, 7:6). He worked as a herald of righteousness in a world that was filled with violence (2 Peter 2:5). He risked losing everything he owned to follow God's specific instructions (Genesis 6:22).

## The Initial Response
Noah did not rebel or question the LORD (Genesis 6:22). He responded with immediate and complete obedience (Genesis 6:22). He did all that God commanded him to do (Genesis 7:5). There is no record of him asking for signs or making excuses (Genesis 6:22).

## Noah's Obedient Reaction
Noah spent many years laboring on the ark as the LORD had commanded (Genesis 6:22). He gathered the gopher wood and applied the pitch to seal the vessel (Genesis 6:14). He carefully followed the dimensions provided by God to ensure the safety of his family (Genesis 6:15, 7:5). Noah also gathered all the necessary food for his family and the animals (Genesis 6:21). He was a herald of righteousness who warned his generation of the coming judgment (2 Peter 2:5). When the time came, he entered the ark with his wife, his three sons, and their wives (Genesis 7:7). He also oversaw the gathering of every kind of animal as the LORD directed (Genesis 7:8-9). Noah trusted that the LORD would keep His promise even when the floodwaters had not yet appeared (Hebrews 11:7). He showed remarkable patience and faith during the long period of construction (Genesis 6:22). He did not waver in his commitment to the task (Genesis 7:5). Noah's reaction was characterized by a quiet and steady trust in the word of God (Genesis 6:22). He waited for the LORD to give the signal to enter the ark (Genesis 7:1). After everyone was inside, the LORD closed the door behind them (Genesis 7:16). Noah remained in the ark for over a year as the waters covered the earth (Genesis 7:11, 8:14). He sent out birds to check if the land was dry (Genesis 8:6-12). He did not leave the ark until God told him it was safe to do so (Genesis 8:15-16).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD brought a global flood that covered even the highest mountains. He protected the ark and its inhabitants through the storm.
> The waters prevailed and increased greatly on the earth, and **the ark floated on the face of the waters**. . . . **He blotted out every living thing that was on the face of the ground**, man and animals and creeping things and birds of the heavens. They were blotted out from the earth. **Only Noah was left, and those who were with him in the ark**. — Genesis 7:18,23 (ESV)
By preserving the vessel, the LORD fulfilled His promise that Noah would **come into the ark** (Genesis 6:18). He used the flood to **destroy all flesh** as He had spoken (Genesis 6:17). Noah and the animals were **kept alive** in the midst of total destruction (Genesis 6:19).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the salvation of Noah and his family (Genesis 7:23). They became the new ancestors of all humanity (Genesis 9:1,19). The animals were also preserved to repopulate the earth (Genesis 8:17). After leaving the ark, Noah built an altar and offered sacrifices to the LORD (Genesis 8:20). The LORD smelled the pleasing aroma and promised never to curse the ground again because of man (Genesis 8:21). He established a covenant with Noah and all living creatures, symbolized by the rainbow (Genesis 9:12-13). This covenant promised that a flood would never again destroy all flesh (Genesis 9:15). The world was given a fresh start (Genesis 9:1). Noah's faith ensured that the human race and the animal kingdom would continue (Genesis 9:19).

## Conclusion
Noah faced the problem of a world consumed by violence and corruption. He had the limitation of being a lone righteous man in a wicked society. However, the LORD promised to save him through the construction of an ark. Noah responded with total obedience and sacrificed his time and social standing. God intervened with the miracle of the flood and the protection of the ark. The result was the preservation of life and a new covenant for all generations. Noah sacrificed his old life and comfort. He gained the survival of his family and the favor of the Creator. This story demonstrates that God is faithful to those who trust Him. He provides a way of escape even when judgment is certain.
