# David: The Shepherd King

An insignificant shepherd boy is plucked from the fields to become the leader of a nation. He must face a giant and years of exile before wearing the crown. Discover how David's trust in the LORD'S promise sustained him through every trial.

## The Problem of Failed Leadership
Israel was in a state of turmoil under its first king, Saul, who had disobeyed the commandments of the LORD (1 Samuel 13:13). Because of Saul's rebellion, the LORD had rejected him from being king over Israel (1 Samuel 15:23). The Philistines, a powerful enemy, constantly threatened the borders of the land and oppressed the people (1 Samuel 13:5,19-20). Saul was plagued by a harmful spirit and was increasingly unstable and fearful (1 Samuel 16:14, 18:12). The political environment was tense as the people saw their leader losing God's favor and their enemies gaining ground (1 Samuel 13:6-7). The problem was that the nation lacked a righteous leader who would hearten the people and lead them in the ways of the LORD (1 Samuel 13:14, 16:1).

## David's Humble Condition
David was the youngest of eight sons and was out in the field keeping the sheep (1 Samuel 16:11). He was not even considered by his father when the prophet Samuel came to sacrifice to the LORD (1 Samuel 16:5,11). David did nothing special to get God's attention before **He** spoke to Samuel (1 Samuel 16:1,7). He was simply performing his lowly duties as a shepherd, protecting the flock from lions and bears (1 Samuel 17:34-36). He was a youth, ruddy and with beautiful eyes and was handsome (1 Samuel 16:12).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD instructed the prophet Samuel to anoint the one **He** had chosen from among the sons of Jesse.

> Then the LORD said to Samuel, "How long will you grieve over Saul, since I have rejected him from being king over Israel? Fill your horn with oil, and go. I will send you to Jesse the Bethlehemite, for **I have provided for Myself a king among his sons**." . . . Then Samuel took the horn of oil and anointed him in the midst of his brothers. And the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon David from that day forward. — 1 Samuel 16:1,13 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
David had to sacrifice the simple life of a shepherd for the dangers of the royal court (1 Samuel 16:19-21). He risked his life when he volunteered to fight the giant Goliath, whom no one else dared to face (1 Samuel 17:32). Later, he had to sacrifice his home and safety to flee from Saul's jealous rage (1 Samuel 19:12, 21:10). He lived as a fugitive in caves and foreign lands for years (1 Samuel 22:1, 27:1).

## David's Initial Response
David did not hesitate or rebel against the calling. He accepted the anointing in silence (1 Samuel 16:13). He continued to serve his father and later King Saul with humility (1 Samuel 16:21, 17:15). When the honor of the LORD was challenged by Goliath, he was indignant and ready to defend God's name (1 Samuel 17:26). He did not seek the throne by force but waited for the LORD'S timing (1 Samuel 24:6, 26:9).

## David's Faithful Reaction
David responded to the threat of Goliath with absolute trust in the power of the LORD (1 Samuel 17:37). He refused the king's armor and went out with only his staff and five smooth stones (1 Samuel 17:38-40). He declared to the Philistine that the battle was the LORD'S and that the LORD would deliver him into his hand (1 Samuel 17:45-47). During his years as a fugitive, David repeatedly spared Saul's life because he was the LORD'S anointed (1 Samuel 24:10, 26:11). He gathered a band of outcasts and protected the people even while being pursued by the king (1 Samuel 22:2, 23:1-5). He consulted the LORD before every major decision and battle (1 Samuel 23:2, 30:8). Even when he sinned greatly, he repented with a broken heart and accepted God's discipline (2 Samuel 12:13, 24:10). He spent his life writing psalms that expressed his deep trust and dependence on the LORD (Psalm 23, 27). He prepared the resources for the building of the temple even though he was not permitted to build it himself (1 Chronicles 22:2-5). His reaction was one of lifelong devotion and trust in the character of the LORD (**He**).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD gave David victory over his enemies and established his kingdom as **He** had promised.

> And David put his hand in his bag and took out a stone and slung it and struck the Philistine on his forehead. The stone sank into his forehead, and he fell on his face to the ground. So David prevailed over the Philistine with a sling and with a stone... And all the tribes of Israel came to David at Hebron and said, "Behold, we are your bone and flesh." . . . So all the elders of Israel came to the king at Hebron, and King David made a covenant with them at Hebron before the LORD, and **they anointed David king over Israel**. — 1 Samuel 17:49-50; 2 Samuel 5:1,3 (ESV)

The LORD fulfilled the promise that **I have provided for Myself a king among his sons** (1 Samuel 16:1).

## The Lasting Result
David's reign brought stability and prosperity to Israel (2 Samuel 8:15). He united the tribes and conquered Jerusalem, making it the capital city (2 Samuel 5:6-9). He defeated the Philistines and other neighboring nations, securing the borders of the land (2 Samuel 8:1-14). His faithfulness led to the Davidic Covenant, where the LORD promised that his throne would be established forever (2 Samuel 7:12-16). This covenant ultimately pointed to Jesus, the Son of David, who would reign over an eternal kingdom (Luke 1:32-33). David's life and psalms continue to encourage believers to trust in the LORD'S faithfulness through all generations.

## Conclusion
David faced the problem of a failing kingdom and a vengeful king who sought his life. He was limited by his youth and his status as a lowly shepherd. However, the LORD promised to provide a king and anointed David for the task. David responded with courageous faith and a heart that sought after God's own heart. The LORD intervened by giving him victory over Goliath and preserving him through years of exile. The result was a united nation and an eternal covenant that blessed the whole world. David sacrificed his safety and comfort, but he gained a kingdom and the honor of being an ancestor of the Messiah. His story proves that the LORD looks at the heart and is faithful to fulfill every promise **He** makes. We can trust the LORD to be our strength and our shield.
