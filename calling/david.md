# David: The Shepherd King

A young shepherd boy steps onto a battlefield where seasoned warriors tremble. With only a sling and a handful of stones, he faces a giant who has defied the armies of Israel. This unexpected champion carries a secret anointing that will change history.

## The Problem of a Fearful Nation
King Saul had disobeyed the LORD, leading to his rejection as the ruler of Israel (1 Samuel 15:23,26). The Spirit of the LORD had departed from Saul, and a harmful spirit from the LORD tormented him (1 Samuel 16:14). Simultaneously, the Philistines gathered their armies for battle against Israel (1 Samuel 17:1). A champion named Goliath, who was over nine feet tall, stood and shouted defiance at the ranks of Israel for forty days (1 Samuel 17:4,8,16). He challenged any man to fight him, but Saul and all Israel were greatly afraid and dismayed (1 Samuel 17:11). The nation lacked a leader with the courage to trust God against this overwhelming threat (1 Samuel 17:11,24). The problem was that the current king had lost divine favor, and the people were paralyzed by fear of a giant enemy (1 Samuel 16:1, 17:11).

## David's Humble Condition
David was the youngest of the eight sons of Jesse (1 Samuel 16:10-11). He was responsible for keeping his father's sheep in the wilderness (1 Samuel 16:11). When the prophet Samuel came to Bethlehem to offer a sacrifice, David was not even invited to the initial gathering (1 Samuel 16:5,11). He did nothing special to seek God's attention or a royal title (1 Samuel 16:11). He was simply being faithful in his humble duties when the LORD chose him (1 Samuel 16:11-12).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke to Samuel, directing him to Jesse's house to find the next king. After Jesse's older sons were rejected, the LORD commanded Samuel to anoint the young shepherd.

> And the LORD said, "**Arise, anoint him, for this is he.**" Then Samuel took the horn of oil and **anointed him in the midst of his brothers**. And the Spirit of the LORD rushed upon David from that day forward. — 1 Samuel 16:12-13 (ESV)

Later, when David faced Goliath, he declared the LORD'S intent to deliver the enemy into his hands.

> Then David said to the Philistine, "You come to me with a sword and with a spear and with a javelin, but I come to you in the name of the LORD of hosts, the God of the armies of Israel, Whom you have defied. **This day the LORD will deliver you into my hand**, and I will strike you down and cut off your head. . . that **all the earth may know that there is a God in Israel**, and that all this assembly may know that the LORD saves not with sword and spear. For **the battle is the LORD'S, and He will give you into our hand**." — 1 Samuel 17:45-47 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
David had to sacrifice the simple peace of his life as a shepherd (1 Samuel 16:11). He risked his life by stepping onto the battlefield without armor to face a seasoned warrior (1 Samuel 17:38-40). He also risked the ridicule of his older brothers, who mocked his intentions (1 Samuel 17:28). By accepting the anointing, he entered a path that would eventually lead to years of being hunted by King Saul (1 Samuel 18:11, 19:10).

## David's Initial Response
David showed no rebellion or hesitation when called from the fields (1 Samuel 16:12-13). He quietly returned to his sheep and later served in Saul's court when summoned (1 Samuel 16:19-23). When he arrived at the battle camp, he was shocked by the fear of the soldiers (1 Samuel 17:26). Instead of being afraid, he asked what would be done for the man who killed the giant (1 Samuel 17:26). He was ready to act where others failed (1 Samuel 17:32).

## David's Eventual Reaction
David told King Saul that he would go and fight the Philistine (1 Samuel 17:32). He recounted how the LORD had delivered him from the paw of the lion and the bear while he tended sheep (1 Samuel 17:34-37). He trusted that the same God would deliver him from Goliath (1 Samuel 17:37). David refused the king's armor because he was not used to it (1 Samuel 17:38-39). He chose instead to take his staff, five smooth stones from the brook, and his sling (1 Samuel 17:40). As the giant moved toward him, David did not retreat but ran quickly toward the battle line (1 Samuel 17:48). He spoke with absolute confidence in the LORD'S power to save (1 Samuel 17:45-47). He believed that the battle belonged to the LORD and not to human strength (1 Samuel 17:47). His faith was rooted in his past experiences of God's protection in the wilderness (1 Samuel 17:37). David's response was a total reliance on the name of the LORD of hosts (1 Samuel 17:45).

## The Divine Miracle
God gave David the strength and accuracy to defeat the giant with a single stone, fulfilling the promise of victory.

> And David put his hand in his bag and took out a stone and slung it and **struck the Philistine on his forehead. The stone sank into his forehead, and he fell on his face to the ground.** So **David prevailed over the Philistine with a sling and with a stone**, and struck the Philistine and killed him. . . Then David ran and stood over the Philistine and took his sword and drew it out of its sheath and killed him and cut off his head with it. . . When the Philistines saw that their champion was dead, they fled. — 1 Samuel 17:49-51 (ESV)

God fulfilled **His** promise through David that **He** will give you into our hand and all the earth may know that there is a God in Israel (1 Samuel 17:46-47).

## The Lasting Result
The victory over Goliath caused the Philistine army to flee in terror (1 Samuel 17:51). The men of Israel and Judah rose up with a shout and pursued their enemies to the gates of Ekron (1 Samuel 17:52). David became a national hero, and the women sang of his great success (1 Samuel 18:7). This event cemented David's reputation and led him to a high position in Saul's army (1 Samuel 18:5). Ultimately, it was a major step toward David becoming the king of all Israel as the LORD had promised (2 Samuel 5:1-3). The miracle demonstrated to the entire nation that God was still with them (1 Samuel 17:46).

## Conclusion
David faced a giant enemy and a fearful nation. As a young shepherd, he lacked the military training of King Saul. However, the LORD had anointed him and provided the courage he needed. David trusted in the LORD'S past faithfulness to overcome his present fear. By sacrificing his safety, he gained the deliverance of his people and the fulfillment of his calling. The miracle of Goliath's defeat proved that God does not see as man sees but looks on the heart. David's willingness to rely on the LORD resulted in a victory that glorified God before all nations. This story teaches us that we can trust the LORD'S faithfulness even when facing giants. **He** will never forsake those who depend on **Him**.
