# Jehoshaphat: The King Who Praised His Way to Victory

When three nations joined forces to annihilate the kingdom of Judah, King Jehoshaphat faced an impossible choice. Should he fight an overwhelming enemy or trust a promise that sounded like madness? Discover how a song of praise became a weapon of war and changed everything.

## The Problem of a Massive Alliance
Jehoshaphat was the king of Judah during a time of intense regional conflict (2 Chronicles 17:1). The Moabites, Ammonites, and some of the Meunites formed a massive military alliance to invade the land (2 Chronicles 20:1). They approached from beyond the Dead Sea, already reaching En-gedi (2 Chronicles 20:2). This great multitude threatened to destroy the kingdom and drive the people out of the land God had given them as an inheritance (2 Chronicles 20:11). Judah's own military strength was insufficient to repel such an overwhelming force (2 Chronicles 20:12). The political landscape was one of fear and uncertainty as the enemy moved rapidly toward Jerusalem (2 Chronicles 20:3). The problem was that Jehoshaphat and his people faced certain defeat and national extinction by a superior enemy horde (2 Chronicles 20:12).

## Jehoshaphat's Humble Condition
When Jehoshaphat heard the news of the approaching army, he was afraid (2 Chronicles 20:3). He did not immediately call for his generals or seek foreign alliances (2 Chronicles 20:3). Instead, he set his face to seek the LORD and proclaimed a fast throughout all Judah (2 Chronicles 20:3). All the people gathered from every city to seek help from the LORD (2 Chronicles 20:4). Jehoshaphat stood in the assembly at the temple and prayed for divine help (2 Chronicles 20:5-6). He acknowledged God's sovereignty and Judah's total helplessness (2 Chronicles 20:12).

## The Divine Calling
The Spirit of the LORD came upon Jahaziel, a Levite, in the midst of the assembly (2 Chronicles 20:14).

> "Listen, all Judah and inhabitants of Jerusalem and King Jehoshaphat: Thus says the LORD to you, '**Do not be afraid and do not be dismayed at this great horde, for the battle is not yours but God's.** Tomorrow go down against them. Behold, they will come up by the ascent of Ziz. You will find them at the end of the valley, east of the wilderness of Jeruel. **You will not need to fight in this battle. Stand firm, hold your position, and see the salvation of the LORD on your behalf**, O Judah and Jerusalem.' Do not be afraid and do not be dismayed. Tomorrow go out against them, and the LORD will be with you." — 2 Chronicles 20:15-17 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Jehoshaphat had to sacrifice his military strategy and reliance on conventional warfare (2 Chronicles 20:21). He risked his life, his throne, and the lives of his people by marching out to meet a superior enemy without a shield-wall in front (2 Chronicles 20:21). Instead of putting his best soldiers in the lead, he appointed singers to praise the LORD (2 Chronicles 20:21). He risked the total slaughter of his nation if the LORD did not intervene as promised (2 Chronicles 20:11-12).

## Jehoshaphat's Initial Response
Jehoshaphat's initial response to the threat was one of fear (2 Chronicles 20:3). However, he did not allow his fear to lead to rebellion or despair (2 Chronicles 20:3). He immediately turned to the LORD in prayer and led his people in a national fast (2 Chronicles 20:3-4). He did not express doubt in God's power but rather expressed his total dependence on it (2 Chronicles 20:12).

## Jehoshaphat's Faithful Reaction
After hearing the word of the LORD, Jehoshaphat bowed his head with his face to the ground (2 Chronicles 20:18). All Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem also fell down before the LORD, worshiping **Him** (2 Chronicles 20:18). The Levites stood up to praise the LORD with a very loud voice (2 Chronicles 20:19). The next morning, they rose early and went out into the wilderness of Tekoa (2 Chronicles 20:20). As they went out, Jehoshaphat stood and encouraged the people to believe in the LORD their God so they would be established (2 Chronicles 20:20). He told them to believe **His** prophets so they would succeed (2 Chronicles 20:20). When he had taken counsel with the people, he appointed those who were to sing to the LORD and praise **Him** in holy attire (2 Chronicles 20:21). These singers went before the army, declaring that the steadfast love of the LORD endures forever (2 Chronicles 20:21). Jehoshaphat chose to respond with praise before the victory was visible (2 Chronicles 20:21). He led his army not with weapons drawn, but with songs of thanksgiving (2 Chronicles 20:21). This act of faith demonstrated his complete trust in the word spoken by Jahaziel (2 Chronicles 20:15-17). He did not hesitate to follow the instruction to go down against the enemy (2 Chronicles 20:20). His leadership turned a moment of national terror into a procession of worship (2 Chronicles 20:21).

## The Divine Miracle
As they began to sing and praise, the LORD supernaturally intervened to destroy the invading army (2 Chronicles 20:22).

> "And when they began to sing and praise, **the LORD set an ambush against the men of Ammon, Moab, and Mount Seir, who had come against Judah, so that they were routed.** For the men of Ammon and Moab rose against the inhabitants of Mount Seir, devoting them to destruction, and when they had made an end of the inhabitants of Seir, **they all helped to destroy one another.**" — 2 Chronicles 20:22-23 (ESV)

The miracle fulfilled God's promise that **they would not need to fight** (2 Chronicles 20:17). The salvation of the LORD was seen as the enemies turned on each other until not one survived (2 Chronicles 20:24).

## The Lasting Result
When Jehoshaphat and his people reached the lookout, they saw only dead bodies on the ground (2 Chronicles 20:24). They spent three days gathering the immense amount of spoil, which was more than they could carry (2 Chronicles 20:25). On the fourth day, they assembled in the Valley of Beracah to bless the LORD (2 Chronicles 20:26). The army returned to Jerusalem with joy, playing harps, lyres, and trumpets (2 Chronicles 20:27-28). The fear of God came upon all the surrounding kingdoms when they heard how the LORD had fought against the enemies of Israel (2 Chronicles 20:29). Consequently, the kingdom of Jehoshaphat was at peace (2 Chronicles 20:30). God gave him rest on every side (2 Chronicles 20:30).

## Conclusion
Jehoshaphat faced a hopeless situation where three nations united to destroy Judah. He was limited by fear and a lack of military strength to face the great multitude. However, God promised that the battle was not his but God's. Jehoshaphat responded by humbling himself, seeking the LORD, and eventually marching out with singers in the lead. God intervened by causing the enemy armies to destroy each other. The result was a miraculous victory without Judah firing a single shot, followed by great wealth and national peace. Jehoshaphat sacrificed his military pride and security by trusting in praise over weapons. In the end, he gained a preserved kingdom, the respect of neighboring nations, and a deeper testimony of God's faithfulness. His story proves that we can trust the LORD even when the odds are stacked against us.
