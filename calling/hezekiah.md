# Hezekiah: a Prayer for Life

A king receives a death sentence from a prophet but refuses to accept it as the final word. His desperate prayer in the face of mortality and a looming invasion reveals the heart of a God Who hears and heals. Discover how faith added years to a life.

## The Dual Crisis
King Hezekiah ruled over Judah during a period of intense geopolitical pressure (2 Kings 18:1). The Assyrian Empire had already destroyed the northern kingdom of Israel and was now threatening Jerusalem (2 Kings 18:9,13). Sennacherib, the king of Assyria, sent messengers to mock Hezekiah and the LORD (2 Kings 18:19,25, 19:10-13). Amidst this national crisis, Hezekiah became sick and was at the point of death (Isaiah 38:1). The prophet Isaiah came to him with a message from the LORD (Isaiah 38:1). He told the king to set his house in order because he would not recover (Isaiah 38:1). Hezekiah faced the dual problem of an incurable illness and the imminent destruction of his kingdom by a ruthless enemy (Isaiah 37:1, 38:1).

## Hezekiah's Humble Condition
Hezekiah was in a state of deep physical and emotional distress (Isaiah 38:1,3). He turned his face to the wall and prayed to the LORD (Isaiah 38:2). He did not seek help from physicians or foreign gods but turned immediately to the God of Israel (Isaiah 38:2-3). He wept bitterly as he pleaded his case before the LORD (Isaiah 38:3). Hezekiah did not perform a fast or visit the temple at that moment (Isaiah 38:2). He simply cried out to the LORD from his sickbed (Isaiah 38:3).

## The Divine Calling
The word of the LORD came to Isaiah with a new message for the dying king.

> Go and say to Hezekiah, "Thus says the LORD, the God of David your father: **I have heard your prayer; I have seen your tears. Behold, I will add fifteen years to your life. I will deliver you and this city out of the hand of the king of Assyria, and will defend this city.** And this shall be the sign to you from the LORD, that the LORD will do this thing that He has promised: **Behold, I will make the shadow cast by the declining sun on the dial of Ahaz turn back ten steps.**" — Isaiah 38:5-8 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Hezekiah had to sacrifice his reliance on his own royal power and understanding (Isaiah 37:1-4). He risked appearing weak to his subjects and the surrounding nations by admitting his total dependence on the LORD (Isaiah 38:2-3). He had to let go of the natural expectation of death and trust a supernatural promise (Isaiah 38:1,5). He surrendered his life and the future of Jerusalem into the hands of the LORD (Isaiah 37:14-20).

## Hezekiah's Initial Response
Hezekiah's initial response to the news of his death was one of intense grief and immediate prayer (Isaiah 38:2-3). He did not rebel against the LORD'S first message through Isaiah (Isaiah 38:1). He accepted the gravity of his situation but appealed to the LORD'S mercy (Isaiah 38:3). He reminded the LORD of his faithful walk and whole heart (Isaiah 38:3).

## Hezekiah's Faithful Reaction
Hezekiah responded to the new promise with faith and gratitude (Isaiah 38:9). He asked for a sign to confirm that the LORD would indeed heal him (2 Kings 20:8). When given the choice between the shadow going forward or backward, he chose the more difficult sign of the shadow turning back (2 Kings 20:9-10). He composed a song of praise to commemorate his deliverance from the pit of destruction (Isaiah 38:9-20). He acknowledged that it was for his own welfare that he had great bitterness (Isaiah 38:17). He recognized that the LORD had cast all his sins behind **His** back (Isaiah 38:17). Hezekiah also took the practical step of applying a cake of figs to his boil as the prophet instructed (Isaiah 38:21). He trusted that the LORD would use this simple means to bring about the promised healing (Isaiah 38:21). Hezekiah's trust extended beyond his personal health to the safety of Jerusalem (Isaiah 37:14-20). When he received the threatening letter from the Assyrians, he went up to the house of the LORD and spread it before **Him** (Isaiah 37:14). He prayed for deliverance not just for safety but so that all the kingdoms of the earth would know that the LORD is God alone (Isaiah 37:20). Hezekiah remained steadfast in his trust even when the situation looked hopeless (Isaiah 37:1). He believed that the God Who could move the sun could also move the heart of a king and destroy an invading army (Isaiah 37:33-35, 38:8).

## The Prophetic Miracle
The LORD performed a double miracle of physical healing and national deliverance. **He** moved the sun to provide a sign and sent an angel to destroy the Assyrian host.

> "**So the sun turned back on the dial the ten steps by which it had declined.** . . **And the angel of the LORD went out and struck down 185,000 in the camp of the Assyrians.** . . Thus says the LORD, the God of David your father: 'I have heard your prayer; I have seen your tears. Behold, I will add fifteen years to your life. I will deliver you and this city out of the hand of the king of Assyria, and will defend this city.'" — Isaiah 37:36, 38:5,8 (ESV)

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in Hezekiah living for another fifteen years (Isaiah 38:5). Jerusalem was miraculously spared from the Assyrian conquest (Isaiah 37:36-37). The king of Assyria departed and returned to Nineveh where he was later killed (Isaiah 37:37-38). The nations around Judah saw the power of the LORD and the favor **He** had shown to Hezekiah (2 Chronicles 32:23). Hezekiah was exalted in the sight of all nations from then on (2 Chronicles 32:23). His healing and the city's deliverance became a testimony to the faithfulness of the God of Israel (Isaiah 38:19). The next generation was able to continue in the land because of this intervention (Isaiah 38:19).

## Conclusion
Hezekiah faced a double crisis of terminal illness and national destruction. His own strength and royal status were useless against these problems. Despite his personal limitations and the finality of the prophet's initial word, he turned to the LORD in desperate prayer. God responded with a promise of healing and protection. Hezekiah sacrificed his reliance on earthly power and accepted the LORD'S supernatural sign. The miracle of the sun's shadow and the destruction of the Assyrian army proved that God is faithful to those who trust **Him**. Hezekiah gained fifteen years of life and the preservation of his kingdom. His story teaches us that the LORD is never far from those who cry out to **Him**. **He** is the God Who hears prayers and delivers **His** people from the shadow of death.
