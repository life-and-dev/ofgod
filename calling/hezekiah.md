# Hezekiah: Facing an Invincible Foe

A king receives a letter of surrender from the most powerful empire on earth. With his capital surrounded and his people terrified, he must decide whether to trust in human alliances or the word of an invisible God. His choice will determine the fate of a nation.

## The Assyrian Threat
Sennacherib, king of Assyria, had conquered all the fortified cities of Judah (2 Kings 18:13). Hezekiah had tried to pay tribute by stripping the gold from the doors of the temple, but the Assyrian threat remained (2 Kings 18:14-16). Sennacherib sent his Rabshakeh to Jerusalem with a massive army (2 Kings 18:17). The Assyrians mocked Hezekiah and the LORD, claiming that no god had ever delivered a nation from their hand (2 Kings 18:33-35). They warned the people not to let Hezekiah deceive them with promises of divine help (2 Kings 18:29-30). The people were silent and in deep distress (2 Kings 18:36-37). Jerusalem was the last stronghold left in a land devastated by war (2 Kings 18:13). The problem was that the kingdom of Judah faced total annihilation by an unstoppable military machine (2 Kings 19:3).

## Hezekiah's Humble Condition
Hezekiah was a righteous king who trusted in the LORD (2 Kings 18:5). When he heard the mocking message from the Rabshakeh, he rent his clothes and covered himself with sackcloth (2 Kings 19:1). He went into the house of the LORD to seek **Him** (2 Kings 19:1). He also sent his officials to Isaiah the prophet to request prayer for the remnant (2 Kings 19:2-4). Hezekiah specifically turned to the LORD in his moment of extreme desperation (2 Kings 19:1). He did not seek another political alliance but waited for a word from God (2 Kings 19:1-5).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke to Hezekiah through the prophet Isaiah to reassure him and to judge the arrogance of Sennacherib.

> Thus says the LORD: Do not be afraid because of the words that you have heard, with which the servants of the king of Assyria have reviled Me. Behold, **I will put a spirit in him, so that he shall hear a rumor and return to his own land, and I will make him fall by the sword in his own land**. — 2 Kings 19:6-7 (ESV)

After Hezekiah received a threatening letter, the LORD spoke again:

> Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel: Your prayer to Me about Sennacherib king of Assyria I have heard. This is the word that the LORD has spoken concerning him: She despises you, she scorns you. The virgin daughter of Zion. She wags her head behind you. The daughter of Jerusalem. Whom have you mocked and reviled? Against Whom have you raised your voice and lifted your eyes to the heights? Against the Holy One of Israel! By your messengers you have mocked the LORD, and you have said, With my many chariots I have gone up the heights of the mountains, to the far recesses of Lebanon. I cut down its tallest cedars, its choicest cypresses. I entered its farthest lodging place, its most fruitful forest. I dug wells and drank foreign waters, and I dried up with the sole of My foot all the streams of Egypt. Have you not heard that I determined it long ago? I planned from days of old what now I bring to pass, that you should make fortified cities crash into heaps of ruins. While their inhabitants, shorn of strength, are dismayed and confounded, and have become like plants of the field and like tender grass, like grass on the housetops, blighted before it is grown. But I know your sitting down and your going out and coming in, and your raging against Me. Because you have raged against Me and your complacency has come to My ears, **I will put My hook in your nose and My bit in your mouth, and I will turn you back on the way by which you came**. And this shall be the sign for you: this year eat what grows of itself, and in the second year what springs of the same. Then in the third year sow and reap and plant vineyards, and eat their fruit. And the surviving remnant of the house of Judah shall again take root downward and bear fruit upward. For out of Jerusalem shall go a remnant, and out of Mount Zion a band of survivors. The zeal of the LORD will do this. Therefore thus says the LORD concerning the king of Assyria: **He shall not come into this city or shoot an arrow there, or come before it with a shield or cast up a siege mound against it.** By the way that he came, by the same he shall return, and he shall not come into this city, declares the LORD. For **I will defend this city to save it**, for My own sake and for the sake of My servant David. — 2 Kings 19:20-34 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Hezekiah sacrificed his pride and his reliance on political alliances like Egypt (2 Kings 18:21). He risked the immediate destruction of Jerusalem by refusing to surrender to Sennacherib (2 Kings 18:30). He placed the lives of his entire population on the line by trusting in a miraculous intervention (2 Kings 19:10-13,19). He chose to trust the word of a prophet over the visible might of a superior army (2 Kings 19:6-7,32). He gave up the option of a peaceful, albeit enslaved, existence (2 Kings 18:31-32).

## Hezekiah's Initial Response
Hezekiah was initially terrified and sent a message of deep distress to Isaiah (2 Kings 19:3). He acknowledged that the day was a day of distress, rebuke, and disgrace (2 Kings 19:3). He compared the nation to a woman who had no strength to give birth (2 Kings 19:3). However, he did not rebel against God or turn back to idols (2 Kings 19:1). He immediately sought the LORD'S help instead of despairing or surrendering (2 Kings 19:1).

## Hezekiah's Faithful Reaction
Hezekiah received a second, more threatening letter from Sennacherib's messengers (2 Kings 19:14). He went up to the house of the LORD and spread the letter before the LORD (2 Kings 19:14). He prayed a fervent prayer acknowledging that the LORD alone is God over all the kingdoms of the earth (2 Kings 19:15). He asked the LORD to bow down **His** ear and hear, and to open **His** eyes and see (2 Kings 19:16). He admitted that the Assyrians had indeed destroyed other nations, but noted those nations' gods were only wood and stone (2 Kings 19:17-18). He begged the LORD to save them so that all kingdoms might know that **He** alone is God (2 Kings 19:19). Hezekiah remained faithful and clung to the LORD (2 Kings 18:6). He did not depart from following **Him** but kept the commandments (2 Kings 18:6). He waited patiently for the LORD to fulfill the promise given through Isaiah (2 Kings 19:20,35).

## The Divine Miracle
The LORD struck the Assyrian camp and protected Jerusalem without a single arrow being fired.

> And that night the angel of the LORD went out and struck 185,000 in the camp of the Assyrians. And when people arose early in the morning, behold, these were all dead bodies. Then Sennacherib king of Assyria **departed and went home and lived at Nineveh**. And as he was worshiping in the house of Nisroch his god, Adrammelech and Sharezer, his sons, **struck him down with the sword** and escaped into the land of Ararat. And Esarhaddon his son reigned in his stead. — 2 Kings 19:35-37 (ESV)

God fulfilled **His** promise that Sennacherib would **not come into this city or shoot an arrow there** and that **I will defend this city to save it** (2 Kings 19:32,34). Sennacherib did indeed **return to his own land** and **fall by the sword in his own land** as the LORD had spoken (2 Kings 19:7,37).

## The Lasting Result
Jerusalem was miraculously delivered from a certain slaughter (2 Kings 19:35). The surrounding nations saw the power of the LORD and the faithfulness of Hezekiah (2 Chronicles 32:23). Many brought gifts to the LORD and precious things to Hezekiah so that he was exalted in the sight of all nations (2 Chronicles 32:23). The remnant of the house of Judah again took root downward and bore fruit upward (2 Kings 19:30). Hezekiah's trust preserved the Davidic line and the holy city for another generation (2 Kings 19:34). The king of Assyria was humbled and eventually killed by his own sons (2 Kings 19:37). The threat that had once seemed invincible was completely removed by the hand of the LORD (2 Kings 19:35).

## Conclusion
Hezekiah faced the overwhelming might of the Assyrian Empire with no human hope of victory. His kingdom was reduced to a single city, and his faith was publicly mocked. He acknowledged his own limitations and turned to the LORD in humble prayer. God promised to defend the city and judge the arrogant king. Hezekiah responded by spreading his problems before the LORD. God intervened by sending an angel to destroy the enemy army. Hezekiah sacrificed the security of surrender, but he gained the miraculous salvation of his people and the exaltation of God's name. This story shows that no enemy is too great when we trust in the LORD'S faithfulness. **He** is the God Who defends those who call upon **Him**. Despite Hezekiah's earlier failure to buy peace, the LORD did not forsake him in his hour of need.
