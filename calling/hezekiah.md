# Hezekiah: Facing an Unstoppable Army

A godly king finds his city surrounded by the world's most powerful empire with no hope of military victory. His only defense is a mocking letter and a desperate prayer. What happens when a leader chooses to trust a silent God over a visible and unstoppable army?

## The Problem of an Assyrian Invasion
Sennacherib, king of Assyria, had already conquered all the fortified cities of Judah (2 Kings 18:13). He sent a great army to Jerusalem and delivered a message through his Rabshakeh to mock King Hezekiah and the LORD (2 Kings 18:17,19). The Assyrians claimed that no god of any nation had been able to deliver them from their hand (2 Kings 18:33-35). They warned the people not to trust in Hezekiah or the LORD (2 Kings 18:30). They suggested that the LORD **Himself** had told them to destroy the land (2 Kings 18:25). Hezekiah's people were silent and terrified as they faced certain destruction (2 Kings 18:36). The Assyrian king demanded a massive tribute of gold and silver (2 Kings 18:14). Hezekiah paid this by stripping the temple, yet the threat remained (2 Kings 18:15-16). Jerusalem was like a booth in a vineyard, isolated and besieged by an overwhelming force (Isaiah 1:8). The problem was that Israel's military power was non-existent compared to Assyria and their only hope was divine intervention (2 Kings 19:3).

## Hezekiah's Humble Condition
When Hezekiah heard the words of the Rabshakeh, he tore his clothes and covered himself with sackcloth (2 Kings 19:1). He went into the house of the LORD to seek **Him** in deep humility (2 Kings 19:1). He sent his officials to Isaiah the prophet to ask for prayer (2 Kings 19:2-4). Hezekiah acknowledged his complete helplessness (2 Kings 19:3). He compared the situation to a woman in labor who has no strength to deliver her child (2 Kings 19:3). He did not rely on his own wisdom but humbled himself before the LORD (2 Kings 19:1).

## The Divine Calling
The LORD spoke to Hezekiah through the prophet Isaiah, the son of Amoz. Isaiah sent a message to the king containing the exact words of the LORD in response to Hezekiah's prayer regarding Sennacherib.

> "Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel: Because you have prayed to Me concerning Sennacherib king of Assyria, this is the word that the LORD has spoken concerning him:
>

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Hezekiah had to sacrifice his pride and his reliance on military alliances (2 Kings 18:21). He risked the total destruction of Jerusalem and his own life by refusing to surrender to the Assyrian king (2 Kings 19:10). He chose to trust in the word of a prophet rather than the visible power of the world's greatest army (2 Kings 19:6-7). He risked being remembered as the king who allowed his people to be slaughtered (2 Kings 18:30).

## The Initial Response
Before this crisis, Hezekiah had tried to appease Sennacherib by paying a massive tribute of three hundred talents of silver and thirty talents of gold (2 Kings 18:14). He even stripped the gold from the doors and doorposts of the temple of the LORD to give it to the king of Assyria (2 Kings 18:16). This initial attempt to buy peace through compromise failed to stop the Assyrian advance (2 Kings 18:17). However, when the final threat came, he did not rebel against God but turned to **Him** immediately (2 Kings 19:1).

## Hezekiah's Faithful Reaction
Hezekiah's ultimate reaction was one of complete reliance on the LORD (2 Kings 19:14). When he received the second, even more threatening letter from Sennacherib's messengers, he went up to the house of the LORD (2 Kings 19:14). He spread the letter out before the LORD, symbolically presenting the problem to **Him** (2 Kings 19:14). He prayed a powerful prayer, acknowledging that while the Assyrians had indeed destroyed other nations and their man-made gods, the LORD was the only true God (2 Kings 19:15-18). He asked the LORD to save them so that all the kingdoms of the earth would know that **He** alone is God (2 Kings 19:19). Hezekiah did not try to negotiate further or look to Egypt for help (Isaiah 30:1-2). He waited for God's response through the prophet Isaiah and held his ground in the besieged city (2 Kings 19:6-7). He encouraged his people to be strong and courageous (2 Chronicles 32:7). He told them that with the king of Assyria was an arm of flesh, but with them was the LORD their God to help them and to fight their battles (2 Chronicles 32:8). The people took confidence from his words (2 Chronicles 32:8). Hezekiah also took practical steps to prepare the city, such as blocking the water sources and building the Broad Wall (2 Chronicles 32:2-5,30). Yet, his heart remained fixed on the promise of the LORD rather than his own fortifications (2 Chronicles 32:8). He proved that he trusted the LORD more than he feared the most powerful man on earth (2 Kings 18:5).

## The Divine Miracle
God acted swiftly and decisively to fulfill the promise **He** made through Isaiah (Isaiah 37:36). **He** sent an angel to the Assyrian camp to destroy the army that had mocked **Him** (2 Kings 19:35).

> "And that night **the angel of the LORD went out and struck 185,000 in the camp of the Assyrians.** And when people arose early in the morning, behold, these were all dead bodies. Then **Sennacherib king of Assyria departed and went home and lived at Nineveh.** And as he was worshiping in the house of Nisroch his god, Adrammelech and Sharezer, his sons, struck him down with the sword and escaped into the land of Ararat. And Esarhaddon his son reigned in his stead." — 2 Kings 19:35-37 (ESV)

The miracle fulfilled God's promise that the king of Assyria would not enter the city or even shoot an arrow there (2 Kings 19:32). **He** defended the city just as **He** had said **He** would (2 Kings 19:34).

## The Lasting Result
The miraculous deliverance resulted in the immediate preservation of Jerusalem and the survival of the kingdom of Judah (2 Kings 19:35). The surrounding nations saw the power of the LORD and brought gifts to Hezekiah, and he was exalted in their sight (2 Chronicles 32:23). The threat from Assyria was broken, and Sennacherib never returned to bother Judah again (2 Kings 19:36). Hezekiah's faith became a legend (2 Kings 18:5). He was remembered as a king who trusted in the LORD more than any king of Judah before or after him (2 Kings 18:5). The remnant of Judah was allowed to take root downward and bear fruit upward, just as Isaiah had prophesied (Isaiah 37:31). The temple remained a place of worship, and the line of David was preserved (2 Kings 19:34). Most importantly, the name of the LORD was glorified among the nations as the God **Who** truly delivers **His** people (2 Kings 19:19).

## Conclusion
Hezekiah faced the problem of an overwhelming Assyrian invasion that mocked his God and threatened his kingdom. Despite his initial failure to buy peace with temple gold, he eventually responded with deep humility and absolute trust. He sacrificed his military security to rely on the word of the LORD. God promised to defend Jerusalem and turn the enemy back. **He** kept this promise by sending an angel to strike the Assyrian army. The result was a total victory without a single arrow being fired by the Judeans, leading to national survival and the exaltation of the LORD'S name. Hezekiah sacrificed his own understanding and resources, but he gained the protection of the Almighty and the preservation of his people. His story teaches us that when we are surrounded by impossible circumstances, we can trust in the faithfulness of the LORD **Who** defends those who seek **Him**.
