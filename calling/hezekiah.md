# Hezekiah: Kingdom Under Siege

A massive army surrounds the capital city, demanding total surrender and mocking the power of the Creator. The king has already tried to buy peace with gold, but now his only hope lies in a desperate prayer. Discover how a single night of divine intervention changed the fate of a nation.

## The Threat of Assyrian Conquest
Hezekiah was the king of Judah (2 Kings 18:1). The Assyrian empire was expanding rapidly and had already destroyed the northern kingdom of Israel (2 Kings 18:9-12). Sennacherib, the king of Assyria, invaded the fortified cities of Judah and captured them (2 Kings 18:13). Hezekiah attempted to buy off the invader with silver and gold from the temple, but the Assyrians still marched on Jerusalem (2 Kings 18:14-17). The Rabshakeh, an Assyrian official, stood before the walls and shouted insults at the people (2 Kings 18:19-25). He claimed that no god could deliver them from the hand of the Assyrian king (2 Kings 18:33-35). He mocked Hezekiah for trusting in the LORD (2 Kings 18:30). The political environment was one of absolute terror and impending doom (2 Kings 19:1-3). The people of Jerusalem were trapped and outnumbered by a ruthless military machine (2 Kings 18:17). The clear problem was that the city faced certain destruction and the people faced exile or death at the hands of a mocking enemy (2 Kings 19:10-11).

## Hezekiah's Faithful Condition
Hezekiah was a righteous king who did what was right in the eyes of the LORD (2 Kings 18:3). He removed high places and broke pillars of idolatry (2 Kings 18:4). He trusted in the LORD so that none like him were among the kings of Judah (2 Kings 18:5). He held fast to the LORD and did not depart from following **Him** (2 Kings 18:6). Before God responded, Hezekiah went to the temple in sackcloth (2 Kings 19:1). He did not seek special favor but simply turned to the LORD in distress (2 Kings 19:1).

## The Divine Calling
God spoke to Hezekiah through the prophet Isaiah to assure him of the coming victory and the downfall of the Assyrian king.

> Then Isaiah the son of Amoz sent to Hezekiah, saying, “Thus says the LORD, the God of Israel: Your prayer to Me about Sennacherib king of Assyria I have heard.” This is the word that the LORD has spoken concerning him:
>
> “She despises you, she scorns you— the virgin daughter of Zion; she wags her head behind you— the daughter of Jerusalem. Whom have you mocked and reviled? Against whom have you raised your voice and lifted your eyes to the heights? Against the Holy One of Israel! By your messengers you have mocked the LORD, and you have said, ‘With my many chariots I have gone up the heights of the mountains, to the far recesses of Lebanon; I felled its tallest cedars, its choicest cypresses; I entered its farthest lodging place, its most fruitful forest. I dug wells and drank foreign waters, and I dried up with the sole of my foot all the streams of Egypt.’
>
> Have you not heard that I determined it long ago? I planned from days of old what now I bring to pass, that you should turn fortified cities into heaps of ruins, while their inhabitants, shorn of strength, are dismayed and confounded, and have become like plants of the field and like tender grass, like grass on the housetops, blighted before it is grown. But I know your sitting down and your going out and coming in, and your raging against Me. Because you have raged against Me and your complacency has come into My ears, **I will put My hook in your nose and My bit in your mouth, and I will turn you back on the way by which you came.**
>
> And this shall be the sign for you: this year eat what grows of itself, and in the second year what springs of the same. Then in the third year sow and reap and plant vineyards, and eat their fruit. And **the surviving remnant of the house of Judah shall again take root downward and bear fruit upward.** For out of Jerusalem shall go a remnant, and out of Mount Zion a band of survivors. The zeal of the LORD will do this.
>
> Therefore thus says the LORD concerning the king of Assyria: **He shall not come into this city or shoot an arrow there, or come before it with a shield or cast up a siege mound against it. By the way that he came, by the same he shall return, and he shall not come into this city**, declares the LORD. For **I will defend this city to save it, for My own sake and for the sake of My servant David.**” — 2 Kings 19:20-34 (ESV)

## The Necessary Sacrifice
Hezekiah had to sacrifice his own efforts to save the kingdom through diplomacy or bribery (2 Kings 18:14-16). He risked his life and the lives of all his subjects by refusing to surrender to Sennacherib (2 Kings 19:10-13). He had to sacrifice his reputation as a wise leader in the eyes of the Assyrians, who saw his trust as foolishness (2 Kings 18:20). He gave up his pride and humbled himself before the LORD (2 Kings 19:1). He sacrificed the comfort of a peaceful reign to face the terror of an imminent siege (2 Kings 19:3).

## Hezekiah's Initial Response
When King Hezekiah heard the words of the Rabshakeh, he tore his clothes and covered himself with sackcloth (2 Kings 19:1). He was deeply distressed and sent for the prophet Isaiah (2 Kings 19:2). He confessed that it was a day of trouble, rebuke, and disgrace (2 Kings 19:3). He expressed fear that the Assyrians would prevail if the LORD did not intervene (2 Kings 19:4). His initial reaction was one of grief and urgent alarm (2 Kings 19:1). He recognized that his own strength was insufficient for the coming battle (2 Kings 19:3).

## Hezekiah's Fervent Reaction
Hezekiah eventually responded to the threat by turning completely to the LORD in prayer (2 Kings 19:14). When he received the threatening letter from the messengers of Sennacherib, he went up to the house of the LORD (2 Kings 19:14). He spread the letter out before the LORD as a physical sign of the burden he carried (2 Kings 19:14). Hezekiah prayed and acknowledged that the LORD alone is God over all the kingdoms of the earth (2 Kings 19:15). He confessed that **He** is the one **Who** made heaven and earth (2 Kings 19:15). He asked the LORD to incline **His** ear and hear the words of Sennacherib (2 Kings 19:16). He admitted that the Assyrians had indeed destroyed many nations and cast their gods into the fire (2 Kings 19:17-18). He acknowledged that those were not gods but the work of men's hands (2 Kings 19:18). Hezekiah then pleaded for salvation so that all the kingdoms of the earth might know that the LORD alone is God (2 Kings 19:19). He chose to trust the word of the LORD delivered through the prophet Isaiah (2 Kings 19:20). He did not seek an alliance with Egypt or any other nation at this critical moment (2 Kings 19:14-19). He waited for the LORD'S answer while the enemy remained at the gates (2 Kings 19:20). He remained faithful to his commitment to the LORD despite the visible power of the Assyrian army (2 Kings 18:5-6). His reaction was one of total reliance on the sovereignty and faithfulness of God (2 Kings 19:19).

## The Divine Miracle
God sent **His** angel to destroy the Assyrian army and save Jerusalem from destruction (2 Kings 19:35).

> And that night **the angel of the LORD went out and struck down 185,000 in the camp of the Assyrians.** And when people arose early in the morning, behold, these were all dead bodies. . . For **I will defend this city to save it, for My own sake and for the sake of My servant David.** — 2 Kings 19:34-35 (ESV)

By striking down the massive host, the LORD fulfilled **His** promise that the king of Assyria would **not come into this city or shoot an arrow there** (2 Kings 19:32). **He** defended Jerusalem just as **He** had spoken through the prophet Isaiah (2 Kings 19:34).

## The Lasting Result
The miracle resulted in the immediate withdrawal of the Assyrian army from the land of Judah (2 Kings 19:36). Sennacherib returned to Nineveh and was later killed by his own sons while worshiping in the house of his god (2 Kings 19:37). The city of Jerusalem was spared from destruction and exile (2 Kings 19:34). The neighboring nations saw the power of the LORD and the deliverance **He** provided for His people (2 Chronicles 32:23). Hezekiah's faith was vindicated, and he was exalted in the sight of all nations (2 Chronicles 32:23). The kingdom of Judah experienced a period of peace and prosperity following this divine intervention (2 Chronicles 32:22). The word of the LORD through Isaiah proved to be true and reliable (2 Kings 19:35).

## Conclusion
Hezekiah faced the overwhelming problem of an Assyrian invasion that threatened to wipe out his kingdom. He was limited by a small army and the failure of his previous attempt to buy peace with gold. However, the LORD promised to defend Jerusalem and strike down the enemy. Hezekiah responded by humbling himself in prayer and trusting God's word through the prophet. God's intervention through the death of the Assyrian army in a single night proved **His** absolute faithfulness. Hezekiah sacrificed his reliance on earthly riches and diplomacy, but he gained the survival of his nation and the glory of God's name. His story demonstrates that we can trust the LORD even when surrounded by impossible odds. **He** is the God **Who** hears our prayers and never forsakes those who put their trust in **Him**.
