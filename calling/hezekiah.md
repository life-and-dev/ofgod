# Hezekiah: Trusting the LORD of Hosts

A king stands on the walls of his last remaining city while a massive, undefeated army surrounds him. The enemy general mocks his faith and promises total destruction. He must decide if he will surrender to save his life or trust in a God he cannot see.

## The Assyrian Threat
The kingdom of Judah was in great peril (2 Kings 18:13). Sennacherib king of Assyria had come up against all the fortified cities of Judah and took them (2 Kings 18:13). The Assyrians were known for their extreme cruelty and military might (2 Kings 18:33-35). Hezekiah had tried to pay off the king of Assyria with silver and gold from the temple (2 Kings 18:14-15). However, Sennacherib was not satisfied and sent a great army to Jerusalem (2 Kings 18:17). The Rabshakeh mocked Hezekiah and the LORD, saying that no god of any nation had delivered his land out of the hand of the king of Assyria (2 Kings 18:33). He told the people not to let Hezekiah deceive them into trusting the LORD (2 Kings 18:30). The problem was that Judah was a small nation facing a world empire that had never been defeated (2 Kings 19:10-13).

## Hezekiah's Faithful Condition
Hezekiah did what was right in the eyes of the LORD (2 Kings 18:3). He removed the high places and broke the pillars (2 Kings 18:4). He trusted in the LORD, the God of Israel (2 Kings 18:5). He held fast to the LORD (2 Kings 18:6). He did not depart from following **Him** (2 Kings 18:6). Hezekiah did nothing special to get God's attention before the LORD responded (2 Kings 18:7). He simply lived a life of constant trust and obedience (2 Kings 18:6).

## The Divine Word
When Hezekiah heard the words of the Rabshakeh, he went into the house of the LORD and sent to Isaiah the prophet (2 Kings 19:1-2). The LORD spoke to Hezekiah through Isaiah (2 Kings 19:20).

## The King's Sacrifice
Hezekiah had to sacrifice his own pride and his desire to take matters into his own hands (2 Kings 19:14-15). He risked the total slaughter of every inhabitant of Jerusalem if the LORD did not intervene (2 Kings 18:27). He sacrificed the safety of a surrender to Babylonian or Egyptian alliances (2 Kings 18:21, 20:12). He put the lives of his entire family and nation on the line based solely on a prophetic word (2 Kings 19:19).

## The Initial Response
When the threat first came, Hezekiah was filled with deep distress (2 Kings 19:1). He tore his clothes and covered himself with sackcloth (2 Kings 19:1). He initially tried to solve the problem with money from the temple of the LORD (2 Kings 18:15-16). He admitted to the king of Assyria that he had done wrong and offered to pay whatever was demanded (2 Kings 18:14).

## Hezekiah's Bold Reaction
After his initial attempt at appeasement failed, Hezekiah turned completely to the LORD (2 Kings 19:1). He went into the house of the LORD to seek **Him** (2 Kings 19:1). He sent his officials to Isaiah the prophet to ask for prayer (2 Kings 19:2-4). When he received a threatening letter from the king of Assyria, he did not hide it or panic (2 Kings 19:14). Instead, he went up to the house of the LORD and spread it before the LORD (2 Kings 19:14). He prayed a powerful prayer acknowledging that the LORD alone is God (2 Kings 19:15). He asked the LORD to bow down **His** ear and hear the mockery of Sennacherib (2 Kings 19:16). He acknowledged that the Assyrians had indeed destroyed other nations and their gods (2 Kings 19:17-18). However, he pleaded with the LORD to save Judah so that all the kingdoms of the earth might know that the LORD alone is God (2 Kings 19:19). He chose to trust the word of the LORD given through Isaiah even as the enemy was at the gate (2 Kings 19:20). He did not surrender despite the terrifying speech of the Rabshakeh (2 Kings 18:36). The people remained silent and trusted their king's leadership (2 Kings 18:36).

## The Midnight Miracle
The LORD acted swiftly to protect **His** people and fulfill **His** promise.

> And that night the angel of the LORD went out and **struck down 185,000 in the camp of the Assyrians**. And when people arose early in the morning, behold, these were all dead bodies. Then **Sennacherib king of Assyria departed and went home** and lived at Nineveh.
>
> — 2 Kings 19:35-36 (ESV)

The LORD fulfilled **His** promise that the king of Assyria **shall not come into this city** and that **I will defend this city to save it** (2 Kings 19:32,34).

## The Glorious Result
The kingdom of Judah was saved from certain destruction (2 Kings 19:35). The mockers were silenced and the pride of Assyria was broken (2 Kings 19:36). Sennacherib was later killed by his own sons while worshipping his god, proving that his god could not protect him (2 Kings 19:37). Hezekiah's faith was vindicated before his people and the surrounding nations (2 Kings 20:12). The LORD was glorified as the one true God **Who** has power over empires (2 Kings 19:19). The nation enjoyed a period of peace and prosperity under Hezekiah's reign (2 Kings 20:13,20).

## Conclusion
Hezekiah faced the impossible problem of a world empire threatening to wipe out his nation. He was a king with no military hope and a past failure of trying to buy off his enemy. Yet, the LORD promised to defend Jerusalem for **His** own sake. Hezekiah sacrificed his reputation and the safety of surrender to trust in the LORD. God's miracle in the Assyrian camp saved the city without a single arrow being fired by the Jews. The result was the preservation of the line of David and the glorification of God's name among the nations. Hezekiah sacrificed his gold and his pride, but he gained a miraculous deliverance and a legacy of faith. This proves that we can trust the LORD with our most important treasures, for **He** is faithful to **His** word.
