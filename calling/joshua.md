# Joshua: Starting a War

A new leader stands at the edge of a land filled with giants and fortified cities. He must follow in the footsteps of a legend while carrying the destiny of an entire nation. His courage will be tested by a fortress that seems impossible to conquer.

## Leadership Crisis
Moses the servant of the LORD was dead (Joshua 1:1). The nation of Israel stood at the banks of the Jordan River with no human way to cross into their inheritance (Joshua 3:1). The Promised Land was occupied by powerful nations living in great, walled cities (Numbers 13:28). The previous generation had failed to enter the land because of their unbelief and fear of the inhabitants (Numbers 14:1-4). Joshua was left to lead a people with a history of rebellion and stiff-necked behavior (Deuteronomy 9:6). He faced the daunting task of following the greatest leader Israel had ever known (Deuteronomy 34:10). The core problem was that the people needed to conquer a land they were physically overmatched to take while undergoing a transition of leadership (Joshua 1:2). They were surrounded by enemies who were stronger and taller than they were (Deuteronomy 9:1-2).

## Servant Heart
Joshua was the son of Nun and had served as Moses' assistant since his youth (Numbers 11:28). He was one of only two spies who had remained faithful and trusted God forty years earlier (Numbers 14:6-9,30). Joshua was possibly in a state of transition and mourning the death of Moses (Deuteronomy 34:8-9; Joshua 1:1-2). He was a man in whom was the Spirit (Numbers 27:18).

## Divine Commission
The LORD spoke directly to Joshua to command him to lead the people and to promise **His** presence.

> "Moses My servant is dead. Now therefore **arise, go over this Jordan, you and all this people, into the land** that I am giving to them, to the people of Israel. Every place that the sole of your foot will tread upon I have given to you, just as I promised to Moses. From the wilderness and this Lebanon as far as the great river, the river Euphrates, all the land of the Hittites to the Great Sea toward the going down of the sun shall be your territory. No man shall be able to stand before you all the days of your life. Just as I was with Moses, so I will be with you. I will not leave you or forsake you. Be strong and courageous, for you shall cause this people to inherit the land that I swore to their fathers to give them. Only be strong and very courageous, being careful to do according to all the law that Moses My servant commanded you. Do not turn from it to the right hand or to the left, that you may have good success wherever you go. **This Book of the Law shall not depart from your mouth, but you shall meditate on it day and night, so that you may be careful to do according to all that is written in it.** For then you will make your way prosperous, and then you will have good success. Have I not commanded you? **Be strong and courageous. Do not be frightened, and do not be dismayed**, for the LORD your God is with you wherever you go." — Joshua 1:2-9 (ESV)

## Command Weight
Joshua had to sacrifice his own plans and safety to lead millions of people into war (Joshua 1:2). He risked his reputation as a leader if the people failed or rebelled as they had with Moses (Numbers 14:10). He had to set aside his personal grief for Moses to fulfill the divine mandate (Joshua 1:1-2). He sacrificed his comfort and security to face the giants and the fortified cities of Canaan (Joshua 1:9). He placed his life on the line by standing at the front of the nation (Joshua 3:7).

## Initial Hesitation
The Bible does not record Joshua rebelling or refusing the call (Joshua 1:10). However, the LORD'S repeated command to be strong and courageous suggests that Joshua may have felt fear or intimidation (Joshua 1:6,7,9). He had seen the people try to stone him and Moses in the past (Numbers 14:10). The weight of replacing Moses was a burden that required divine reassurance (Joshua 1:5,17). He did not hesitate once the command was given (Joshua 1:10-11).

## Courageous Step
Joshua responded to the LORD'S calling with immediate and decisive action (Joshua 1:10). He commanded the officers of the people to prepare provisions for the journey (Joshua 1:11). He reminded the tribes of Reuben, Gad, and the half-tribe of Manasseh of their commitment to help their brothers conquer the land (Joshua 1:12-15). He sent two spies secretly to view the land and the city of Jericho (Joshua 2:1). Joshua rose early in the morning to lead the people from Shittim to the Jordan (Joshua 3:1). He obeyed the instruction to have the priests carry the ark into the water first (Joshua 3:8). He commanded the people to sanctify themselves for the wonders the LORD would do (Joshua 3:5). After crossing the Jordan, he followed the LORD'S command to circumcise the new generation and celebrate the Passover (Joshua 5:2-10). When he encountered the commander of the army of the LORD, he fell on his face and worshiped (Joshua 5:14). He followed the strange military strategy for Jericho without questioning the LORD (Joshua 6:6-12). He marched the people around the city once a day for six days in silence (Joshua 6:14). On the seventh day, he led them around the city seven times as commanded (Joshua 6:15). He gave the command to shout when the trumpets made the long blast (Joshua 6:16). He ensured that the city and everything in it was destroyed (Joshua 6:17). Joshua's reaction was one of meticulous obedience to every detail of the divine instructions (Joshua 6:27).

## Jericho Miracle
The LORD supernaturally destroyed the walls of Jericho to give the city to Israel. He demonstrated that the victory belonged to **Him** alone.

> So the people shouted, and the trumpets were blown. As soon as the people heard the sound of the trumpet, the people shouted a great shout, and **the wall fell down flat**, so that the people went up into the city, every man straight before him, and **they captured the city**... So **the LORD was with Joshua, and his fame was in all the land**. — Joshua 6:20,27 (ESV)

By causing the massive walls to collapse at the sound of a shout, the LORD fulfilled His promise that He would give the land to the people (Joshua 1:2). The fall of the city proved that He was indeed with Joshua just as He had been with Moses (Joshua 1:5, 6:27). No man was able to stand before them because the LORD fought for Israel (Joshua 1:5, 10:14).

## Canaan Conquest
The miracle at Jericho resulted in the total conquest of the first major stronghold in Canaan (Joshua 6:21). It struck terror into the hearts of all the other inhabitants of the land (Joshua 2:9-11, 5:1). Joshua's leadership was firmly established in the eyes of all Israel (Joshua 4:14). The people learned that their success depended on their obedience to the LORD'S commands (Joshua 1:8). The family of Rahab was spared because of her faith and became part of the nation of Israel (Joshua 6:25). The victory at Jericho opened the way for the further conquest of the Promised Land (Joshua 10:40). It served as a lasting testimony of the power of the LORD to deliver His people (Joshua 24:11). The nation of Israel finally began to possess the inheritance that had been promised to their fathers centuries before (Joshua 21:43-45).

## Conclusion
Joshua faced the overwhelming problem of leading a nation into war against fortified cities while following the greatest leader in their history. He was a humble servant who had to overcome the fear and intimidation of a monumental task. The LORD promised to be with him and to give him every place his foot would tread. Joshua responded with total obedience and courage, sacrificing his safety and personal comfort for the sake of the nation. God's intervention through the miracle of the falling walls of Jericho proved His absolute faithfulness to His word. Joshua sacrificed his reliance on conventional warfare, but he gained the victory for Israel and the fulfillment of the ancient covenant. This story demonstrates that we can trust the LORD in every transition. He is the God Who never leaves His servants and always provides a way to victory.
