# Identity

Also known as the "Holy Spirit" or "Set-apart Spirit" or "Holy Ghost" or "Ruach HaKadosh" depending on [Bible translation](../../word/translations.md).

[Holy](../../word/terms/holy.md) means something that belongs exclusively to God or that is set apart for God's purpose or benefit. So when writers write about the "Holy Spirit", it means this Spirit belongs to God.

This project understands the Holy Spirit as God's active Spirit, power, presence, and way of acting, proceeding from the Father—not a separate coequal person and not Jesus Himself. Nicene Christianity understands the Holy Spirit as a distinct divine person fully sharing the one divine essence with Father and Son, not a mode, creature, or divine part.

Peter and Paul use "the Spirit of Christ." [Trinitarian](../../son/trinity.md) readers commonly take this language with other personal-language texts as support for the Spirit's distinct personhood. This project reads it in context as God's Spirit at work in and through Christ, not as Jesus becoming the Spirit. Translation choices and shortened expressions require contextual reading; they do not by themselves settle the question.

Jesus never said that he will send his own spirit and Jesus never said that he will return in the form of an invisible spirit.

The context of the scriptures that are often used to "proof" that the Holy Spirit is Christ are:

| Verse            | Meaning of "the Spirit of Christ"                                                                                                                                       |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Romans 8:9       | "the Spirit" of [the body](../../kingdom/christ/body.md) of Christ versus "the law" of Moses (read the entire book especially Romans 2:29, 5:1-6, 7:4-6, 8:1-7; 1 Corinthians 12:4-30) |
| Galatians 4:6    | "the Spirit" that also accepted and declared God's Son (Matthew 3:16-17; Mark 1:10-11; Luke 3:21-22; Romans 1:3-4)                                                      |
| Philippians 1:19 | "the Spirit" that proclaims Christ's gospel (Philippians 1:15-18)                                                                                                       |
| 1 Peter 1:11     | "the Spirit" that prophesied Christ's sufferings (1 Peter 1:10,12)                                                                                                      |

These scriptures do not require the conclusion that Christ is the Spirit; they should be read in context.

# Attributes

* The Spirit gives life (Job 33:4; John 6:63)
* Nobody can direct or council the Spirit of the LORD (Isaiah 40:13)
* The Spirit is God's interaction with His creation (Luke 11:20), like "the finger of God".
* The Spirit was promised by Jesus (Luke 24:49; John 14:16-17,26, 15:26, 16:7-8; Acts 1:4-5,8)
* The Spirit is compared with the wind: The Spirit is invisible, but you see the Spirit's power (John 3:8)
* The Spirit does not speak in its own authority (John 16:13)
  * The Spirit does not lie (Numbers 23:19)

# What the Spirit do

## Personal language, agency, and personification

Scripture calls the Spirit Helper and attributes speech, teaching, grief, guidance, agency, will, and intercession to the Spirit (John 14:26; 16:13; Ephesians 4:30; Acts 8:29; 1 Corinthians 12:11; Romans 8:26-27). Nicene Christianity understands these as acts of the distinct divine person who fully shares the one divine essence with Father and Son.

This project retains every claim but reads it as God personally acting through His own Spirit: God helps, speaks, teaches, grieves over rebellion, guides, assigns gifts, and helps believers pray. The masculine Helper term (*paraklētos*) explains masculine grammar where it appears. Scripture also personifies Wisdom, sin, and death, so personal wording does not require a separate coequal person. This does not make the Spirit a mere force: it is God's real active presence and action. John 15:26 says the Spirit proceeds from the Father, and John 16:13 says it does not speak on its own authority.

* The Spirit searches everything, even the depths of God. (1 Corinthians 2:10-11)
* The Spirit instructs (Psalm 143:10; John 16:13-14, Acts 1:16), for example:
  * Israelites in the desert (Nehemiah 9:20)
  * Jesus (Matthew 4:1, Mark 1:12, Luke 4:1-2)
  * Simeon (Luke 2:25-32)
  * Ananias (Acts 9:17)
  * Peter (Acts 10:19-20)
  * Instructing Philip to join the Ethiopian (Acts 8:29)
  * Instructing Peter to join the Caesareans (Acts 11:12)
  * Instructing leaders of the church at Antioch to sent Paul and Barnabas for a mission (Acts 13:2)
  * Church leaders (Acts 15:28-29)
  * Paul (Acts 16:6-10, Acts 19:21, Acts 20:22-23)
* The Spirit anoints prophets and Jesus (Isaiah 61:1; Acts 10:38); Jesus receives and follows this anointing (Luke 3:22; 4:1, 18).
* The Spirit reveal God visibly (Ezekiel 39:29)
  * Stephen (Acts 7:55-60)
* The Spirit ministered to Jesus (Luke 4:18-19; Matthew 12:28)
* The Spirit reborn believers (John 3:6; Titus 3:5)
* The Spirit comforts (John 14:16,26 (KJV); John 15:26 (KJV))
* The Spirit helps (John 14:16,26 (ESV); John 15:26 (ESV); John 16:7; Romans 8:26)
* The Spirit guides in truth (John 16:13)
* The Spirit reveals the future as it is revealed to him (John 16:13; 1 Corinthians 2:10,13)
* The Spirit glorifies Jesus (John 16:14); Jesus sends the promised Spirit from the Father (John 15:26).
* The Spirit gives understanding and wisdom (Isaiah 11:2)
* The Spirit ministers to the body of Christ (1 Corinthians 12:4-30)

# The Spirit's characteristics

The Holy Spirit is God's Spirit by which He interacts with His creation; see [What is the Holy Spirit?](what-is-spirit/what-is-the-holy-spirit.md). This project therefore understands the Spirit's personal language as God's own personality and action present with believers, not as a separate coequal person. The fruit of someone led by God's Spirit is (Galatians 5:16-25): 
  * love
  * joy (1 Thessalonians 1:6-7), for example:
    * Disciples (Acts 13:52)
  * peace (John 15:26; Romans 14:16-17), for example:
    * Amasai, chief of the Thirty (1 Chronicles 12:17-18)
  * long-suffering (Romans 15:16), for example:
    * by interceding for believers (Romans 8:26-27) 
  * kindness
  * goodness
  * faith
  * meekness
  * self-control 

Another way to determine which spirit is leading a person is that you will notice that someone who is lead by God's Spirit:
  * will not curse Jesus (1 Corinthians 12:3)
  * says that "Jesus is Lord/Master" (1 Corinthians 12:3)
  * leads people to adopted sons (heirs) and glorification, instead of slavery and fear (Romans 8:15-17)

# The Spirit's relationships.

## When the Spirit become your enemy

* Caused by rebellion (Isaiah 63:10-13, Acts 7:51-53)
* Caused by blasphemy: speaking against the Holy Spirit (Matthew 12:31-32, Mark 3:28-29, Luke 12:10), e.g.
  * Ananias and his wife (Acts 5:3-10)

## When the Spirit become your friend

* Brings blessing/prosperity (Isaiah 32:13-15, Isaiah 44:3-4)
* Received at salvation (Psalm 51:11-12)

What the Holy Spirit does for believers:

* The Spirit enables leaders, for example:
  * Othniel the son of Kenaz (Judges 3:10)
  * Gideon (Judges 6:34)
  * Jephthah (Judges 11:29)
  * Saul (1 Samuel 11:6-7,10)
  * David (1 Samuel 16:13)
  * Amasai, chief of the Thirty (1 Chronicles 12:17-18)
  * Azariah the son of Oded (2 Chronicles 15:1)
  * Micah (Micah 3:8)
  * Jesus (Isaiah 42:1, Matthew 12:18)
  * Daniel (Daniel 6:3)
  * Church leaders (Acts 20:28, 1 Thessalonians 1:5)
* The Spirit enables people to be boldly witnesses to Jesus (Acts 1:8), for example:
    * Peter and apostles (Acts 4:8-10, Acts 5:29-32)
    * Congregation (Acts 4:31)
    * Paul (Acts 18:5, Romans 9:1-2)
* The Spirit enables people to experience God's presence (Psalm 51:11, Psalm 139:7, Haggai 2:4-5)
* The Spirit instruct people (Ezekiel 2:2, 3:24, 11:5; Acts 8:29, 11:12, 13:2)
* The Spirit leads believers' intercessions (Romans 8:26-27)
* The Spirit change believers back to the image of God (2 Corinthians 3:18)
* The Spirit seals believers until the day of redemption (Ephesians 1:13, Ephesians 4:30)
